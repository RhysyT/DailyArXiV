1. A Single Line-Emitter Identified in an Unbiased Search with JWST/NIRSpec: Multi-Object Coronagraphy for Slitless Spectroscopy[2610.12553]
Abstract

We report the detection of a previously unknown object with a single emission line within the Hubble Ultra Deep Field, using a new JWST NIRSpec technique called Multi-Object Coronagraphy for Slitless Spectroscopy (MOCSS) with the G140M/F100LP observing mode. The MOCSS technique involves opening almost all the shutters on the NIRSpec microshutter array, while simultaneously closing the shutters that lie on top of known low redshift objects, facilitating a wide field slitless spectroscopic search without contamination from bright objects. This observational technique has enabled the detection of a strong emission line in four different dither positions and in two position angles, and thus cannot be artificial. This line is detected at 1.60 $\rm \mu m$ with a line flux of $[2.5\pm 0.3]\times10^{-18} \rm erg\ s^{-1} cm^{-2}$ and a FWHM of $13.2\pm2.0$Å, and does not correspond to any known source with some of the deepest HST and JWST imaging in existence. Based upon the absence of imaging detection, we place a lower limit on the observed frame equivalent width (EW) of 7620Å on this object, which strongly limits the number of possible lines that this line can be. Given the wavelength, an EW limit that is near the ceiling of expectation, and the lack of other lines in the bandpass, this line cannot be easily explained by [OII] at $z=3.29$, [OIII] at $z=2.20$, H$\alpha$ at $z=1.44$, and Ly$\alpha$ at $z=12.16$. We discuss possible AGN and transient phenomena that could explain the finding.

2. When dust growth takes over: the origin of dust in post-reionization galaxies[2610.12591]
Abstract

The post-reionization epoch ($z\sim4$-6) marks a critical phase in galaxy evolution, during which galaxies rapidly assemble their stellar mass, enrich their interstellar medium, and build up substantial dust reservoirs. The physical processes responsible for such rapid dust enrichment remain uncertain. We investigate the dust enrichment history of the ALPINE-CRISTAL-JWST (ACJ) sample using the semi-analytic galaxy formation model GAlaxy Evolution and Assembly (GAEA), which follows galaxy assembly, chemical enrichment, and dust evolution. We select model galaxies matching the observed stellar masses, star formation rates, and metallicities of the ACJ sample, compare their dust properties with observed scaling relations, and trace their enrichment histories and environments. The model reproduces the observed dust mass-stellar mass relation and the dust-to-stellar mass, dust-to-gas, and dust-to-metal ratios as functions of stellar mass and metallicity. Reproducing these constraints requires efficient dust growth in the interstellar medium; stellar dust production alone cannot account for the observed dust reservoirs, even under the most optimistic assumptions. The combination of stellar mass, star formation rate, and metallicity provides a good predictor of galaxy dust content. Our model suggests rapid dust build-up consistent with the previously noted rapid metal enrichment, with evidence for a transition around $z\sim8$-9 below which dust growth becomes the dominant dust formation channel. Finally, ACJ-like galaxies preferentially reside in locally overdense environments, suggesting that environment may have contributed to their accelerated evolution. Taken together, these results support rapid and coupled stellar, metal, and dust build-up in ACJ galaxies during the post-reionization era.

3. Exploring Case B I: The Footprints of Hidden Radiation Fields in H I Balmer Decrements[2610.12715]
Abstract

Theory predicts that H I emission from photoionized clouds should lie close to the Case B limit established by Menzel and Baker, and observations of nearby nebulae have largely confirmed this expectation. However, recent observations of more distant high-redshift systems have revealed a small fraction with striking departures from Case B. What additional physics might be at work in these objects? This paper is part of a series exploring the physical processes that can produce such departures. In Paper I (D26), we show that Case B remains remarkably robust over a broad range of physical conditions, consistent with observations of nearby nebulae. Here, we explore one additional ingredient: a soft, non-ionizing radiation field. Such a continuum can strongly perturb the Balmer spectrum. The largest effects occur for radiation fields resembling log(Teff K)\sim 3.2-4.2 blackbodies, where photoexcitation from the metastable 2s level alters the hydrogen level populations and subsequent radiative cascade. The resulting Halpha/Hbeta and Hgamma/Hbeta ratios follow a different trajectory from dust reddening, allowing the two effects to be distinguished using multiple Balmer lines. In unresolved systems, a cool source located near the line-emitting gas may provide an additional radiation field distinct from the hot stars responsible for ionization. Balmer decrements may therefore carry information about otherwise hidden components of the radiation field surrounding the emitting gas.

4. Bridging Theory and Observation: Synthetic Far-infrared Insights into Star Formation Efficiency[2610.12729]
Abstract

The star formation efficiency per free-fall time ($\epsilon_{\rm ff}$) quantifies how efficiently giant molecular clouds (GMCs) convert gas into stars and is often observed to be orders of magnitude lower than expected for free-fall collapse. Observers typically estimate $\epsilon_{\rm ff}$ by mapping far-infrared (FIR) dust emission to infer gas surface densities ($\Sigma_{\rm gas}$) and counting embedded protostars, assuming simplified lifetimes and masses. Using the fiducial STARFORGE radiation-magnetohydrodynamics simulation of an $M_{\rm cl} = 2\times10^4 M_\odot$ GMC that self-consistently models star cluster formation and stellar feedback, we generate synthetic FIR observations and apply the same methodologies used in GMC surveys to investigate this discrepancy. We present the first $\epsilon_{\rm ff}-\Sigma_{\rm gas}$ analysis in a fully feedback-regulated GMC simulation that resolves the formation of stellar systems. Our synthetic measurements reproduce the low observationally inferred efficiencies, showing that feedback-regulated star formation naturally produces $\epsilon_{\rm ff} \sim 1-3\%$ without requiring extreme initial conditions. We also find that $\epsilon_{\rm ff}$ varies strongly over a GMC's lifetime, suggesting that much of the observed scatter reflects evolutionary sampling rather than intrinsic cloud-to-cloud differences. Finally, by comparing observational and simulation definitions, we show that methodological assumptions introduce systematic biases, with a transition near $\log \Sigma_{\rm gas} \approx 2.3 M_\odot {\rm pc}^{-2}$ that depends on resolution. Above this threshold, the smoothing of dense structure increases both the inferred free-fall time and enclosed gas, with the latter dominating and suppressing $\epsilon_{\rm ff}$. Below the threshold, the discrepancies are primarily driven by star formation rate assumptions.

5. Predicted state-to-state collisional rate coefficients from neural networks[2610.12743]
Abstract

Molecular spectra observed in the interstellar medium are commonly analyzed using non-local thermodynamic equilibrium models, which requires accurate state-to-state collisional rate coefficients. Close-coupling calculations become prohibitively expensive for transitions from highly excited rotational states, therefore available datasets are often truncated at low initial states and temperatures; and since each new system also requires an accurate ab initio potential energy surface, many detected molecules have no collisional data. We employ two machine learning strategies to predict rotational state-to-state rate coefficients of molecules included in the train set beyond the range of initial states and temperatures computed so far, and predicting those of a molecule absent from training and validation sets. Feed-forward neural networks are trained on state-to-state de-excitation rate coefficients of 24 linear molecules colliding with para-H2. Scheme 1 includes all systems in the train+validation set, with 70%, 80%, and 90% initial-state boundaries and a 90% temperature boundary. Scheme 2 is trained on 22 systems described only by transition and molecular properties, and predicts two excluded systems, CS and CF+, whose astrophysical impact is explored with non-LTE calculations. For the 90% boundary, Scheme 1 predicts 99.4% of the test transitions within a factor of 2, and no prediction deviates from the reference value by more than a factor of 4.7. This model was used to complete 18 datasets. Scheme 2 reproduces the rate coefficients of two excluded systems within a factor of 3 for more than 92% of their transitions. Both schemes provide near-instantaneous complements to close-coupling calculations: Scheme 1 extends the range of a molecule already included in the training set with high accuracy, while Scheme 2 gives a reasonable first estimate for a molecule that has not been studied.

6. Decomposing the Kinematics of SNR G180.0-01.7 with Medium-Resolution Spectra[2610.12880]
Abstract

The exceptionally large angular diameter and intrinsic optical faintness of the Galactic supernova remnant (SNR) G180.0-01.7 (S147) have historically hindered a comprehensive spectroscopic census of its filamentary structure. We present the first dedicated medium-resolution (R   7500) spectroscopic study of S147 using the LAMOST Medium-Resolution Spectroscopic survey of Galactic Nebulae (MRS-N). By implementing multi-Gaussian decomposition on the H$\alpha$, [NII], and [SII] emission complexes and applying combined spectroscopic and spatial criteria, we disentangle the shock-ionized filaments of the SNR from the pervasive foreground and background Diffuse Ionized Gas (DIG). Our analysis reveals a distinct bimodal radial velocity distribution for the SNR: a low-velocity population tracing the decelerated forward shock at a dense cavity wall, and a high-velocity population (with absolute radial velocities of   70 $\pm$ 30 km/s) corresponding to ejecta within a low-density cavity. The co-existence of these populations provides kinematic evidence for the "supernova-in-a-cavity" scenario. Furthermore, we detect enhanced [SII]/H$\alpha$ ratios in the DIG along the eastern and northern boundaries of the remnant, signaling shock compression of the ambient interstellar medium and highlighting remnant's multifaceted feedback.

7. An Ultraviolet Instrument on the Habitable Worlds Observatory: Considerations and Capability Trades on the Multi-Object, Integral-field, and High-resolution modes of a potential UVI[2610.12974]
Abstract

The Habitable Worlds Observatory (HWO) is NASA's next-generation astrophysics platform, delivering transformative capability in the search for life and study of the origins of the universe. Amongst the instruments under consideration for HWO is a multi-purpose ultraviolet spectrograph and imager that provides a deep toolbox for some of the most pressing science investigations of the next several decades. In this paper, we present a basic draft model of a potential UVI, including a multi-object spectrograph, far ultraviolet imager, high resolution spectrograph, and integral-field spectrograph. The draft designs for these channels of the UV instrument serve as a basis for a general discussion of the instrument architecture and design challenges, while also identifying potential trades, enhancements, and other considerations that the HWO project will need to address as it moves into the formulation phase. We conclude with a presentation of several scientific simulations that have helped guide the capabilities of the draft design. This paper is intended to serve as a summary of UV instrument modes, technology, and potential design ahead of planned instrument studies in the late 2020's, and then Phase A in the early 2030's.

8. Redshifted Cool Gas in the Disturbed HI Disk of the Starburst Dwarf NGC 4214[2610.13039]
Abstract

We report redshifted cool gas toward the central star-forming region of the nearby starburst dwarf galaxy NGC 4214. HST/COS spectra show low-ionization absorbers redshifted relative to the systemic velocity, most clearly in SII ($\Delta v=19.8\pm2.6\ {\rm km\,s^{-1}}$) and supported by MgII ($\Delta v=30.3\pm3.0\ {\rm km\,s^{-1}}$), while PII remains tentative ($\Delta v=7.5\pm3.4\ {\rm km\,s^{-1}}$). The Keck/HIRES spectra also reveal a redshifted CaII absorption component ($\Delta v=25.6\pm1.5\ {\rm km\,s^{-1}}$). Because the absorption is detected against the central UV or optical continuum, the gas must lie in the foreground of the star-forming region and is moving away from the observer and toward the background UV source in projection. A VLA–THINGS HI spectrum extracted at the COS position within one VLA beam also shows a redshifted neutral component at $v_{\rm hel}=329.2\pm0.9\ {\rm km\ s^{-1}}$, offset by $38.2\pm1.3\ {\rm km\ s^{-1}}$ from systemic and by $27.6\pm1.0\ {\rm km\ s^{-1}}$ from the dominant local HI component. The FAST HI mapping further reveals an extended, kinematically disturbed neutral disk, with residual structures relative to a warped-disk model. Together, the redshifted UV absorption, the redshifted HI component, and the disturbed large-scale HI velocity field show that the central anomalous cool gas is observed in a galaxy with a disturbed extended neutral disk. This geometry suggests that the redshifted cool gas may trace inflow or recycled fallback toward the central region, although warped-disk or non-circular disk motions cannot be fully excluded.

9. A Relationship Between Fast Radio Burst Dispersion Measure and Host Galaxy Inclination[2610.13062]
Abstract

It is commonly thought that fast radio bursts (FRBs) trace star formation, and that they are produced by magnetars. Both hypotheses imply that their sources are hosted primarily (but not exclusively) in galactic disks. Although there is evidence in that direction, it has yet to be shown conclusively. In addition, a tension exists with the fact that at least one FRB source is found in a globular cluster far from the disk of the host. If FRBs originate primarily in disks, the galaxy orientation could leave an impression in the sample of observed FRBs, either as a selection bias against FRBs in edge-on galaxies or as a correlation with DMHost. Statistical tests against the inclinations of FRB host galaxies, derived from parametric morphology applied to optical and near-infrared imaging, can search for these effects. Such a search requires an adequately large sample of known FRB hosts that have been imaged to a sufficient depth, while estimates of DMHost also require the redshift of each host to be known. These conditions are best met in the Commensal Real-time ASKAP Fast Transient (CRAFT) survey sample of FRB host galaxies, for which the majority have been imaged at optical wavelengths with FORS2 on the Very Large Telescope (VLT). Here these data are fitted with the morphology software GALFIT to derive the elliptical axis ratio q (a proxy for inclination) for 34 hosts from the CRAFT sample, detected with ASKAP and followed up with VLT imaging. We find no bias against FRB detections from edge-on galaxies; instead, we uncover a new relationship between the inclination of an FRB host galaxy and DMHost. This is consistent with a primarily disk-based origin for CRAFT FRBs. Using this relationship we are able to reduce the scatter of the host sample about the Macquart relation by 40%, demonstrating a new empirical method for estimating DMHost that requires only deep optical imaging.

10. The Population of Type 1 Active Galactic Nuclei in Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument. I. Profile-dependent Measurements of the Low-redshift Sample[2610.13218]
Abstract

We present a spectral analysis of a large sample of low-redshift type 1 active galactic nuclei (AGNs) selected from the first data release of the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI DR1). For objects at $z\le0.9$, we perform spectral fitting and ML-assisted refinement, yielding 114,754 type 1 AGNs. We use two fitting schemes that differ only in the broad-line model: the broad Balmer components are fitted with either a Lorentzian function or a sum of Gaussians. The continuum and [O III] luminosity measurements agree closely between the two schemes, whereas the Gaussians scheme generally yields larger broad-H$\beta$ full width at half maximum (FWHM) values, larger virial black hole masses, and lower accretion rates. The adopted broad-Balmer profile also changes the decomposition of narrow Balmer emission. Specifically, the anti-correlation between [O III]$\lambda5007$/narrow H$\beta$ and accretion rate is stronger under the Gaussians scheme with a Spearman rank correlation coefficient $r_{\rm s}=-0.412$, than under the Lorentzian scheme with $r_{\rm s}=-0.206$; the same tendency is observed in the narrow-line Seyfert 1 galaxies (NLS1s) subset ($r_{\rm s}=-0.435$ and $-0.259$ for the Gaussians and Lorentzian schemes, respectively), suggesting that the Gaussians scheme may provide a more self-consistent separation of the broad and narrow H$\beta$ components. Moreover, the consistent correlations recovered under both profile prescriptions favor a continuous type 1 accretion-state sequence, with NLS1s occupying its high-accretion end rather than constituting a distinct population.

11. The Silk Nebula: A Newly Discovered Giant [OIII] Emission Filament[2610.13329]
Abstract

We report [O \textsc{iii}] narrow-band imaging of an extremely large-scale nebula, the Silk Nebula, discovered by the SWONS (Super Wide-Field [O \textsc{iii}] Narrow-band Survey), together with multi-fiber medium-resolution spectroscopic data covering the H$\alpha$, [N \textsc{ii}], [S \textsc{ii}], and [O \textsc{iii}] emission lines, acquired with the Large Sky Area Multi-Object Fiber Spectroscopic Telescope (LAMOST) as part of the Medium-Resolution Spectroscopic Survey of Nebulae (MRS-N). Based on 2,684 high-quality spectra, we measured its physical parameters, including relative line intensities, radial velocities (RVs), and full widths at half-maximum (FWHMs). The [S \textsc{ii}]/H$\alpha$ ratio of the nebula significantly exceeds the shock-excitation threshold of 0.4. Its location in BPT diagnostic diagrams and spatial morphology of [O \textsc{iii}] both support the optical emission is dominated by shock-heated gas. The oxygen abundance is near solar and spatially uniform, consistent with a shock propagating into the ambient interstellar medium. Because [O \textsc{iii}] is the only emission line that clearly traces the nebular morphology, we base our distance estimate exclusively on the [O \textsc{iii}] kinematics, obtaining a fiducial distance of $\sim$160 pc. At this distance, the nebula has a physical extent of $\sim$28 pc along the north-south direction. We suggest that the Silk Nebula is a large [O \textsc{iii}] filament possibly associated with a supernova remnant, although a diffuse-ionized-gas (DIG) or ionized-bubble origin cannot be ruled out with the present data, and one of the largest angular-scale [O \textsc{iii}] emission structures documented to date. Future deep X-ray observations and shock-modeling efforts are required to firmly establish its nature.

12. X-Shooter view of planetary nebulae microstructures I: The case of NGC 7009 knots[2610.13587]
Abstract

The properties of NGC 7009 knots are investigated by employing UV-to-near-infrared data from the X-Shooter spectrograph. These structures belong to the category of low-ionization structures (LISs), which are characterized by enhanced emission from low-ionization species and whose origin remains enigmatic. Our main goal is to conduct the first dedicated spectroscopic study of LISs using X-Shooter, that covers a wide spectral range from the UV to the near-IR, simultaneously. As a pilot study, we focus on NGC 7009 to investigate its ionized, atomic, and molecular gas, aiming to better characterize the physical properties of its LISs and explore their largely unexplored molecular content. The spectrum was processed through the ESO Reflex pipeline, and the physical properties of LISs were derived with the latest version of PyNeb. More than 250 emission lines were detected in the spectra of NGC 7009 LISs, including rare, forbidden transitions from low- ionization species such as Fe+ and Ni+, as well as two transitions from P+, identified here for the first time in LISs. The detection of multiple shock tracers, together with the shock excitation of [Fe II] and [O I], as revealed by emission-line diagnostics, supports the presence of shocks at LISs. The richness of the spectrum allowed us to derive electron temperatures and densities at the LISs using both collisionally excited and optical recombination lines. In the near-IR spectrum of the LISs, nine ro-vibrational H2 emission lines were detected, seven of which were reported here for the first time. These lines were used to construct the H2 excitation diagram, providing new insights into the physical conditions of the nebular gas. The excitation of molecular hydrogen is most likely dominated by collisional processes through shocks.

13. High Resolution Spectro-Polarimetric Observations of the Optical Filaments surrounding NGC 1275 with ESPaDOnS[2610.13719]
Abstract

We analyze archival high spectral resolution ESPaDOnS spectropolarimetric observations (R = $\lambda/\Delta\lambda$ = 68000) of three targeted locations within the filamentary nebula surrounding NGC 1275. We present here the analysis of the main filaments emission lines, namely: [NII]$\lambda6584$, H$\alpha$, and [NII]$\lambda6548$, across our three pointings displaying clear detectable emission, with one also showing additional [SII]$\lambda6716$ and [SII]$\lambda6731$ emission line doublet. Using a Markov Chain Monte-Carlo (MCMC) algorithm and thanks to the exceptionally high spectral resolution of the observations, we resolve for the first time each of the emission lines within one of the pointings into a set of three emission components with a remarkable velocity dispersion contrast ($\sigma_1 \sim 25$ km/s, $\sigma_2 \sim 90$ km/s and $\sigma_3 \sim 208$ km/s). We also perform flux ratios of the three detected emission components, which reveal differences in electron density and ionization sources. Consequently, the ESPaDOnS observations appear to reveal for the first time the overlap of an outer quiescent filament and a central turbulent filament likely affected by the AGN activity at a distance of $\sim 6.8''$ ($\sim 2.5$ kpc) from the central BCG. Finally, ESPaDOnS polarimetric data of one of the pointing allows us to confirm an average linear polarization degree of $\sim 0.15-0.14\%$ and an angle of polarization of $5.3\pm25.7^\circ$ for the optical emission of the filaments, as well as polarization dips associated with the H$\alpha$ emission line and its multiple emission components. We interpret these features and measurements as tentative polarization due to dust scattering.

14. Turbulence modes in molecular clouds in M33: a challenging test of the Brunt-Federrath framework[2610.13726]
Abstract

Understanding the turbulent structure of molecular clouds is essential for linking galactic environment to the regulation of star formation. Although Galactic molecular clouds have been studied extensively, extending turbulence-mode analysis beyond the Milky Way remains challenging because of the limited spatial resolution and dynamic range of extragalactic observations. We assess the applicability of the Brunt & Federrath (2014; BF14) framework to molecular clouds in M33. We apply BF14 to 128 $^{13}$CO(2-1) clouds, selecting a primary quality-controlled sample of 54 clouds and a more restrictive 25-cloud subsample defined using a stricter extraction-padding criterion (PAD=2) for quantitative this http URL of cloud and noise power spectra, Fourier-space cutoffs, extraction padding, and Monte Carlo uncertainties demonstrate that the retained spatial frequencies are dominated by astrophysical signal and that the inferred turbulence-mode fractions are robust against reasonable analysis choices. Both samples yield consistent distributions of the solenoidal fraction, with mean values of $R \simeq 0.22$ for both samples and median values of $R \simeq 0.18$ and $0.17$, respectively, indicating that compressive motions dominate the turbulent energy budget. No strong systematic variations of $R$ are found with galactocentric distance or tracers of recent star formation. These results demonstrate that, despite the challenging spatial resolution of current extragalactic molecular-line observations, the BF14 framework can be applied reliably to nearby galaxies using spectroscopic observations alone. To our knowledge, this constitutes the first application of the full BF14 formalism to extragalactic molecular-cloud observations.

15. A strategy for astrometric quasar selection with Gaia DR3 and its application to discover new luminous $z>2$ quasars[2610.13742]
Abstract

We aim to provide a cookbook for purely astrometric selection of quasars with Gaia DR3 and to provide an example of its use for the identification of very luminous $z>2$ quasars. We analyse the Gaia DR3 astrometry of confirmed quasars and stationary sources. We show that the proper motion signal to noise $\mu/\sigma_{\mu}$ follows a Rayleigh-like distribution. We also show the importance of properly taking into account the correlation between proper motions in right ascension and declination, as well as the non-linear error propagation. We adopt the Mahalanobis distance to the null proper-motion hypothesis. A scaled $\chi^2$ distribution with $k=2$ and scale $s=1.1485$ is fitted to Gaia-CRF3; the corresponding $\pm2\sigma$ Gaussian-equivalent threshold is $D^2_{M(\mu)}<7.097$. The final criteria combine this with the parallax condition and $\mathrm{ruwe}<1.4$. Applying the new criteria to the SDSS DR16Q confirmed quasars, the recovery rate is $90.25\%$. The suggested purely astrometric selection significantly improves both completeness and purity compared to other strategies used in the literature, reaching a quasar purity of approximately $95\%$ ($\sim2\sigma$ Gaussian-equivalent). It can be used to improve the selection purity and completeness for future quasar surveys. As an example, we use the suggested strategi to locate 24 very bright $z>2$ quasars without redshifts in the literature. Our proposed astrometric selection strategy recovers 95\% of known quasars. Its potential use for new discoveries is demonstrated by the identification of 24 very luminous $z>2$ quasars. Ten of these are new discoveries and for an additional seven we correct the redshifts determined by other methods.}

16. A dust-reddened quasar in a [C II]-luminous host at z=6.55[2610.13758]
Abstract

Most known quasars at $z>6$ were selected through their rest-frame UV emission. Only a small number of reddened quasars have been identified at these redshifts, leaving their nuclear and host-galaxy properties poorly constrained. We present JWST/NIRSpec spectroscopy and ALMA band 6 observations of the quasar J1440+0019 at $z=6.5488$, revealing its dust-reddened nature and jointly characterizing its nucleus and host galaxy. The continuum is best described by an SMC-like extinction law with $E(B-V)=0.20\pm0.03$. Three absorption systems detected in N V, Si IV, and C IV are blueshifted by approximately $900$–$3600$ km s$^{-1}$ and likely trace an ionized nuclear outflow. The broad Mg II line and extinction-corrected continuum yield a black-hole mass of $\log(M_{\rm BH}/M_\odot)=9.04\pm0.42$, a bolometric luminosity of $L_{\rm bol}\simeq1.1\times10^{47}$ erg s$^{-1}$, and an Eddington ratio of $\lambda_{\rm Edd}\simeq0.8$. ALMA observations further reveal its host galaxy to be among the most [C II]-luminous known at $z>6$, with $L_{\mathrm{[C II]}}=(7.21\pm0.25)\times10^9 L_\odot$. For a fiducial dust temperature of 47 K and emissivity index $\beta=1.6$, the dust continuum implies $L_{\rm TIR}=8.9\times10^{12} L_\odot$ and $M_{\rm dust}=4.0\times10^8 M_\odot$. The inferred dynamical mass places J1440+0019 in the region of the $M_{\rm BH}$–$M_{\rm dyn}$ plane occupied by high-redshift blue quasars. These observations reveal a moderately reddened, rapidly accreting quasar in a luminous, dust-rich host during reionization. Its measured nuclear and host-galaxy properties provide an observational reference for identifying and characterizing similar systems in future systematic searches with wide-area infrared surveys.

17. Insights from the stability of methanol maser flare properties I: G9.62+0.20E[2610.13774]
Abstract

The brightest 6.7 GHz methanol maser in the Galaxy is associated with the high-mass star-forming region (HMSFR) G009.62+0.20E. Additionally, this methanol maser shows periodic flaring observed at 6.7 and 12.2 GHz. Several models have been hypothesized to explain the periodicity, including periodic accretion, spiral shocks in the gaps of circumbinary disks, pulsating stars, dicke's superradiance, and a colliding wind binary (CWB) system. We analyzed the time series of the 1.22 km s-1 feature of the 6.7 GHz methanol maser spectrum and the 1.25 km s-1 feature of the 12.2 GHz methanol maser spectrum over the monitoring duration of roughly 20 years, to determine an updated period, analyze the stability and repeatability of the flares, the temporal behavior of the flare profile, and the duty cycle of the flare profile based on the updated period. From these results, it is argued for or against any of the mechanisms that have been proposed to explain the periodicity. Lomb-Scargle (LS) and phase dispersion minimization (PDM) methods were used to determine the methanol maser period at 6.7 and 12.2 GHz. Once the period has been determined, phase-folding or period-folding was used to analyze the flare shape, flare stability, and duty cycle of the flares. The periods of the 6.7 and 12.2 GHz methanol masers were determined to be   243.0 +- 0.3 days. The flares are highly repeatable, stable, and show almost identical shapes, as well as their duty cycles. A replicated kernel density estimator (KDE) of the flare shape reproduces the time series very well over a time span of   8000 days. After careful consideration of the proposed mechanisms for periodic methanol masers, we conclude that all but one, the CWB model, have been ruled out as a possible driver of this flare profile. It has also been established that the CWB model can qualitatively explain the flare profile of the periodic methanol masers.

18. A Comprehensive JWST Slitless Spectroscopic Census of $\sim$12,000 Emission-Line Galaxies at Redshift $0\sim9$ in COSMOS[2610.13920]
Abstract

Emission-line galaxies provide key diagnostics of star formation, chemical enrichment, and AGN activity across cosmic time. We present a systematic census of emission-line sources using JWST/NIRCam wide-field slitless spectroscopy (WFSS) from the COSMOS-3D program, which covers a large contiguous area of 0.33 deg$^2$ and provides F444W grism spectroscopy over $3.9$–$5.1\ \mu$m. By combining slitless spectroscopy with deep multi-band imaging data, we construct a homogeneous spectroscopic sample of 12,275 objects at redshift 0–9. The typical $3\sigma$ line-flux limit reaches $3.2\times10^{-18}\ {\rm erg\ s^{-1}\ cm^{-2}}$. To address source confusion inherent to slitless spectroscopy, we develop a multi-step identification framework that combines information from spectra, images, spectral energy distributions, and spatial distributions. We introduce convolutional neural network and large language model to emulate the human identification process and improve efficiency. The resulting catalog provides spectroscopic redshifts, emission-line measurements, and derived physical properties for all sources. The F444W WFSS enables the detection of multiple emission lines, including primarily [O III], H$\alpha$, He I $\lambda10830$, [S III] $\lambda\lambda9531,9069$, Pa$\beta$, Pa$\alpha$, Br$\beta$, PAH, Br$\alpha$, allowing a continuous census of galaxies across cosmic time. We construct composite spectra in several redshift bins and detect a variety of faint spectral features that are difficult to identify in individual spectra, including higher-order hydrogen recombination lines, CO absorption bands, and [Fe II] lines. This catalog provides a key reference dataset for studies of galaxy evolution and AGN activity, and demonstrates the power of WFSS for constructing large, unbiased spectroscopic samples over wide areas. The full catalog will be publicly available online upon publication.

19. OGLE-2016-BLG-0523: A Low-mass Brown Dwarf or super-Jupiter in a $>100$ au Orbit[2610.13942]
Abstract

To search for very wide-orbit binary-lens events, we cross-match microlensing events detected by OGLE-IV and KMTNet between 2011 and 2024. After removing contaminants from variable stars, we identify OGLE-2016-BLG-0523 as a microlensing event showing two short-lived bumps in its light curve, with the two separated by 8.36 yr. We investigate three scenarios to explain the two bumps, including a binary-lens single-source (2L1S), a single-lens binary-source (1L2S), and two unrelated single-lens single-source events (2$\times$1L1S). The 1L2S interpretation is excluded due to its poorer fit to the data, while the 2$\times$1L1S scenario is disfavored by the low event rate, unphysical total source flux, and unphysical color for one of the two putative sources. For the surviving 2L1S interpretation, the Einstein timescales of the two bumps are $8.343 \pm 0.076$ days and $3.231 \pm 0.047$ days, respectively, implying a binary mass ratio of $q = 0.15$. A Bayesian analysis indicates that the primary lens is most likely a low-mass M dwarf, while the companion is probably a low-mass brown dwarf or super-Jupiter. The inferred projected separation is about 450 au, making the companion the widest low-mass object discovered via microlensing to date. Our discovery highlights both the promise and challenges of identifying very wide, low-mass companions through microlensing. Although upcoming facilities such as Roman and Earth 2.0 space telescopes will improve sensitivity to such events, overcoming degeneracies and distinguishing them from variable phenomena will remain essential for building a robust statistical sample.

20. Beyond diagonal approximations: improved covariance modeling for pulsar timing array data analysis[2506.13866]
Abstract

Pulsar Timing Array (PTA) searches for nHz gravitational-wave backgrounds (GWBs) typically model time-correlated noise by assuming a diagonal covariance in Fourier space, neglecting inter-frequency correlations introduced by the finite observation window. We show that this diagonal approximation can produce method-dependent shifts in estimates of spectral parameters, especially for pulsars with shallow intrinsic red noise spectra. To address these limitations, we present a method that (i) computes the time-domain autocorrelation on a coarse grid using a fast Fourier transform (FFT), (ii) interpolates it accurately to the unevenly sampled observation times, and (iii) incorporates it into a low-rank likelihood via the Sherman–Morrison–Woodbury identity. Using both analytic covariance comparisons and end-to-end simulations inspired by the NANOGrav 15-year dataset, we demonstrate that our method captures frequency correlations faithfully, avoids Gibbs ringing, and yields estimates closer to the injected values in the realization studied with modest computational cost. As PTA datasets increase in sensitivity and complexity, our approach offers a practical and scalable path to fully accurate covariance modeling for current and future analyses.

21. From cosmological simulations to binary black hole mergers: The impact of using analytical star formation history models on gravitational-wave source populations[2601.20202]
Abstract

Observations of binary black hole (BBH) mergers provide a unique window into the lives of massive stars across cosmic time. Connecting redshift-dependent merger properties to massive star progenitors requires accurate models of cosmic star formation and chemical enrichment histories. Analytical fits for the metallicity-specific cosmic star formation rate density S(Z, z) are commonly used as proxies for the complex underlying star formation history, yet they remain unconstrained. Using the IllustrisTNG cosmological simulations, we evaluate the accuracy of these analytical S(Z, z) prescriptions. By coupling the simulated and analytical S(Z, z) to the population synthesis code COMPAS, we investigate the resulting BBH merger rates and mass distributions. We find that analytical S(Z, z) prescriptions can overestimate BBH merger rates at high redshift ($z\gtrsim6$) by up to a factor of $10$-$10^4$, depending on cosmological simulation resolution, and can introduce spurious features in the BBH mass distribution. For example, they can produce an artificial feature near $8\,M_\odot$ in the primary mass distribution at $z\lesssim2$, which is absent when using the full simulation-based S(Z, z), while simultaneously suppressing high-mass features. These discrepancies arise because simple analytical models fail to capture a high-metallicity bump and a more flattened low-metallicity tail in the simulated S(Z, z) metallicity distribution. Our results highlight the importance of accurate star formation histories for modeling BBH populations, demonstrate the limitation of widely used analytical S(Z, z) fits, and underscore the need for careful integration of cosmological simulations, analytical fits, and population synthesis when interpreting gravitational-wave observations.

22. On the relation between magnetic field strength and gas density in the interstellar medium. II. Density uncertainties and diffuse gas constraints[2603.06838]
Abstract

The relationship between magnetic field strength and gas density is essential to understand the interstellar medium and star formation. Zeeman measurements in dense atomic and molecular gas phases have traditionally been used to directly probe magnetic field strengths in the Milky Way. This allowed derivation of a relationship between magnetic field strength $B$ and gas number density $n$. We recently generalized this relation as a two-part power-law with non-zero slopes and a transition density given as $B/B_0 \propto (n/n_0)^{\alpha_1}$ for $n \le n_0$ and $(n/n_0)^{\alpha_2}$ for $n > n_0$. Here, we extend our previous hierarchical Bayesian framework by incorporating a large body of pulsar observations that probe the diffuse interstellar medium and explicitly modelling density uncertainties through a log-density intrinsic scatter $R_n$ applied to all densities. We also account for magnetic field geometry and measurement uncertainties through a magnetic hyperparameter to estimate $B$. This results in a stronger constraint on the diffuse gas part of the $B$–$n$ relation. Our results confirm a non-zero exponent in the diffuse gas and a broad transition density with our best model and data set yielding maximum a posteriori results of $\alpha_1 = 0.20^{+0.01}_{-0.03}$, $\alpha_2 = 0.65^{+0.06}_{-0.07}$, $n_0 = 3330^{+270}_{-3220}\,\text{cm}^{-3}$, and $B_0 = 10.11^{+0.71}_{-5.97}\,\mu\text{G}$.

23. Far-infrared Polarization Properties of Nearby Star-forming Regions: A New Compendium of SOFIA/HAWC+ Observations[2603.09962]
Abstract

We present a comprehensive polarimetric study of 26 nearby molecular clouds in four far-infrared bands (53 $\mu$m to 214 $\mu$m) using 52 archival SOFIA/HAWC+ datasets{, over half of which are unpublished}. Far-infrared dust polarization observations probe the plane-of-sky magnetic field. To investigate scale-dependent trends, we group the molecular clouds by distance and analyze the data at common angular ($25''$) and common physical (0.052 pc and 0.32 pc) resolutions. The two shorter wavelengths are more impacted by smoothing, exhibiting a larger decrease in percent polarization. We analyze the polarization spectrum — the polarization fraction as a function of wavelength — and find that it depends more strongly on column density than dust temperature. We find a “falling" spectrum at the 0.052 pc resolution, but find a “flat" spectrum at the 0.32 pc resolution, suggesting that resolution plays an important role in the observed polarization spectra. We propose that warm dust grain emission in small-scale structures ($\lesssim$ 0.1 pc) traces different magnetic field geometries only resolved in our {near} regime data. There is no preferred magnetic field orientation across our data, which suggests that the magnetic field in our $\sim$ parsec scale regions is decoupled from the large-scale field that is primarily parallel to the Galactic plane. The relationship between percent polarization and column density varies between clouds, but the correlation between percent polarization and {polarization angle} dispersion is consistent across regions. This compendium highlights the value of observing at multiple far-infrared wavelengths and enables additional population-level studies of magnetic fields and dust across star-forming environments.

24. A possible detection of gas rotation in galaxy groups via the kinetic Sunyaev-Zeldovich effect[2603.14494]
Abstract

We find a clear redshift dipole in the stacked data for the selected SDSS groups. 27. Reduced incidence of little red dots at z < 3 based on number density and halo mass evolution[2606.02773]
Abstract

An intriguing puzzle in extragalactic astronomy is the scarcity of Little Red Dots (LRDs) at $z < 3$, compared to their higher abundance at earlier cosmic epochs. To investigate this, we measure the overdensity for 98 specpically confirmed LRDs at $3<z<7$, and find that these LRDs predominantly reside in under-dense regions at $z > 4$ but shift to more typical galaxy environments at $z \sim 3.5$. Concurrently, cross-correlation analyses show that their dark matter halo masses grow rapidly, from $\lesssim 10^{10.1} \, M_{\odot}$ at $z \sim 7.5$ to $\sim 10^{11.3} \, M_{\odot}$ at $z \sim 3.5$, approaching the halo masses of normal galaxies at lower redshifts. 33. "Mega-Earths" are likely an artifact of stellar activity[2610.12600]
Abstract

Empirical relations and planet formation simulations suggest an upper mass limit for rocky planets of $\sim$10M$_{\oplus}$, as cores above this will almost certainly undergo runaway gas accretion. Nevertheless, a small number of planets appear to have densities consistent with rocky Earth-like compositions above this threshold, indicating that unusual formation and evolutionary scenarios are capable of producing extremely massive rocky planets called "Mega-Earths." However, all of these planets orbit active stars with log$R'_{HK}$ $>$-4.75. We find a <1$\%$ probability that all literature ultra-massive rocky planets would coincidentally orbit such active stars, and find no astrophysical reason to expect Mega-Earths to preferentially form around active stars. 38. Combination of multi-observatory photometry to determine asteroid rotation periods[2610.13223]
Abstract

This study aims to determine asteroid rotation periods from sparse, unevenly sampled, multi-observatory photometry using the Lomb-Scargle (L-S) method. The photometric measurements of the asteroids are taken from the circulars of the Minor Planet Center (MPC). Twelve asteroids, four of them with known rotation periods, were analyzed with two complementary methods. This paper presents the first experimental validation of InteRFeX, a non-uniform receive only (passive) array located at the Nova Systems Space Precinct in Peterborough, Australia, designed for space domain awareness applications. It operates over a frequency range of 70 to 300 MHz and is capable of functioning in a range of observational modes, including as a coherent passive bistatic radar for space surveillance, which is the focus of this work. InteRFeX exploits commercial broadcast transmissions as illuminators of opportunity, combining cross-ambiguity function processing for bistatic range and range-rate retrieval with MUSIC-based direction-of-arrival (DoA) estimation for angular localization. The International Space Station (ISS) serves as the experimental target, with measurements evaluated against Two-Line Element (TLE) orbit predictions. Taking advantage of the dual-polarized array, all four reference–surveillance polarization combinations are evaluated, revealing differences in detection rate and mean signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) over the observation pass, with bistatic-range RMSEs of approximately 1 km relative to the TLE-derived reference. Independently, the MUSIC-based DoA estimates exhibit a mean angular separation of approximately $0.45^\circ$ from the TLE-derived reference directions. The angular deviations are further decomposed into along-track and cross-track components, enabling a more detailed characterization of the DoA offsets relative to the TLE-derived reference trajectory. The processing framework also includes a first-order bistatic link-budget analysis to estimate the expected SNR over the observation geometry. These results demonstrate the feasibility of passive-radar space domain awareness using radio-interferometric array technology and establish the proposed signal-processing pipeline as a basis for a dedicated coherent passive-radar mode within InteRFeX.

41. Assessing PLATO's Discovery Potential across Different Observing Strategies[2610.13686]
Abstract

The PLATO mission, planned to launch in spring 2027, will observe a fixed Southern field, LOPS2, for at least 2 years with the goal of discovering and deriving precise parameters for planets as small as Earth around FGK dwarfs and sub-giants. Afterwards, PLATO could continue to monitor the same field or move to a Northern field, LOPN1, overlapping with the Kepler field. We assess these two observing strategies in terms of sensitivity of detecting small and long period planets, and subsequently we estimate yields for both observing strategies. Within this we account for including the Kepler data in the LOPN1 field, monitoring of up to 8 years of the LOPS2 field, and the sensitivity improvement from combining TESS and PLATO data since both fields have been and will be continued to be observed with TESS. We find that by monitoring both fields, and hence more stars, the total number of newly discovered planets is maximised. However, to optimise the number of discovered small and long periods planets, long-term monitoring of one field produces the best results. The combination of Kepler and PLATO data improves detection sensitivities; however, as this only covers a fraction of the LOPN1 field and stellar sample, it improves the yields of small and long period planets only marginally. Hence, to detect and characterise small and long period planets in this sparsely explored exoplanet parameter space, a longer continuous observation of the LOPS2 field is best.

42. Phantom Zernike Wavefront Sensor: Phase Contrast with Engineered Focal Plane Reference Speckles[2610.12807]
Abstract

We introduce the Phantom Zernike Wavefront Sensor (phantom ZWFS), an engineered reference extension of phase-contrast wavefront sensing in which controlled pupil plane modulation generates off-axis coherent speckle structures that undergo localized focal plane phase shifts. These references may replace the central ZWFS reference when the on-axis PSF core is required for another optical function, such as photonic science coupling, or may augment a conventional central ZWFS response to provide additional wavefront information. Using a monochromatic scalar model, we derive the resulting carrier structure and show how the engineered references can be recovered through spatial demodulation and incorporated into calibrated reconstruction. A simple four-reference realization demonstrates that the reference geometry redistributes photon sensitivity across spatial frequency magnitude and orientation, producing mode dependent changes in linearity and cross-talk rather than a uniform improvement over a classical ZWFS. We then consider differential piston on a seven segment aperture as an application specific example. A hybrid sensor retaining the central ZWFS phase region and adding six engineered references that match the segment geometry gives similar precision near the phased state but substantially improved performance at larger piston excursions. Under the adopted photon-noise-limited benchmark, the hybrid reduces the nonlinear reconstruction error and provides better conditioned optical encoding of the piston state than an idealized vector ZWFS, allowing more precise piston estimation from the same detected photon flux at larger piston excursions.

43. The PLATO Input Catalogue of targets (tPIC) for the first Long Pointing Field[2604.03369]
Abstract

The ESA PLAnetary Transits and Oscillations of stars (PLATO) mission is designed to detect terrestrial planets in the habitable zones of solar-type stars. Owing to telemetry constraints, the selection of PLATO targets must be performed in advance. We present the first public release of the PLATO Input Catalog of targets (tPIC2.2), which provides the list of stars that will be observed during PLATO's first Long-duration Observation Phase field at South (LOPS2) as part of its core science program. We used astrometric and photometric data from Gaia data release 3 (DR3), together with three-dimensional maps of the local interstellar medium, to identify stars belonging to the PLATO stellar samples in accordance with the mission requirements. The tPIC comprises 217,741 stars, including 202,315 FGK dwarfs and subgiants, 15,037 M dwarfs, and 789 known planet-host stars. The median distances of the samples are 512 pc for FGK stars and 133 pc for M dwarfs. We estimated interstellar reddening for almost all targets and developed an algorithm to infer fundamental stellar parameters (effective temperature, radius, and mass) homogeneously from astrometric and photometric observables. The tPIC fulfills all the science requirements of the PLATO mission. It also includes a list of stars that host known exoplanets, confirmed or still candidate, located within the LOPS2 field.

44. Inpainting to search for premerger massive black hole binaries in realistic LISA datasets[2605.13738]
Abstract

A key science target of the Large Interferometer Space Antenna (LISA) is to carry out multi-messenger observations of massive black hole binaries, observing the merger simultaneously in gravitational waves and with electromagnetic observatories. Identifying that a merger is happening and providing an updating estimate of the sky location in the hours, days and weeks before the merger is critical to enable electromagnetic observations of the merger event. In this work we demonstrate and compare two methods for premerger identification of massive black hole binaries; a zero-latency filter approach and, for the first time, an approach using an “inpainting” technique. We apply these methods to the LISA Data Challenge dataset 2a–Sangria-HM–and demonstrate the successful recovery of the 14 signals in the dataset that we expected to be identifiable at least half a day before merger. We show that the inpainting method can identify premerger signals even when gaps are present in the data, demonstrating the recovery of a signal even when 3 day-long data gaps are added to the 14 days preceding merger. Finally, we explore the challenge of overlapping signals, using a region of overlapping signals in the Sangria-HM dataset, all of which merge within a 10-day window, and show how removing signals that have been confidently identified from the data allows us to identify quieter signals in the same period.

45. Fast Astronomical Transients in Archival Photographic Plates: Using optical aberrations as a tool for discerning real images, from plate artifacts[2606.08319]
Abstract

The detection of fast astronomical transients in photographic plates from the Palomar sky surveys conducted in the 1950s, was subject to the criticism that such transients could be just the effect of otherwise unaccounted for plate artifacts. In this paper, we show that transient images exhibit the coma aberration pattern expected from off-axis point sources recorded through the telescope optics, a signature that plate artifacts cannot naturally reproduce. Although the data does not by themselves establish the physical origin of the light that generated the images, they lend support to hypotheses that do not rely on instrumental effects to explain transients.

46. Neutrino Telescope Event Classification on Quantum Computers[2506.16530]
Abstract

Quantum computers represent a new computational paradigm with steadily improving hardware capabilities. In this article, we present the first study exploring how current quantum computers can be used to classify different neutrino event types observed in neutrino telescopes. We investigate two quantum machine learning approaches, Neural Projected Quantum Kernels (NPQKs) and Quantum Convolutional Neural Networks (QCNNs), and find that both achieve classification performance comparable to classical machine learning methods across a wide energy range. By introducing a moment-of-inertia-based encoding scheme and a novel preprocessing approach, we enable efficient and scalable learning with large neutrino astronomy datasets. Tested on both simulators and the IBM Strasbourg quantum processor, the NPQK achieves a testing accuracy near 80%, with robust results above 1 TeV and close agreement between simulation and hardware performance. A simulated QCNN achieves a  70% accuracy over the same energy range. These results underscore the promise of quantum machine learning for neutrino astronomy, paving the way for future advances as quantum hardware matures.

47. The Instrumented Baffle at the Input Mode Cleaner of Advanced Virgo Plus: Four years of Successful Operation as a Monitor of Stray Light[2603.29873]
Abstract

The suspended end mirror of the Input Mode Cleaner cavity in the Advanced Virgo Plus interferometer was equipped with an instrumented baffle in Spring 2021, serving as a demonstrator of the technology in preparation for the installation of large instrumented baffles in the main arms of the interferometer. This baffle includes tens of sensors positioned near the mirror to enable monitoring of stray light within the cavity. In this contribution, we assess the stability and performance of the instrument after four years of operation. After introducing the main characteristics of the baffle, we study the distribution of stray light and show that the instrumented baffle can be used to monitor laser stability and alignment within the cavity. Finally, we perform a noise level assessment with data from the final stages of the O4b commissioning and conclude that the baffle does not introduce any additional disturbance to the normal operation of the interferometer.
