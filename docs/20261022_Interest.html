1. Black Holes are about Quantum Information[2610.28185]
Abstract

We review and analyse quantum information-theoretic approaches to black hole evaporation that purport to bring us closer to a resolution of the black hole information paradox. Our focus is on the Page-time paradox and on the contribution of the Hayden-Preskill model to understanding information release under the assumption of unitary evaporation. In this model, the black hole is taken to be a finite-dimensional quantum system whose dynamics can be represented by a quantum circuit, and its information-processing and communication abilities are studied. We identify the assumptions underlying this modelling and highlight two key features, fast scrambling dynamics and entanglement as a resource, that underpin the information release in the model. We argue that this reasoning exemplifies a broader change in how information is used in black hole physics, in which quantum information theory now provides a useful operational modelling framework. This application avoids earlier philosophical objections insofar as it is not based on epistemic ignorance and does not regard information as a substance. This line of reasoning has become central to the debate and, as such, warrants greater attention from the philosophy of physics community.

2. Why Hadamard states?[2606.22767]
Abstract

In QFT on curved spacetime, the Hadamard condition is often presented as a necessary condition on 'physically reasonable' states of the quantum field, and plays a central role in many theoretical and foundational applications - ranging from proofs of the renormalizability of Wick polynomials to derivations of the Hawking temperature. Yet despite this, the philosophical and foundational underpinnings of the Hadamard condition remain murky. I critically discuss existing motivations for the Hadamard condition in the literature, before arguing in favour of an alternative motivation for the Hadamard condition, according to which it is best understood as a necessary and sufficient condition for the existence of a well-defined operator product of a certain form on a sufficiently large space of observables of the quantum field, satisfying a variety of further conditions. This clarifies the role and status of the Hadamard condition, including its relationship to the equivalence principle, to well-definedness of physical quantities such as Wick polynomials and the expectation value of the stress-energy operator, and the sense in which Hadamard states are 'vacuum-like'.
