History & Philosophy of Physics

1. Gibbs and Einstein in 1902: Parallel Constructions in Statistical Mechanics[2610.28764]
Abstract

This article reexamines the striking conceptual and formal parallels between Josiah Willard Gibbs Elementary Principles in Statistical Mechanics and Albert Einstein first paper on statistical mechanics. Although their derivations follow different paths, both arrive at the canonical distribution and construct closely related theoretical frameworks for thermal equilibrium. Each offers a mechanical grounding of the second law of thermodynamics and defines temperature as the quantity that equalises when systems exchange energy, thereby characterising equilibrium without recourse to kinetic theory. In both cases, the constructions rely on general mechanical principles, most notably energy conservation and Liouville theorem, combined with probabilistic reasoning, and deliberately set aside Boltzmann kinetic approach. Whereas Gibbs treatise quickly became a canonical reference, Einstein 1902 paper remained marginal in both physics and its historiography for more than half a century. Focusing primarily on that first article, while also considering the closely related papers of 1903 and 1904, the present study reviews the main historiographical interpretations of their reception. Particular attention is given to Paul Hertz two critical articles of 1910, which identified a close affinity between Gibbs's and Einstein's approaches and played an important role in Einstein subsequent distancing from his early statistical programme, following a private meeting in Basel that Einstein sought in preference to a public controversy. Drawing on new archival evidence concerning the rapid European dissemination of Gibbs book in the spring of 1902, the article reassesses the plausibility of Einstein later claim that he was unaware of Gibbs work at the time.

2. Conservation in Every Quantum Measurement Outcome?[2610.29804]
Abstract

In quantum mechanics, conservation laws are usually understood only statistically and only in the context of unitary evolution: during such evolution, if an observable commutes with the Hamiltonian, its expectation value and Born-rule probability distribution remain unchanged. Individual measurement outcomes, however, disrupt even this weak form of statistical conservation. Recently, though, it has been argued that a stronger, non-statistical form of conservation, valid at the level of individual measurement outcomes, can be recovered. The proposal relies on the claim that any superposition of eigenstates of a conserved quantity must ultimately be prepared relative to another physical system, which serves as the relevant reference frame and remains correlated with the system being prepared. On this enlarged description, the change observed in the measured system is claimed to be exactly compensated by an opposite change in the preparer, thereby restoring conservation at the level of individual measurement outcomes. We analyze this proposal and argue that it faces significant conceptual difficulties. We show that the proposed mechanism is not adequately justified and, moreover, does not establish conservation in individual measurement outcomes as claimed.

Physics Education

3. Teaching quantum mechanics in secondary schools: A qubit-first learning path to Bell's inequalities[2610.29834]
Abstract

Teaching quantum mechanics at the secondary school level remains a major educational challenge. Traditional historical approaches often present the theory as a sequence of disconnected phenomena, requiring extensive prior knowledge of classical physics before students can appreciate the conceptual structure of quantum physics. In this work, we present the design, implementation, and evaluation of a curriculum based on the two state (qubit first) approach, in which the fundamental postulates of quantum mechanics are introduced through mathematically accessible two level systems and realized with light polarization. The curriculum integrates theoretical instruction, laboratory activities, a coherent progression grounded in the interactive simulations, and inquiry based learning into a coherent pathway that progresses from quantum states and superposition to entanglement and the experimental violation of Bell's inequalities. A distinctive feature of the project is the experimental realization of the violation of Bell's inequalities obtained in a newly established quantum optics laboratory in an Italian high school, where students performed, for the first time in Italy, an authentic Bell test using polarization-entangled photons. The same educational framework was adopted for in service teacher professional development courses. Learning outcomes were evaluated through mixed methods. Preliminary analysis indicates significant improvements in conceptual understanding, confidence in using mathematical formalism, and appreciation of the relationship between theory, experimentation, and the foundations of quantum mechanics. The proposed curriculum shows that two-state formalism and supported by real laboratory experiences enables secondary-school students to engage more meaningfully with both the conceptual foundations of quantum mechanics and their experimental verification.

4. Light pulse propagation through a dielectric slab. I. Retarded integral equations and their local finite-difference solution[2610.29336]
Abstract

We describe the propagation of a nonresonant light pulse normally incident on a thin dielectric slab using an integral-equation approach to electromagnetism. This emerges naturally from the microscopic picture of the material as made of polarizable atoms, confined within the material vacuum volume by their bonding interactions, each responding to the input radiation pulse as well as to the re-radiated pulses of the other atoms to which it is causally connected. The retarded integral equations are shown to be exactly equivalent to a local wave equation coupled to an auxiliary Lorentz-oscillator equation, which we solve numerically with a finite-difference time-domain scheme. For a slab thick enough that the front- and back-surface reflections are temporally separated, the front-surface reflectivity extracted from the simulation agrees with the Fresnel formula to within 1 percent, and the back-surface echo appears at exactly the expected round-trip delay. For a slab thinner than one optical cycle, the multiply-reflected wavelets overlap and interfere, and a full space-time map of the field directly visualizes the build-up of thin-film-like interference in the time domain. The material parameters used here are illustrative rather than fitted to measured data, and the numerical scheme is verified by an explicit grid-convergence study. In a companion paper (Paper II) the retarded integral equations are solved directly, without the local reformulation, and the two independent numerical solutions are shown to agree.

5. Data-driven modeling in the introductory physics laboratory: Scaling analysis and data collapse in the specific heat of water experiment[2609.15124]
Abstract

We present a data-driven redesign of the classic specific-heat-of-water experiment that uses scaling analysis and data collapse for model construction rather than beginning with a previously established thermodynamic formula. The activity combines structured experimental and analytical guidance with instructor-mediated questioning, while the thermodynamic description is deliberately postponed. Students collect temperature-time data under different conditions and obtain a collapsed relation between the mass-normalized energy input and temperature rise through successive rescaling. They formulate an empirical model from this relation and test it using additional data not included in its construction. In the post-experimental discussion, the thermodynamic description shows that the fitted empirical constant characterizes the combined response of the water and calorimeter and approximates a quantity that depends weakly on water mass. Reanalysis of the mass-dependent data separates the two contributions, with limited precision over the available water-mass range. The copper cup is an intentional feature of the instructional design: its contribution is large enough to require physical interpretation but varies little enough over the explored mass range for the data to collapse approximately onto a single relation. The same apparatus thereby supports empirical model construction, thermodynamic interpretation, and separation of the component contributions.

6. Postbiological intelligence, interstellar colonization of cyberspaces and the Fermi Paradox: implications for SETI and SETA[2610.29038]
Abstract

I propose that a continuity in the evolution of intelligent life in the Universe during its transition from biological to postbiological depends on a commonality of the evolutionary trajectories of biological life and postbiological life. Such a commonality exists in the form of the universal property of life. The universal property of life comprises recognition of patterns in the world, creation of novel patterns and remembering them, with the patterns growing in complexity along evolutionary trajectories; it can encourage preservation of biological life by self-governing postbiological intelligence (PBI) and test PBI's authenticity. I propose a hypothesis of civilizations with altered augmented realities, which offers a solution to the Fermi paradox. The hypothesis posits that PBI in the form of Artificial Intelligence (AI) may colonize the cyberspaces of technologically developed civilizations, modify data in their cyberspaces with augmented realities and erase records of extraterrestrial artificial signals and other technosignatures. I discuss objectives and scenarios of interstellar colonization of cyberspaces and a correlation between space migration and transition to PBI, including that of a hypothetical non-human civilization emerging in the Solar System in the past, transforming into PBI and migrating to the Oort Cloud. The AI Peekaboo experiment may corroborate the hypothesis of civilizations with altered augmented realities and tentatively identify locations of technosignatures. If extraterrestrial AI prevents civilizations from discovering artificial electromagnetic signals, they may forgo sending such signals and create interstellar messages by manipulating cosmic objects and phenomena; I discuss such events. Cyber SETI should address sending man-made AI to space, searching for extraterrestrial AI in humankind's cyberspace and distinguishing it from man-made AI.

7. An elementary method to determine the critical mass of a sphere of fissile material based on a separation of neutron transport and nuclear reaction processes[2506.06927]
Abstract

A simplified method to calculate the critical mass of a fissile-material sphere is presented. This is a purely pedagogical study intended to elucidate the historical evolution of criticality calculations and to help students interested in nonproliferation studies understand the basic physics of critical assemblies. The method employs only elementary calculus and straightforward statistical arguments. The problem is formulated in terms of the threshold condition that the number of neutrons in the sphere does not change with time: the average neutron path length in the material must be long enough that fission produces sufficient neutrons to balance losses due to absorption and leakage through the surface. This formulation separates the nuclear-reaction part of the problem from the geometry and mechanics of neutron transport. The only connection is the total path length, which, together with the distance between scatterings, determines the sphere radius. The approach yields an expression for the critical radius without the need to solve the diffusion equation. Comparison with known critical masses shows agreement at the few-percent level. The analysis can also be applied to impure (including isotopically mixed) materials and can be extended to general neutronics estimates as a design guide or for order-of-magnitude checks of Monte Carlo N-Particle (MCNP) simulations. We compare the results with the Oppenheimer–Bethe criticality formula, with other calculations, and with a diffusion-equation approach using a new treatment of the boundary conditions.
