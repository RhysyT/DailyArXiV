2. The PAU Survey: Self-supervised image denoising for flux-preserving photometry[2611.05447]
Abstract

Context. Deep photometric surveys increasingly rely on low signal-to-noise imaging, where noise affects source detection, flux measurements, photometric redshifts, and sample selection. Self-supervised denoising methods are attractive because they do not require ground-truth images, but applying them to quantitative photometry requires controlling the flux biases they may introduce. Aims. We evaluate and improve Noise2Void (N2V) for denoising real astronomical images, focusing on preserving fluxes, reducing photometric scatter, and retaining the main source features across a wide magnitude range. Methods. We use real observations from the Physics of the Accelerating Universe Survey (PAUS) together with PAUS-like simulated images. We show that standard N2V systematically underestimates galaxy fluxes, especially for faint galaxies. To mitigate this bias, we add a bounded-output layer that limits large negative outputs, and fine-tune the model with low-rank adaptation (LoRA) and two flux-aware loss terms that preserve the flux scale and improve repeated-exposure flux consistency. Results. For PAUS galaxies with 22 <= i_AB < 23 in the NB645 band, the standard N2V flux-ratio distribution peaks far below unity, while the bounded-output model restores it close to the original PAUS flux scale. The flux-aware LoRA fine-tuning reduces the width of the repeated-exposure flux-difference distribution to sigma_68 = 0.529 in NB645, an effective 47% reduction in random flux scatter relative to the original PAUS uncertainty scale, while keeping the median flux ratio close to unity. Conclusions. Denoising methods for astronomical surveys must be evaluated with photometric diagnostics, not image-quality metrics alone. With flux-aware modifications, self-supervised denoising can be adapted to low-SNR survey images while preserving the flux information needed for quantitative analysis.

3. Brewing another binary in the Teapot: Nandou=Phi Sagittarii[2611.05455]
Abstract

The iconic Teapot asterism in Sagittarius is particularly rich in intermediate-mass stars in multiple systems. Here we combine VLTI/GRAVITY, archival spectroscopic observations, past radial velocity measurements and photometry to solve for the orbital and stellar parameters of the binary Nandou=Phi Sagittarii. It has a period $P=238.844\pm0.011$ days, a semi-major axis $a=1.32\pm0.10\text{ au}$ and eccentricity $e=0.418\pm0.004$. The precision in the dynamical masses $M_A=3.8\pm0.9 M_{\odot}$ and $M_B=1.6\pm0.4 M_{\odot}$ is currently limited by the parallax $p=12.5\pm1.0 \text{ mas}$. We find that the original Hipparcos 1997 Intermediate Astrometric Data (IAD) are consistent with the expected photocenter semi-major axis $a_{\mathrm{phot}} \sim 5 \text{ mas}$, while the IAD from the Hipparcos 2007 new reduction yield a too small $a_{\mathrm{phot}} = 1.7\pm0.3 \text{ mas}$, confirming the suspicion that a too aggressive modeling of the satellite attitude can lead to overfitting that suppresses the orbital motion. Matching the primary's temperature $T_{\mathrm{eff,A}} = 12000\pm250 \text{ K}$ and radius $R_{\mathrm{A}} = 5.1\pm 0.4 R_{\odot}$ to stellar evolutionary models implies that it is a $M_A \simeq 4 M_{\odot}$ star near the end of the main-sequence. Nandou will eventually most likely become a symbiotic star.

4. How sky subtraction scales affect Intracluster light measurements[2611.05467]
Abstract

Deep imaging surveys are opening a new window on the low surface brightness universe. However, the recovery of diffuse emission depends on the survey depth and how the sky background is treated. Estimating the sky on scales comparable to astrophysical sources risks biasing flux measurements. This effect is particularly significant for intracluster light, the most extended diffuse baryonic component of galaxy clusters. We show in a $M_{500}$–$z$ plane that the sky subtraction scales employed in the Legacy Survey of Space and Time Data Preview 2 and Hyper Suprime-Cam Public Data Release 3 (HSC-PDR3) impact clusters with $M_{500}\gtrsim10^{13.4}\,\mathrm{M_\odot}$ at all redshifts, whereas HSC-PDR2 only oversubtracts the most massive systems at $z < 0.1$. The larger sky subtraction scale anticipated for the full LSST survey will mitigate this bias.

5. CosmoVerse Data Challenge: How Tip of the Red Giant Branch Analysis Choices Shape the Inferred Hubble Constant[2611.05468]
Abstract

Tip of the red giant branch (TRGB)-based determinations of the Hubble constant ($H_0$), the current expansion rate of the Universe, depend on choices made in selecting and analyzing stellar photometry and in assembling the sample of calibrator galaxies hosting Type Ia supernovae (SNe Ia). The CosmoVerse Data Challenge invites participants to analyze public photometric catalogs within a shared distance-ladder framework, opening each step of the measurement to the community and testing how these choices propagate into $H_0$. Participants independently selected photometric catalogs, galaxies, and TRGB measurement settings, while using a common SN Ia compilation and fitting procedure to convert distances into $H_0$. Supported by public notebooks and a training school, the challenge received 26 submissions from 22 participants. All submissions used the same analysis notebook, many participants were trainees at the school, and the true galaxy distances are unknown; the results therefore characterize the sensitivity of the submissions to analysis and sample-selection choices and are not a combined determination of $H_0$ or an estimate of the systematic uncertainty in published TRGB measurements. The inferred $H_0$ values have a median of $77.10\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}$ and a standard deviation of $7.38\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}$ $(5.11\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}$ for the scaled median absolute deviation), $\sim 3.4$ $(\sim 2.4)$ times the typical quoted uncertainty of a submission. Differences in the measured tip of the anchor galaxy NGC 4258 add a coherent shift in the distance scale. The challenge shows how different choices within a common framework can produce substantially different values of $H_0$, and provides an open basis for tracing these differences and for making datasets and tools more transparent and accessible.

6. Searching for Signatures of i-process Nucleosynthesis in Metal-Rich, Lead-rich Barium Stars[2611.05470]
Abstract

The intermediate neutron-capture process ($i$-process) has been invoked to explain the peculiar compositions of carbon-enhanced metal-poor (CEMP) stars enriched in both slow- ($s$) and rapid- ($r$) neutron-capture elements. Recent literature has questioned its operation at higher metallicities, but observational evidence remains limited. We analyse Gemini/GHOST and McDonald/Tull spectra of six $\rm -0.7 < [Fe/H] < -0.1$ stars identified in GALAH by their extreme Ba and Eu (or Sm) compositions ($\gtrsim$10$\times$ solar). We also reanalyse archival spectra of two literature $i$-process candidates. We determine abundances in up to 42 elements (Li through Pb), $^{12}$C/$^{13}$C, and Mg isotopic ratios, and compare to literature barium and CEMP stars and multiple asymptotic giant branch (AGB) model grids. Three stars exhibit extreme [Pb/Fe] (+1.79$\pm$0.19 to +2.50$\pm$0.32 dex), and two extreme [Eu/Fe] (+1.10$\pm$0.11 to +1.30$\pm$0.11 dex), distinguishing them from typical metal-rich barium stars. Two of these three exhibit abundance patterns that are inconsistent with $s$-process models and best explained by existing $i$-process predictions. The remaining sample is either consistent with $s$-process enrichment or suggestive of atomic diffusion. Both literature candidates are well-fit by the $i$-process model, but conventional $s$-process enrichment cannot be excluded. Our findings reinforce that metal-rich AGB stars contribute to the production of elements classically attributed to the $r$-process. These results identify promising candidates for $i$-process enrichment near solar metallicity and motivate additional $i$-process models to understand the contribution of this nucleosynthetic process to the chemical evolution of the Milky Way disk.

7. Dynamics of self-gravitating halos with decaying dark matter[2611.05474]
Abstract

We study the evolution of self-gravitating spherical dark matter halos composed of dark matter particles that decay to a slightly less massive particle and a massless particle. The small kick velocity imparted to the daughter particle upon decay heats the halo. If the kick velocity is small compared with the characteristic velocity, the halo remains bound but puffs out. We also consider, for the first time, decaying halos with anisotropic initial conditions and find non-negligible effects on the resulting density profile. To simulate the halo evolution, we utilize the recently developed \texttt{NSphere} code across a broad range of decay times and kick velocities. We train an emulator to provide a mapping between the decay parameters and the relaxed density profile. We discuss how the resulting halo-scale signatures complement existing cosmological constraints on decaying dark matter and can be tested by upcoming surveys.

8. Euclid. Predicting scaling relations for emission-line galaxies using simulated data[2611.05482]
Abstract

The Euclid spectroscopic surveys will substantially increase the number of observed emission-line galaxies. We assess the ability of the Euclid Wide Survey (EWS) and Euclid Deep Survey (EDS), at the final DR3 depth, to recover the star-forming main sequence (MS), dust-mass relation (DMR), and mass-metallicity relation (MZR) from emission-line measurements. We use MAMBO (mocks with abundance matching in Bologna) spectroscopic simulations processed with the official Euclid pipelines. The MS is traced over $0\leq z\leq 4$ using Pa$\beta$, H$\alpha$, and [OII] as SFR tracers. The shallow EWS flux limit biases the MS towards higher normalisation and a flatter slope by selecting the brightest, most strongly star-forming galaxies. The deeper EDS enables almost complete sampling with Pa$\beta$ and H$\alpha$ at log$_{10}(M_*/M_\odot)\geq10.3$ over $0\leq z\leq 1.88$, with mild biases at both redshift limits. The [OII]-based MS is increasingly biased towards higher SFRs at the highest redshifts. The DMR is traced through the Balmer decrement over $1.45\leq z\leq 1.88$ and $0.9\leq z\leq 1.88$ in the EWS and EDS, respectively. The derived colour excess agree well with the input values, although detecting the faint H$\beta$ line restricts the sample to less obscured galaxies at fixed $M_*$, particularly in the EWS; the DMR is better sampled in the EDS. Finally, a golden sample detected in multiple optical rest-frame emission lines at $1.49\leq z\leq 1.88$ recovers gas-phase metallicities with typical accuracy of 0.1 dex at log$_{10}(M_*/M_\odot)\geq10$, while lower-mass galaxies show biases up to 0.15 dex. Relative to H-band limited sample, this sample preferentially traces the upper MS and lower MZR. Despite the simplified simulations lacking effects such as mutual spectral contamination and detector persistence, these forecasts provide a benchmark for future Euclid spectroscopic analyses.

9. Euclid: Forecasting the number counts of bright line emitters in the Euclid Wide Survey and Euclid Deep fields[2611.05484]
Abstract

The Euclid mission will revolutionize our understanding of the visible Universe by collecting images and spectra of millions of galaxies across cosmic time and environments. Here, we forecast the number counts of galaxies emitting [OII], H\b{eta}, [OIII], H{\alpha} and Pa\b{eta} lines in the Euclid Wide Survey (EWS) and Deep Survey (EDS). We compare predictions from different models, including semi-analytic models and empirical scaling relations. We then simulate spectra for galaxies in the MAMBO lightcone using FastSpec, designed to rapidly generate one-dimensional spectra as observed by Euclid. We process these simulated spectra with the official Euclid spectroscopic pipeline to recover redshifts and line fluxes and derive expected counts. Predictions for H{\alpha}+[NII] emitters are broadly consistent across mocks and with existing observations. In the EWS, relative RMS scatter among mock predictions for integrated counts is approximately 19% for H{\alpha}+[NII], 44% for [OII] and 41% for [OIII], reflecting greater model dependence for oxygen-line forecasts. After accounting for flux losses and redshift misidentification, corrected flux measurements recover most intrinsic line counts, especially in the EDS. From the MAMBO-based FastSpec simulations, we predict that Euclid will detect about 42 million H{\alpha} emitters in the EWS and 1.7 million in the EDS. The same simulations predict approximately 0.5 and 0.8 million [OII] and [OIII] emitters, respectively, in the EDS, and about 1.3 and 7.5 million in the EWS. These non-H{\alpha} forecasts remain subject to substantial model-dependent uncertainties, as shown by scatter among the different mocks. These samples will be instrumental in pushing large-scale structure and baryon acoustic oscillation studies beyond redshift z 2, extending Euclid cosmological and galaxy evolution studies beyond what is achievable with H{\alpha} alone.

10. The M87 Black Hole Mass from JWST and Keck Stellar Kinematics Spanning 10 pc to 10 kpc[2611.05486]
Abstract

We combine JWST NIRSpec and Keck KCWI observations to construct a contiguous 2D map of M87's stellar kinematics spanning three decades in projected radius, from ${\sim}10$ pc to ${\sim}10$ kpc, and measure the mass of its central supermassive black hole. The innermost stellar kinematic measurements probe projected radii as small as ${\sim}3$% of the black hole's sphere of influence radius, or approximately 30,000 Schwarzschild radii. The NIRSpec spectra within $R < 0.45''$ are contaminated by quasiperiodic "wiggle" artifacts. We develop hyphen, which uses detector spectral traces to predict how the wiggle frequency varies with wavelength, then fits and subtracts the wiggle pattern from each spectrum in each exposure before combining the data. The cleaned spectra reveal a pronounced central rise in stellar velocity dispersion and $h_4$. Triaxial Schwarzschild stellar orbit modeling with the TriOS code, fitting ${\sim}$5,000 kinematic constraints across 618 spatial bins from JWST and Keck, yields a black hole mass of $M_{\mathrm{BH}}=(5.38 \pm 0.13\pm 0.22)\times10^9\,M_\odot$. This result is consistent with our prior KCWI-only determination but with the statistical uncertainty reduced by a factor of 2-3, now subdominant to the systematic uncertainty in the distance ($D$) to M87. Our inferred mass-to-distance ratio is $\theta_g=GM_{\mathrm{BH}}/(c^2 D) = 3.16 \pm 0.08\, \mu$as, whose 90% credible interval overlaps with that obtained from the multi-epoch Event Horizon Telescope (EHT) analysis, providing a robust and independent prior for future EHT studies.

11. A pocket of oxygen in a near-pristine galaxy at z=4.2: chemical inhomogeneity at 10-parsec scales in LAP2[2611.05487]
Abstract

We aim to constrain the oxygen abundance, the ionizing properties, and the spatial structure of LAP2, a strongly lensed (with a magnification $\mu$ 50) oxygen-deficient galaxy at z=4.2 behind the galaxy cluster Abell 2744. We present new JWST/NIRSpec IFU PRISM observations ( 13.6 hr) of LAP2, which we combine with archival JWST/NIRCam imaging. LAP2 is intrinsically extremely faint (M_UV=-12.7) and has a low stellar mass (M $10^5~M_\odot$), comparable to that of massive star clusters. It consists of a compact core ($R_{eff}$ 7 pc) embedded in a more diffuse emission (with $R_{eff}$ 20 pc). Strong H$\alpha$ emission (EW$_{\rm rest}\simeq880$ Å, $\log\xi_{\rm ion}\simeq25.3$), a blue UV slope ($\beta_{\rm UV}\simeq-2.5$, down to $\simeq-3.1$ in the core), and a Balmer decrement consistent with no dust attenuation indicate a young ($\lesssim10$ Myr), dust-free burst. [OIII]$\lambda$5007 is not detected in the integrated spectrum, implying [OIII]/H$\beta\leq0.61$ ($3\sigma$) and, using an extrapolated strong-line calibration, $12+\log({\rm O/H})\leq6.4$ ($\lesssim0.5\%\,Z_\odot$). This places LAP2 among the most metal-poor galaxies known. Faint [OIII] emission is, however, detected in the core (with $\rm4\sigma$ significance), where we derive $12+\log({\rm O/H})\simeq6.6$ ($\sim1\%\,Z_\odot$). This difference can be explained either by local self-enrichment, requiring only $\rm \sim0.2\,M_\odot$ of newly synthesized oxygen, or by a higher ionization parameter in the core. The upper limit on HeII$\lambda$1640 (EW$_{\rm rest}<39$ Å, $3\sigma$) does not exclude a contribution from metal-free stars. LAP2 extends the census of spectroscopically confirmed EMPGs to star-cluster masses and to $M_{\rm UV}\approx-13$. Its location in a low-density region supports a scenario in which underdense environments preserve nearly pristine gas well after reionization.

12. She Sees Shells by the Stellar Shore: A Kinematic View of NGC 253's Stellar Halo[2611.05493]
Abstract

The stellar halos of nearby galaxies are fossil records of ancient mergers, providing insight into the formation and growth of these systems. These halos often contain substructure in the form of streams, shells, and debris fields that make up dynamically young remnants of past accretions. These remnants can be probed with resolved-star spectroscopic observations, giving insight into the kinematics of bygone satellite companions. Such observations have been made for the Milky Way (MW) and Andromeda, but are much more challenging and expensive to do outside the Local Group. The Local Volume has an abundance of MW-mass galaxies with richly structured halos, such as NGC 253. Leveraging the power of Magellan's M2FS fiber-fed multiobject spectrograph, we have obtained medium-resolution spectra of 225 stars centered on NGC 253's southwestern shell. These spectra enabled us to make the first-ever measurement of the resolved-star stellar kinematics for 89 stars in NGC 253's halo. We also detect substructure in several ways and identify regions likely to be accreted and in-situ, finding among them a low-velocity region coincident with the previously reported SW shell we believe was accreted recently, and an in-situ component that traces the outer disk. After subtracting NGC 253's systemic velocity, we measure median line-of-sight velocities of $-34_{-12}^{+16}$ km/s and $167_{-14}^{+16}$ km/s for the accreted and in-situ populations, respectively. This work highlights the utility of kinematics to differentiate between accreted and in-situ stars, and acts as a pathfinder for future observations with next-generation 30 m telescopes and wide-field multiobject spectrographs.

13. ReveaLLAGN 2: Exploring the Nuclear Gas and Stellar Kinematics of Nearby LLAGNs with JWST/NIRSpec IFU[2611.05496]
Abstract

Low-luminosity active galactic nuclei (LLAGN) represent the most common mode of black hole accretion in the local Universe, yet their nuclear structure, stellar and gas kinematics remain poorly constrained at parsec scales. We investigate the nuclear regions of eight nearby representative LLAGN, the ReveaLLAGN sample: M87, M104, M94, M81, NGC1052, NGC4258, NGC5128 and NGC4395. Using JWST/NIRSpec data, we probe the stellar, molecular hydrogen, hydrogen recombination, and high ionisation coronal lines kinematics and their spatial distribution, exploring how detectability and spatial distributions of these lines vary across different Eddington ratios. The stellar LOSV maps reveal a slowly rotating disk in the nuclei of all our targets, characterised by an anti-correlation with the h3 Gauss-Hermite moment. Across the sample, the stellar velocity dispersion rises toward the central black hole. Only in M104 and M94 does the velocity dispersion display a more intricate pattern that departs from a simple disk configuration, indicating the presence of multiple kinematic components in these galaxies. Across the sample, the gas distribution and kinematics exhibit rotation that is frequently decoupled from the stellar rotation, suggesting that the stellar gravitational potential is decoupled from the current accretion state. The gas kinematics are often visually aligned with the radio jet, presenting broader velocity dispersions along the jet direction. Line-ratio maps indicate that the dominant excitation mechanism is photoionisation by the central AGN, with mechanical heating from shocks becoming important only at higher Eddington ratios. Across the sample, we detect coronal lines in all LLAGN when using nuclear-aperture spectra, and we can obtain spatially resolved kinematics only in NGC5128 and NGC4395, the highest Eddington ratio sources.

14. DESI DR2 Results III: The Mass of the Milky Way[2611.05518]
Abstract

We present measurements of the mass of the Milky Way using distant halo stars selected from the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI) Data Release 2 (DR2). We apply four dynamical modeling methods to 10,036 RR Lyrae (RRL) and blue horizontal branch (BHB) stars with 6D phase space coordinates that span from $\sim$10 to 95 kpc from the Galactic center, the largest samples of their kind. All methods are applied to mock surveys based on cosmological simulations that include generating individual tracer stars, convolving their phase-space coordinates with DESI and Gaia DR3-like uncertainties, and tailored models of the survey selection functions. By comparing the performance on these mocks, we quantify how systematic biases from both methodology assumptions and galaxy disequilibrium impact each estimate. The mock-based systematics, which differ by method and tracer, are used to correct the individual estimates and to derive joint estimates using a rigorous formalism that accounts for per-method biases and residual correlations between methods. We obtain a joint estimate of the mass within 80 kpc to be $M(r < 80 \mathrm{\,kpc}) = 6.36^{+0.53}_{-0.48} \times 10^{11}$ M$_\odot$ and a virial mass estimate of $M_\mathrm{200c} = 1.19^{+0.14}_{-0.12} \times 10^{12}$ M$_\odot$, with similar results when combining only RRL-based or BHB-based measurements. These results provide a roadmap for future DESI efforts, as well as those in other spectroscopic surveys, to reliably constrain the mass of our Galaxy.

16. Constraining the Milky Way Mass profile with data driven distribution function using DESI DR2 halo stars[2611.05520]
Abstract

We employ a novel data-driven dynamical modeling method, the empirical distribution function (emPDF), to constrain the Milky Way mass profile using a large sample of halo stars from the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument Milky Way Survey (DESI MWS). The method does not assume any functional form of the tracer distribution. Deviations from the steady-state assumption and the effects of observational errors are further tested and calibrated using a number of realistic mock observations (AuriDESI). Applying the calibrated method to two classes of tracers in DESI, we derive a best-constrained virial mass and concentration of $\log M_\mathrm{200c}/M_{\odot}=12.05_{-0.12}^{+0.21}(_{-0.18}^{+0.26})$ and $\log c_\mathrm{200c}=1.20_{-0.16}^{+0.11}(_{-0.24}^{+0.20})$ for BHBs. The two tracer populations provide overall consistent measurements of the MW halo mass profile. 17. A distribution-function estimate of the Milky Way mass profile from outer halo stars in DESI DR2[2611.05521]
Abstract

We present measurements of the Milky Way mass using blue horizontal-branch (BHB) and RR Lyrae (RRL) stars from the second data release of DESI (DR2), covering its first three years of operations. We employ 279 BHBs and 213 RRLs at $\sim\!50$–$80$ kpc from the Galactic center, selected from catalogs roughly twice as large as those of DESI DR1, used in our previous mass measurement. We apply the Galactic Mass Estimator (GME), a hierarchical Bayesian framework that assumes power-law forms for the potential and the tracer density, and accounts for distance and velocity uncertainties. Validating the method on 24 AuriDESI mock catalogs (six Auriga halos at four viewing angles), we find that it overestimates the enclosed mass by $\sim\!15\%$ at 80 kpc, and correct our results accordingly. We obtain $M(<80\,{\rm kpc}) = 0.64^{+0.06}_{-0.06}$ and $0.53^{+0.05}_{-0.05} \times 10^{12}\,M_\odot$ for BHBs and RRLs, respectively, while including Sagittarius stream stars lowers these masses by $\sim\!10$\%. Extrapolating to 100 kpc, we obtain $M(<100\,{\rm kpc}) = 0.74^{+0.07}_{-0.07}$ and $0.62^{+0.06}_{-0.07} \times 10^{12}\,M_\odot$, and anchoring an NFW halo to our GME constraints at 65 kpc yields virial masses of $M_{200} = 1.12^{+0.14}_{-0.12}$ and $0.89^{+0.11}_{-0.10} \times 10^{12}\,M_\odot$. BHBs yield systematically higher masses than RRLs across the full radial range, reflecting the impact of tracer choice even within a single method. This work is part of a wider DESI DR2 Key Project effort to measure the Milky Way mass and constrain systematics arising from different samples and methodologies.

18. On the Detectability of Compact Multi-Planet Systems via Gravitational Microlensing[2611.05535]
Abstract

Compact multi-planet systems orbiting ultracool dwarfs, such as TRAPPIST-1, may be a major population of terrestrial worlds with poorly constrained demographics. Individual close-in planets are expected to escape microlensing detection, but whether a compact system can produce a detectable collective signal remains to be quantified. We calculate the microlensing signal from each TRAPPIST-1 planet, and of all seven combined, if the system were a lens toward the bulge. For a 0.09 $M_\odot$ lens at 4 kpc, $R_E \approx 1.21$ AU, placing the planets at $s = 0.010$–$0.051~\theta_E$, 12–63 times interior to the lensing zone. We measure each signal as the residual after fitting a single-lens model, which accounts for the barycentric dipole. Because the quadrupole term cancels between the two images to first order, the residual scales as the octupole ($q s^3$), reaching $|\delta A / A| \sim 10^{-6}$. The seven planets combined yield $\Delta\chi^2 \sim 10^{-3}$ even at 1 mmag precision, five orders of magnitude below the standard detection threshold. Detection requires increasing all planet masses by a factor of $\sim$300, whereas the resonant chain loses its guarantee of Hill stability at a factor of $\sim$7. Only a lens within $\sim$15 pc of the source or the Sun places the outer planets in the lensing zone. The Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope is therefore not expected to detect a TRAPPIST-1-like system through microlensing, whereas its transit channel in the same fields can detect such planets in large numbers, marking a sharp boundary in the Galactic census between microlensing and transit sensitivity.

19. Identifying Compact Object Candidates using Gaia DR3 Astrometric Accelerations and Jerks[2611.05536]
Abstract

Gaia Data Release 3 (DR3) provides astrometric measurements for more than a billion sources, hundreds of thousands of which have non-single-star astrometric solutions that indicate deviations from linear proper motion. Although sources with orbital solutions have been extensively inspected for compact objects via the astrometric mass function, two other solution types–the 7-parameter constant-acceleration solutions characterized by $\dot{\mathbf{\mu}}$, and the 9-parameter variable-acceleration solutions characterized by $\dot{\mathbf{\mu}}$ and $\ddot{\mathbf{\mu}}$–have not been as carefully explored. In this work, we develop a methodology for selecting sources within the Gaia DR3 9-parameter non-single-star astrometric solutions that are most likely to host massive compact companions. We assume that a source is the luminous star in a binary orbit around a dark object and derive analytic expressions for the sky-projected accelerations and jerks for three binary populations with different orbit approximations: inclined eccentric, face-on eccentric, and face-on circular. Using POSYDON, we develop a fourth, more realistic population of binaries, each consisting of a luminous star and a black hole. For each population, we address observational biases using gaiamock and select only the systems it fits with a 9-parameter solution. We find that binaries likely to host massive compact companions can be identified from the characteristic joint astrometric accelerations and jerks exhibited by the luminous stars, despite 9-parameter fits under-constraining full orbits. Finally, to illustrate the utility of our approach, we analyze a historical object of interest, HD 12871, and examine 9-parameter fits of Gaia BH3-like binaries.

20. Revisiting the Stellar-Dynamical Mass of the Central Black Hole in NGC 3227[2611.05547]
Abstract

We present a new stellar-dynamical mass for the supermassive black hole in the nearby Seyfert galaxy NGC 3227. We determined the line-of-sight velocity distributions of stars in the nuclear region of NGC 3227 using archival spatially-resolved $K$-band spectra obtained with NIFS at Gemini Observatory. The surface brightness profile of the galaxy is described by four components, measured from a WFC3 F547M image from the Hubble Space Telescope. We constructed dynamical models using the Schwarzschild orbit-superposition method implemented in the open-source code $\texttt{FORSTAND}$. We find a best-fit black hole mass of $M_{\mathrm{BH}}$=$(1.2\pm0.5) \times 10^7$ $M_{\odot}$. This mass is consistent with a previous mass from R. I. Davies et al. based on stellar dynamics using orbit-based modeling, but our results make use of higher signal-to-noise data from which the higher-order moments of the stellar LOSVDs are constrained, thus providing a more precise $M_{\mathrm{BH}}$. Our result is also consistent with the $M_{\mathrm{BH}}$ constraint from velocity-resolved reverberation mapping reported by M. C. Bentz et al.. And while our best-fit mass is slightly smaller than the mass constraint from gas-dynamical modeling reported by E. K. S. Hicks & M. A.
