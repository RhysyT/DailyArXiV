1. The search for the Higgs Boson and the EBH mechanism[2611.03315]
Abstract

The searches for the Higgs boson at LEP and the Tevatron between 1980 and 2000 are set in the context of the underlying theory of spontaneous symmetry breaking and early phenomenological studies. The evolution of direct searches at LEP and the Tevatron are reviewed, as well as indirect constraints from precision electroweak data. Finally, the state of play in 2000 is summarised, together with the perspectives it offered for later searches.

2. Invariant universal averages and Bertrand's paradox: a response to Shackel[2611.00981]
Abstract

In 2014, with D. Aerts we proposed a solution to the hard problem of Bertrand's paradox by means of a universal average over different ways of randomizing an entity. In his recent monograph, N. Shackel raises objections concerning the approximation by cellular densities, the restrictions used in their construction, the apparently trivial limit of their averages, the choice of a second-order probability law, and the dependence of the result on the naming of chords. We revisit these objections using the invariance-based formulation of the universal average. The first objection is well taken, and the approximation argument is repaired: an explicit sequence of bivalent cellular densities provides weak approximation of any prescribed probability measure, with an error bound for intervals. We show that any second-order selection law invariant under rearrangements of equal-length cells has the same uniform barycenter, independently of the particular densities and weights employed. For cellular densities, this conclusion holds exactly at every finite resolution. Thus, non-uniqueness of the second-order law does not prevent uniqueness of the resulting average within the indifferent class. Finally, we prove that no probability measure can be invariant under both these rearrangements and arbitrary changes of interval lengths. The reference geometry therefore cannot be obtained by averaging alone: it must be supplied by the physical modelization of the randomized entity. These results clarify the scope, and the limitations, of our original distinction between the easy and hard problems of Bertrand's paradox.

3. Holistic Versus Fragmented Multiverses: Empirical Access via Causal and Grounding Signatures[2509.04918]
Abstract

Several objections have been raised against the scientificity of multiverse scenarios. This chapter defends the scientificity of certain multiverse scenarios by distinguishing fragmented from holistic multiverses. Concepts of multiverse used in physics are almost always holistic: they embed universes within a broader, unifying physical or metaphysical multiversal structure that can, in principle, leave empirical signatures inside the universes. I develop a classification of such signatures and show how it applies to a number of scenarios from quantum theory, cosmology, and string theory. The framework clarifies why objections such as the `this universe' objection and a newly articulated generalization, the epistemic isolation objection, fail against scientifically motivated multiverses.

4. Der Stern von Bethlehem – Einige Theorien und Hintergruende: Von der altorientalischen Astrologie ueber Kepler zu Newton[2509.15387]
Abstract

Many have have taken in hand to write a treatise on the Star of Bethlehem, particularly on Kepler's explanation as a stellar birth, triggered by Mars joining a great conjunction (a meeting of Jupiter and Saturn), as he observed it in October 1604. It is the aim of my short treatise to introduce, at a hopefully well digestible level, into underlying antiquity's concepts of physics, astrology, and astronomy around the time of Christ's birth and to demonstrate their essential developments up to the early modern period. In the latter, Kepler played a significant role. His contributions enabled him to calculate back to the year 7 BC with astonishing precision, locating a triple great conjunction in Pisces, which was then complemented by Mars. This is confirmed by both Babylonian tradition and modern astronomy. The following background provided may be specifically helpful for a more theologically oriented audience, also because the controversy surrounding "geocentric" and "heliocentric" world systems (Copernicus) is touched upon. And, because the overall context raises questions for theology, with which the epilogue concludes.

5. From Rock Tumbling to Telescope Mirror Grinding: How Optical Perfection Emerges from Random Motion[2611.01673]
Abstract

When two glass disks are rubbed together by hand with an abrasive between them, their surfaces spontaneously converge to perfectly matching spherical curves, accurate to tens of nanometers. How does such optical precision emerge from an essentially random manual process? Starting from Preston's law of material removal, we derive a first-principles explanation. Symmetries of the grinding stroke imply that planes and spheres are the generic equilibria of the process. Their stability is governed by a hierarchy of timescales: small-scale irregularities decay exponentially on the grinding timescale $T_\text{grind}$, while large-scale deviations from a sphere diffuse away on the longer spherizing timescale $T_\text{sphere} \propto (R_\text{mirror}/R_\text{stroke})^2\, T_\text{grind}$. Gravity and finite disk size destabilize planar surfaces, but for sufficiently short strokes spheres remain stable ($T_\text{deform} \gg T_\text{sphere}$), with only their radius of curvature varying adiabatically. The same framework unifies telescope mirror grinding with everyday phenomena: rock tumbling (explaining why rotary tumbling rounds edges more than vibratory, and why neither produce spheres) and sanding (explaining why small flat surfaces inevitably round). These examples offer an accessible classroom context for stable and unstable equilibria, exponential relaxation, diffusion, multi-timescale analysis, and the emergence of the Laplacian operator from averaging over a local area.

6. Soccer: a quantitative analysis of team resilience and the miracle of Bern[2203.06933]
Abstract

Resilience is the ability to positively respond to adversity. It has been studied in psychology for several decades, with focus on how individuals overcome traumata or cope with setbacks and obstacles in their professional career. Research on resilience in the sport context is rather new. Activities are based on insights that in highly competitive environments, tiny effects tip the scales. A key question of measuring resilience in sports is what parameters to measure. Here a novel concept is proposed to measure the resilience of soccer teams. The relative frequency of matches is determined, where a soccer team, which is initially trailing by 2 goals, finally succeeds to win the match or at least to reach a draw. The analysis is applied to the last 59 seasons of the German premier soccer league Bundesliga. The empirical data are compared with a theoretical model derived from Poisson distributions. It is shown how leading teams in the premier soccer league differ from the average with respect to resilience, which provides further insights into the hidden secrets of top soccer teams.

7. Beating the average: how to generate profit by exploiting the inefficiencies of soccer betting[2303.16648]
Abstract

In economy, markets are denoted as efficient when it is impossible to systematically generate profits which outperform the average. In the past years, the concept has been tested in other domains such as the growing sports betting market. Surprisingly, despite its large size and its level of maturity, sports betting shows traits of inefficiency. The anomalies indicate the existence of strategies which shift betting from a game of chance towards a game of skill. This article shows an example for an inefficiency detected in the German soccer betting TOTO 13er Wette, which is operated by state-run lottery agencies. Gamblers have to guess the outcome (win, draw, loss) of 13 soccer matches listed on a lottery tip. Applying stochastic methods, a recipe is presented to determine hit rates for single match outcomes. More important, the recipe provides the number of lottery tips required to achieve a specific number of strikes (number of correct match forecasts per lottery tip) for any given level of safety. An approximation is derived to cope with large numbers in hypergeometric distributions, valid under certain constraints. Overall, the strategy does lead to returns exceeding the aggregated lottery fees, resulting in moderate, but consistent profits. It is briefly discussed if lessons learned from soccer betting can be transferred back to financial markets, because gamblers and retail investors face similar challenges and opportunities.
