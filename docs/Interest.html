History & Philosophy of Physics

1. The Relation Before Lorenz: Kirchhoff, 1857, and the Symbols Lorenz Inherited[2610.14122]
Abstract

A relation between the quantities now called the scalar potential and the vector potential, of the same form, up to a sign, as the one the literature attributes to Ludvig Lorenz in 1867, was written ten years earlier by Gustav Kirchhoff, with the same status: derived, unnumbered and unnamed. This paper documents, on the basis of the 1857 originals, that Kirchhoff obtains the relation as the result of a calculation and does not impose it; that the quantity now called the vector potential bears no name at all in his text, whereas the scalar one bears the name "Potentialfunction", potential function, in both papers; that Lorenz writes those same letters, $\Omega$, U, V and W, and inherits with them the absence of a name for the three components, although the relation he writes is in other quantities, $\alpha$, $\beta$ and $\gamma$, and carries the opposite sign; and that the English translation of one of the two papers suppresses the word "function" from the term "Potentialfunction", with which Kirchhoff names his scalar quantity. The paper concludes that the pattern documented in the 1867 paper predates Lorenz and comes from the source from which he starts: a relation without number or name, components without a denomination, and a vocabulary added later.

2. Diverging paths to dark-matter discovery[2610.14137]
Abstract

The scientific discovery of dark matter has been pursued in two very different methodological and epistemic contexts. A microphysical context centered around the laboratory detection of a new particle, and a macrophysical context based on the astronomical identification of a non-luminous gravitating component. The justification for the salience of methodological diversity is that these different methods are complementary ways to discovering aspects of the same entity. The weakly interacting massive particle (WIMP) made this expectation scientifically credible by connecting a particle candidate to a cosmological abundance, experimental searches, and explanations of astronomical phenomena. What happens to this connection when the WIMP loses its central position? I analyze 201,627 NASA Astrophysics Data System records to examine the organization of research following the decline of WIMP prominence. Astrophysics and high-energy physics remain jointly dominant, while WIMP-related mentions decline in both fields and successor candidates receive different degrees of attention. Concept networks show that WIMP-related research remains central across the literature, even as cross-field connections become less concentrated around it. These indicators measure research attention; they do not establish what scientists would accept as a discovery. I interpret the findings through a framework distinguishing constitutive identification, cosmic attribution, explanatory adequacy, and the cross-domain identification needed to connect them. A particle, compact-object population, or gravitational theory can resolve different parts of the inherited problem, and a partial or multicomponent discovery can leave other parts open. 'Diverging paths' describes this branching of possible resolutions.

3. Observational Indistinguishability and the Beginning of the Universe[2603.04159]
Abstract

Can we infer whether all of physical reality began to exist? Several novel results are offered suggesting a negative verdict. First, two necessary (but not necessarily sufficient) conditions are offered for a cosmic beginning. Second, I discuss three theorems which show that, in a wide range of spacetimes, observers cannot infer the global structure of their spacetime; one of the three shows that spacetimes satisfying a condition necessary for a cosmic beginning are observationally indistinguishable from spacetimes which do not satisfy that condition. Next, I offer two extensions to the aforementioned theorems. The two extensions show that, in an equally wide range of spacetimes, observers cannot collect sufficient data to determine whether the application conditions for the class singularity theorems are satisfied or whether their spacetime satisfies the two necessary conditions for a cosmic beginning. Lastly, a reply is offered to the objection that the skeptical consequences of the aforementioned theorems can be overcome with induction. Importantly, all past singular dust FLRW spacetimes are observationally indistinguishable from counterparts which, while sharing a number of important local properties, either do not include a singularity to the past of every point or else do not have the sort of time ordering intuitively required for a cosmic beginning.

4. Characterizing adopters of named active learning methods in physics: An interview study[2610.14311]
Abstract

Active learning has been shown to be more beneficial for student learning than lecturing. Consequently, researchers have developed named active learning strategies for use in physics instruction. To increase adoption and support implementation of these named methods, it is important to understand the backgrounds and experiences of instructors who use them. In this study, we interviewed 20 introductory physics and astronomy instructors who use one of Student-Centered Active Learning Environment with Upside-Down Pedagogies (SCALE-UP), Investigative Science Learning Environment (ISLE), Tutorials in Introductory Physics and Astronomy, or Peer Instruction to gain insight into their teaching experiences, professional development, pathways to learning about their method, and supports for and difficulties in implementation. We find for our sample that all implementers are involved with the discipline-based education research community, but there is variation in the extent of engagement. While instructors identify a variety of supports for their implementation, such as from the broader education community and departmental administration, the difficulties they experience are representative of both long-standing issues associated with active learning adoption (e.g., timing constraints) and new issues related to emergent technologies (e.g., Artificial Intelligence). Finally, we uncover differences in implementers across the four named methods, particularly related to their training for the method and the supports they received for implementation. Together, these findings underscore the importance of supporting multiple dissemination avenues for named active learning methods, point to a possible need for more training opportunities for SCALE-UP and ISLE in particular, and identify nascent issues in the contemporary active learning classroom that future work may further characterize and address.

5. Six objects reported at 480 mph in a declassified military infrared video show brightness flicker at bird wingbeat rates[2610.08692]
Abstract

In September 2026 the U.S. Department of War released an infrared video recorded from a military aircraft over the Middle East, with a mission report describing six small spherical objects moving at an estimated 480 miles per hour. We measure the brightness of each object in every frame. All six brighten and dim steadily at 7.1-7.9 times per second, each at its own rate and out of step with the others. Tests with planted constant-brightness objects show that neither compression nor the sensor produces this flicker. The same analysis, applied to the Galileo Project's ground-based infrared cameras near Las Vegas, finds flying animals beating steadily at 3.4-10.0 times per second, within the range of bird wingbeats. A nearly stationary object at 60-80% of the aircraft's height would produce the reported ground-projected speed of 480 miles per hour. The six objects are best explained as a small flock of birds flying nearer to the aircraft than the crew assumed. The same analysis also finds seven objects with similar beats, at 3.0-11.0 times per second, in four other released military videos. More broadly, this work shows that even heavily redacted videos of unidentified anomalous phenomena can be analyzed using reproducible, quantitative methods to test specific hypotheses. Releasing the redacted sensor and platform data would provide information that could refute these hypotheses.
