History & Philosophy of Physics

1. Schrödinger's unpublished hidden-variable model in the 1944-45 debate over the "Collapse of Determinism"[2610.20752]
Abstract

In the mid-1940s, the question of whether quantum mechanics could be supplemented by deterministic hidden variables resurfaced in a debate in ^textit{Nature}. I show that Erwin Schrödinger drafted a hitherto unpublished response to this controversy. In it, Schrödinger provided an explicit hidden-variable model, thereby showing that such models could not be ruled out. I reconstruct the historical context of this forgotten manuscript and publish here the first transcription and analysis. Importantly, Schrödinger's proposal should be understood as a proof of principle rather than as evidence of his commitment to determinism.

2. On Algebraic Background-Independence[2610.22042]
Abstract

It has recently been claimed that algebraic methods can be used to give a background-independent description of semiclassical gravity. This paper aims to provide an entry point into recent algebraic approaches to quantum gravity and to analyse and critically assess the conception of background-independence here involved. We discuss an algebraic framework for formulating an algebra of observables along the worldline of an observer in a cosmological setting. By 'gravitationally dressing' the observables to the observer's worldline, the reference to the spacetime background can be set aside at the level of the abstract *-algebra. Thus this *-algebra is taken to be independent of any particular spacetime realisation, with different states on the algebra corresponding to different backgrounds. We compare this algebraic framework to three influential notions of background-independence in the literature and argue that none of these fully captures the sense in which the *-algebra is taken to be background-independent. Building on this, we extract a novel notion of algebraic background-independence tied to the representation-independence of the *-algebra. This algebraic conception motivates two further concepts of background-independence tailored to semiclassical theories: semiclassical background-independence and perturbative background-independence. We suggest that these provide useful tools for evaluating the scope and limitations of semiclassical approaches to quantum gravity.

Physics Education

3. Integrative Thinking in Computational Physics Design Essays[2610.22154]
Abstract

This study investigates integrative thinking in undergraduate computational physics design essays (CPDEs) within an engineering design-based introductory physics laboratory. Integrative thinking refers to the interaction of science, mathematics, computation, design, and metacognitive reflection during problem solving. Using the Ways of Thinking for Engineering Design-based Physics (WoT4EDP) framework, we examine how levels of integrative thinking can be characterized and manifested across teams with different levels of academic performance. We analyzed CPDEs from 14 student teams in a first-semester calculus-based physics course. Teams were grouped into high-, middle-, and low-performing categories based on members' final examination performance, enabling comparison of integrative thinking across academic performance levels. CPDEs were analyzed using thick description and an analytic rubric characterizing three levels of integrative thinking: superficial, intermediate, and substantial. Findings illustrate variation in how students coordinate multiple ways of thinking within engineering design-based physics tasks and provide a structured lens for examining integrative thinking across levels of academic performance.

4. Singular wave functions and quantum collapse in inverse-power potentials[2610.21044]
Abstract

Quantum collapse (QC) occurs under the action of the inverse-power potential U(r)=-U_{0}/(2r^{n}) in the framework of the Schroedinger equation. The corresponding stationary wave functions feature an infinite number of spatial oscillations at $r\rightarrow 0$. The ground state (GS) is missing in the presence of the QC, but the available bound states at finite energies may be physically relevant, as they are normalizable. This work aims to produce singular stationary wave functions. The analytical findings represent an asymptotic form of the stationary wave functions at $r\rightarrow 0$, which fill a continuous spectrum, and some exact solutions for the wave functions, obtained under appropriate conditions. As a regularized version, we also consider the potential truncated at a finite difference from the center. The regularized wave functions feature a finite number of spatial oscillations, the missing GS reappears, and the spectrum of the bound states is discrete.

5. Popularity-driven interactions in multilayer bounded-confidence opinion dynamics[2610.21610]
Abstract

Social media users interact with their own social networks but also with platform-wide content, where a small number of highly popular users may receive a disproportionate share of attention. In this paper, we study how those interactions outside the social networks and user popularity regulate the formation of opinion clusters. We design a bounded-confidence opinion dynamics model on a multilayer network consisting of a social network coupled to a fully connected explore layer of potential interaction contacts. We show that uniform sampling on the explore layer increases mixing across social neighbourhoods and reduces opinion diversity while also weakening the influence of the underlying social network. We then introduce a popularity-driven interaction mechanism in which contact selection is biased by a time-evolving popularity score, so that frequently selected users become increasingly likely to be re-selected. The resulting concentration of explore-layer interactions on a small number of highly popular users reduces the homogenising effect of the explore layer, increasing opinion diversity. The resulting changes relative to a single-layer system depend on the underlying social network structure. As the popularity inequality increases, a small number of highly popular users can act as opinion attractors and sustain polarisation in regimes where the corresponding model without popularity heterogeneity converges to consensus.

6. Beyond the Beacon: State and Corporate Competition after an Information-Rich Technosignature Detection[2610.21645]
Abstract

The 2026 update to the Declaration of Principles Concerning the Conduct of the Search for Extraterrestrial Intelligence provides a framework for post-detection practice. Like earlier versions, it addresses information content directly: a confirmation report should record any detected content, and the underlying data, methods, and code should be openly preserved and disseminated. The declaration is necessarily concise and is intended to be supported by supplementary material that examines specific issues in greater depth. This paper contributes to that process by considering the possible detection of an information-rich technosignature: a signal carrying data, imagery, or machine-readable content. Whereas the prevailing concept of a detection remains that of a beacon, a signal with recoverable and potentially exploitable content would shift much of the post-detection challenge from verification to information stewardship, while encouraging competitive efforts to decode and use the signal. Few studies have addressed this scenario directly, and the geopolitical literature has so far framed post-detection competition mainly in terms of state actors. Large corporations now possess the computing power, AI capabilities, and, in some cases, receiving infrastructure needed to independently pursue an extraterrestrial signal without any obligation to disclose their findings. This largely unexamined dimension materially changes the post-detection landscape. These considerations bear directly on the declaration's commitment to openness and transparency, and on how that commitment can be upheld when powerful actors outside the SETI community may not share it. I identify information-rich technosignatures as a topic for supplementary guidance and offer a preliminary set of questions for the community to consider.

7. Missed Detections and False Alarms in a CNN Technosignature Detector on Breakthrough Listen Data[2610.22050]
Abstract

Radio technosignature searches rely on machine learning detectors to shrink the candidate pool before a human looks at it. They are validated on held-out data from the training distribution, which excludes the cases where they are most likely to fail: a technosignature has unknown morphology, and terrestrial interference is unbounded in variety. I trained a standard competition detector once, froze it, and probed it with 15,000 injections across ten morphologies and five amplitudes, placed into real Green Bank backgrounds. Two directions were tested: genuine signals with morphologies other than a straight narrowband line, and interference resembling the training data. Recall did not follow the predicted ordering, and it fell as signals brightened, even for a needle of the kind the detector was trained on (from 0.590 to 0.090). One interference morphology reached a false positive rate of 0.627, comparable to the detector's own baseline recall of 0.5525 on genuine signals. Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping (Grad-CAM) showed attention along the injected path in one missed family by a pre-specified test, and visually in a second. A pre-specified, time-averaged ON-OFF filter removed false alarms and genuine detections at the same rate. The detector reads only the ON panels. Since the only evidence distinguishing a genuine candidate from terrestrial interference lies in the discarded OFF panels, it cannot tell if a signal originates from the target star; it can only tell if it resembles the training data. This explains the false alarm directly; the missed signals reflect what the detector learned to recognise, which reading the OFF panels would not by itself change.
