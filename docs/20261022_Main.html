1. A Unified Model for the Gravitational Wave Background and Quasar Luminosity Functions[2610.28680]
Abstract

The supermassive black hole mass function (BHMF) is a necessary ingredient in our understanding of how BHs grow and evolve with their host galaxies. Estimations of the BHMF based on observations of local BHs, quasars, and gravitational wave data yield inconsistent results, leading to large uncertainties in, e.g., BH accretion and seeding models. The nanohertz gravitational wave background (GWB) has been a powerful tool to provide constraints on the BHMF across cosmic time. We take posterior BHMFs that describe the GWB and fit them to bolometric quasar luminosity functions (QLF) across $0.2 \leq z \leq 6.2$ to constrain the Eddington ratio distribution function (ERDF). Compared to a fiducial BHMF, based on galaxy and a local black hole mass scaling relation, the BHMFs that match the GWB can reproduce the observed QLFs with lower Eddington fractions and without requiring significant super-Eddington accretion. We use a double power-law form of the ERDF and find that the fits using the GWB-inferred BHMF have systematically lower break-points and marginally steeper slopes in the high-Eddington fraction portion of the function. Furthermore, the best-fit QLFs using the GWB-inferred BHMFs provide better fits to the high-luminosity end of the QLF data than the fiducial model. For both models the ERDF shows significant redshift evolution of the break-point which reaches a maximum around $z \sim 2$. Overall, our prescription can produce realistic QLFs from BHMFs and our GWB-based model is consistent with electromagnetic and gravitational wave data sets.

2. Poisson Clustering and Seed Effect of PBHs: Implications for Microlensing, Gravitational Waves, Little Red Dots and Ultra-Faint Dwarf Galaxies[2610.28723]
Abstract

The existence of primordial black holes (PBHs) could have significant effects on the formation of cosmic structures through both the seed effect and the Poisson effect. For an extended PBH mass function, this should lead to dark virialised bound clusters of black holes with modest mass, together with central black holes of larger mass. We consider the combination of three effects: the inevitable gravitational binding of small-scale inhomogeneities due to Poisson and seed fluctuations; the dilution through dynamical heating of all clusters smaller than around $10^{6}\,M_{\odot}$; the survival of clusters in the outer Galactic halo (i.e. towards the Magellanic Clouds) but their destruction in the central halo region through tidal disruption and collisions. We examine the implications of this for the constraints on the fraction of dark matter in PBHs from microlensing surveys and gravitational-wave events. Although the clustering of PBHs with the expected mass and radius is unlikely to relax the microlensing limits, the decline of the Galactic rotation curve might do so, allowing much of the dark matter to be in solar-mass PBHs formed at the quantum chromodynamics transition. We also emphasise that binary merger rates must be revised: binaries formed in PBH clusters can dominate the merger rate, which may be compatible with the LIGO-Virgo-KAGRA gravitational-wave limits if PBHs provide the dark matter. More positively, Ultra-Faint Dwarf Galaxies and Little Red Dots might arise through a combination of the seed and Poisson effects. Our conclusions could be modified for PBHs produced from primordial fluctuations with exponential non-Gaussian tails, since the clustering would then exceed the Poissonian prediction, opening up new possibilities to explain dark matter with solar-mass PBHs.

3. Exploring the origin of slow-rotating pulsars: Emission from STARS simulated white dwarfs[2610.28728]
Abstract

An increasing number of slow-rotating pulsars and long-period transients have been observed in recent years. The periods of these sources can be significantly longer than typical pulsar sources, ranging from   100 - 1000 s, or even longer. The observed periods and period derivatives predict high magnetic fields, higher than the Schwinger field, when we consider conventional pulsar models of dipole-driven emission from neutron stars. Interestingly, the periods of these sources are consistent with the current observations of white dwarfs (WDs). We explore the possibility of magnetic WDs, simulated using the stellar evolution code STARS, as the origin of some of these unusual pulsar observations. We simulate WDs with enhanced magnetic fields due to accretion after their formation. We also briefly touch on the more exotic possibility of super-Chandrasekhar WDs as the origin for these sources. We find that expanding the parameter space to include the possibility of a WD origin provides an alternate and viable explanation for these unusual observations.

4. The (missing) gamma-ray counterparts to EP events[2610.28734]
Abstract

While the connection of Fast X-ray Transients (FXTs) detected by Einstein Probe (EP) with Gamma-ray Bursts (GRBs) is increasingly compelling, most lack gamma-ray counterparts, leaving their relation to GRBs unclear. We performed a systematic search for gamma-ray counterparts to the 72 transients detected by EP in its first year, using targeted searches in Fermi/GBM and Fermi/LAT data, complemented by SVOM/GRM, SVOM/ECLAIRs, Swift/BAT and Konus-Wind. For the visible events without a gamma-ray counterpart, we derived simultaneous $3\sigma$ flux upper limits in the 10-1000 keV and the 0.1-1 GeV band. We find that 23 events (32%) have a gamma-ray counterpart (seven sub-threshold), while 49 events (68%) have none, despite most being visible. For events with a gamma-ray counterpart, the X-ray flux over the gamma-ray $T_{\rm 90}$ is systematically higher than over the full X-ray duration, with flux ratios $1 \lesssim F_{\rm [10-1000\,keV]}/F_{\rm [0.5-4\,keV]} \lesssim 100$, suggesting that gamma-rays are preferentially detected during the brightest X-ray phases. For events without gamma-ray counterpart, the most stringent Fermi/GBM upper limits imply $E_{\rm peak} \lesssim 30$-100 keV and, for the 14 events with measured redshifts, $L_{\rm iso}\lesssim 10^{48}-9\times10^{51}$ erg/s. If classical GRB correlations hold in this regime, these lower energetics imply lower bulk Lorentz factors ($\Gamma_0 \lesssim 100$), as confirmed in a few cases by afterglow constraints. No significant GeV emission was detected by Fermi/LAT, yielding $1 \lesssim F_{0.1-1\,{\rm GeV}}/F_{0.5-4\,{\rm keV}} \lesssim 10$, placing the first constraints on a potential synchrotron self-Compton component for this new population. Although individual origins remain uncertain, many EP FXTs could be compatible with the GRB population, extending it toward lower $E_{\rm peak}$, luminosities, and bulk Lorentz factors.

5. Saturation Level of the Streaming Instability[2610.28736]
Abstract

In proto-planetary disks, gas turbulence can cluster dust grains and catalyze the formation of planetesimals. This process depends on the strength of the turbulence. Here, we evaluate the turbulence strength that is induced by the streaming instability. Based on the amount of free energy available, we estimate the viscous parameter to be alpha   St Pi^2 f(epsilon), where St (< 1) is the Stokes number of the streaming grains, Pi is the dimensionless pressure gradient, and f (epsilon) is an explicit function of epsilon, the local dust-to-gas density ratio. This estimate explains the results of many previous studies, and is also supported by our additional tailor-made simulations. We discuss two easy applications of our result, one is a self-consistent dust scale height, one is the astrophysical relevance of a pressure bump. Lastly, while this work allows one to characterize the streaming turbulence, we emphasize our current inability to connect the strength of turbulence to the formation of planetesimals.

7. Exploring PSR J0901-4046 as an ultra-slowly rotating neutron star versus a white dwarf[2610.28741]
Abstract

The source PSR J0901-4046 shows a very long rotational period, of $\approx 75.88$ s. In fact, its long period suggests that it could be a white dwarf. We examine the properties of the source and compare the outcomes under the hypotheses of the source being a highly magnetized white dwarf (B-WD) and neutron star. We find that assuming it to be a B-WD with a surface field $\sim 10^{10}$ G explains many properties, including its storage/rotational energy, explaining luminosity. On the contrary, the neutron star-based idea, particularly if examined under the conventional spin down mechanism, requires an unusually high surface magnetic field $\gtrsim 10^{14}$ G, even exceeding $10^{16}$ G, depending on the obliquity angle and emission mechanism. The source also sits very close to AR Sco, a white dwarf pulsar, in the period and period-derivative plane. We suggest that the source is a B-WD. Along with AR Sco, it being a B-WD constrains the death line model. B-WD hypothesis alleviates the necessity of an unusually high magnetic field hindering pair production for PSR J0901-4046 under the neutron star hypothesis. We argue that some rotation-powered pulsars with extreme properties may possibly be white dwarfs rather than neutron stars.

8. Gravothermal Evolution of Self-Interacting Dark Matter Halos across Knudsen Numbers[2610.28745]
Abstract

If the dark matter self-interacts elastically via a light mediator, then at relative velocities above a scale $w$ dictated by particle physics parameters, the cross section scales as $\sigma \propto v^{-4}$. Depending on the value of $w$, the self-interaction cross section in low-mass halos could be (much) larger than has been previously studied extensively. We perform kinetic simulations of isolated self-interacting dark matter halos using the code KiSS-SIDM for a wide range of halo masses, concentrations, and cross sections from $50 \,\mathrm{cm}^2 \, \mathrm{g}^{-1}$ to $250{,}000 \, \mathrm{cm}^2 \, \mathrm{g}^{-1}$, including several velocity-dependent and fully differential (anisotropic) cross sections. We demonstrate that for idealized NFW halos the collapse time can be predicted from the Knudsen number at the scale radius of the halo. We perform similar calculations for halos extracted from cosmological simulations using either $N$-body (collisionless) or SPH (strongly collisional) dynamics, and then evolve cutouts with KiSS-SIDM. For these halos, the collapse times differ from the idealized NFW simulations by up to a factor of a few. Further, we calibrate a hydrostatic fluid model to these results, which solves the structure equations as a boundary value problem via two-point shooting. The fluid calculation predicts that for velocity-independent cross sections, a convective region of the halo develops late in the collapse. The collapse times agree well between the kinetic simulations and the fluid code, especially in the velocity-independent case. The density profiles agree well in some cases, which include all of the velocity-dependent cross section runs.

9. A new view of the cosmic-ray knee after LHAASO[2610.28751]
Abstract

Recent LHAASO measurements offer a new view of the cosmic-ray knee by combining the all-particle spectrum and mean logarithmic mass with proton and light-component spectra across the PeV region. This contribution reviews these results and their implications for Galactic acceleration and transport. Approximately charge-ordered proton and helium knees coexist with different spectral evolution, while their turnover shapes constrain source populations and escape mechanisms. 10. Can we constrain the binary neutron star merger time from kilonova light curves?[2610.28775]
Abstract

The joint observation of gravitational waves (GWs) from a binary neutron star (BNS) merger (GW170817), together with a short Gamma-Ray Burst (GRB 170817A) and a kilonova (KN, AT2017gfo), marked the birth of multimessenger astronomy with GWs, and demonstrated the informative power of multimessenger observations. More BNS merger and KN detections are expected , thanks to the increasing sensitivity of GW detectors and the advent of new electromagnetic facilities such as the Vera C. Rubin Observatory. However, the association between a BNS merger and a KN might not be trivial: the detection of a KN might not coincide with the onset of the EM emission, making it difficult to define a temporal window to assess the coincidence with a GW event. In this work we investigate the possibility to constrain the BNS merger time from KN light curves, exploring different observing conditions and with focus on the Vera C. Rubin Observatory. We used different KN models and different inference frameworks to simulate and fit KN light curves. We explored different cadences and wavelength coverages for observations, to investigate how the different observing conditions can affect the capability to recover the BNS merger time. The tests performed also include real data related to AT2017gfo, plus a few GRBs that have been associated to possible KN candidates. We find that, under realistic observing conditions with the Vera C. Rubin Observatory, the BNS merger time can be recovered with a precision of 5.5 days (90\% CI) with a best-case scenario with an accuracy of 0.18 day and a precision of 0.74 day (90 \% CI). Results can slightly improve (by $\sim$ 5 \%) if the source redshift is available. Upper limits near the merger time can also improve the results, reducing the width of the posterior by $\sim60$ \%. The BNS merger time estimate could also benefit from EM follow-up observations with other telescopes.

11. A Comprehensive X-ray Characterization of the Pulsar Wind Nebula CTB 87: Evidence for Reverse-Shock Interaction from Its X-ray and Broadband Emissions[2610.28781]
Abstract

We present a comprehensive X-ray study of the pulsar wind nebula (PWN) CTB 87, utilizing new NuSTAR observations and archival Chandra and XMM-Newton data. Our joint analysis reveals that the PWN's X-ray spectrum exhibits significant curvature, well described by a broken or cutoff power law. The broken power-law fit yields photon indices $\Gamma_{1}=1.89\pm 0.05$ and $\Gamma_{2}=2.73 \pm 0.10$ below and above a break energy of $E_{\rm b}=5.12 \pm 0.26$ keV, respectively, while the cutoff power-law fit gives $\Gamma=1.23 \pm 0.10$ and $E_{\rm c} = 5.29 \pm 0.67$ keV. Spatially resolved spectroscopy confirms a previously observed radial softening of $\Gamma$, indicating significant synchrotron cooling as electrons move outward from the pulsar. We employed leptonic emission scenarios to model the broadband emission from CTB 87, focusing on the measured X-ray spectrum and the radial profiles. While an evolutionary one-zone model can explain the general features of the broadband spectral energy distribution (SED), its predictions do not fully reproduce the observed X-ray spectrum. Our multi-zone modeling demonstrates that the hard electron distribution required to explain the X-ray spectrum cannot account for the observed gamma-ray SED. This discrepancy suggests the presence of an additional electron population, possibly linked to the reverse shock interaction, and highlights CTB 87 as an evolved PWN whose broadband emission cannot be captured by these simple SED models. We discuss the potential origins for this second electron population and its implications for understanding the evolution of PWNe.

12. Probing the QPO and spectro-temporal evolution of MAXI J1750-327 with XPoSat during its first detected outburst : Constraints on black-hole mass and spin[2610.28817]
Abstract

We present a comprehensive timing and broadband spectral study of the newly discovered black hole low-mass X-ray binary candidate MAXI J1750-327 during its August 2026 outburst, using 11 continuous observations obtained with the XSPECT instrument onboard XPoSat. During epochs 1-2, we detect prominent type C QPO exhibiting a systematic upward frequency drift, accompanied by a prominent low-frequency noise component, with a clear transition from type-C to type-B, with subsequent disappearance of QPOs after epoch 4. Broadband spectral modeling reveals that this timing transition is directly mirrored by a fundamental restructuring of the accretion geometry. The inner disk temperature abruptly increases before settling into a steady range, accompanied by a steep reduction in disk normalization across the transition from epoch 3 to 4. This behavior signifies an active dynamical inward migration of the thin accretion disk toward the innermost stable circular orbit (ISCO). The Comptonized flux declines sharply after the QPO turns off, suggesting a transition from a hard to soft state, through intermediate states. We also observed that the spectro-temporal correlations provide compelling physical support for the Lense-Thirring (LT) precession framework to be the origin of the type-C QPOs. Using a Bayesian framework, we constrain the black hole mass to be $M_{\rm BH}=7.92^{+6.19}_{-3.72} M_{\odot}$ and the dimensionless spin parameter to be $a_*=0.163^{+0.116}_{-0.056}$ by jointly modeling the observed type-C QPO frequencies with the global LT precession model and marginalizing over the uncertainties in distance, inclination, and diskbb normalization.

13. Spherization of the velocity field of cosmological perturbations near density extrema[2610.28825]
Abstract

In this work, we consider a mechanism that tends to make the velocity field of linear cosmological perturbations near density extremum points (maxima or minima) spherically symmetric. It is shown that this mechanism begins to operate from the moment the Universe enters the stage of nonrelativistic matter domination ($z<z_{eq}\simeq 3600$) and rather quickly makes the velocity distribution near the density extremum practically isotropic. However, if the density profile of the initial perturbation is noticeably aspherical, its gravitational field subsequently destroys this isotropy.

14. White dwarf pollution in wide binaries I: the effects of stellar evolution[2610.28896]
Abstract

Context. Atmospheric pollution in white dwarfs is interpreted as evidence for the accretion of rocky material from remnant planetary systems. Wide binary companions can influence the delivery of planetary material onto white dwarfs, making it essential to understand the interplay between stellar evolution and binary perturbations. Aims. We investigate how uncertainties in stellar mass-loss histories affect the dynamical evolution of remnant planetary systems in wide binaries, and assess their relative importance compared with binary perturbations in driving the accretion of rocky material onto white dwarfs. Methods. We perform REBOUND N-body simulations incorporating time-dependent stellar parameters interpolated from MESA evolutionary tracks and tidal interactions, following the dynamical evolution up to the white dwarf stage. We explore two stellar-evolution models with distinct mass-loss histories for wide binary systems hosting a Jupiter-mass planet and a planetesimal disc, spanning a range of binary architectures. Results. The adopted stellar-evolution model has only a minor effect on the survival of the planet, mainly affecting the timing and efficiency of planetesimal delivery during the white dwarf stage. Instead, the architecture of the binary primarily controls the pollution outcome: wider binaries produce the highest rates of accretion onto the white dwarf, consistent with the observed prevalence of polluted white dwarfs in wide binaries. Moreover, planetesimal reservoirs remain long-lived, with survival times increasing with binary separation, enabling Gyr-long metal pollution. Conclusions. Our results suggest that the architecture of wide binaries, rather than uncertainties in stellar evolution, is the dominant factor controlling the dynamical delivery of rocky material onto white dwarfs.

15. X-ray Comptonization in an Accreting Kerr Black Hole System with a Tilted Disc and Thermal Corona: A General-Relativistic Monte Carlo Simulation. II. Polarization Signatures[2610.28938]
Abstract

Relativistic precession of the inner accretion flow is widely invoked to explain low-frequency QPOs, and the Relativistic Precession Model (RPM) predicts phase-dependent X-ray polarization. However, IXPE observations challenge simple precession and Comptonization models; for example: SwiftJ1727.8-1613 shows weak or undetected QPO-phase polarization modulation, while IGRJ17091-3624 shows a hard-state polarization degree (DOP) of  9%, versus  4% in other hard-state black hole binaries. In Paper I, we presented a general-relativistic Monte Carlo (GRMC) model of X-ray reprocessing in a misaligned, warped two-zone disc around a Kerr black hole with a hot, optically thin corona. We computed energy spectra, photon path-length and arrival-time distributions, and X-ray lags. Here, we extend the model to track photon polarization using Walker-Penrose parallel transport and polarized Compton and disc scattering. We calculate the energy-resolved degree and angle of polarization and their precession-phase modulation versus coronal optical depth, outer-disc albedo, photon-injection radius, and observer inclination. We find that gravitational light bending raises the mean DOP and enhances modulation, whereas coronal scattering and reflection from the misaligned outer disc lower the DOP and suppress modulation. The steady outer-disc polarization further dilutes the inner-flow signal. Their competition can produce weak, nearly unmodulated polarization or a high, strongly modulated signal. Strong scattering and reflection can explain SwiftJ1727.8-1613's weak modulation, while strong light bending and weak secondary scattering can explain IGRJ17091-3624's high DOP. Our results provide a framework for interpreting unusual X-ray polarization and testable predictions for constraining black hole parameters, coronal properties, and disc-warp geometry with IXPE, XPoSat, and eXTP.

16. Interpreting the $γ$-ray emission from Westerlund 1: hadronic or leptonic?[2610.28951]
Abstract

High-energy $\gamma$-ray emission has been detected from several star clusters, indicating that these sources are sites of cosmic-ray acceleration. However, it is still unclear whether the $\gamma$-ray emission is of hadronic or leptonic origin. We present a comprehensive study of the $\gamma$-ray emission from Westerlund 1, under the assumption that high-energy particles are accelerated at the cluster WTS. We investigate both the hadronic and leptonic scenarios by fitting simultaneously the spectrum and morphology of the $\gamma$-ray emission. We numerically solve the transport equations for non-thermal protons and electrons for a given $K_\rm{ep}$ and derive their energy and spatial distributions. $\gamma$ rays are produced either by hadronic pp interactions of protons with the target gas or by inverse Compton scattering of relativistic electrons (both primary and secondary) with the ambient photon fields. We also calculate the synchrotron emission from these electrons. The predicted SEDs are compared with the $\gamma$-ray measurements by Fermi-LAT and HESS and with the X-ray upper limit derived by eROSITA. For reasonable model parameters, we find that in the case of Bohm diffusion, both the leptonic and hadronic scenarios can account for the observed multi-wavelength spectrum and $\gamma$-ray morphology. However, in the case of Kraichnan diffusion, better motivated in star clusters, only the hadronic scenario can explain the observations, while the leptonic scenario fails to reproduce the highest-energy $\gamma$-ray data points. For the leptonic scenario, the magnetic field immediately downstream of the shock can be constrained to be $\lesssim$ a few $\mu$G thanks to the eROSITA upper limit. On the other hand, in order to explain the $\gamma$-ray observations with hadronic emission alone, the gas density inside the bubble should be $\gtrsim 1.2 \, \rm{cm}^{-3}$.

17. Direct Injection of Chemically-Enhanced Plasma into a Solar Flare Arcade[2610.28961]
Abstract

The magnetic reconnection event that drives "standard" solar flares ejects material from the reconnection site, loading mass directly onto the newly-reconnected flare arcade. To et al (2024) suggested that this model can explain observations of the distribution of elemental abundances measured in post-flare loops: the reconnection exhaust directly injects plasma with coronal abundances onto the arcade, flowing from the apex downwards. We test this idea directly by modeling the spatiotemporal variation of elements with low first ionization potential (FIP), and directly inject material near loop apices. We show that this direct injection is capable of producing enhancements similar in magnitude and spatial spread across the corona to those observed during the gradual phase of an X11.9 flare. We also show that direct chromospheric heating strongly limits the width of the low-FIP enhancement to a narrow region near the injection site. The enhancement of low-FIP elemental abundances naturally explains bright knots commonly observed near the top of flare arcades.

18. Rotation in the Lagoon Nebula with K2[2610.29006]
Abstract

A low-mass star's rotation rate drives its magnetic field, and in the case of young stars, also affects accretion and planetary formation. Observations of the rotation rates of young stars help constrain models of star and planet formation. We present rotation rates from K2 observations of stars in the  1-2 Myr Lagoon Nebula, one of the youngest clusters with space-based rotation rates available. From 414 light curves we obtain reliable rotation periods for 325 stars; 124 of them are disk-bearing and 201 are disk-free. This constitutes a significant increase in the collection of K2 rotation rates available for  1-5 Myr stars. The overall distribution is broadly consistent with expectations, in that the slowest rotators are mid-K and both lower and higher masses are on average faster rotating. There is weak evidence that disked stars rotate more slowly. There are a few complex periodic variables, but they appear to be surprisingly high mass compared to prior known examples of this kind of object.

19. $ν$Gundam: a unified framework for point-redshift and PDF-aware spatial correlation functions[2610.29116]
Abstract

Context. Photometric-redshift uncertainties redistribute galaxy pairs along the line of sight (LOS), diluting spatial clustering measurements. Propagating full redshift probability density functions (PDFs) through standard pair-count estimators remains computationally demanding. Aims. We present $\nu$Gundam, a unified framework to propagate radial PDFs at the pair-count level while retaining a common interface for exact, compressed, and stochastic projected-clustering calculations. Methods. The code implements four modes: exact empirical-PDF integration, Gaussian-mixture compression, Monte Carlo (MC) sampling, and a quantile-compression method. All modes accumulate pair counts on a resolved ($r_\mathrm{p},\pi$) grid, from which $\xi(r_\mathrm{p},\pi)$ and $w_\mathrm{p}(r_\mathrm{p};\Pi)$ are reconstructed. We validate these with DESI galaxies and CosmoDC2 mocks. Results. The PDF-aware modes produce nearly indistinguishable LOS redistribution kernels, while point-photo-$z$ estimates discard probabilistic pair information. Radial uncertainty mainly redistributes the clustering signal to larger $\pi$; the depth required to recover the truth projection scales with the pairwise radial dispersion. For practical settings, MC, quantile, and Gaussian-mixture compression reproduce the exact result with fractional RMS residuals below 5%. Applied to DESI Bright Galaxy Survey samples, MC sampling recovers spectroscopic clustering shapes and relative amplitudes for $r_\mathrm{p}\gtrsim0.8\,h^{-1}\mathrm{Mpc}$ using sample-dependent recovery depths of  400 and  $180\,h^{-1}\mathrm{Mpc}$ for red and blue galaxies. Conclusions. Projected clustering with redshift PDFs is a pair-redistribution problem. When propagated consistently, the signal is recovered accurately and efficiently. The integration depth is sample dependent and should be part of the measurement definition rather than a fixed choice.

20. Investigating a TeV-Halo Candidate around PSR J1928+1746 with HAWC[2610.29127]
Abstract

We present an updated High-Altitude Water Cherenkov (HAWC) analysis of the crowded Galactic-plane area near PSR J1928+1746, which was previously linked to the extended source 3HWC J1928+178. With 2904 days of HAWC data, improved event reconstruction, and a multicomponent likelihood model that includes six TeV source components and Galactic diffuse emission, we describe the region using six components and find a distinct, moderately extended source close to PSR J1928+1746. 22. Double-Shock Radiation in Supernova Wind Breakout and EP260321a[2610.29226]
Abstract

We study the supernova (SN) shock breakout (SBO) from an optically thick stellar wind. The SN ejecta-wind interaction forms a forward shock (FS) plus reverse shock (RS) structure. The RS's internal energy and shock velocity are both a fraction $\mu\lesssim1/6$ of the FS's. The lower RS velocity makes it easier to build up local thermal equilibrium (LTE) and generate blackbody (BB) emission. In the same time a $\mu^{-1}\gtrsim6$ times larger non-BB emission may be produced by the likely non-LTE FS. We apply the theory to analyze the recent detected type Ic-BL SN2026gzf associated X-ray flash EP260321a, and find it is naturally accounted for by a SN wind breakout: the $\sim0.13$ keV, thermal SBO emission can be produced by the RS during the interaction of a typical type Ic-BL SN ejecta ($E_k\sim10^{52}$erg and $M_e\sim1M_\odot$) and a stellar wind with a mass $M_w\sim10^{-4}M_\odot$ confined within a radius $r_w\sim10^{13}$cm; the X-ray rise time is mainly determined by the RS emission time, whereas the long duration and decrease time are due to the scatterings of SBO photons by a distant shell-like medium which has a large photosphere, $r_{ph}\sim10^{14}$ cm; the early, within 30 ks, optical emission can be naturally explained by the cooling emission of a shocked pure C/O envelope, since the observed SBO onset is delayed by $\gtrsim$ a few ks due to the large medium photosphere. BB+non-BB components from RS and FS, respectively, may be common in SN wind breakouts. SBO observations can be crucial in the study of progenitor stars by providing information for the ejecta from the SN explosion and the nearby wind from the pre-SN activities.

23. General Relativistic Simulations of the Nonlinear Evolution of r-modes in Rapidly Rotating Neutron Stars[2610.29259]
Abstract

The r-modes in rotating neutron stars are a promising source of continuous gravitational waves, and may grow or be excited to amplitudes $\alpha\lesssim1$. We perform general relativistic simulations of nonlinear $l=|m|=2$ r-modes in a rapidly rotating neutron star with spin frequency $f_{spin}\approx792$ Hz, relaxing the Cowling and small-$\alpha$ approximations. The star is idealised as an unmagnetised, one-component, perfect fluid with a polytropic equation of state. The r-mode frequency is measured to be $f_{r-mode}=1014$ Hz for initial amplitude $\alpha_0=0.2$. The dimensionless frequency $\kappa\approx0.7197$ agrees with an estimate from linear perturbation theory to within 1.7%. $f_{r-mode}$ increases quadratically with amplitude, and $\kappa$ decreases by 3.4% across simulations where $\alpha_0$ is varied through the range $0.2\leq\alpha_0\leq1.2$. The gravitational wave strain calculated from the Newman-Penrose scalar implies $h_{r-mode}\approx9.7\times10^{-22}$ at $10$ kpc for $\alpha_0=0.2$, which agrees with Einstein's quadrupole formula to within 35%. We also report on nonlinear phenomena observed in our simulations, e.g. differential rotation, amplitude decay, and signatures of nonlinear mode coupling in the spacetime oscillations.

24. Enhanced Mass Transfer from Extended Shock-Supported Stellar Chromospheres[2610.29323]
Abstract

Mass transfer (MT) in binary systems is a crucial and complex process. Even before the donor's photosphere fills its Roche lobe, matter from its outer atmosphere can reach the inner Lagrange point, thus initiating interaction in the so-called optically thin phase. 80. Seesaw Cosmology[2607.27325]
Abstract

We study perturbative reheating in which the inflaton transfers its energy to the Standard Model through right-handed neutrinos (RHNs) responsible for light-neutrino masses via the type-I seesaw mechanism. We refer to the resulting nonstandard thermal history as $seesaw$ $cosmology$. When produced relativistically and sufficiently long lived, the RHNs generate a characteristic sequence of inflaton, relativistic-RHN, nonrelativistic-RHN, and Standard Model radiation domination. We solve the Boltzmann system while retaining the production-time dependence of the nonthermal RHN distribution and its relativistic-to-nonrelativistic transition. The Standard Model temperature rapidly approaches a plateau during inflaton domination and subsequently scales as $a^{-1/4}$ and $a^{-3/8}$ during relativistic- and nonrelativistic-RHN domination, respectively. We investigate the implications of seesaw cosmology for dark-matter production. Direct production through inflaton decays can be enhanced relative to conventional reheating by a factor of order $m_\phi/(2m_N)$, while ultraviolet freeze-in exhibits the critical temperature powers $p = 12$ and $20$, leading to potentially large contributions before the final radiation-dominated era. Seesaw cosmology therefore connects neutrino-mass generation, the pre-BBN thermal history and phenomena such as dark-matter production and baryogenesis.

81. Families of relative periodic orbits in the planar three-body problem via consecutive alignments[2609.01585]
Abstract

Relative periodic orbits (RPOs) are solutions of the three-body problem that are periodic in a uniformly rotating reference frame and, in general, quasi-periodic in inertial coordinates. We present a numerical procedure for computing and continuing one-parameter families of RPOs of the planar Newtonian three-body problem. The method exploits consecutive syzygies, understood here as configurations in which the three bodies are aligned and their velocities satisfy the corresponding symmetry conditions. Matching the positions and momenta at two consecutive alignments reduces the computation of RPOs to a low-dimensional nonlinear problem. Its solutions are then numerically continued, and linear stability is determined from the nontrivial eigenvalues of the rotated monodromy matrix after removing the neutral directions associated with conserved quantities and continuous symmetries. The procedure is applied to several mass distributions and initial configurations, producing families of Poincaré, Hill, and binary-type solutions. These families exhibit transitions from nearly circular to highly eccentric motion, changes of stability near resonances and turning points, and absolute periodic solutions when the rotation angle is a rational multiple of 2{\pi}. In the Hill families, the continuation connects satellite configurations with circumstellar motion as the smallest body loses its gravitational binding to the intermediate body. Circumbinary and circumstellar configurations are also obtained in the binary regime. The results illustrate the dynamical diversity of RPOs and provide coherent three-body motions that can be used as prescribed trajectories in restricted four-body models.
