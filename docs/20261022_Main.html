1. On the Nature of the BeWD System Nova FQ Circinus[2610.26957]
Abstract

The classical nova FQ Cir (Schaefer et al. 2026) has recently been shown to have a Be star donor, and has been identified as a High Mass Cataclysmic Variable (HMCV), with an orbital period of $\sim 2.04d$ (twice the 1.02d periodogram peak). Using the extensive OGLE optical archive, together with existing observations from ASAS-SN we instead find significant modulation between $\sim 50-60$ days and propose that the orbital period is likely closer to $\sim 62d$. We interpret the low amplitude 1.02 d modulation as a non-radial pulsation of the Be star. We also reassess the HMCV classification based on properties of known Be star-white dwarf systems.

2. Euclid Bypass end-to-end simulations for spectroscopic galaxy clustering performance assessment[2610.26990]
Abstract

We present a simulation framework for Euclid galaxy clustering measurements that reproduces the essential behaviour of the spectroscopic reduction chain, bypassing the computation required by full pixel-level simulations. The framework comprises two complementary tools, FastSpec, which simulates 1-dimensional slitless spectra with a simplified instrument and noise model, and Pypelid, which computes detection probabilities on input catalogues, accounting for the survey dithering sequence and spatially varying noise. We develop calibrated prescriptions for the survey selection function, redshift errors, and redshift interloper populations. Applied to the Euclid Wide Survey, the baseline selection yields a redshift purity of 74% and a sample completeness of 45%, which improves with the use of Euclid photometric colours. The redshift error distribution is found to have a Gaussian core of width $\sigma_z = 9.4 \times 10^{-4}$, with no significant trend over $0.9 < z < 1.8$. Emission lines [OIII]5008 and [SIII]9530 misidentified as H$\alpha$ contribute a few to ten per cent depending on redshift, while catastrophic redshift measurement failures dominate at low signal-to-noise ratio. Simulating the Data Release 1 footprint, we find that complex survey selection effects can be corrected with a forward-modelled random catalogue, recovering the monopole and quadrupole of the galaxy 2-point correlation function. The simplified noise model does not include residual contamination from overlapping spectra, or other artefacts seen in early Euclid data; this will be revisited with DR1 data. The framework provides an efficient route for characterising the Euclid spectroscopic selection function at the precision required for galaxy clustering science, and is the basis for systematic error assessments in Euclid galaxy clustering analyses.

3. Gamma-ray Bursts as Cosmological Probes Calibrated with Strongly Lensed Quasars and Neural Networks[2610.27009]
Abstract

Gamma-ray bursts (GRBs) extend distance measurements to redshifts beyond those routinely probed by Type Ia supernovae, but their use in cosmology is limited by calibration circularity and possible redshift-dependent effects. We investigate strongly lensed quasars as external calibrators of the Amati and Combo GRB relations. We reconstruct the luminosity-distance relation using an artificial neural network trained on TDCOSMO strong-lensing distances, with the absolute scale inherited from the lensing-only calibration. To avoid extrapolation, only GRBs within the lens-supported redshift interval are used for calibration. We then infer cosmological parameters through a hierarchical Bayesian analysis of flat $\Lambda$CDM, with $w_0w_a$CDM examined as a consistency test. For flat $\Lambda$CDM, Amati yields $H_0=78.26^{+17.87}_{-16.30}\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}$ and $\Omega_m=0.670^{+0.223}_{-0.276}$, while Combo yields $H_0=78.71^{+13.83}_{-11.29}\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}$ and $\Omega_m=0.635^{+0.235}_{-0.242}$. Amati is relatively insensitive to the phenomenological redshift term, whereas Combo shows a stronger dependence. Injection–recovery tests indicate that an unmodelled positive Combo trend can bias $H_0$ and $\Omega_m$ upward. Strong-lens calibration therefore offers a route to extending the GRB Hubble diagram, but the inferred $H_0$ is not an independent GRB measurement: it retains the TDCOSMO distance scale. The precision is limited by GRB scatter, redshift-dependent systematics, and lens-modelling uncertainties.

4. Planets surviving common envelope evolution should be rare, massive, and orbit very close to their host star[2610.27055]
Abstract

The discovery of exoplanets around white dwarfs uniquely informs our understanding of planetary survival through stellar evolution. Common Envelope (CE) evolution is one of the proposed mechanisms that could shape the orbital architecture of these planets around stellar remnants. In this work, we predict the occurrence rate and orbital separation/planet mass distributions for planets that could form around white dwarfs through CE evolution. We simulate populations of giant-planet systems, sampling initial conditions from observed mass and semi-major axis distributions for main sequence stars. We evolve the host star from main sequence to white dwarf to assess which planetary configurations could survive, and we predict the resulting post-CE planet population. Our results suggest that such planets should be rare, massive, and have very close orbital separations. We find that most of the surviving planets have masses of $>10\,M_{\rm{Jup}}$ and orbital separations $<1.4$ times the tidal disruption radius, $a_{disrupt}$ ($\lesssim0.006$ AU, also called the Roche limit), which is in contrast to high eccentricity migration, which produces planets with orbital separations $>2a_{disrupt}$. The properties of CE survivors from our simulations contrast with the known closely-orbiting planet WD 1856+534 b, suggesting a different mechanism may be responsible for its formation.

5. Small Collisions, Big Impact: The Sensitivity of Atmospheric Temperatures to Collision-Induced Absorption[2610.27067]
Abstract

Collision-induced absorption (CIA) data are relevant to determining atmospheric temperatures and, in turn, the habitability of a diverse set of planetary atmospheres. However, these data remain sparse across broad wavelength and temperature ranges. Nevertheless, multiple, sometimes disagreeing, sources of experimental and theoretical data can be available for the same CIA pair. In this paper, we assess the impact of these varying sources on atmospheric temperatures and the limitations that the available data coverage can introduce. In order to inform future measurements and calculations, we test additional cases with extrapolated wavelength and temperature ranges. We first introduce a measure of the importance of CIA opacities depending on pressure and temperature by calculating the Rosseland mean with and without each CIA pair's contribution. Secondly, we employ each CIA source in a radiative transfer model of idealised atmospheres which maximise the effect of the CIA pair of interest. Our findings highlight the uncertainties related to the current data. Temperature differences between CIA data sources range from $\sim$10 K when sources overlap in wavelength and temperature coverage to >100 K when spectral gaps or temperature limits differ. Spectral windows or narrow temperature coverage can also hinder the model's convergence of the temperature profile. This work aims to provide guidance on the selection of CIA opacity datasets for atmospheric modelling and to identify the wavelength and temperature regimes in which further laboratory measurements and theoretical calculations are most urgently needed. We publish all our findings in detail in the form of an accessible website.

7. Beyond static torques: Self-consistent migration and dynamical trapping of low-mass planets in dusty disks[2610.27071]
Abstract

The rapid inward migration of low-mass planets remains a primary theoretical challenge in planet formation. While recent studies show that the solid component of protoplanetary disks can exert strong positive torques to counteract the negative gas torques, traditional static fixed-orbit models fail to capture the fully coupled dynamics of a migrating planet. In this work, we present 2D high-resolution, two-fluid hydrodynamical simulations using a comoving reference frame to track the self-consistent orbital evolution of embedded planets across a comprehensive six-dimensional parameter space. By allowing the planet to dynamically respond to disk-exerted torques, we identify a wide diversity of migration regimes, involving inward/outward migration and oscillatory radial confinement. We also find that massive cores can overcome dust-induced barriers, experiencing dust-mediated runaway outward or inward migration. Conversely, in dust-rich environments continuous gas-dust momentum exchange combined with planet torques dynamically regenerates co-orbital dust asymmetries, creating robust dynamical attractors where super-Earths undergo stable, oscillatory confinement, effectively halting inward drift without requiring pre-existing pressure bumps or disk substructures. This self-induced trapping mechanism could be relevant in the outer disk, beyond the water-ice line in environments with a enhanced local dust-to-gas ratio, where icy pebbles are moderately coupled to the gas. In low-metallicity or highly diffusive disks, planets generally adopt monotonic inward decay, apart from cases with outward migration. Ultimately, the diversity of these self-consistent trajectories demonstrates that static torque approximations are insufficient. Evaluating fully coupled kinematic feedback is essential for accurately predicting planetary survival and the final architecture of planetary systems.

8. DDELenS: Tomographic Weak Lensing Simulations with Dynamical Dark Energy[2610.27088]
Abstract

We present a suite of 128 cosmological simulations in $w_0w_a$CDM cosmologies with massive neutrinos, designed to enable simulation-based inference of dynamical dark energy parameters from weak gravitational lensing and other cosmological observables. Each simulation follows $1024^3$ dark matter particles in a $512\,h^{-1}$Mpc periodic box, with cosmological parameters sampled using a Sobol sequence over the ranges $\Omega_m \in [0.28, 0.35]$, $\sigma_8 \in [0.74, 0.90]$, $w_0 \in [-1.0, -0.5]$, and $w_a \in [-1.5, 0.0]$, motivated by recent results from the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument survey. The nonlinear evolution is computed with $\texttt{PKDGRAV3}$ using the N-body gauge to consistently incorporate relativistic corrections from massive neutrinos, photons, and dark energy. For each cosmology, we generate 10,000 ray-traced convergence maps at six source redshifts $z_s \in \{0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0, 2.5, 1100\}$ using $\texttt{LensTools}$, and additionally provide halo catalogues and projected halo overdensity fields for galaxy–lensing cross-correlation analyses. We generate 100,000 realizations at the fiducial cosmology for the construction of the covariance matrix. This simulation suite provides a foundation for field-level inference and non-Gaussian statistics with next-generation weak lensing surveys such as the Rubin Observatory LSST, Euclid, and the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope, as well as CMB lensing experiments including the Simons Observatory.

9. Slowly Rotating Fully Convective Stars at $\lesssim$ 1 Gyr: Evidence from the Hyades and Praesepe[2610.27112]
Abstract

We report the discovery of 10 stars in the Praesepe and Hyades clusters with unusually slow rotation that are likely not field contamination. Even though our search was general, all slow rotators are in the fully convective domain. We find that the fraction of slow rotators in the clusters is more than $5\sigma$ above the distribution expected from field contamination. They provide a potential link between the period distribution of the young open clusters and the field. These stars could be a distinct birth population, or they could be an indication that the onset of strong magnetic breaking is earlier than previously realized in this domain. Photometric variability and X-ray luminosity suggest the later but more data is needed to confirm. Searches for similar populations in younger clusters will help determine when slow rotators first emerge and constrain the timescale and physical mechanisms governing angular-momentum loss in fully convective stars. The discovery of these stars also highlights the importance of long-baseline photometric observations for detecting the long rotation periods of low-mass stars.

10. Ammonia-Hydrogen Phase Equilibria in Planetary Envelopes from First-Principles-Based Machine-Learning Molecular Dynamics[2610.27136]
Abstract

Ammonia and hydrogen are major constituents of planets within and beyond the solar system, yet how they interact in planetary envelopes is poorly known. We perform large-scale two-phase coexistence simulations of the ammonia-hydrogen system from 500 to 4000 K and 0.1 to 100 GPa, driven by machine-learning interatomic potentials trained on density functional theory calculations. From these we map the compositions of coexisting hydrogen-rich and ammonia-rich phases, and the critical curve that separates conditions of complete miscibility from those of phase separation. The critical temperature increases with pressure, from 500 K at 0.1 GPa to 2000 K near 30 GPa, and we find no phase separation at 3000 and 4000 K up to 100 GPa. The slope of the critical curve decreases substantially between 0.3 and 30 GPa, and the curve nearly flattens above about 30 GPa and 2000 K, where molecules begin to give way to atomic species. The coexistence curves are asymmetric with an ammonia-rich critical composition. For the expected envelope compositions, the phase boundary lies close to, and intersects, published thermal profiles of Uranus and Neptune, implying ammonia may be raining out in these planets today. In contrast, in warm sub-Neptunes such as TOI-270 d, ammonia and hydrogen are likely miscible in all proportions. Together with previous studies, our results suggest that silicates, water and ammonia separate from hydrogen in sequence as planets cool, with implications for their compositional, thermal and structural evolution.

11. Numerical shock broadening and supernova neutrino conversion: implications for DUNE[2610.27158]
Abstract

Hydrodynamical simulations spread the supernova shock front over a few grid zones, a width set by the numerical scheme rather than by the physics of the shock. Where the front crosses the H resonance, that width can make the computed MSW evolution artificially adiabatic. We quantify the effect on six three-dimensional blue-supergiant explosion models from the Garching archive by propagating individual rays twice: through the stored profile, and with the front replaced by a narrow reconstruction, 2.2 km wide, that approximates a sudden crossing while keeping the densities on either side. The stored fronts are 1.6 to 1.7 grid zones wide. Across twelve configurations, each chosen inside the energy band in which that model's front meets the resonance, the reconstruction raises the ray-averaged crossing probability $P_H$ by 0.44 to 0.76 in absolute terms, median 0.56; narrowing the reconstruction thirty-fold changes each difference by at most 0.055. Along all 12878 rays of one configuration, the sudden-limit formula evaluated on each ray's own densities agrees closely with the propagated reconstructed result. The change is largest when the density jump spans the resonance, over a band of energies that can be estimated from the presupernova density once the shock radius and the compression are known, with tails outside it. In a separate calculation with a parametrised shock and a published emission model, time-dependent conversion changes the $\nu_e$ charged-current count in a 40 kt argon detector by -4.4%, with a sign set by the flavour composition of the source. Folding the reconstructed three-dimensional fronts with a consistent emission model remains to be done.

12. The Imprint of Core-collapse Asymmetries on Supernova Shock Breakout[2610.27183]
Abstract

Shock breakout is the first electromagnetic signal of a supernova, generating a highly luminous and fast-timescale burst of thermal radiation. Motivated by recent theoretical progress in producing successful three-dimensional supernova explosions of massive stars, we undertake a detailed numerical study on the impact that the core-collapse asymmetries might have on the breakout signal. We perform the first fully 3D multi-group radiation hydrodynamics simulations of shock breakout for the case of a red supergiant explosion. We find that large-scale asymmetries formed during the core-collapse process can extend the breakout flash duration to days, with the timescale set by the supernova shock fully wrapping around the progenitor star. Using detailed 3D radiative transfer post-processing, we generate synthetic multi-wavelength light curves and spectra to more closely connect to observations. The light curves show a significant viewing-angle dependence of the overall shape and peak brightness of shock breakout. We find that core-collapse asymmetries have the largest impact on the breakout light curve when observed in shorter UV wavelengths. On the other hand, at optical wavelengths, we find a diminished impact of the asymmetric breakout that more closely resembles a 1D spherical breakout. Our results show how multi-dimensional effects originating from the core-collapse mechanism can play a major role in setting the shock breakout properties, and are particularly relevant to upcoming UV space missions such as ULTRASAT and UVEX.

13. Evolution of Massive Main-Sequence Stars in Binary Population Synthesis. II. Consequences for Black Hole High-Mass X-ray Binaries and Binary Black Holes[2610.27186]
Abstract

Interactions and mass loss during the main-sequence (MS) phase of massive binary stars can significantly alter their subsequent evolution and the properties of the black holes (BHs) they ultimately produce. We use the rapid binary population synthesis code \textsc{COMPAS}, incorporating a new treatment of MS evolution that self-consistently tracks the convective core under arbitrary mass-loss or mass-gain histories. We explore the properties of black hole high-mass X-ray binaries (BH-HMXBs) and binary black holes (BBHs) formed under this new framework. We find that a more detailed treatment of MS evolution is necessary to reproduce the high BH masses observed in BH-HMXBs, which are otherwise absent under prescriptions that do not take the internal structure of MS stars into account. Matching the observed rates and orbital periods favors case A mass transfer that is more non-conservative than commonly assumed. We further find that mass transfer of helium-enriched material during case A mass transfer is common in massive binaries, with potential consequences for the structure of the accreting star. For the BBH population, the new MS framework alters the chirp-mass and mass-ratio distributions of detectable mergers, and case A mass transfer can make a substantial contribution to the BBHs formed through the stable mass transfer channel. These systems show a strong preference for near-equal mass ratios, broadly consistent with recent constraints from gravitational-wave observations.

14. Are Dynamical Dark-Energy Cosmologies Old Enough?[2610.27209]
Abstract

Recent analysis combining baryon acoustic oscillation measurements with cosmic microwave background and Type Ia supernova data show an apparent preference for a time-dependent dark-energy equation of state within $w_0w_a$ parameterizations. Here we test the robustness of this result against an independent cosmological consistency requirement, namely that the expansion history must provide sufficient time to accommodate the oldest observed stellar populations. Motivated by recent stellar and globular-cluster age determinations, we impose progressively stronger lower bounds, $t_0 \geq 13.6$, $13.75$, $13.8$, and $13.85$ Gyr, on the expansion histories allowed by DESI DR2+CMB+Pantheon+ data. We find a systematic displacement of the posterior in the $(w_0,w_a)$ plane toward the $\Lambda$CDM prediction, $(w_0,w_a)=(-1,0)$, as the minimum allowed age is increased. For $t_0 \geq 13.85$ Gyr, the Mahalanobis separation from $\Lambda$CDM decreases from $2.56\sigma$ to $1.72\sigma$, bringing the $\Lambda$CDM prediction inside the 95\% credible region. Our results show that the current evidence for dynamical dark energy is sensitive to independent cosmic-age information and that stellar chronometry provides a direct robustness test of the DESI-favored expansion histories. Requiring sufficiently old cosmologies can appreciably reduce the statistical preference for departures from the standard $\Lambda$CDM model.

15. Large-scale Vortices in the Solar Photosphere: Statistical Properties and the Reliability of Local Correlation Tracking[2610.27222]
Abstract

Vortices are observed throughout the solar atmosphere, spanning a wide range of spatial scales. Although small-scale vortices have been studied extensively, mesoscale vortices remain less explored, and it is still unclear whether they can be reliably recovered from observations. Mesogranular-scale motions are recovered from the R2D2 simulations by applying Gaussian spatial smoothing to the horizontal velocity field with full width at half maximum (FWHM) values of 2.5-4.0 Mm. To evaluate the accuracy of local correlation tracking (LCT) and its suitability for identifying mesogranular vortices, we apply LCT to synthetic intensity maps at $\tau = 1$ using the same spatial windows and temporal averaging windows of 10-60 minutes. Vortices are identified in both the reference and LCT-derived velocity fields using the $\Gamma$ method. For all tested parameter combinations, LCT underestimates the number of mesogranular-scale vortices. The best overall recovery is obtained for relatively small spatial smoothing scales (FWHM $\leq 3.5$ Mm) and short averaging windows ($\leq 20$ minutes), which provide the most reliable vorticity and lifetime statistics while maintaining strong agreement in vortex radius and area distributions. Our analysis shows that only a modest fraction of LCT-detected vortices correspond to vortices identified in the reference velocity field (3.54%-17.88%), depending on FWHM and temporal averaging. This indicates that vortex-driven dynamics inferred from LCT-detected vortices should be interpreted with caution. Our results suggest that applying the $\Gamma$ method to LCT-derived velocity fields can recover useful statistical properties of mesoscale vortical structures, especially their temporal trends and characteristic sizes.

16. The Future of Gamma-Ray Bursts as Probes of the Epoch of Reionization in the Era of JWST and the Extremely Large Telescope[2610.27282]
Abstract

The Epoch of Reionization (EoR) is a key era of cosmological history that remains poorly understood. An examination of the Ly-alpha damping wing of high-redshift GRBs can provide direct measurements of the fraction of neutral hydrogen (i.e., neutral fraction) of the IGM in the vicinity of the GRB host galaxy, in addition to the neutral column in the host along the sight-line to the GRB. The latter quantity can help infer the fraction of ionizing photons escaping the galaxy (i.e. escape fraction); the average escape fraction being a key input to EoR models. New and upcoming observatories such as the JWST and the Extremely Large Telescope (ELT) can obtain more robust estimates of the hydrogen column density and neutral fraction around high-redshift GRBs. We simulate spectra for the JWST NIRSpec and ELT HARMONI spectrographs using different GRB magnitudes and exposure times to determine their capabilities for measuring column densities and neutral fractions with the Ly-alpha damping wings of high-redshift GRBs. We find that both spectrographs will be highly effective for obtaining column density estimates. Despite the presence of telluric lines, we find that ELT HARMONI will be a powerful instrument for retrieving accurate neutral fraction estimates, but JWST NIRSpec will be vital for obtaining neutral fraction measurements for bright GRBs that fall in redshift ranges where ELT is less effective. Additionally, shorter JWST Target of Opportunity (ToO) follow-up times would greatly increase the accuracy of neutral fraction estimates obtained with NIRSpec spectra.

17. The presence and order of QCD phase transitions from gravitational-wave observations of neutron star mergers[2610.27287]
Abstract

Gravitational-wave observations of neutron star mergers allows us to probe the dense portion of the nuclear equation of state. We develop a formalism for testing the existence and order of phase transitions in the cores of neutron stars using only gravitational-wave observations of their inspiral. We test our formalism on data from the first binary neutron star mergers GW170817 and GW190425, and use synthetic injections assuming the next and uber-next upgrades of the LIGO observatories, A+ and A#, as well as with Cosmic Explorer. 18. Source velocities of fast radio bursts constrained by their dispersion measure variations[2610.27311]
Abstract

Growing evidence suggests that many fast radio bursts (FRBs) originate from extragalactic magnetars. Like all neutron stars, magnetars may be kicked at birth to large velocities, the values of which directly inform the physics governing stellar explosions and compact object formation. A subset of repeating FRBs show significant variability in their dispersion measures (i.e., electron column densities) over time, often exhibiting apparently secular trends. Here we show that all repeating FRBs with significant dispersion measure variations can be generically explained by motion of the source. We use existing measurements to constrain the minimum space velocities of the FRB sources, which are found to be $\gtrsim 20-2800\,{\rm km\,s}^{-1}$. We simultaneously constrain the plasma properties of the circumsource media, which are comparable to those of compact HII regions. For sources with compact radio nebulae, we directly infer the maximum source ages, and find them to be consistent with the ages of known Galactic magnetars. Our findings suggest that significant DM variability is a hallmark of young sources born with natal kicks through prompt formation channels. Applying our methods to a larger sample with continued monitoring of repeating FRB dispersion measures will reveal key physics of compact object formation.

SN 2023iop shows that the most luminous 87A-like events occupy an underexplored region of massive-star evolution.

21. Exomoonlight Sonata: On the Fate of Exomoons Around Migrating White Dwarf Planets[2610.27484]
Abstract

The survival of exomoons around close-in white dwarf planets offers a potential constraint on their migration histories and an alternative pathway to post-main-sequence habitability. We investigate whether moons can survive the tidal circularization of planets via high-eccentricity migration, and consider a hierarchical three-body system (white dwarf-planet-moon) after an initial eccentricity excitation of the planetary orbit. Using an analytic equilibrium tidal model, we compare the evolution of the planetary and satellite orbits and examine the conditions required for an evection resonance. We show that the moons of pseudosynchronously rotating planets always migrate inwards since the planetary corotation radius always exceeds the Hill radius, independent of mass and orbital eccentricities. Abstract

23. Probing Nearby Electron Sources and Local Diffusion with the TeV All-Electron Spectrum[2610.27635]

The transport law extends to the Kerr-Newman-Vaidya family, and the regime where the dynamics matter is shown to lie far outside astrophysical mass-loss rates.
