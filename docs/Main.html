1. Fast X-ray Transients as Premature-Dissipated Gamma-Ray Bursts with Magnetic-Dominated Jets[2611.23380]
Abstract

Fast X-ray transients (FXTs) are extragalactic X-ray flashes with little or no accompanying $\gamma$-ray emission. We propose that FXTs and classical gamma-ray bursts (GRBs) share a common origin in Poynting-flux-dominated jets powered by the Blandford–Znajek mechanism. In this picture, FXTs arise when the kink instability triggers premature dissipation at small radii ($\sim 10^{11}$ cm), yielding low Lorentz factors ($\Gamma \lesssim 50$) and high compactness ($\ell' \gg 1$) that thermalize the emission into the soft X-ray band. When the kink criterion is not met, the jet accelerates to ultra-relativistic velocities ($\Gamma > 100$) and dissipates at larger radii ($\sim 10^{15}$ cm) as a standard GRB. This scenario can naturally explain the observed thermal spectra, low $E_{\rm peak}$, supernova associations, jet-break absence, and day-scale afterglow delays across five well-studied FXTs. EP240801a is explained as a boundary event, where premature dissipation does not exhaust the full magnetic energy; the residual energy is further dissipated at a typical GRB radius, producing the observed $\gamma$-ray prompt emission with an $\sim 80$ s time lag. A key observational prediction is that FXTs may exhibit $e^+ e^-$ annihilation line features sweeping from a few MeV to hundreds of keV within $\sim 0.01$ s.

2. Mining the time axis with TRON. IV. MeerKAT detects a radio burst from a nearby M dwarf[2611.23447]
Abstract

We report the detection of a minute-timescale, highly circularly polarised ($\sim100\%$) radio burst from an active M dwarf (spectral type M1Ve), PM J14111$-$6155, located 19.6 pc from the Sun. Identified in the 1.28 GHz SMGPS using 8-second integrations from the TRON-pipeline, the burst peaked at 7.7 mJy. Visibility-based dynamic spectra reveal a drifting, band-limited emission feature ($\sim$300 MHz FWHM) with a maximum cut-off near 1.65 GHz, associated with electron cyclotron maser instability (ECMI), implying the existence of large-scale magnetic topologies ($B_* \approx 0.59$ kG). A non-detection ($3\sigma$ upper limit) four hours later points to intermittent or rotationally modulated beamed emission. An analysis of both TESS and ground-based survey data failed to recover an unambiguous rotational period for the optical counterpart. Follow-up SALT spectroscopy confirms its active chromospheric state, exhibiting prominent emission in the Ca {\sc ii} infrared triplet, Balmer lines, and persistent double-peaked H$\alpha$ emission ($44.6\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$ peak separation) attributable to optically thick self-absorption. This discovery demonstrates that the coordinated use of time-series optical photometry, high-resolution spectroscopy, and high-cadence spectropolarimetric radio imaging enables direct probing of coherent radio emission from the magnetically active atmospheres of nearby M dwarfs.

3. Probing horizonless compact objects through observable signatures and images of accretion disk[2611.23452]
Abstract

The observed images of M87$^*$ and Sgr A$^*$ by Event Horizon Telescope motivate us to study the spacetime geometry and accretion flow dynamics around compact objects beyond the Kerr/Schwarzschild scenario. In this work, we investigate the properties of a steady, inviscid, axisymmetric, advective accretion flow around the Zipoy-Voorhees ($\gamma$-metric) spacetime by solving the relativistic hydrodynamic equations. The $\gamma$-metric is a static, axisymmetric generalization of the Schwarzschild geometry and is characterized by a mass ($M_\bullet$) and a deformation parameter ($\gamma$). We examine how the deformation parameter $\gamma$ influences the effective potential, the location and nature of the critical points, and the resulting accretion flow topologies. Then, using relativistic thermal bremsstrahlung emissivity, we compute the spectral energy distribution and bolometric luminosity of the flow. We find that $\gamma$ affects the luminosity in opposite ways for the flow having outer critical point (O-type) and that having inner critical point (I-type). Finally, combining the flow properties with general relativistic ray tracing, we construct synthetic images of the accretion disk for different values of $\gamma$ and observer inclination angles. The resulting observed flux distribution independently confirms the trends obtained from the luminosity and spectral analyses. Although the shadow of the $\gamma$-metric has been shown to be consistent with the observed shadow radii of M87$^*$ and Sgr A$^*$, its accretion flow topology, the resulting radiative properties and image differ from the Schwarzschild case, thereby giving a complementary way to probe the deviations from the Schwarzschild paradigm.

4. Maximising the Open Magnetic Flux of a Star[2611.23455]
Abstract

We consider how to distribute the magnetic flux on a star's surface in order to maximise the proportion of open magnetic flux predicted by the potential field source surface model. The proportion of open flux is shown to be maximised by concentrating all of the positive and negative flux at antipodal points. For a typical source surface height, this enhances the open flux by 35% compared to a dipolar distribution. Even if the flux is spread over finite polar caps – as on the Sun around Solar Minimum – the open flux is still substantially enhanced compared to the dipole. Nevertheless, we show that the dipole approximation over-estimates the open flux for this kind of flux distribution.

5. An inspiral of a star inside a common envelope with its giant companion[2611.23470]
Abstract

The formation of compact stellar binaries requires rapid orbital tightening of their progenitor binary stars. This is theorized to happen via Common Envelope Evolution (CEE), when the stars orbit in a loosely bound, shared convective envelope, triggering an inspiral due to frictional drag. However, this inspiral in such a system has never been observed directly. Here, we present observations of AT 2025abao, a transient eruption in the Andromeda Galaxy, that originates from the CEE of a  7M$_{\odot}$ asymptotic giant branch (AGB) star with its companion. Extensive pre- and post-eruption spectroscopy is best explained as a  0.7M$_{\odot}$ companion inspiraling within the convective AGB envelope that powers a seven year period of gradual brightening - the first direct observations of frictional drag energy dissipation induced by CEE. The subsequent merger of the companion with the primary core within a few (<10) orbits powers the ejection of the outermost  10% of the stellar envelope at speeds  10x the primary escape speed, and empirically constrains the fraction of dissipated orbital energy available to unbind the convective common envelope, $0.01\lesssim \alpha \lesssim 0.4$. This end-to-end characterization using multi-survey observations demonstrates a path towards mapping the landscape of CEE outcomes with upcoming surveys.

6. Fuzzy Dark Matter as a diffractive barrier: Wave-optics Signatures of Ultralight Fields on Lensed Gravitational Waves[2611.23580]
Abstract

Gravitational waves (GWs) propagate through the universe from sources at high redshift, experiencing the gravitational fields of the intervening matter distribution. Halos composed of ultralight dark matter (ULDM) contain strong density fluctuations, reflecting the interference pattern in the field distribution of the ultralight bosons. In the wave-optics regime, ULDM fluctuations act as a diffractive grating, imprinting frequency-dependent modulations of GWs, in addition to magnification and other gravitational lensing effects. Since lensing depends on the projected surface density, we derive its approximate probability distribution and show how it departs from a Gaussian distribution depending on the halo profile and coherence length. We then quantify how the GW diffraction signatures produced by the granules depend on macroimage magnification and parity. The ULDM interference network imprints stochastic diffraction fluctuations at the $O(1)\%$ level on GWs moderately lensed by the host when the Fresnel scale is comparable to the granule coherence length $r_F(f)\sim\ell_c$. These fluctuations can be enhanced to $\gtrsim10\%$ when the host macroimage forms near the critical curve or for Type II macroimages. For massive black hole binaries observed by LISA, the diffraction signature can be detectable around the ULDM particle mass $m_\phi\sim10^{-21}$ eV, providing either a smoking-gun signature of ULDM or new constraints on $m_\phi$.

7. Strongly Suppressed Cosmic-Ray Diffusion in a Wind-Blown Cavity Environment of SNR G21.8-3.0[2611.23744]
Abstract

We report the discovery of a GeV $\gamma$-ray source spatially offset from SNR G21.8-3.0, while no significant high-energy emission is detected from the remnant itself. Using 16 yr of \emph{Fermi}-LAT observations together with $^{12}$CO ($J=1-0$) data, we show that the GeV emission is spatially associated with a localized molecular cloud located just outside a gas cavity whose boundary closely follows the SNR shell. This configuration supports a scenario in which cosmic rays (CRs) escaping from the remnant propagate through a progenitor-carved low-density cavity and illuminate nearby dense gas. Modeling of the cloud emission requires a diffusion coefficient suppressed by a factor of several tens relative to the canonical Galactic value, indicating strongly inhibited CR transport around the remnant. Rather than serving as a simple low-density escape channel, the wind-blown cavity appears to regulate CR propagation through local magnetic structure and enhanced turbulence. This system therefore provides evidence that circumstellar structures can control the escape and early transport of CRs around supernova remnants.

8. The Population of Radio Hypernebulae from Mass-Transferring Massive Binaries[2611.23783]
Abstract

Massive binary evolution is punctuated by brief phases of highly super-Eddington mass transfer (MT) onto neutron stars and black holes, presaging luminous transients such as supernovae, common envelope events, and gravitational wave sources. These hyper-accreting phases power relativistic jets and slower mass-loaded outflows, inflating extended nebulae of relativistic particles. Though observed in the local universe as ultra-luminous X-ray binaries and micro-quasars, the present census is biased against shorter-lived and hence rarer but potentially even more extreme MT systems: so-called “hypernebulae” (HNbe). We use the binary population synthesis code (\textsc{COMPAS}) to estimate the MT population, which spans a wide range of MT rates $\dot{M} \sim 10^{2-7}\,\dot{M}_{\rm Edd}$ and source lifetimes $t_{\rm acc} \sim 10^{0-6}$ years. Combining this synthetic population with a semi-analytic model for their time-dependent radio emission, we predict the observable properties of HNbe over cosmic time. HNbe exhibit a broad GHz luminosity range extending to $L_{\nu, \rm peak} \sim 10^{28-30}\,\mathrm{erg\,s^{-1}\,Hz^{-1}}$, and up to $\sim 10^{2}$ ($\sim 10^{5}$) HNbe are detectable by the wide-field radio surveys VLASS (DSA-2000) in our conservative model. A fraction of these systems should possess angular sizes and spatial offsets from star-forming regions (due to the first supernova kick) resolvable by VLBI. The most luminous donors, helium Hertzsprung-gap stars, may be detectable coincident with radio HNbe with Roman imaging out to distances $\sim 30$ Mpc. Our findings suggest HNbe as promising probes of the pathways linking massive binaries to energetic transients.

9. Inverse Reconstruction of Two-Field Potentials for a DESI-Motivated Phantom-Divide Transition[2611.23906]
Abstract

A phantom-divide transition favored by recent cosmological observations poses an inverse problem: which field potentials realize the inferred expansion history, and what features are uniquely fixed by it? We reconstruct local potentials for a canonical-phantom scalar pair with standard matter and radiation, targeting Metastable Emergent Dark Energy histories constrained by CMB, DESI BAO, and Type Ia supernova data. The coupled-field Friedmann evolution reproduces the target background to numerical accuracy across the transition, with regular perturbations through the crossing. At the phantom divide, the transition slope sets a dimensionless potential-energy release rate insensitive to the partition of kinetic energy. We identify local potential deformations that preserve both background expansion and linear dynamics. This construction gives a classical field realization of the inferred transition and disentangles observationally fixed energy balances from interaction terms requiring additional theoretical input.

10. The Geometric Imprint of a Tilted Accretion Disk in Gravitational Waves[2611.23914]
Abstract

Gravitational wave astronomy has opened a new window onto black holes and the dynamics of strong-field gravity, while accretion flows offer a complementary and natural laboratory for probing strong-field physics. In this work, we show that the gravitational wave emission from an initially tilted accretion disk carries a distinct imprint of its orientation. We perform three dimensional general relativistic magnetohydrodynamic simulations and evaluate the gravitational waves through Teukolsky equation. For an aligned disk, the emission is concentrated in low-$|m|$ modes, where $m$ is the azimuthal quantum number. For the initially tilted disk, higher-$|m|$ modes make a larger relative contribution. As the tilted flow subsequently aligns with the black hole spin, the contribution of $|m|\ge2$ modes decreases, showing strong correlation with the tilt angle. This indicates that the gravitational waves generated by the black hole environment carries geometric information about the surrounding matter. Our results establish a route towards using multimode gravitational radiation to probe the geometry and dynamical evolution of accretion flows in the strong-field region.

11. Constraining Accretion Heating in Neutron Stars with Carbon Instability and Quiescent Cooling[2611.23955]
Abstract

In accreting neutron stars, different heating depths can produce similar quiescent cooling when the heating strength and core temperature are adjusted. For KS 1731-260, we compare thermal histories with accretion heating either in a shallower region that overlaps the carbon fuel layer or in a deeper region below it. Without the carbon instability condition, the two histories fit ten quiescent Chandra spectra nearly equally well. When local carbon instability is required at the observed superburst epoch, the deeper region needs stronger accretion heating than the shallower region. After the thermal and spectral parameters are refitted, the deeper history retains more heat beneath the fuel and produces excess early quiescent emission. A larger fitted absorbing column partly attenuates this excess but makes the late spectra too faint. The fuel temperatures at the superburst epoch are similar in the two histories, so the same prescribed energy release produces nearly identical superburst decays. Including carbon burial, consumption and nuclear heating reduces the accretion heating needed for rapid burning to begin near the observed superburst; shallower heating still fits the quiescent spectra better. This preference persists across the tested fuel depths, fusion rates, distances and absorption treatments. In synthetic examples, carbon in older material allows deeper heating to match the cooling of a shallower-heating reference. Inferring the heating location therefore requires a fuel distribution and thermal history consistent with ignition, burst emission and quiescent cooling.

12. Positron escape from dark matter spikes in black hole X-ray binaries[2611.23983]
Abstract

Chan and Lee present a model where the AMS-02 positron excess is explained by dark matter (DM) annihilation in spike halos around nearby black holes A0620-00 and XTE J1118+480. Their model assumes a uniform magnetic and diffusive environment en route to Earth. Thus, it depends on the assumption that one can ignore the small-scale, near-horizon environment in the diffusion-loss equation. We demonstrate that this is not the case. An accurate magnetic field model near the horizon means, without increasing the required cross-section by a factor of roughly 500 to 1400, depending on the transport near the horizon, the positron flux from these sources cannot account for a significant fraction of the AMS-02 excess.

13. Two Faint Tidal Disruption Events Discovered in a Pilot Search with the Wide Field Survey Telescope[2611.23988]
Abstract

We report the discovery of TDE 2025alrm/WFST250129blnve and TDE 2025amkg/WFST251021dbmwk in the first six months of the wide-field survey (WFS) performed by the 2.5-meter Wide Field Survey Telescope (WFST). Both events are apparently faint among known optical tidal disruption events (TDEs), with peak magnitudes of $g \sim 20.0$ and $g \sim 19.6$, respectively. Blackbody fits to the multi-band photometry yield roughly constant temperatures of $\sim 10^{4}$ K for both sources, with peak bolometric luminosities of $10^{43.6}$ erg s$^{-1}$ and $10^{43.8}$ erg s$^{-1}$, respectively. Optical spectra obtained during the declining phase display characteristic broad Balmer, He II, and N III emission lines, classifying both sources as H+He TDEs. Host-galaxy SED fitting suggests stellar masses on the order of $10^{9} M_{\odot}$ and black hole masses of $\sim10^{6} M_{\odot}$, which are typical of optically selected TDE hosts. The pilot discovery of these two faint TDEs along with dozens of candidates, demonstrates the capability of WFST to discover TDEs, owing to its exceptional survey depth. Furthermore, these events highlight the value of the $u$ band in multi-band surveys for TDE selection, particularly for distinguishing TDEs from supernovae at early phases. With continued operations, WFST is expected to substantially expand the known TDE population, especially by uncovering nearby low-luminosity events and high-redshift TDEs, both of which remain poorly explored.

14. SN 2025wiu: A Slowly Evolving Type Ib Supernova with an Extraordinary Post-maximum Bump[2611.24204]
Abstract

Stripped-envelope supernovae (SESNe) originate from massive progenitors that have lost most or all of their outer envelopes prior to core collapse. Recent advances in optical time-domain surveys have revealed a growing population of peculiar SESNe with unusual light-curve morphologies, pointing to energy sources beyond the standard radioactive-decay scenario. In this work, we present multi-band photometric and spectroscopic observations of SN 2025wiu, a peculiar Type Ib supernova discovered by the Wide Field Survey Telescope (WFST). SN 2025wiu is characterized by a high luminosity and exceptionally slow photometric evolution, with a prominent post-maximum bump peaking at $\sim50$ days after the primary maximum. Its early light curve also shows a weak excess consistent with shock-cooling emission from a compact progenitor with a radius of $\sim4\,R_\odot$. The spectra show only modest evolution, with persistently weak line features and no narrow emission lines detected even at $\sim110$ days after maximum. By modeling the primary peak and late-time tail with a combination of radioactive decay and additional energy sources, we isolate the post-maximum bump, which has a roughly symmetric profile with a peak luminosity of $\sim2.4 \times 10^{42}~\mathrm{erg\,s^{-1}}$ and a characteristic duration of $\sim42$ days. We discuss circumstellar medium (CSM) interaction, accretion, magnetar activity, and a double $^{56}$Ni distribution as possible origins of the bump. The latter three scenarios face challenges in reproducing the post-maximum bump, while delayed CSM interaction remains a possible explanation.

15. Possible origins of the two γ-ray components C0 and C1 in IC 443[2611.24300]
Abstract

Recent LHAASO observations of IC 443 reveal that its high-energy $\gamma$-ray emission can be decomposed into two components with clearly distinct spatial morphologies and spectral properties, namely the compact component C0 and component C1 with an angular extent of 0.67$^\circ$. We aim to explain the $\gamma$-ray emission mechanisms responsible for the two components in IC 443 and to further investigate their respective possible astrophysical counterparts. We model components C0 and C1 independently using two different physical scenarios. For component C0, we adopt an SNR-MC hadronic model that accounts for finite source-size effects to describe the escape and diffusion of high-energy particles and their $\gamma$-ray production via pp interactions with the surrounding molecular clouds. For component C1 , we employ a spatially dependent multi-zone leptonic transport model and describe the evolution of high-energy electrons under the combined action of advection, diffusion, and energy losses using a Fokker–Planck-type equation. The model reproduces the SEDs of components C0 and C1 well. Component C0 exhibits a pronounced $\pi^0$-decay feature, and the maximum energy of its escaping particles reaches the PeV scale. The $\gamma$-ray emission from component C1 can be explained by inverse Compton scattering. Its diffusion coefficient is approximately two orders of magnitude lower than that in the surrounding interstellar medium, and no significant synchrotron counterpart is detected. The $\gamma$-ray emission from IC 443 involves the combined action of multiple physical mechanisms. Component C0 may be a potential PeVatron candidate, whereas component C1 may correspond to a potential TeV halo candidate.

16. Analytical formulae for planet-planet or moon-moon tides[2611.24416]
Abstract

Almost negligible among the solar system planets, mutual tides raised by a secondary on another secondary orbiting the same primary may gain increased importance in tightly packed systems, e.g., between the components of close-in exoplanetary systems or between the moons of gas giants. In this work, we derive an analytical formula for the planet-planet tidal potential and for the effect of mutual tides on the rotational and orbital evolution of moons or planets in a coplanar configuration. The procedure follows closely the steps of W. M. Kaula (1961), and the resulting potential and derived quantities are presented in the form of an expansion into modes of different frequencies (in the temporal domain) and spherical harmonic components (in the spatial domain). The analytical formulae are implemented into a code and compared with an independent numerical solution.

17. A lepto-hadronic perspective on the properties of the candidate neutrino-emitter blazar 5BZQ J0808+4950[2611.24418]
Abstract

The role of active galactic nuclei in the production of high-energy neutrinos has been highlighted by the association of the blazar TXS 0506+056 and the Seyfert galaxy NGC 1068 with IceCube neutrino events. In particular, blazars, with their powerful jets, are potential sites of neutrino production via (photo-)hadronic interactions. The gamma-ray bright blazar 5BZQJ0808+4950 is consistent with a spatial anisotropy in the IceCube neutrino all-sky maps. An individual high-energy neutrino event, IC110929A, was also reported in the direction of this blazar. Although the correlation between the gamma-ray bright blazars and the neutrino production remains inconclusive, 5BZQ J0808+4950, with a luminous accretion disk serving as a photon field for the photo-hadronic interactions, offers the opportunity to test the relation between neutrino production and the radiative output of the blazar jet. We compile available multi-wavelength data and analyze the gamma-ray light curve of 5BZQ J0808+4950, leading us to define six contemporaneous epochs to model the source's spectral energy distribution and its evolution over time. We use a lepto-hadronic, time-dependent framework to model 5BZQ J0808+4950, taking into account external photon fields. We find that the solutions corresponding to the different states the source exhibits are explained mostly by a change in the particles' injection properties. The correlation between the predicted X-ray and the neutrino luminosity in this source is stronger than the correlation between gamma-ray and neutrino luminosity. We predict that the rate of neutrinos detectable by IceCube for this blazar ranges from $\sim 10^{-3}$ to $\sim10^{-1}$ neutrinos per epoch. Assuming Poisson statistics, the predicted neutrino number contemporaneous with the interval in which the high-energy neutrino event IC110929A was detected, is compatible with the detection of one neutrino.

18. Sun-like stars show lowered X-ray activity together with over-rotation at beyond-solar ages[2611.24459]
Abstract

In recent years, substructure in the age-rotation relationship of cool, sun-like stars has been identified, showing deviations from the historical Skumanich law. Since magnetic activity is expected to be the physical root cause of the stellar spin-down, namely via the magnetic braking process mediated through stellar winds, changes in spin-down should correspond to unusual activity behaviour. We selected a sample of old sun-like stars in the solar neighborhood with asteroseismic age estimates and measured rotation periods, and present an analysis of their magnetic activity using deep X-ray observations from XMM-Newton. We observe that sample stars less massive than the Sun (late-G and early-K) agree well with the established rotation-activity relationship, while early-G and late-F stars with masses above solar tend to be unusually X-ray dim for their rotation period. The latter stars rotate faster than same-colour stars in the solar-age open cluster M67, despite being older than M67. Those stars are characterized by either low metallicity ([Fe/H]$\approx -0.2$ dex compared to solar) or by a mix of low coronal temperatures ($< 1.5$ million K) and very low X-ray surface fluxes ($\lesssim 10^4$ erg s$^{-1}$ cm$^{-2}$). We conclude that there is sufficient dynamo activity for coronal emission in the sample stars, since we successfully detect the majority of them in our X-ray observations. We argue that stars with extremely low coronal temperature and/or low X-ray flux through the stellar surface may produce an inefficient spin-down through hampered angular momentum shedding. If this is indeed the cause for their over-rotation, the onset age of this effect would need to be at an age of approximately 3 Gyr. However, this behaviour is at odds with studies of stars in the open cluster M67 with a cluster age of $\sim4$ Gyr, which do not show a faster-rotating population of sun-like stars.

20. Interpretable attention-based neural posterior estimation for fast radio burst cosmology[2611.24542]
Abstract

Fast radio bursts (FRBs) probe the integrated free-electron density along extragalactic sightlines and can be used to study late-time cosmic evolution. Conventional Bayesian analyses require repeated evaluations of computationally expensive likelihoods, making inference increasingly challenging as FRB catalogs grow. In this work, we develop an interpretable attention-based neural posterior estimator (AttNPE) for rapid posterior inference of the physical parameters from FRB catalogs with measured redshifts. The attention encoder compresses the catalog into a low-dimensional representation, and a conditional masked autoregressive flow maps samples from a base distribution to the seven-dimensional posterior. AttNPE yields posterior distributions in close agreement with Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC), recovering the parameter correlations in both simulated and observed FRB catalogs. For the observed catalog, AttNPE generates $5\times10^4$ posterior samples in $0.036$ s after training, compared with $72.8$ h for the reference MCMC run, giving an elapsed-time ratio of $O(10^6)$. The attention-weight analysis shows that intermediate-redshift FRBs ($0.3\leq z<0.7$) account for the largest share of the total attention averaged over simulated catalogs following the observed redshift distribution, consistent with the combined influence of source abundance and redshift-dependent sensitivity. These results demonstrate the potential of interpretable neural posterior estimation for efficient and repeated inference in FRB cosmology.

21. Cosmological First-Order Phase Transitions with Full Nonlinear Gravity[2611.24581]
Abstract

Cosmological first-order phase transitions can contribute to the stochastic gravitational wave background. For decades, these first-order phase transitions have been studied using a Friedmann–Lemaître–Robertson–Walker background where the gravitational waves are treated as linear perturbations. Here, we present simulations of a first-order phase transition in the presence of fully nonlinear general relativity, using the BSSN formalism. We simulate a universe that contains a scalar field alongside a relativistic radiation fluid. We focus on GUT-scale transitions in which bubbles nucleate near the horizon size. We describe a method to construct initial data that satisfies the Hamiltonian and momentum constraints, starting from a Coleman–de Luccia-like bubble profile and adjusting the bubble radius. We compute the gravitational wave spectrum directly from the trace-free extrinsic curvature and find a present-day peak amplitude of $\Omega_{\rm GW,0}h^2 \sim 5\times 10^{-11}$, consistent with earlier results that do not include nonlinear gravity. The peak frequency depends on the symmetry-breaking scale and could fall anywhere from the LISA band to GHz frequencies. At late times, the field fragments into long-lived oscillons, which push its effective equation of state toward that of matter.

22. Thermodynamics of gas giants accreting via shocks: the role of molecular hydrogen dissociation[2611.24615]
Abstract

Within the core-accretion framework, gas giants acquire most of their mass through accretion shocks during runaway gas accretion. The shock thermodynamics—governed by radiative cooling, accretion history, and equation of state (EoS)—sets the post-shock gas entropy and initial thermal state, strongly influencing both observables and subsequent evolution. We aim to build a coupled evolutionary model predicting the initial thermodynamic conditions, intrinsic luminosities, and upper limits on magnetic field strengths of gas giants across diverse accretion histories. We couple radiation hydrodynamics and $\mathrm{H_2}$ dissociation to long-term planetary interior evolution by training a physics-informed neural network (PINN) predictor on 1000 first-principles simulations resolving the shock physics. This PINN provides post-shock outer boundary conditions for MESA to evolve the planetary envelope beyond runaway accretion. The PINN predicts higher post-shock temperatures than the canonical cold-start model, with the discrepancy widening with planetary mass, while post-shock entropy jumps sharply when $\mathrm{H_2}$ dissociates at $\sim 2000$ K. Gas giants evolved with PINN-derived boundary conditions achieve intrinsic luminosities 4–10 times higher than cold-start models. Under optimistic assumptions, the maximum magnetic field strength at $2 R_{\mathrm{J}}$ reaches a few gauss for a $0.3 M_{\mathrm{J}}$ planet, $\sim 100$ G for $1 M_{\mathrm{J}}$, and $\sim 500$ G for $3 M_{\mathrm{J}}$. Incorporating detailed radiation hydrodynamics and realistic thermodynamics into accretion shock models yields higher initial entropies, brighter intrinsic luminosities, and stronger magnetic fields, providing improved constraints on the masses and accretion rates of directly imaged exoplanets.

23. Early spectra of AT2017gfo from scaled 3D merger ejecta: mass–velocity distribution, 3D structure, and treatment of line opacity[2611.24627]
Abstract

We present ARTIS radiative transfer calculations for neutron star merger ejecta with line-by-line opacities, wavelength-calibrated lanthanide data, and time-dependent r-process compositions and thermalisation, testing how the early kilonova depends on the mass-velocity distribution, dimensionality, and line treatment. We rescale 3D dynamical ejecta from a merger simulation in density and velocity to approximate AT2017gfo. Mass scaling alone provides good agreement with the bolometric light curve but inconsistent spectra. With velocities additionally halved, the model also reproduces the observed spectra at 1.4, 2.4, and (albeit less accurately) 3.4 days. The Sr II triplet contributes most to the 10500 $\mathring{\mathrm{A}}$ peak, and La III and Gd III to the 13500 $\mathring{\mathrm{A}}$ shoulder. A 2D azimuthal average mixes lanthanide-poor with lanthanide-rich regions, smooths the density, and makes the averaged model three times fainter at 1 day and redder throughout than the 3D model, highlighting the importance of 3D modelling. We compare the line-by-line macroatom treatment with expansion-opacity and line-binned schemes with thermal redistribution, using identical atomic data. Luminosities agree to $\lesssim 10$ per cent after 1 day, but the expansion-opacity scheme, unlike converged line-binned schemes, overestimates the spectral flux by up to 20 per cent, which can bias inferred abundances.

24. Stratonovich approximation for halo bias: An efficient framework within excursion set theory[2611.24632]
Abstract

Selected astrophysical methods for testing particle-physics models are reviewed. Stellar structure and evolution constrain additional channels of energy loss and transport; high-energy photon propagation probes photon mixing with new particles and modifications of electron-positron pair production; large-volume neutrino telescopes are used to search for magnetic monopoles, neutrinos from superheavy dark-matter decay, and non-standard neutrino propagation. Recent applications of these methods are discussed, involving the tip of the red-giant branch in the color-luminosity diagram, gamma-ray burst 221009A, and data from the Baikal Gigaton Volume Detector (Baikal-GVD) and the IceCube experiment. In each case, particle-physics parameters are inferred through a forward model that accounts for the response to new physics, propagation, detection, and nuisance parameters.
