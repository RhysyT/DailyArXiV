1. Likely Metal Scarring in the Magnetic White Dwarf LHS 2534[2610.40741]
Abstract

There are only three white dwarfs known to possess metal-enriched polar caps due to magnetically guided accretion. These rare systems offer a unique opportunity to study planetesimal accretion and how magnetic fields influence the spreading of material across the atmosphere. To better study these processes however, more targets are needed. Here we report our findings from an observing campaign on the magnetic white dwarf LHS 2534. Time-resolved spectra at the MMT show clear variability in the strength of the Na I and Cr I features. We find that the strengths of these features vary over a period of 19.46 and 19.36 hours respectively, closely matching the 20.22 hour period determined from K2 photometry. Our findings demonstrate the ability to detect and study these variable objects with spectroscopy, and time-series observations of similar targets in the future will provide more insight into how these atmospheres form.

2. Hints of self-interacting dark matter from strong gravitational lensing[2610.40766]
Abstract

An analysis of 29 quadruply imaged quasars hints at dark matter halo densities consistent with gravothermal core collapse, a key prediction of self-interacting dark matter. We interpret this evidence through elastic Yukawa scattering, combining lensing with upper limits on the cross-section strength from galaxy clusters and Milky Way satellite evaporation. Favored models have dark matter masses of $20$-$80\,{\rm GeV}$, MeV-scale mediators, and Yukawa-potential strengths near $10^{-3}$, yielding cross-sections of $10^2$–$10^4 \, {\rm cm^2\,g^{-1}}$ at $10\,{\rm km\,s^{-1}}$. Cosmological, direct and indirect detection bounds on MeV-scale mediators motivate a neutrinophilic scalar dilepton that decays invisibly without Higgs mixing. These results demonstrate how inferences on core collapse constrain dark sector physics.

3. Surprisingly normal? GW241011 and GW241110 in the context of hierarchical merger classification[2610.40774]
Abstract

Uncovering the origins of merging binary black holes is a key goal in gravitational-wave astrophysics, but identifying the formation channel of a particular binary is complicated by imprecise measurements and uncertain theoretical predictions. While classification often involves computing metrics from an event's inferred properties, measurement precision varies across parameter space. Using the perhaps-surprising low masses of the hierarchical merger candidates GW241011 and GW241110 as a backdrop, we consider the classifiable distribution, which steps beyond detectability to assess what events may be correctly characterized as part of a population. Interpreting classification outcomes, and assessing the falsifiability of theoretical model predictions, are benefit by understanding the regions of parameter space that a priori enable—and do not enable—confident measurement. For hierarchical mergers, we demonstrate that mass ratio is the largest intrinsic driver of identification, while orbital inclination and signal-to-noise ratio are the key extrinsic factors. Further, we show GW241011 and GW241110 achieve classification metrics consistent with similar simulated hierarchical mergers. Model selection and classification require the context of measurement capacity, especially as catalogs offer growing numbers of events with seemingly unusual properties.

4. Missing Mergers: On the Unexpected Delay-Time Distribution of Binary Neutron Star Mergers[2610.40782]
Abstract

The LIGO-Virgo-KAGRA (LVK) collaboration has detected two binary neutron star (BNS) mergers, GW170817 and GW190425. While no clear electromagnetic counterpart was discovered for GW190425, a coincident counterpart to GW170817 (GRB170817A, AT 2017gfo) was discovered in an early-type galaxy, NGC 4993. Using broad-band photometry and an integrated optical spectrum, we measure a mass-weighted age of $12.4^{+0.5}_{-0.7}$ Gyr (90% credible) for NGC 4993, which is an estimate of the delay time between star formation and merger for the GW170817 progenitor. BNS systems are expected to have a power-law delay-time distribution (DTD) with a minimum delay time, $t_{\rm min}$, of  10 Myr and index $\alpha \approx -1$. This canonical DTD, convolved with the cosmic star-formation history, predicts  16 BNS mergers at delay times of  100 Myr for every BNS merger at 10 Gyr; under it, the chance of detecting even one merger as old as GW170817 is  1.8%. The long delay time of GW170817 therefore implies many "missing" shorter-delay mergers if the canonical DTD holds. A DTD with $t_{\rm min} = 12.6$ Gyr and $\alpha = -1$ is favored over the canonical DTD by a factor of 40-140, and over short gamma-ray burst and Galactic double-NS DTDs by factors of 80-280. These results are in tension with current population synthesis models, the Milky Way double NS population, and the SGRB population. Explaining the LVK population requires either a dramatically different separation distribution than commonly assumed, an additional channel with a long delay time and comparable rate to an isolated-binary formation scenario, or a large population of BNS mergers missed in the LVK analysis. This result has wide-ranging implications for binary-population synthesis, chemical enrichment models, connections between BNS mergers and SGRBs, and future gravitational wave observations.

5. Model-independent and reconstruction-free evidence for cosmic acceleration from DESI baryon acoustic oscillations[2610.40785]
Abstract

While there is ample evidence that the universe's expansion is accelerating, most of this evidence relies on assuming a shape of the relative expansion rate, $H(z)/H_0$, informed by a cosmological model or a kinematical approach. Model-independent analyses are common for supernova datasets but rarely applied to other data. In this work, we use uncalibrated baryon acoustic oscillations (BAO) from DESI DR2 for a model-independent test of acceleration, which makes no assumptions about the theory of gravity or the energy content of the universe. We test for cosmic acceleration with three model-independent statistics that rely purely on the measured Alcock-Paczyński parameter, the isotropic BAO, and the line-of-sight BAO, and do not require any reconstruction between redshift bins unlike previous analyses. Accounting for the look-elsewhere effect, among all statistics we find $2.4\sigma$ - $4.1\sigma$ evidence for cosmic acceleration within $0 \leq z \leq 0.934$ when assuming flatness; allowing for spatial curvature, $|\Omega_k| \leq 1$, at least two of the three statistics retain $2.4\sigma$ - $3.5\sigma$.

6. Cosmic merger rates and population evolution of binary black hole mergers in AGN disks[2610.40787]
Abstract

Active galactic nuclei (AGN) disks are promising sites for hierarchical binary black hole (BBH) mergers, which next-generation gravitational-wave detectors will probe out to high redshift. Predicting their contribution requires linking BBH evolution in AGN disks to the poorly constrained high-redshift evolution of active supermassive black holes (SMBHs). We introduce ARISE (AGN Rates and Intrinsic Stellar-BH Evolution), a framework that embeds semi-analytical models of BBH evolution in AGN disks into a cosmological description of the AGN number density, SMBH mass function, Eddington-ratio distribution, and nuclear metallicity. We consider a conservative AGN-abundance model anchored to observed luminosity functions, as well as Eddington-limited and super-Eddington scenarios based on the CAT cosmological model, compatible with JWST observations of high-redshift AGNs. The merger-rate density is set primarily by the disk viscosity parameter $\alpha$: at $z = 0.2$, we find a rate of $12$–$37$ Gpc$^{-3}$ yr$^{-1}$ for $\alpha = 0.01$ and $0.5$–$6$ Gpc$^{-3}$ yr$^{-1}$ for $\alpha = 0.1$. The redshift evolution depends strongly on how SMBHs grow. The rate density peaks at $z \simeq 1.8$, $3.3$, and $3.9$ ($1.5$, $3.6$, and $3.9$) for $\alpha=0.01$ ($0.1$) in the conservative, Eddington-limited, and super-Eddington models, respectively, and in the super-Eddington model it stays nearly flat out to $z \simeq 10$. For $\alpha = 0.1$, AGN disks account for $\sim 2$–$40\%$ of the LVK-inferred merger rate, consistent with current upper limits from GW–AGN associations. For $\alpha = 0.01$, they match or exceed it. The Einstein Telescope (ET) should detect $\sim 10^{5}$ ($\sim 10^{4}$) AGN-disk mergers per year for $\alpha = 0.01$ ($0.1$). ET will test the AGN channel at redshifts where the AGN population models differ most strongly, providing independent constraints on the growth of early SMBHs.

7. The Importance of Curium-250 to the $γ$-Ray Signature of $r$-Process Remnants[2610.40907]
Abstract

Identifying the astrophysical sites of the heavy rapid neutron-capture process ($r$-process) remains one of the most prominent unsolved problems in nuclear astrophysics. Emitted $\gamma$-rays from the decay of unstable nuclei have been identified as a potential observable marker on both prompt and delayed timescales which could provide conclusive evidence of the presence of $r$-process activity. In this work, we identify and discuss the importance of $^{250}$Cm to the $\gamma$-ray spectra of remnants, showing that the decay spectrum of $^{250}$Cm dominates the $\gamma$-ray spectra of $r$-process ejecta across a broad timescale ($\sim$ 2,000 - 20,000 years). We highlight the uncertain nature of this spectra: $^{250}$Cm may decay via $\alpha$-decay, $\beta$-decay, and spontaneous fission, but the decay branchings are not experimentally known, and each decay channel produces unique spectral lines which may be observable. We examine the potential for observation of the most prominent spectral signatures, finding that for conventional neutron star merger remnants, $\gamma$-ray lines will be detectable in current and future detectors only in the very near galaxy ($\sim$ 1 kpc), but other sites which could produce a larger ejecta mass may have greater observable distances. Our findings highlight the potential of actinide decay chains to indicate r-process nucleosynthesis and further underscore the necessity of measuring the nuclear properties that define their characteristic spectral signatures.

8. Completeness and dynamical stability in exoplanet occurrence rates[2610.40964]
Abstract

Exoplanet population studies are often quantified through the "occurrence rate" of certain types of planet. This value can be calculated from a survey of a sample of stars by taking the "detection rate" and correcting with a completeness factor. This completeness is calculated via detection limits on each individual star, and can be calculated either individually for each detected planet or averaged in a certain area of parameter space. We test whether either the average completeness or individual completeness performs better than the other. We also test whether including dynamical stability constraints in the detection limits improves the occurrence rate results. We use synthetic sets of exoplanetary systems and calculate occurrence rates for each and compare to what the value would be with perfect information. We compare how close each method is to the ground truth to assess which is most suitable. Overall, in our synthetic sets, the average completeness is more reliable though it can be a problem if there is a sharp change in the underlying population within the area of parameter-space considered. We find that using dynamical stability conditions causes a bias in the occurrence rate and do not recommend using these.

9. GRB 241113B: the first SVOM and EP gamma-ray burst A soft GRB likely seen on-axis, from soft X-rays to gamma-rays[2610.41005]
Abstract

In 2024, two new missions were launched to detect high-energy transients. EP and SVOM aim to observe soft transients from X-ray rich gamma-ray bursts (XRR), X-ray flashes (XRF), to fast X-ray transients (FXT). GRB 241113B, the first soft gamma-ray burst (GRB) jointly detected by EP and SVOM from soft X-rays to gamma-rays (0.5–1000 keV). GRB 241113B has an $E^{obs}_{peak} \approx 35$ keV and a redshift (z) likely between 0.5 and 3.5, with $T_{90,\gamma} \approx 42$ s (4–30 keV). This burst can be classified as either an XRR or XRF, depending on the fluence ratio used. The afterglow is achromatic and featureless with a normal decay ($\alpha \approx -1.40$). The prompt emission does not satisfy the shock-breakout relation, and thus a geometry comprised of a jet is preferred. However, the afterglow fitting favors a jet viewed on-axis dominated by synchrotron emission due to electrons in a slow cooling regime, interacting with a wind medium. The bulk Lorentz factor ($\Gamma$) indicates that the motion of the jet is ultrarelativistic ($\Gamma_{0}>100$), which discards the dirty fireball model. Hence, the jet baryon loading should be compatible with that of classical GRBs (C-GRBs). The prompt spectral properties of GRB 241113B ($\alpha$, $E^{\mathrm{obs}}_{\mathrm{peak}}$, $E_{\gamma,iso}$) are comparable to those of XRFs/XRRs and are in the continuity of the C-GRB distributions, setting it apart from the extragalatic FXT population solely detected by EP. Hence, the SVOM mission, with the benefit brought by the instrument ECLAIRs with its high sensitivity and low energy threshold (at 4keV), is able to detect the potential $\gamma$-ray counterpart of soft transients also observed by EP and help to differentiate them from FXTs. The latter show the interest of the joint operation of both missions to better study the landscape of soft transients.

10. Vacuum Phase Transition and Decay to Cross the Phantom Divide[2610.41077]
Abstract

We show that the vacuum metamorphosis model with decay fits current baryon acoustic oscillation and supernova distance data and cosmic microwave background measurements significantly better than $\Lambda$CDM and nearly as well as $w_0w_a$CDM with the same number of parameters. Vacuum metamorphosis with decay provides a physically motivated model of dark energy and an explanation of current cosmological data. In this scenario, non-perturbative quantum effects of a massive scalar field freeze the Ricci scalar curvature when it reaches a critical value proportional to the mass squared, inducing a phase transition in the scalar field, giving a phantom dark energy equation of state $w<-1$. After the phase transition, vacuum energy decays into light particles, providing a natural mechanism for the dark-energy equation of state to cross the phantom divide from $w<-1$ to $w>-1$, in agreement with current cosmological indications, without requiring an explicit coupling to matter.

11. HAWC, Fermi-LAT and Chandra Detections of High Energy Particles in the S106 Star Forming Region[2610.41163]
Abstract

Local star-forming regions have recently been the subject of scrutiny as cosmic-ray accelerators and sources of ultra-high-energy gamma-ray emissions. The Cygnus-X region is one of the richest star forming regions in the galaxy and over the last few decades has been extensively studied. Here we report the detection of a star-forming region located in the southern part of Cygnus-X, Sharpless 106 (S106), using the HAWC Observatory. The S106 region is spatially located near the cataloged gamma-ray source 1LHAASO J2027+3657. In this work, we perform a multi-wavelength analysis of S106, by combining HAWC data with re-analysis of Fermi-LAT and archival Chandra data. 12. Effect of quark-hadron phase transitions on the neutron star mass distribution[2610.41242]
Abstract

The neutron star (NS) mass distribution serves as a crucial probe of the dense matter equation of state (EOS) and core-collapse supernova explosion mechanisms. Although observations reveal a bimodal NS mass distribution, existing theoretical simulations often struggle to naturally reproduce the high-mass peak, and the impact of the quark-hadron phase transition (PT) in high-density nuclear matter remains largely unexplored. Employing the 1D general-relativistic hydrodynamics code GR1D with hybrid EOSs incorporating a first-order PT (RDF1.1 and RDF1.2), we systematically simulate core-collapse supernovae for progenitors with zero-age main-sequence masses ranging from 13 to 120 $M_\odot$. Our results reveal a threefold impact of the PT onset density on the final supernova fate: in low-compactness progenitors, an early PT truncates accretion and produces less massive NSs; in the critical-compactness regime, it facilitates successful explosions; and in high-compactness progenitors, it accelerates black hole formation. Compared with the $\sim 1.4\,M_\odot$ peak typical of the conventional core-collapse scenario, the inclusion of the PT produces NSs with masses centered around $1.70\,M_\odot$ for RDF1.1 and within $1.55$–$1.70\,M_\odot$ for RDF1.2. Furthermore, we identify a novel formation channel whereby low-compactness progenitors with failed initial shocks can form more massive NSs via a PT-induced second shock. Due to the fixed nature of the PT boundary, the final NS masses exhibit a pronounced clustering effect. This study elucidates the critical role of the quark-hadron PT in shaping the NS mass distribution, providing important astrophysical constraints on the properties of high-density nuclear matter and the formation mechanism of massive NSs.

13. Spectroscopic observations of periodic bursty ejections during a dome-spire-shaped jet[2610.41343]
Abstract

Context: The study investigates the complexity of the formation and evolution of a dome-spire-shaped jet (or anemone jet) that originated in an active region and was presumably driven by null-point magnetic reconnection. Aims: We aim to provide a quantitative estimation of multi-temperature plasma dynamics and physical parameters of the jet and its blob-like features, together with its temporal evolution in three-dimensional perspective. Methods: For this, we analyze unique sit-and-stare spectral (Si IV, Mg II k, and C II) and slit-jaw imaging 1400 Å and 2796 Å observations obtained with the Interface Region Imaging Spectrograph (IRIS), with a slit uniquely positioned just above the assumed reconnection site. Co-observations taken with the Fast Imaging Solar Spectrograph of the Goode Solar Telescope in the chromospheric H$\alpha$ and Ca II 8542.10 Å lines were also analyzed. These data are complemented by EUV imaging information from the Atmospheric Imaging Assembly on board the Solar Dynamics Observatory. Results: The IRIS observations reveal that the jet is formed by multiple periodic ($\sim$30 s) plasma ejections with blob-like appearance, each associated with strongly blueshifted Si IV 1402.77 Å transition-region emission reaching about $-300$ km/s. More importantly, the ejections are grouped into well-defined packets, each lasting approximately 3–5 min. In contrast, only a single Doppler component is observed in the chromospheric Ca II line. Multi-Gaussian decomposition of the Si IV line profiles reveals a highly accelerated plasma, with line-of-sight speed toward the observer increasing linearly from $\sim$30 to 112 km/s over 66 s, corresponding to an acceleration of 1.3 km/s$^2$. The kinetic energy release rate is estimated in the range of $\sim$1.0$\times10^{24}$–1.0$\times10^{25}$ erg s$^{-1}$.

14. Relative Influence of Coronal Properties of CME in their Evolution and Geo-effectiveness[2610.41376]
Abstract

Uncertainties in the coronal mass ejection (CME) properties near the Sun remain a major challenge for accurate space weather forecasting. Understanding the sensitivity of CME evolution to the initial parameters is therefore essential for improving the predictions. We investigate the influence of physical CME properties prescribed at 0.1 AU on their subsequent heliospheric propagation and geoeffectiveness at Earth, using the SWASTi framework. We perform a systematically sampled ensemble of 400 global magnetohydrodynamic simulations by varying the initial CME density, speed, magnetic flux and temperature. We quantify the relative influence of these parameters on CME evolution, plasma properties at L1, and the resulting geomagnetic response. The analysis reveals a clear hierarchy of influence: initial speed primarily controls the peak radial velocity and arrival time at L1, magnetic flux controls the magnetic-field strength, and density governs the peak density and magnetopause compression. Primary parameters account for only half the output variance, highlighting the collective influence of other parameters. Density exhibits the broadest influence across the analyzed outputs. We find a mean arrival-time sensitivities of 2.43h per 100 km/s variation in injection speed and 1.99h per $10^{-18}$ kg/m$^3$ variation in injection density. The CME volume exhibits a two-phase evolution, characterized by an initial gradual expansion followed by a power-law regime with a median expansion index of 2.38. These results establish parameter-specific priorities for CME forecasting, highlighting the importance of constraining CME speed for arrival-time prediction, density for duration and pressure-driven effects, and magnetic flux for geomagnetic response at Earth.

15. Identifying a High-confidence Candidate of Stellar Coronal Mass Ejection on an M dwarf[2610.41452]
Abstract

Although coronal mass ejections (CMEs) potentially play an important role in shaping the habitability of exoplanets, the firm detection of flaring-associated CMEs on remote stars remains a challenge task at current stage. We here report time-resolved spectroscopic follow-up observations of a superflare (GWAC 251130A) on an M-dwarf, triggered by the SVOM/GWAC high-cadence transient survey. With an amplitude of $\approx2.5$ mag in $R-$band, the bolometric flaring energy is estimated to be $1.2^{+1.0}_{-0.5}\times10^{35}$ erg. The CME mass and corresponding kinetic energy along the line-of-sight are estimated to be $\sim3.2\times10^{19}$ g and $\sim2.1\times10^{32}$ erg, respectively, which reinforces the slightly larger expelled mass with depressed kinetic energy in stellar CME than in the solar flare-CME events.

16. Enhanced Detectability of Very-High-Energy Gamma Rays from Off-Axis Short Gamma-Ray Bursts: Impacts of Structured Jets and External Inverse-Compton Scattering[2610.41476]
Abstract

The binary neutron star merger GW170817 established a direct link between gravitational waves and short gamma-ray bursts (sGRBs), whose afterglows are commonly interpreted as nonthermal emission from structured relativistic jets viewed off-axis. Some sGRBs exhibit extended or plateau X-ray emission, suggesting prolonged central-engine activity. These X-ray photons can serve as seed photons for external inverse-Compton (EIC) scattering by relativistic electrons in the jet, producing very-high-energy (VHE) gamma rays. However, how the angular structure of the jet affects the detectability of EIC emission remains insufficiently explored. Here, we investigate EIC emission from off-axis structured jets and assess its detectability with the Cherenkov Telescope Array Observatory (CTAO). We find that EIC emission powered by extended and/or plateau X-rays produces detectable VHE signals from nearby sGRBs, particularly during the early afterglow, significantly outshining synchrotron self-Compton emission. In our adopted models, Gaussian and power-law jets produce brighter off-axis EIC emission than a top-hat jet, extending the viewing-angle range over which VHE emission may be detectable. These results demonstrate that early VHE observations with CTAO can probe both prolonged central-engine activity and the angular structure of relativistic jets in sGRBs.

17. Disk-Corona Energy Balance Evolves with Flux State in the Quasar PDS 456[2610.41509]
Abstract

We investigate the long-term X-ray spectral evolution of the highly luminous, radio-quiet quasar PDS 456 using AstroSat observations obtained in 2019 and 2021 together with archival XMM-Newton, NuSTAR and Suzaku observations spanning 2001-2024. The AstroSat observations provide valuable coverage following the bright flaring state observed in 2018, revealing that the source underwent a relatively quiescent phase in 2019 before re-brightening in 2021. During the AstroSat epochs, PDS 456 exhibits a harder-when-brighter spectral trend, whereas the long-term XMM-Newton, NuSTAR, and Suzaku observations confirm the overall softer-when-fainter behavior. This behavior is consistent with the presence of a very cool corona ( 15 keV), possibly driven by a strong incident photon field for upscattering, as suggested by a prominent big blue bump. Broadband spectral modeling of simultaneous XMM-Newton and NuSTAR observations, complemented by the 2021 AstroSat flaring epoch and interpreted within the self-consistent physical framework reXcor, indicates that the disk-corona energy balance evolves with flux. In low-flux states, the spectrum is reflection-dominated, implying efficient illumination of the inner accretion disk by a compact corona. In contrast, high-flux states show an enhanced contribution from the warm Comptonizing region at larger coronal heights. The Fe K{\alpha} emission line displays a positive correlation with the 2-10 keV continuum flux at low to intermediate flux levels, while deviations at the highest flux states suggest a reduced relative reflection contribution as the warm corona becomes energetically dominant.

18. From Anoxic to Oxic Earth: Detectability of Atmospheric Biosignatures across Stellar Hosts with ELT/ANDES[2610.41621]
Abstract

Earth's atmosphere evolved from an anoxic, methane-bearing Archean state through Proterozoic oxygenation to the O$_2$-rich modern atmosphere. However, most biosignature detectability studies focus on modern Earth, leaving earlier atmospheric states comparatively unexplored. We investigate how atmospheric evolution and stellar environments influence the detectability of O$_2$, CH$_4$, H$_2$O, and CO$_2$, and their potential to distinguish anoxic from oxygenated atmospheres. We construct two complementary photochemical grids: representative Archean, Proterozoic, and modern-Earth scenarios around the Sun, and four geological epochs (3.9, 2.0, 0.8 Ga, and modern Earth) across FGKM hosts. Combining one-dimensional photochemical modeling, high-resolution transmission spectral simulations, and instrument-noise modeling, we assess molecular detectability with ELT/ANDES under optimistic photon-noise-limited conditions. For solar-host Archean and Proterozoic models, CH$_4$ requires approximately 33 and 42 transits, respectively, to reach the adopted 5$\sigma$ criterion, whereas O$_2$ remains below 3$\sigma$ within the observing limits. Modern-Earth O$_2$ reaches 3$\sigma$ after approximately 38 transits. Across the FGKM grid, O$_2$ does not reach 5$\sigma$ for the 3.9 Ga models. However, For the M3V, K2V, and M8V proxies, modern-Earth O$_2$ reaches 5$\sigma$ in 9, 15, and 20 transits, respectively, whereas CH$_4$ requires 11, 12, and 28 transits at 3.9 Ga and only 2–4 transits across the oxygenated epochs. These results reveal a strong O$_2$ detectability contrast around M and K-dwarfs, but not Sun-like hosts in the YJH bands. Robust biosignature interpretation requires complementary molecular constraints, including informative non-detections, within the context of atmospheric evolution and stellar environment.

19. SN 2025dys: A luminous supernova explosion from a partially stripped helium star[2610.41729]
Abstract

We present well-sampled multi-band imaging and low- to medium-resolution spectroscopic observations of the early-discovered Type II supernova (SN) 2025dys. The photometric evolution exhibits a rise time of $\sim$ 18 days ($r$ band) and a rapid decline transitioning to the radioactive decay tail at $\sim$ 70 days. SN 2025dys is among the high-luminosity events with a peak pseudo-bolometric luminosity of $L_{\rm opt}=8.57\times10^{42}\,\mathrm{erg\,s^{-1}}$. Early X-ray emission was identified, displaying a luminosity of $7.55\times10^{40}\,\mathrm{erg\,s^{-1}}$. The observational investigation points to a dense, hydrogen-rich circumstellar medium with a mass of $M_{\rm CSM}\approx0.5$–$0.7\,\mathrm{M_\odot}$, suggesting a partially stripped, hydrogen-poor inner ejecta with a relatively low mass of $M_{\rm ej}\approx2.5\,\mathrm{M_\odot}$. These physical parameters indicate an extreme mass-loss episode before the explosion, characterized by a high mass-loss rate of $\dot{M} \gtrsim 0.01\,\mathrm{M_\odot\,yr^{-1}}$. SN 2025dys represents a stripped progenitor, intrinsically resembling a Type IIb or even a Type Ib SN, that exploded within a dense, hydrogen-rich circumstellar environment.

20. Long-term variability of the 35-day cycle in Her X-1[2610.41734]
Abstract

Her X-1 is an eclipsing X-ray binary system consisting of an accreting neutron star and an optical component HZ Her. In addition to the 1.24-s X-ray pulsations and the 1.7-d orbital period, the system also shows a 35-day X-ray variability related to the precessional motion of a tilted and twisted accretion disk around the neutron star. The long-term persistence of the 35-day X-ray variability suggests the presence of a stable clock mechanism, most likely free precession of the magnetized neutron star. We have studied the long-term behavior of the 35-day cycle of Her X-1 using all available CGRO/BATSE, RXTE/ASM, Swift/BAT, Fermi/GBM, MAXI data and archival turn-on moments of the 35-day cycle spanning more than 50 years. We conclude that the decay of the 35-day period as inferred from the O-C analysis can be interpreted in terms of the direct relationship between the change of the precessional period of the accretion disk to the long-term decrease of the period of the free precessional neutron star - due to continued accumulation of accreted matter.

21. Gamma-Ray Emission from LS 5039: A 30-500 GeV Excess and Phase-Resolved Broadband Spectral Modeling[2610.41739]
Abstract

LS 5039 is a well-studied $\gamma$-ray binary exhibiting strong orbital modulation from GeV to TeV energies. We present a phase-resolved analysis of 17.3 yr of \textit{Fermi}-LAT observations and identify an additional $\gamma$-ray excess in the $\sim30$–$500$ GeV at a significance of $\sim7.9\sigma$. The phase-resolved spectra show pronounced orbital dependence above $\sim10$ GeV, with the inferior-conjunction more complex than the superior-conjunction. To investigate the broadband spectral energy distribution, we combine the \textit{Fermi}-LAT spectra with published H.E.S.S.\ and HAWC measurements and compare purely leptonic and lepto-hadronic models using the Bayesian information criterion. In both intervals, relativistic-Maxwellian and magnetospheric components dominate below $\sim10$ GeV, while shock-accelerated electrons account for much of the GeV–TeV continuum. The purely leptonic fits leave residuals at $30$–$500$ GeV and underpredict the HAWC measurements above $\sim100$ TeV. In the local one-zone intrabinary shock apex model, synchrotron cooling and Klein-Nishina suppression make the ultra-high-energy emission difficult to reproduce, although more complex leptonic scenarios remain possible. Adding a phenomenological proton component improves the broadband description, with stronger relative support during superior-conjunction than inferior-conjunction. However, the proton parameters remain weakly constrained and the fast-escape hadronic interpretation is energetically challenging, so a hadronic origin is not uniquely established. Future high-sensitivity, phase-resolved observations at multi-TeV and ultra-high energies will be required to test this interpretation.

22. Inferring dominant acceleration mechanisms using the longitudinal spread of solar particle intensities: 28 May 2021 event[2610.41749]
Abstract

Solar energetic particles (SEPs) are typically accelerated during solar eruptions and propagate along magnetic field lines in the inner heliosphere. These eruptions include shock waves driven by coronal mass ejections (CMEs) and magnetic reconnection in solar flares. Disentangling these source mechanisms within the measurements has been a long-standing problem for space physics researchers.
