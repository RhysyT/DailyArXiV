1. Early X-ray emission as an observational diagnostic of gamma-ray burst progenitors[2611.12945]
Abstract

Identifying the progenitors of gamma-ray bursts (GRBs) from the properties of their early high-energy emission detected by wide-field satellites is crucial for advancing our understanding of GRB physics and for multi-messenger astronomy. Current classification methods, which rely primarily on prompt emission properties, exhibit large scatter and are challenged by recently detected events. In this Letter, we propose a new method to classify GRB progenitors using their early X-ray emission, which is detectable by current wide-field X-ray monitors. We performed a systematic time-resolved spectral analysis of the X-ray steep decay phase for a sample of 23 collapsar-driven GRB candidates detected by the Neil Gehrels Swift Observatory. We fitted the spectra over the $0.3 - 150 \, \rm keV$ energy range using both a physical synchrotron model and the empirical Band function, allowing us to constrain the spectral peak energy and the bolometric flux. We find a correlation between the rest-frame synchrotron cooling frequency, $\nu_{c,z}$, and the isotropic-equivalent luminosity, $L_{\rm iso}$, that is clearly separated from the $\nu_{c,z} - L_{\rm iso}$ relation followed by merger-driven GRB candidates. A similarly distinct separation is found in the correlation between the rest-frame peak energy, $E_{p,z}$, and $L_{\rm iso}$ obtained with the Band model. Finally, we provide a prescription for classifying fast X-ray transients detected by the Wide-field X-ray Telescope aboard the Einstein Probe mission, as its narrow energy range ($0.5 - 4 \, \rm keV$) limits spectral modelling to a simple power law. We show that the photon index and X-ray luminosity of the steep decay spectra clearly separate collapsar- and merger-driven candidate events, making them powerful diagnostics of GRB progenitors.

2. Standalone Validation of a Pulsar Evolution Subroutine for NBODY6++GPU: Seven Test Scenarios[2611.12973]
Abstract

Pulsars in globular clusters provide critical constraints on the evolution of compact objects. However, the treatment of neutron stars in standard N-body codes is outdated, and requires a better implementation of magnetic field decay, spin evolution, and accretion driven recycling that shape their observable properties. We present a standalone validation of a pulsar evolution subroutine for NBODY6++GPU, systematically tested across seven controlled scenarios prior to integration into full cluster simulations. These scenarios isolate distinct evolutionary regimes, including young pulsar spin down, millisecond pulsar weak torque evolution, accretion driven recycling in X-ray binaries, environmental torques in dense cores, wide binary systems, and compact binaries in the dipole spin-down regime. Through two separate numerical implementations, we achieve cross validation demonstrating better than 0.1% agreement across all physical quantities and timescales. Our validation confirms that analytical solutions are reproduced with high precision for isolated pulsars, gigayear scale evolution (tau_c   1.0 Gyr) matches observations of millisecond pulsars in clusters like 47 Tucanae, and accretion driven recycling operates with physically realistic timescales, yielding a final period of 1.0 ms and a period derivative of approximately -5.9e-19 s/s, with accretion dominance by a factor of about 14. We demonstrate that environmental torques remain negligible even in the densest cluster environments, while compact binary spin down is validated; gravitational wave orbital decay is deferred to future work. All validation codes, test suites, and integration instructions are publicly available to support future applications.

3. The Ordinary Days of Extraordinary Blazar 3C 454.3[2611.13035]
Abstract

The blazar 3C 454.3 (z = 0.859) is one of the brightest and most variable flat-spectrum radio quasars in the sky. Here, we present a multi-wavelength analysis of this enigmatic object in its quiescent states during June 2022 and May 2023 coincident with its first X-ray polarimetric and hard X-ray observations with International X-ray Polarimetric Explorer (IXPE) and Nuclear Spectroscopic Telescope Array (NuSTAR). Though no X-ray polarization was detected, NuSTAR detected the source on all occasions during this period. We generated five broadband spectral energy distributions (SEDs)of the blazar using quasi-simultaneous observations taken with the Fermi-Large Area Telescope, NuSTAR, and Neil Gehrels Swift Observatory. The hard X-ray flux measured by NuSTAR exhibited mild flux variations. The SEDs were modeled with a one-zone leptonic emission framework using the JetSet package. The optical-ultraviolet spectrum of 3C 454.3 revealed an upturn which we reproduced with the standard accretion disk model. The X-ray to gamma-ray spectrum of 3C 454 was found to be well explained by the external Compton (EC) process with the seed photons provided by the BLR and accretion disk. These results provide intriguing clues about the multi-wavelength behavior of 3C 454.3 during its quiescent activity state.

4. The Impact of a Magnetic Field on stochastically generated g-modes[2611.13082]
Abstract

Magnetic fields have been proposed as a mechanism for suppressing low-frequency gravity modes in stellar interiors, providing a means of constraining internal magnetic field strengths. However, previous suppression criteria have largely relied on linear theory. Here, we use two-dimensional nonlinear magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) simulations in a core convecting star to investigate the interaction of gravity waves with magnetic fields of varying strengths and geometries. Using both global and local field geometries, we find that amplitude reduction is gradual and frequency dependent, with low-frequency modes preferentially attenuated while higher-frequency modes can be amplified. In order to achieve significant suppression we require a magnetic field strength at least an order of magnitude larger than predicted by commonly used linear criteria. Its likely that this difference arises because magnetic fields can themselves excite waves through Maxwell stresses, offsetting magnetic attenuation. Our results demonstrate that strong magnetic modifications to wave propagation do not necessarily result in equivalent suppression of the global oscillation spectrum. The linear criteria typically used to infer field strengths based on mode suppression does not capture all relevant physics and should be used with care.

5. Less is More: Unbiased Galaxy Clustering from Fewer Merger Trees[2611.13083]
Abstract

Galaxy clustering could strongly constrain galaxy formation models but is rarely used, because the two-point correlation function (2PCF) requires a complete galaxy catalogue at every point in parameter space. Semi-analytic models (SAMs) process each halo merger tree independently, so running a SAM on $m$ of $k$ disjoint partitions of the trees, each spanning the full box, cuts the cost by $k/m$. Because a halo's galaxies are kept or discarded together, one-halo pairs are diluted less than two-halo pairs, and the naive estimate overstates small-scale clustering by a factor $k/m$. We introduce the Sparse and Unbiased Clustering Estimator (SaUCE), which removes this bias with weights set by $m$ and $k$ alone. It has no free parameters, gives exactly unbiased pair counts, and applies to any catalogue built from a random subset of whole haloes and to any pair-count statistic. Running a SAM on 7 per cent of the P-Millennium merger trees, 68 per cent of random selections recover the full-box $\xi(r)$ at $z=0$ to within 0.05 dex in each bin from 0.03 to $30\,h^{-1}\,\mathrm{Mpc}$, at a density of $10^{-2}\,h^{3}\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-3}$: a fourteen-fold computational saving. The required fraction rises with redshift and as the number density falls, is the same for two model variants, and transfers between boxes through the galaxy and pair counts. The scatter, derived in closed form, is strongly correlated between bins, so a likelihood must use the full covariance. A recipe for choosing the fraction makes the 2PCF affordable for model calibration and simulation-based inference.

6. Unidentified Sources in the 4FGL-DR4 Fermi-LAT Catalog as Potential Dark Matter Satellites[2611.13095]
Abstract

The nature of dark matter (DM) remains unknown, standing as one of the most pressing questions in modern physics. We focus on indirect DM detection via gamma-ray emission from annihilating weakly interacting massive particles (WIMPs). Within the $\Lambda$CDM cosmological framework, the hierarchical assembly of structure predicts a rich population of DM halos and subhalos. If WIMPs compose DM, annihilation inside these subhalos would generate gamma rays that could be detectable by instruments such as the Fermi-LAT. Since 2008, Fermi-LAT has cataloged several thousand of gamma-ray sources, with a third lacking clear astrophysical association. Some of these unassociated sources (unIDs) may actually be dark satellites, i.e., Galactic subhalos not massive enough to retain gas or stars and thus with no counterparts at other wavelengths. Starting from the 14-year Fermi-LAT catalog (4FGL-DR4), we conduct a systematic search for dark satellite candidates among its 2428 unIDs. We first design and apply a series of filters based on expected properties of dark satellites to reduce our sample. Through an exhaustive spectral Fermi-LAT data analysis, we find that none of the remaining unIDs shows a statistically significant preference for a DM interpretation. We place conservative constraints on the $\langle\sigma v\rangle-m_{\chi}$ DM parameter space, which are a factor of 2-3 above the thermal relic cross section value at the lowest WIMP masses in a conservative scenario, and rule out thermally-produced WIMPs below $\sim$50 GeV in the $b\bar{b}$ channel when assuming the sensitivity reach of the method. Our results emphasize the distinctive value of unID-based searches: they yield constraints that complement those derived from classical DM targets, such as dwarf spheroidal galaxies, and probe a unique, cosmologically motivated population of dark satellites otherwise inaccessible to observations.

7. Fast Scrambling Cosmology[2611.13100]
Abstract

Quantum gravity arguments such as trans-Planckian censorship suggest that de Sitter space can only last for a time of order $H^{-1}\ln(M_{\rm Pl}/H)$, corresponding to the fast-scrambling time of black hole and cosmological horizons. We ask whether this clock leaves an imprint on cosmic expansion, via a minimal modification of the Raychaudhuri equation that adds a term $-\Gamma H^2/\ln(M_{\rm Pl}^2/H^2)$ to $\dot H$, with $\Gamma={\cal O}(1)$. The slowly varying $\sigma\equiv\Gamma/\ln(M_{\rm Pl}^2/H^2)\ll 1$ is nearly constant at late times. De Sitter space is replaced by a slowly evolving vacuum (an exact Lambert-$W$ solution), and for constant $\sigma$ the expansion rate is found in closed form for arbitrary matter sources. The correction acts as dark energy with two components: a vacuum mode with $w=-1+2\sigma/3$, and a term that integrates over the history of the cosmic fluids, so that any change in the equation of state of a species leaves a permanent residual; e.g., neutrinos becoming non-relativistic shift the dark energy density by ${\cal O}(\sigma m_\nu^4)$. A covariant clock-field completion reproduces this background, and a term that rescales the gravitational constant governing cosmic expansion removes its short-wavelength instability in the dark-energy era. Big Bang nucleosynthesis constraints give a proxy bound $\Gamma=-3.7\pm6.1$ (68% confidence), while the negative-$\Gamma$ branch qualitatively follows the $w_0w_a$CDM dark energy evolution inferred from DESI DR2 at $z\gtrsim1$. The early-time tracking terms amount to a similar rescaling, for which existing fits to cosmic microwave background (CMB), baryon acoustic oscillation (BAO) and supernova data translate into the proxy estimate $\Gamma\simeq-2.8\pm0.9$ ($\sigma\simeq-0.01$ today). These background-level results motivate a dedicated likelihood analysis of structure formation in this theory.

8. Accretion variability as a probe of black holes and their environments: Light curve signatures of a single hot spot[2611.13109]
Abstract

The compact radio source at the centre of the Milky Way, Sagittarius A*, exhibits temporal broadband brightness variability. The observed timescales of such fluctuations, ranging from minutes to hours on horizon scales, arise from the complex interplay of gravitation, plasma, and light propagation processes in the immediate vicinity of the supermassive black hole. Kinetic and fluid simulations associate this variability with turbulence and the formation of magnetised, localised plasma structures. Focusing on a single fluctuating compact emission region, a 'hot spot', we study how intrinsic source fluctuations map onto different regions of the observer's screen. Our aim is to identify distinct imprints of light propagation effects, which encode information both about the source and the underlying space-time geometry. We simulate image time series by propagating the emitted light to a distant observer's sky plane in Minkowski, Schwarzschild, and Kerr space-times. We then compute the corresponding observed light curves and power spectra, both for the full observed images and for selected angular sectors of the observer's sky plane. We derive a semi-analytical model for the power spectrum of a fluctuating hot spot in flat space-time, reproducing all qualitative features of curved-space-time spectra at moderate inclinations. Moreover, we describe how such power spectra are affected by space-time geometry, inclination, and source velocity, and show that orthogonal two-sector angular decompositions of the sky plane provide information on system orientation and lensing effects. The physical effects highlighted in this paper are expected to be present in the timing signature of accretion-flow variability, as observed by current instruments and future photometric and interferometric facilities. Further work is needed to investigate if our conclusions hold for realistic disc configurations.

9. Planetary material is a major source of hydrogen in helium-atmosphere white dwarfs[2611.13128]
Abstract

Many white dwarfs with helium-dominated atmospheres show traces of hydrogen in their spectra. On average, the total mass of hydrogen within their convective envelopes increases with age, but there is no consensus on the origin of such hydrogen. Hypotheses include upward diffusion from hidden reservoirs deep within the envelope, or accretion, alongside heavier elements, of planetary material. We construct a simple model that includes both pathways, and test it using two independent samples of helium-atmosphere DB white dwarfs from the DESI and SDSS spectroscopic surveys, selected to have Gaia colours BP-RP > -0.25, corresponding roughly to 10000 < $T_{\rm eff}$ < 19000K. We find that at least $22 \pm 2$ per cent of the helium-atmosphere white dwarfs with detectable hydrogen must have accreted some of it from their planetary systems, via ices, hydrated minerals or atmospheres. We thus demonstrate that planetary hydrogen is a necessary ingredient to explain the spectral evolution of white dwarfs as a population, rather than only extreme hydrogen-enriched cases.

10. Searching for Planets around Altair with JWST/NIRcam[2611.13132]
Abstract

Altair (alpha Aql) is one of the nearest stars to the Sun at 5.13 pc and its age has recently been revised to 88+/-10 Myr, making it one of the youngest stars in the Solar neighborhood. This makes Altair a compelling target for high-contrast direct imaging searches for giant planets. We present two epochs of simultaneous F444W and F200W MASK430R JWST/NIRCam coronagraphic imaging observations. After post-processing, these data achieve F444W contrast levels of 2 x 10^-6 at 1" (5.1 au), 1.5 x 10^-7 at 2" (10.3 au), and a few x 10^-8 beyond 4"(20 au). Using the Sonora Flame Skimmer models, this corresponds to sub-Jupiter mass sensitivity beyond 5 au and sub-Saturn mass sensitivity beyond 10 au. We identified several candidate point sources in the field in the first epoch data. Second epoch observations confirmed that none of the candidates were co-moving with Altair and their colors are consistent with background stars. Altair has no known companions, no detected debris disk, and is not associated with any stellar association. Despite this, its youth and proximity ensure it will remain a high-priority target for future high-contrast imaging observations. We additionally discuss Altair as a potential reference star for future observations, presenting measurements of background sources in the field and showing that Altair will remain at least a few arcsec from background stars over the next 10-20 years, making it a reliable reference target for upcoming imaging campaigns including the Roman Space Telescope Coronagraph Instrument.

11. Near-Ultraviolet and Optical Observations of the White Dwarf ZTF J0328$-$1219 Reveal Variable Periodicity in its Transiting Debris System[2611.13136]
Abstract

We observed the transiting debris disk system around the white dwarf ZTF J0328$-$1219 using the Hubble Space Telescope (HST) in 2025 October, obtaining near-ultraviolet (NUV) spectroscopy with STIS over five consecutive HST orbits. Our abundance analysis supports the accretion of a chondritic body. We complemented our HST program with 16 days of optical time-series photometry beginning in 2025 October, which reveal variable periodicity and rapidly evolving transit morphology. Previous monitoring by TESS during Sectors 4 and 31 confirmed a predominant, stable transit orbital period, $P_{\rm A} = 9.937$ hr, along with a second, transient period, $P_{\rm B} = 11.2$ hr. Our 2025 autumn light curves show transits are dominated by $P_{\rm B} = 11.17$ hr with $P_{\rm A}$ initially appearing as a weaker, secondary feature and eventually returning to predominance. We also identify a candidate transit period of 7.64 hr during the final days of the 2025 autumn campaign, although its significance and persistence remain uncertain. We add simultaneous $ugr$ light curves collected with ULTRACAM in 2024 November that are dominated by $P_{\rm A}$ and show no significant color term. We further compare the transit depths in optical photometry concurrent with our NUV observations, again measuring a lack of transit-dependent extinction except during the fifth HST orbit where a reddening term may arise. The observed variable periodicity and potentially changing color behavior illuminate ongoing dynamical processes, namely collisions, and demonstrate the opportunity for studying dust physics in real time at white dwarf debris disks.

12. Identification of chromospheric dynamical signatures in the 2022 December 27 solar flare with DKIST[2611.13147]
Abstract

Spectroscopic observations by the Daniel K. Inouye Solar Telescope (DKIST) with unprecedented capabilities provide new insights into the evolving fine structure in the chromosphere during solar flares. We analyzed Ca II 8542Å spectra, formed in the middle chromosphere, of a minor flare observed by DKIST's Visible Spectropolarimeter in a single raster lasting 1.5 hours. We characterized the dynamic features in the spectra, then identified the spatial scales and context of these features during the evolution of flare heating. To do so, we developed a multi-layer, hierarchical clustering algorithm, with which 17,422 line profiles of flaring spectra were characterized into 19 clusters of similar spectral features. We then examined the spatial structure of the spectral clusters and also the time when the spectra were observed with respect to the local heating history inferred from observations by the Atmosphere Imaging Assembly. The spectral clusters form a set of spatially coherent structures, comprised of a central kernel with an enveloping halo. The smallest kernel size that is identified has a width of $\le 400$ km. Furthermore, spectral clusters in the pore and plage exhibit distinct properties such as kernel size, line shift, and spatial variation, which may be a result of different background atmosphere, and may also reflect different stage of local heating. High-resolution observations with improved cadence can further our understanding of the evolution of chromospheric fine structure.

13. Detectability of Seyfert galaxies in point-source and stacking searches with a global network of neutrino telescopes[2611.13247]
Abstract

Recent evidence for high-energy neutrino emission from NGC 1068 and other nearby Seyfert galaxies suggests that active galactic nuclei (AGNs) may constitute a class of astrophysical neutrino sources. Using a physically motivated neutrino emission model based on stochastic particle acceleration in magnetized AGN coronae, we investigate the detection prospects of X-ray-bright Seyfert galaxies with a detector network comprising IceCube, Baikal-GVD, KM3NeT/ARCA, and P-ONE. We find that the Circinus Galaxy is the most promising point-source target in the Southern sky and will be detected at the 5$\sigma$ level by 2038 at the latest, provided that the Seyfert population accounts for the diffuse astrophysical neutrino flux up to 100 TeV and Circinus is at least as efficient a neutrino emitter as the average source in the population. Individual detections of other nearby Seyferts are expected to remain challenging, making stacking searches informed by X-ray observations the most promising approach for discovering them within the next decade. Our results demonstrate that joint analyses across multiple neutrino telescopes, together with future MeV gamma-ray observations, will be essential for establishing Seyfert galaxies as astrophysical neutrino sources and provide a means for testing the coronal origin of their neutrino emission.

14. Understanding Coronal `Seeing' using High Angular Resolution Radio Observations of Active Solar Emissions[2611.13255]
Abstract

Solar radio bursts are generated through the plasma emission mechanism by beams of accelerated electrons travelling along magnetic field lines. They serve as powerful probes of the ambient coronal plasma and magnetic fields. At low frequencies, however, propagation through the turbulent, magnetised coronal plasma strongly modifies their observed properties. Disentangling these propagation effects from the intrinsic nature of the emission therefore remains a major challenge. Despite the long history of observations, there are only a few that leverage high-angular-resolution imaging. This is primarily due to the prevailing expectations that scattering from coronal density inhomogeneities significantly increases the apparent source size, obscuring any fine-scale structures that might be present. While small-scale features are routinely observed in EUV and X-rays, few are detected at meter wavelengths. Numerous studies have focused on quantifying the effect of scattering on the observed apparent size of a point solar radio source. Naturally, their results vary significantly depending on the specifics of the model used to describe coronal inhomogeneities and the scattering prescription applied – ranging from sub-arcseconds to arcminutes. Recent high angular resolution observations with the Square Kilometre Array Observatory (SKAO) -pathfinders, uGMRT and LOFAR, have revealed unexpectedly compact sources, with sizes as small as $\sim9$ arcseconds in type-I noise storms. Forthcoming high-fidelity and angular resolution SKAO observations will transform our understanding of solar radio emissions by constraining scattering models, improving coronal diagnostics, and revealing fine structures crucial for understanding solar radio emissions and propagation effects.

15. Cryohydrologic Flow and Pingo Formation Within Freezing Impact Melts in Occator Crater, Ceres[2611.13299]
Abstract

Ceres is a volatile-rich dwarf planet in the asteroid belt and a potential relict ocean world. It exhibits surface features indicative of subsurface brine activity, particularly within Occator Crater. High-resolution imagery from NASA's Dawn Mission reveals landslides, gullies, carbonate faculae, and mound-like features resembling terrestrial pingos on the floor of Occator Crater. Some of these landforms have been hypothesized to form from water, either from the ascent of deep crustal brines or from the freezeout of impact melt. Here, we test the hypothesis that pingo-like features can be formed via the freezing of shallow impact melts. We simulate the heat transfer and groundwater flow dynamics of a freezing impact melt deposit on the floor of Occator Crater. We test a comprehensive parameter space of regolith physical and material properties relevant to the cerean environment. We find that impact melt freezinf produces pressure gradients sufficient to drive hydrologic flow within the melt matrix and could transport fluids to the near surface through hydraulic fracturing. For most of the parameter space tested, water volumes delivered to pingo candidates on the surface meet or exceed those likely required for pingo formation. Analogous hydrologic processes may be important in the shallow subsurface of other regions of Occator Crater and Ceres at large as well as other ice-rich planetary bodies.

16. The climate and potential habitability of a warm and oxygenated Mars[2611.13321]
Abstract

We present the first 3-D Global Climate Model simulations of an oxygen-rich, terraformed Mars. Assuming an O2-CO2 atmosphere, with additional warming from a well-mixed grey gas, we study (1) how surface temperatures change with atmospheric pressure and grey gas opacity, and (2) how temperature, pressure, and other effects limit life as oxygen builds up. We neglect the climate effects of dust and the water cycle, as well as possible soil-chemistry and nutrient limits to life. We find that (1) increasing grey gas opacity and increasing atmospheric pressure both moderate day-night temperature swings, but only slightly reduce seasonal temperature swings. Climate remains harsher in the Southern mid-latitudes relative to the Northern hemisphere. (2) For an atmospheric pressure of 30 hPa (5 times its present-day value), temperatures on Mars would be consistent with lichen and moss, followed at 60 hPa by forests (initially in the Hellas Basin). However, creating a human-breathable atmosphere through photosynthesis alone, at the oxygen production rates of biomes on Earth today, would take at least thousands of years, and require more oxygen than can be made via photosynthesis of known CO2 reserves on Mars alone (unless CO2 is recycled).

17. A TESS Disappearing Act: The Vanishing Transits of TOI-2535 b[2611.13343]
Abstract

Planetary transits require a fortuitous alignment between a planet's orbit and our line of sight, but dynamical interactions can render this geometric coincidence only temporary. We report the discovery of the hot Jupiter TOI-2535 b, the first confirmed planet orbiting a single star whose transits have been directly observed to disappear. Over seven years of photometry from NASA's Transiting Exoplanet Survey Satellite (TESS), the planet's transit chord traversed and eventually moved off the stellar disk entirely, with an impact parameter changing at the rate of $\dot{b} = 0.16\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}$. The transit mid-times also show significant departures of $\pm 6$ hours from a linear ephemeris. Joint modeling of the transit timing and duration variations (TTVs and TDVs) and NEID radial-velocity data indicates the presence of a nearby, mutually inclined ($i_\mathrm{mut} \gtrsim 5^\circ$) planetary perturber, with the TDV being primarily due to precession of the planets' orbital planes. Our dynamical modeling reveals that the perturber is likely of Saturn-mass or greater and in a mean-motion resonance with TOI-2535 b, making this an unusual system that contains two short-period giant planets. The TDVs measured for TOI-2535 b represent one of the most extreme examples of planet-planet interactions driving orbital evolution on human timescales.

18. Early–Narrow and Late–Wide Populations of GRB X-ray Flares[2611.13350]
Abstract

The physical origin of gamma-ray burst (GRB) X-ray flares remains debated, particularly whether they arise from prolonged central-engine activity or from variability in the external shock. Building on our previous analysis of 89 Swift GRBs with measured redshifts, we investigate the rest-frame temporal properties of X-ray flares detected in 61 bursts. We apply a multivariate Gaussian Mixture Model to rest-frame peak times and widths. We identify two statistically distinct populations: early-narrow flares (80%), with characteristic peak times of approximately 10^2 s and w/t_pk <= 1, and late-wide flares (20%), with characteristic peak times of   10^3.5 s and w/t_pk >= 2. Of the 61 single or first flares, 55 belong to the early-narrow population and only 6 to the late-wide population. Of the 36 second flares, 23 (64%) belong to the early-narrow population, with shorter waiting times, whereas 13 (36%) belong to the late-wide population, with substantially longer waiting times. The width-peak-time correlation remains significant for early-narrow flares but not for late-wide flares. Furthermore, we find no correlation between flare width and energy. We find a decline of flare luminosity with peak time, driven primarily by the early-narrow population, but not statistically significant for late-wide flares. These results imply that a simple shift in time, potentially associated with later episodes of central-engine activity, cannot explain the distinction between the two flare populations. On the other hand, the peak times are consistent with expectations from an accretion disk formed following the collapse of a stellar-size massive star. Combined with our previous finding that flare properties are independent of the presence of an X-ray plateau, these results favor prolonged accretion-driven central-engine activity over external-shock variability as the primary origin of X-ray flares.

Abstract

The flux of pulsed emission from the Crab pulsar, just like most of other pulsars, is considered to be steady over time scales much larger than the pulsation period, except for a smooth, secular decrease related to the aging of pulsars. Such a steady assumption prevails in all theoretical investigations of pulsar emission models. 20. Thermal-pulse-resolved Binary Population Synthesis with Detailed Stellar Models: Implications for Long-period WD+MS Formation[2611.13502]
Abstract

The origin of the long-orbital-period white dwarf–main-sequence (WD+MS) binaries revealed by Gaia challenges the traditional association of common-envelope evolution (CEE) with short orbital periods. We develop a thermal-pulse-resolved binary population synthesis framework based on 51 detailed MESA evolutionary tracks with initial masses of 1–6 $M_{\odot}$. Applying these tracks to $5\times10^6$ initially sampled binaries, we follow the time-dependent stellar radius, wind mass loss, angular momentum, and tidal interactions, while calculating the envelope binding energy directly from the donor structure at the onset of Roche-lobe overflow. We find that thermal pulses can trigger binary interaction through envelope expansion and tidal orbital shrinkage, while the weakly bound envelopes of highly evolved thermally pulsing asymptotic giant branch (TP-AGB) donors allow CE ejection with limited orbital contraction. For our standard model, with $\alpha_{\mathrm{CE}}$ = 0.3 and $\alpha_{\mathrm{th}}$ = 0.5, systems with orbital periods above 30 days constitute approximately 11.5\% of the resulting TP-AGB-initiated post-CE binaries (PCEBs). Long-period PCEBs can form across a large range of CE parameters and can reach periods of several hundred days even without any internal-energy contribution when $\alpha_{\mathrm{CE}}$ = 1. Their WD masses peak near 0.6 $M_\odot$, whereas systems containing more massive WDs are rare. Moreover, long-period PCEBs closely follow the single-star initial–final mass relation, providing a potential diagnostic of their formation channel in clusters, where progenitor masses can be independently constrained. Our results identify late TP-AGB CEE as a viable contributor to the observed long-period WD+MS population.

21. A Possible Non-Transiting Companion in the TOI-2109 System Supported by Transit Timing Variations[2611.13505]
Abstract

Transit timing variations are a powerful method for detecting non-transiting planets, particularly in systems near mean-motion resonances. We investigate the ultra-short-period hot Jupiter TOI-2109 b using 87 transit epochs spanning two decades (2006-2025), combining observations from TESS, CHEOPS, HST, and ground-based photometry. We compare five timing models representing different physical scenarios: a linear ephemeris, orbital decay, apsidal precession, a sinusoidal model, and a two-planet n-body dynamical model. The orbital-decay fit yields $\dot{P} = -3.96 \pm 2.05$ ms yr$^{-1}$, providing no significant evidence for ongoing orbital decay. The sinusoidal model reveals a periodic transit-timing-variation signal with an amplitude of $\approx 44\,s$. Dynamical modelling favours an additional non-transiting companion near the 7:3 mean-motion resonance with an orbital period of $1.57813\pm 8.9\times10^{-5}$ days and a mass of $0.70 \pm 0.02\,M_{\rm Jup}$. The two-planet model yields $\Delta \mathrm{BIC}=4.95$ relative to the sinusoidal model, indicating positive evidence that the observed transit timing variations are best explained by gravitational perturbations from an additional planet.

22. AGN feedback destroying group cool cores — a case study on IC 2476 with a remnant radio AGN and an exceptionally low abundance[2611.13506]
Abstract

We present results from deep XMM-Newton observations ($\sim$180 ksec) of the galaxy group IC 2476 in the X-GAP sample, capturing a rare AGN feedback regime, where the central radio AGN has ceased activity but the radio lobes are still detectable. The hot intragroup medium of IC 2476 features a compact cool core, with a size of  10 kpc, and a moderate X-ray luminosity of $\sim3.4\times10^{40}$ erg/s. We measured an unusually low temperature of $\sim$0.45 keV within the cool core. We measured an exceptionally low abundance of below  0.2 solar, even in the central region. The X-ray surface brightness of the group is not symmetric and shows a clear depression in the region overlaid with the eastern radio lobe, suggesting the presence of an X-ray cavity. The scaled entropy of the hot gas within 0.1 r500 of IC 2476 is among the highest in X-ray galaxy groups with a similar system temperature. Our study suggests that the past powerful radio AGN outburst in IC 2476 has disrupted the cool core and the X-ray cavity has dissipated or mixed with the surrounding hot gas since the shutdown of the central AGN. A large cool core may be gradually re-established in the absence of AGN activity. This system therefore provides a great example of AGN feedback destroying the group cool core, even with a moderate radio luminosity at present.

23. OASIS: A Fast Dynamical Halo Finding Algorithm[2611.13542]
Abstract

In recent work (García et al. 2023), we advocated for defining a dynamical halo as the collection of particles that orbit their self-generated potential. 97. Constraints on Complex Electroweak WIMPs from Solar Capture[2609.38316]
Abstract

One of the most convincing realizations of particle dark matter is provided by models based on electroweak multiplets. The lightest neutral state of such a multiplet can be a stable Majorana fermion or a real scalar, and its phenomenology is governed by two parameters: the dark matter mass and the mass splitting $\{m_\chi,\delta\}$. The sub-MeV range for $\delta$ is particularly important as it allows for a large inelastic scattering cross section. In this paper, we analyze solar capture, energy loss, and annihilation of scalar and fermionic electroweak multiplet dark matter, generalizing earlier results for the Higgsino to other representations. We find that, while the case of a doublet is somewhat special, higher representations behave in a self-similar way. For the higher multiplets, IceCube results constrain the splitting to $\delta \gtrsim 350$ keV for all dark matter masses up to $100$ TeV. Assuming thermal freeze-out and a Standard Halo Model velocity distribution, this rules out most models of electroweak dark matter as a possible origin of the single high-recoil event reported by the LZ collaboration. We also show that a high-velocity tail in the dark matter distribution, such as that induced by the Large Magellanic Cloud, raises the splitting preferred by the LZ event above the solar bounds for many of the multiplets.

98. Two-Fluid Radial Stability of Dark-Matter-Admixed Compact Objects: Dark Compact Planets, Dark White Dwarfs, Dark Neutron Stars, and Dark Oysters[2610.41082]
Abstract

We investigate the equilibrium structure and stability against radial oscillations of a variety of two-fluid compact objects containing ordinary baryonic matter admixed with cold dark matter coupled through the background spacetime. For the ordinary fluid, we employ a QCD equation of state compatible with chiral effective field theory at nuclear densities (supplemented by the BPS and Negele-Vautherin models at extremely low densities) and with perturbative QCD in the high-density regime while also satisfying also current astrophysical data. For the dark fluid, we assume self-interacting fermionic matter with particle masses of $m_f=$ 2, 5, 10, and 100 GeV and two self-interaction strengths of $y=0.1$ and $10^3$. By scanning a large range of central pressures for both fluids, we identify the presence of dark compact planets, dark white dwarfs, dark neutron stars and the so-called dark oysters. Importantly, these objects are not only in two-fluid hydrostatic equilibrium, but they are also stable against two-fluid radial oscillations. We find that the relative representation of each class in the radially stable sample is strongly sensitive to the combinations of $m_f$ and $y$, with some combinations entirely suppressing some of these object classes within the sampled domain while favoring others. In addition to these classes, strong self-repulsion supports stable, dark-matter-dominated branches with compact ordinary-matter cores and extended dark halos, approaching the self-interacting fermion-star limit. Finally, we calculate the gravitational redshift at the ordinary-matter surface, $z_{\rm OM}$, including the contribution of extended dark-matter halos for which we found radially stable configurations with $z_{\rm OM}>2$.
