1. A Misaligned Debris Disk relative to the Host Star CPD-72 2713 from JWST/NIRCam Imaging[2610.20700]
Abstract

Observations of debris disks provide a valuable window to investigate the formation and evolution of planetary systems. We present high-contrast JWST/NIRCam observations of CPD-72 2713, a 24 Myr-old star that belongs to the $\beta$ Pic moving group. We obtained the first scattered-light images of the disk in both F210M and F444W filters, revealing a nearly face-on orientation. Forward modeling yields disk radii of $\rm 55.4^{+3.0}_{-3.2}\,AU$ (F210M) and $\rm 39.1^{+4.6}_ {-2.6}\,AU$ (F444W), both significantly smaller than the thermal emission radii measured with ALMA and Herschel/PACS. This discrepancy suggests that scattered light traces the inner region of the disk while thermal emission probes the outer regions. Combining the stellar rotation period ($4.47\pm0.67$ days) from TESS light curves and spectroscopic $v\sin i_*$, we constrained the stellar inclination to $47^{\circ}.2^{+11.1}_{-7.7}$ ($1\sigma$). The minimum star-disk mutual inclinations are $27^{\circ}.6 _{-8.2} ^{+11.5}\ (1\sigma)$ from F210M data and $27^{\circ}.3 _{-10.9}^{ +12.5}\ (1\sigma)$ in F444W data. We find that the minimum star–disk mutual inclination exceeds $10^\circ$ at the $95\%$ credible level in both F210M and F444W, suggesting a misalignment between the scattered-light disk and the stellar equatorial plane. We note that variation among published disk inclination measurements and potential systematic uncertainties may affect this probability. With JWST high-contrast imaging, we rule out planets down to $0.3\ \rm M_{J}$ at $0.5^{\prime\prime}$ and beyond, disfavoring a planet as the driver of the star-disk misalignment.

2. The unified Bound Dark Energy–Bound Dark Matter model: theory and constraints from DESI DR2[2610.20716]
Abstract

The unified Bound Dark Energy–Bound Dark Matter (BDE-BDM) model derives both the dark energy and the whole of the dark matter from a single supersymmetric $SU(3)$ gauge group with $N_f=6$ flavors. Gauge-coupling unification predicts the scale at which it condenses, $\Lc=34^{+16}_{-11}$ eV, before any cosmological data are used, so the dark-energy sector adds no parameter to those of \LCDM\ and the dark-matter sector adds only one. Fitting DESI DR2 BAO, Planck CMB, Planck+ACT DR6 CMB lensing, and three supernova compilations (PantheonPlus, Union3.1, DES-Dovekie) gives, for Union3.1, $\Lc=43.67\pm0.13$ eV, inside the predicted range: a high-energy prediction met by a late-time measurement. The equation of state evolves to $w_0=-0.9262\pm0.0005$ and never crosses the phantom divide. Relative to \LCDM\ the model lowers the DESI BAO $\chi^2$ by $2.95$–$3.67$ and the supernova $\chi^2$ by $2.53$–$4.07$, recovering $57\%$–$71\%$ of the total reduction that \wowa\ obtains by fitting two dark-energy parameters to the same data, and reaching the lower BAO $\chi^2$ of the two models for two of the three compilations. Its $\Delta\mathrm{DIC}$ against \LCDM\ is strong for every compilation, $-8.28$ to $-7.47$, varying by $0.81$ where that of \wowa\ varies by $4.8$. Because the dark-energy sector adds no parameter to those of \LCDM, the likelihood gain survives the Occam penalty: $\ln B=+4.09$, $+4.12$, and $+4.83$ against \LCDM, where \wowa\ gives $-2.11$, $-0.26$, and $-0.56$. Among six models pairing a cosmological constant, the \wowa\ parametrization or bound dark energy with cold or bound dark matter, BDE-BDM has the largest Bayesian evidence.

3. Nonlocal Gravity, Restricted CPL Dynamics and DESI Constraints[2610.20720]
Abstract

Nonlocal modifications of gravity provide a theoretically motivated framework in which cosmic acceleration can emerge dynamically without introducing a fundamental cosmological constant. We revisit the cosmological predictions of the Maggiore–Mancarella nonlocal $R\,\Box^{-2}R$ model, with particular emphasis on its background dynamics and observational viability in light of recent DESI baryon acoustic oscillation measurements. A key feature of the theory is its predictive character: for fixed standard cosmological parameters, each value of the nonlocal parameter determines a specific effective pair $(w_0,w_a)$, so that the model traces a restricted trajectory in the Chevallier–Polarski–Linder plane rather than spanning the full phenomenological parameter space. We construct this mapping explicitly and confront the model with observations by implementing its cosmological dynamics in a Boltzmann solver and performing a Markov chain Monte Carlo analysis using Cosmic Microwave Background, supernova, and DESI BAO data. We find that current datasets constrain the nonlocal parameter close to the $\Lambda$CDM limit, with no statistically significant preference for nonlocal deviations.

4. Compact Dark Matter with Globular Cluster Pulsars[2610.20732]
Abstract

Pulsars are exceptionally stable clocks, and a compact dark-matter object passing near the line of sight to a pulsar delays its pulses through the Shapiro effect. The number of detectable transits is set by the integral over the dark-matter density along the line of sight connecting the Earth and the pulsar. Established pulsars in pulsar timing arrays are typically within one kiloparsec of the Earth and hence sit in a dark-matter density comparable to our local value. In this work, we explore globular clusters in the inner Galaxy as targets to search for compact dark matter. These high-pulsar-density environments can be cheaply observed with a single telescope beam and enjoy a large dark-matter crossing rate. Additionally, since the recently detected nanohertz gravitational-wave background is nearly common to the cluster's pulsars, a multi-pulsar analysis can remove much of its contribution. Ranking known clusters, we find that Terzan 5, with 49 pulsars, offers the best reach. A forty-year campaign of weekly four-hour visits at the sensitivity envisioned for the second phase of the Square Kilometre Array (SKA) would probe compact objects making up $6\%$ of the dark matter at $0.02\,M_\odot$. Finally, we estimate the foreground from stars and stellar remnants in Terzan 5 and along the sightline. It is subdominant near the most sensitive low-mass part of the SKA benchmark, but becomes important over parts of the stellar mass range.

5. Beyond Mergers: the Role of Metallicity in Forming a Reservoir of Massive and Detached Binary Black Hole Systems[2610.20736]
Abstract

Isolated binary evolution is a key pathway to forming binary black hole (BBH) mergers. However, the observed gravitational-wave population indicates it may not account for all mergers, especially those with high primary black hole mass ($\gtrsim 30 M_{\odot}$). Using the binary population synthesis code \texttt{POSYDON}, we simulate populations of binary systems at eight metallicities from $10^{-4}$ to $2\,Z_\odot$ and classify each BH-forming binary as a merging BBH, a detached BBH, or a system disrupted during BH formation. We find that metallicity shapes these populations differently, especially for high BH masses ($\gtrsim 25 M_{\odot}$). At lower metallicities, between $10^{-2}$ and $0.45\,Z_\odot$, massive BHs continue to form but become increasingly unlikely to merge: the number of massive merging BBHs declines sharply, while the detached population remains abundant. For $25$–$35\,M_\odot$ BHs, the detached-to-merging ratio increases by more than two orders of magnitude across this metallicity range. After weighting by cosmic star formation, massive BHs are found predominantly in detached binaries with orbital periods $\gtrsim 10^2$ days. Therefore, isolated binary evolution can form massive BBHs even if it rarely produces massive BBH mergers, leaving behind a massive and detached population that may be driven to merger through dynamical interactions in dense stellar environments.

7. A model for the image structure and spectral energy distribution of relativistic jets[2610.20961]
Abstract

Upcoming advances in high-frequency VLBI promise to enable spatially resolved studies of astrophysical jets across an increasingly large source population. We present here a Python implementation of the semi-analytic model from Tsunetoe et al. (2025) for self-consistently interpreting the spatial and spectral structure of the synchrotron emission from such jets. The model is based on an axisymmetric force-free jet solution supplemented with a prescription for nonthermal electron heating, synchrotron cooling, pitch-angle anisotropy in the electron distribution, and radiative transfer along straight rays. We use this model to explore how the predicted spectral energy distribution depends on the model parameters, identifying approximately self-similar scalings with black hole mass, accretion rate, and electron heating efficiency. We also compare the model predictions against observations of M87 and Sgr A*. For M87, a single fiducial parameter set qualitatively reproduces VLBI images across multiple frequencies and matches the observed longitudinal intensity profile, while also yielding a broadband SED that is quantitatively consistent with the observed radio through X-ray measurements. For Sgr A*, a fit to the quiescent-state SED demonstrates that emission from a collimated jet with comparable nonthermal heating efficiency to M87's is quantitatively consistent with the observed radio through X-ray measurements, predicting a compact jet whose radio size decreases inversely with frequency, consistent with VLBI constraints.

8. Disentangling geometry & growth in cosmic neutrino mass constraints: a more physical split and diagnostic[2610.20977]
Abstract

Recent cosmological data hint at a neutrino mass sum below the minimum value allowed by the neutrino mass oscillation experiments, with a preference for the opposite of massive neutrinos ("negative" neutrino masses). Since massive neutrinos impact the expansion history of the Universe, which in turn modifies the growth of structure, we ask whether such a preference originates from the observed geometry or structure growth, and whether the two agree. In this paper, we introduce a more physical split of $\sum m_\nu$ into parameters $\sum m_\nu^{geo}$ and $\sum m_\nu^{growth}$ based on their physical effects, with extension to negative values. We split the expansion rate at the Boltzmann-code level: $\sum m_\nu^{geo}$ sets $H(z)$ for distance computation, and $\sum m_\nu^{growth}$ sets $H(z)$ for perturbation evolution. Combining Planck primary CMB, Planck+ACT+SPT CMB lensing and DESI BAO data, we find similar sensitivity and "negative" preference from geometry and growth with $\sum m_\nu^{geo} = -0.07\pm0.10$ eV (68% C.L.) and $\sum m_\nu^{growth} = -0.13^{+0.10}_{-0.12}$ eV (68% C.L.). The difference of the two is a null test of the $\Lambda$CDM model, passed by current data at $0.4\sigma$ level. Forecasts on Advanced SO CMB and DESI DR3 BAO show that $0.058$ eV would be rejected at $3.0\sigma$ from the geometry channel alone, if the negative mean of $\sum m_\nu^{geo} = -0.07$ eV persists. The two channels can also provide a powerful new diagnostic between possible origins of the neutrino mass tension. With future CMB and BAO data, our Fisher forecasts show that an incorrect dark energy model displaces $\sum m_\nu^{geo}$ from $0.058$ eV by $3.1$-$4.2\sigma$ and $\sum m_\nu^{growth}$ by $1.1$-$1.3\sigma$, while an underestimated reionization optical depth reverses the pattern, $2.7$-$3.6\sigma$ in $\sum m_\nu^{growth}$ against at most $0.23\sigma$ in $\sum m_\nu^{geo}$. 9. EPRV Asteroseismology of the K dwarfs GJ 570 A and GJ 783 A with ESPRESSO. Empirical mode-amplitude scaling in tension with 3D convection simulations[2610.20985]
Abstract

Solar-like oscillations in K dwarfs have intrinsically low amplitudes, making them challenging to detect in space-based photometry. The advent of Extremely Precise Radial Velocity (EPRV) instrumentation enables Doppler asteroseismology in the mid-K regime, providing a new opportunity to test mode-excitation and damping physics. We investigate the scaling of oscillation amplitudes with stellar parameters and assess whether 3D surface convection simulations reproduce the observed amplitude behavior. High-cadence ESPRESSO radial-velocity time series were obtained over five and six consecutive nights for the K dwarfs GJ 570 A (K4 V) and GJ 783 A (K2.5 V), respectively. We identified oscillation modes from weighted power spectra and extracted them via sine-wave fitting following spectral deconvolution. Peak mode amplitudes were measured from heavily smoothed power spectra. For comparison, we performed 3D radiative-hydrodynamical simulations including excitation and damping-rate calculations. We measured 6 modes for GJ 570 A and 24 for GJ 783 A ($\ell=0$-$2$, and $\ell=3$ for GJ 783 A). The peak radial-mode amplitudes are $2.57 \pm 0.52\:{\rm cm\,s^{-1}}$ and $4.90 \pm 0.81\:{\rm cm\,s^{-1}}$, respectively. These results support a transition from an $\sim(L/M)^{0.7}$ amplitude scaling to a steeper $\sim(L/M)^{1.5}$ scaling, occurring near the G/K-dwarf boundary toward decreasing $L/M$. In contrast, 3D simulations predict an $\sim(L/M)^{0.9}$ scaling for K dwarfs, revealing tension between observations and first-principles convection models. EPRV asteroseismology extends into the mid-K regime and exposes a steep mode-amplitude scaling that our 3D convection simulations do not reproduce. This discrepancy points to missing physics in our implementation of convection-pulsation coupling and/or limitations in the adopted modeling assumptions, motivating further theoretical investigation.

10. Finite-Element Asteroseismology I: Two-Dimensional Adiabatic Oscillations of Rapidly Rotating N=3 Polytropes[2610.21105]
Abstract

We develop a finite-element formulation for linear adiabatic oscillations of axisymmetric uniformly rotating, centrifugally distorted, self-gravitating N=3 polytropes. Equilibrium models are constructed using self-consistent-field iteration and then remeshed on a surface-fitted oblate meridional domain. The quadratic eigenvalue problem describing oscillations for each azimuthal wavenumber $m$ is solved using a conforming two-domain mesh. The density-weighted displacement, $\boldsymbol{u}=\rho^{1/2}\boldsymbol{\xi}$, is defined in the stellar interior, whereas the gravitational-potential perturbation $\delta\Phi$ is solved both inside the star and in an exterior vacuum. The coupled displacement–potential problem is assembled and solved within FEniCSx as a polynomial eigenvalue problem. Without rotation, the axisymmetric modes agree closely with an independent one-dimensional calculation. For finite rotation rates, several clean axisymmetric $p$-mode branches of the spheroidal star successfully reproduce the frequencies tabulated by Reese (2006). The relative frequency differences induced by rotation are smaller than $3\times10^{-4}$ for the low-order mode branches up to $\sim 60$\% of the critical rotation rate. These tests validate both the centrifugally distorted equilibrium and the finite-element implementation. We develop a method to track eigenfunctions continuously as a function of rotation and decompose their angular structure to quantify rotational mode mixing and progressive coupling between different spherical harmonic components. This reveals continuous deformation of individual mode branches from nearly single-$\ell$ structures to strongly mixed states at rapid rotation. This work is intended as a step toward finite-element multidimensional asteroseismology frameworks for modelling realistic rotationally and tidally distorted stellar configurations.

11. Neutrino production at the extreme vicinity of supermassive black hole in NGC 1068[2610.21110]
Abstract

The identification of NGC 1068 as an extragalactic high-energy neutrino source raises the question of the physical conditions required for neutrino production. Motivated by the newly updated IceCube data (down to $\sim 0.2 \,\rm TeV$) for NGC 1068, we present a statistical, model-independent inference primarily constrained by the multi-messenger observations. We calculate neutrino production from hadronic channels (including hadronuclear and photomeson processes) and the accompanying cascade gamma rays, and then employ the Latin hypercube sampling (LHS) method to explore the parameter space comprehensively, constrained by observational data. According to our results, the emission region is tightly limited: $R \gtrsim 10\,R_{\rm s}$ is excluded at $\sim 5\sigma$, and $R \gtrsim 6\,R_{\rm s}$ at $\sim 3\sigma$, where $R_{\rm s}$ is Schwarzschild radius of supermassive black hole (SMBH). Furthermore, reproducing $\gtrsim 80\%$ of the observed neutrino flux further restricts the emission to $R \lesssim 3R_{\rm s}$. Finally, we explore the possible particle acceleration mechanisms and find that stochastic acceleration naturally satisfies all constraints, whereas alternative mechanisms require extreme, fine-tuning parameters. We therefore identify stochastic acceleration as the most viable origin of the neutrino emission from NGC 1068.

12. Orbital Stability of Apsidally Aligned Planetary Systems[2610.21117]
Abstract

The dynamical stability of compact planetary systems is known to depend strongly on orbital eccentricities, but the physical role of apsidal alignment remains poorly understood. We investigate this problem using direct {\em N}-body simulations by separating planetary eccentricities into a systematic component shared by all planets and a random component that determines the degree of apsidal alignment. We find that these two components play fundamentally different dynamical roles. Random eccentricity destabilizes planetary systems by driving neighboring planet pairs into the regime of mean-motion resonance overlap, leading to chaotic evolution. In contrast, systematic eccentricity has little effect on the instability timescale over a wide range of values because the planetary orbits remain in apsidal alignment, preserving nearly fixed relative orbital orientations despite finite eccentricities. As a result, systems that start in apsidal alignment are nearly as stable as initially circular systems. This protection mechanism has a finite domain of validity. Beyond a critical systematic eccentricity, coherent apsidal precession breaks down, differential precession develops, and the instability timescale decreases rapidly. Our results demonstrate that the long-term stability of compact planetary systems cannot be characterized solely by orbital eccentricities but also depends on the maintenance of coherent apsidal precession, providing a new direction for analytical stability criteria.

13. Post-perihelion dust activity and a candidate icy-grain halo in long-period comet C/2023 A3 (Tsuchinshan–ATLAS)[2610.21228]
Abstract

We analyze the post-perihelion coma of the Great Comet of 2024, C/2023 A3 (Tsuchinshan–ATLAS) to characterize dust activity and assess the contribution of sublimating icy grains to a candidate near-nucleus halo. Using archival Zwicky Transient Facility images from 15 February to 26 May 2025, we performed aperture photometry within a fixed projected radius of 40,000 km and derived azimuthally averaged surface-brightness profiles. The comet faded from 12.86 to 14.77 mag as heliocentric distance increased from 2.64 to 3.90 au, with two short-lived brightenings. The effective dust cross section decreased by a factor of 2.4, following $C_{\rm e}\propto r_{\rm h}^{-3.1}$, while the dust mass production rate scaled as $\dot{M}_{\rm d}\propto r_{\rm h}^{-3.41}$, consistent with a contribution from water-ice-driven activity. Extrapolating the fitted relation to perihelion implies a model-dependent cumulative dust mass loss of $3.0\times10^{11}$ kg, equivalent to a globally averaged erosion depth of $\sim1.4$ m for an assumed nucleus radius of 5.9 km. Coma profiles were shallower than the canonical steady-state expectation, suggesting radial changes in dust distribution or scattering properties, possibly due to delayed aggregate fragmentation. A persistent central brightness excess, coincident with less-red inner-coma colors, is consistent with an icy-grain halo. Among the models explored, icy grains of radius $\sim10~\mu$m with carbon fraction $\sim0.1\%$ yield halo radii comparable to those observed during enhanced activity on 21–25 February 2025, with grain lifetimes of $2.8\times10^5$–$3.2\times10^5$ s. No single fixed grain size and composition reproduces the full halo evolution; an increasing carbon fraction could explain the broad halo-radius trend, possibly reflecting progressive refractory enrichment of near-surface material.

Abstract

Such measurements are difficult to perform while the primordial shape parameters are varied at the same time, and those parameters are therefore usually held fixed at the values determined by cosmic microwave background experiments. In this work, we present a methodology that constrains the shape parameters simultaneously with the model-independent distance and growth parameters, using an emulator built upon a hybrid model in which perturbative predictions are supplemented by templates measured from $N$-body simulations. {Because these templates are measured rather than expanded, the model carries non-linear corrections beyond the order at which its perturbative part is truncated, so that its accuracy on small scales is set by the simulations rather than by the reach of the expansion.} A Gaussian process emulator supplies these templates rapidly across the space of scale-dependent parameters, which makes the joint analysis computationally feasible, and the emulator is trained and validated over a range spanning $5\sigma$ of the Planck constraints. Applying the method to the LRG2 sample of DESI DR1, we obtain measurements of the Alcock–Paczynski parameters $\alpha_\perp$ and $\alpha_{||}$ and of the growth rate $f\sigma_8$. {With the tight Planck prior adopted here the constraints differ from those obtained with the shape parameters held fixed by less than a twentieth of their own uncertainties, so that the choice of template contributes at that level to the error budget of the measurement. 16. Thermodynamics of Viscously Heated Circumplanetary Disks[2610.21284]
Abstract

Circumplanetary disks (CPDs) play an important role in the formation of gas giants and their satellites, yet remain poorly understood owing to the lack of observables and comprehensive theoretical models. Incorporating $\alpha$-viscosity, a free-fall prescription of mass infall and boundary layer accretion, we develop an axisymmetric viscous radiation hydrodynamic (RHD) model. We evolve this model for different physical parameters using the RHD code {\tt Guangqi}, which solves RHD equations under the flux-limited diffusion approximation. In eight out of nine simulations, we obtain quasi-steady-state solutions and verify self-consistent energy conversion and transport. Overall, disk thickness increases with accretion rate and decreases with planet mass, consistent with the expected trend in conventional $\alpha$-disk models. On the other hand, we highlight that the boundary layer plays a significant role in setting angular momentum transport and thermodynamics of the inner CPD, yielding “hot” ($s_{\rm real}>11\,k_{\rm{B}}\,m_{\rm{H}}^{-1}$) accreted gas, and potentially leading to dust sublimation. We identify a supersonic accretion flow, amounting to $2\%$–$5\%$ of the total accretion rate, located at high elevations above the CPDs with low angular momentum in our simulations. If the planet is massive ($\gtrsim3\,M_{\rm J}$), this low angular momentum flow impacting the planetary surface could generate observable H$\alpha$ emission when the shock accretion rate is $\gtrsim5.5\times10^{-8}\, M_{\rm J}\,\rm{yr}^{-1}$.

17. Identification of ultra-high-energy neutrinos in vertical air showers with surface detectors[2610.21495]
Abstract

We investigate the potential of surface detector arrays to identify ultra-high-energy neutrinos using vertical air showers with zenith angle $\theta \leq 60^\circ$, a region largely unexplored in current searches. The method exploits the enhanced electromagnetic component at ground level arising from energetic leptons produced in neutrino interactions. Using detailed air-shower and detector simulations, we show that observables sensitive to the electromagnetic and muonic content provide genuine discrimination between neutrino-induced and hadron-induced showers. This is demonstrated both with idealized shower quantities and with realistic detector-level signals based on water-Cherenkov and scintillator detectors. The corresponding neutrino identification efficiency is used to derive the effective mass and, primarily, the effective area of the array under background-rejection requirements appropriate to targeted multi-messenger searches. Although not competitive with inclined-shower searches under the stringent background rejection factors required for diffuse-flux analyses, this approach establishes the feasibility of a vertical detection channel, extending the field of view of surface arrays and providing a complementary tool for multi-messenger observations of transient sources.

18. An HST survey of photometry and multiplicity of brown dwarfs and planetary-mass objects in the field of Taurus[2610.21566]
Abstract

We present a photometric and multiplicity survey of 26 young objects with masses between 6 Mj and 37 Mj and spectral type later than M8 in the Taurus star-forming region with the Hubble Space Telescope. We search for companion candidates using two methods: a dedicated photometric technique based on the combination of colours from multi-band photometry and point spread function fitting. No companion candidates are detected beyond 30 au. With an updated membership and age identification, we determine luminosity, temperature, and mass for the 26 young objects using evolutionary models. We demonstrate that our HST observations are sensitive to companions down to masses of 1 Mj and even lower for some targets by artificial star injection tests. After survey completeness correction, we derive a binary frequency < 6.5% for this sample in the 68% confidence interval for companions with mass ratios between 0.1 and 1 and separations between 30 and 7,000 au. 22. Hydrostatic Equilibrium Constraints on the Fuzzy Dark Matter Mass from Dwarf Spheroidals: Method, Systematics, and Robustness[2610.21722]
Abstract

We present the analysis of a non-parametric estimator of the fuzzy (wave) dark matter (FDM) boson mass based on local hydrostatic equilibrium: the classical gravitational acceleration must be balanced by the gradient of the quantum potential of the Schrödinger-Poisson-Madelung system.
