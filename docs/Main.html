1. Newtonian I-Love-Q relations: An enthalpy-based approach[2610.31730]
Abstract

We present a pedagogical introduction to the I-Love-Q relations within Newtonian gravity using an enthalpy-based formulation of stellar structure. Introducing the specific enthalpy converts Euler's equation into a first integral of hydrostatic equilibrium and recasts the equation of state as a relation between the mass density and the enthalpy. Together with Poisson's equation, these relations provide a unified description of spherical equilibrium and deformations induced by rotation and tides. We illustrate the formalism with polytropic equations of state and a relativistic free Fermi gas. In Newtonian gravity, slow uniform rotation and a weak tidal field excite the same linear quadrupolar response, yielding the exact identity $\bar I^{\,2}\bar Q/\bar\lambda\equiv1$ for the dimensionless (“bar”) observables—independently of the equation of state. This exact identity is distinguished from the approximate universality of the pairwise I-Love-Q relations, whose residual dependence on stellar structure is illustrated by the models considered in this work.

2. Image Morphology of Misaligned Magnetically Arrested Disks[2610.31754]
Abstract

Horizon scale images probe the plasma dynamics and spacetime in the vicinity of accreting black holes. Interpreting these images requires models that connect image morphology to the structure of the underlying accretion flow. Disk misalignment is expected in many systems, yet most horizon scale image analyses assume that the emitting flow is aligned with the black hole spin. We investigate whether tilted accretion flows produce image features distinguishable from axisymmetric flows and whether their effects can be absorbed by changes in aligned model parameters. Using general relativistic magnetohydrodynamic simulations of tilted magnetically arrested disks spanning black hole spins $a^*=-0.9375$ to $0.9375$ and initial disk tilts $0^\circ$ to $30^\circ$, we generate synthetic images and quantify their morphology through fits to an aligned, semi-analytic emission model. Using this approach, we show that high spin prograde models show little sensitivity to disk tilt, as the inner emitting flow aligns with the black hole spin. In contrast, retrograde tilted disks develop warped, non-axisymmetric inner structure that distorts the inner shadow and strongly alters image morphology. While the aligned semi-analytic model reproduces most prograde images well, retrograde tilted disk images are fit less accurately and yield biased inferred parameters, especially for spin. The image morphologies indicate that tilt affects horizon scale images only when misalignment survives into the near horizon emitting region, motivating image models that include disk tilt as an explicit degree of freedom when alignment is uncertain.

3. From Inspiral to Outburst: Modeling a Potential Luminous Red Nova from Betelgeuse[2610.31755]
Abstract

Recent evidence suggests that Betelgeuse may host a stellar companion whose orbit will decay through tidal interactions, ultimately leading to engulfment and merger within the next several thousand years. If confirmed, this system could represent the nearest and best-characterized progenitor of a future luminous red nova (LRN). We investigate whether the shock-breakout and cooling emission preceding the LRN can be used to constrain the properties of the companion and its disruption within the stellar envelope. Using binary stellar evolution calculations, we evolve Betelgeuse with companions of $0.7$, $1.0$, and $2.0 M_\odot$ to the onset of common envelope evolution (CEE). We then perform one-dimensional radiation-hydrodynamic simulations that follow companion disruption, energy deposition, and shock propagation through the envelope. We find that the peak luminosity increases systematically with companion mass, from $2.44\times10^{41}$ to $6.17\times10^{41}$ erg s$^{-1}$ across the $0.7$-$2.0 M_\odot$ models, while the duration increases more modestly from $1.48$ to $1.99$ days and attains apparent magnitudes brighter than Venus as viewed from Earth. Tidal disruption contributes both kinetic energy and mass to the envelope, linking the companion's structure and mass to the energetics of the shock-heated ejecta. The monotonic luminosity sequence demonstrates that the early breakout emission can retain direct information about the engulfed companion. The interaction ejects $6.15$-$11.19 M_\odot$ of hydrogen-rich material, substantially modifying both the envelope structure and circumstellar environment prior to core collapse.

4. Prospects of Detecting Cosmological Domain Walls in the Cosmic Microwave Background[2610.31761]
Abstract

Topological defects are a generic prediction of field theories with spontaneously broken symmetries, and domain walls, the two-dimensional defects left by a broken discrete symmetry, are among the simplest. We study the imprint of dark, purely gravitationally interacting domain walls on the Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB), in the regime where a low symmetry-breaking scale $\eta$ allows the walls to evade the Zel'dovich overclosure bound while remaining cosmologically relevant. The relativistic repulsion of matter by a planar wall carves out an evacuated void bounded by an overdense shell, and we compute the resulting photon deflection field analytically for walls both aligned with and arbitrarily inclined to the line of sight. The deflection stamps coherent, strongly non-Gaussian distortions onto the temperature sky, with a characteristic two-peak structure set by the relativistic displacement scale and an inclination dependence that vanishes for face-on walls. Because these signatures are localised and anisotropic, they are poorly captured by two-point statistics; we therefore analyse them with a curvelet/wavelet decomposition matched to the wall's geometry, and with simulation-based inference, training a Neural Posterior Estimator (NPE) to recover the scattering redshift $z$, energy scale $\eta$, and inclination $\theta$, and a Neural Ratio Estimator (NRE) to evaluate the likelihood-ratio test statistic for limit-setting. Casting these methods as sensitivity forecasts for a South Pole Telescope-like (SPT) survey, we find that the curvelet and wavelet statistical thresholds populate a common $\eta \sim 10-100$ MeV band over the accessible redshift range. The neural-ratio sensitivity curve, which exploits the full map morphology, reaches a factor 1.3-1.9 lower in $\eta$ at $z \gtrsim 20$, or $\sim 2-7$ in the wall tension, landing essentially on the per-resolution-element noise floor.

5. Demographics of Close-In TESS Exoplanets Orbiting FGK Main-sequence Stars II. Spectral-Type Dependence and the Neptunian Landscape[2610.31784]
Abstract

Understanding how planetary demographics depend on host star properties is essential for constraining planet formation and evolution theories. In this paper, we analyse close-in 0.5-16 d planet occurrence rates from the TESS Science Processing Operations Center (SPOC) full-frame image data for individual F-, G-, and K-type main-sequence stellar samples. Integrated over $0.5 < P < 16$ d and $2 < R_\mathrm{p} < 20\,R_\oplus$, the occurrence rates are $12.1^{+3.0}_{-2.3}$, $10.2^{+1.2}_{-1.0}$, and $13.1^{+1.4}_{-1.2}$ planets per 100 stars for F-, G-, and K-type hosts, consistent with each other at $1\sigma$, so any spectral-type dependence is localized rather than global. For transiting hot Jupiters, we measure $0.30^{+0.04}_{-0.03}$, $0.50\pm0.03$, and $0.67^{+0.08}_{-0.07}$ per cent, with the rate peaking at K type; the latter being the first such measurement for K-type hosts. Modelling the Neptunian desert directly in occurrence-rate space, we find that the desert extends to longer orbital periods around hotter stars. Integrating over the desert interior gives $0.069^{+0.045}_{-0.011}$, $0.093^{+0.046}_{-0.022}$, and $0.150^{+0.033}_{-0.026}$ planets per 100 stars, implying about one close-in planet in a hundred lies inside the desert, and the desert is roughly twice as populated around K- as around F-type hosts. A rigorous Bayesian model comparison for the $6$-$8\,R_\oplus$ strip indicates that a distinct Neptunian ridge is not required to explain the measurements.

6. Cluster connectivity in DES Y3 data: impact on galaxy properties[2610.31800]
Abstract

Connectivity, namely the number of cosmic filaments reaching out of groups or clusters, has recently emerged as an informative way to quantify environment. With the advent of wide-area photometric surveys, it becomes possible to measure connectivity on a statistically significant sample of clusters. In this work, we extract the cosmic web from the DES Y3 photometric data and measure the connectivity around clusters from several public catalogs up to $z < 1$. Cosmic filaments are extracted in 2D with DisPerSE (the parametrization of which is calibrated on simulations) in tomographic slices of fixed comoving thickness of 220 Mpc, with a constant sampling across the redshift range ($\sim 0.1$ galaxies Mpc$^{-2}$). We first recover the well-established trend that cluster connectivity increases with cluster mass at all redshifts. We then investigate the properties of cluster member galaxies and find that, at fixed mass, clusters with a higher fraction of red (elliptical) galaxies are more strongly connected to the cosmic web. Furthermore, by examining the radial profiles of the red and elliptical galaxy fractions, we obtain hints that galaxy populations may undergo pre-processing within filamentary structures before being accreted into clusters. Overall, we demonstrate that despite photometric redshift uncertainties and projection effects, our method allows for the quantification of the cosmic web environment around clusters in broad-band photometric surveys. This work is consistent with results based on the Euclid Quick Release 1 (Q1). We plan to publicly release the cluster connectivity catalog as well as the cosmic filament catalog.

7. Best Bulk Bennu (B3): Elemental compositions of aggregate samples from asteroid Bennu[2610.31828]
Abstract

Ivuna-type (CI) carbonaceous chondrites have long served as proxies for the bulk Solar System composition due to their close agreement with solar photospheric abundances and enrichment in volatile elements relative to other chondrite classes. However, CI chondrites have been subject to terrestrial alteration, which may have modified their original mineralogies and redistributed aqueously mobile elements. NASA's OSIRIS-REx mission delivered pristine material from the carbonaceous asteroid Bennu, and initial analyses revealed close compositional similarities with CI chondrites. This provides a valuable opportunity to refine estimates of the bulk Solar System composition. We report concentrations of 63 elements in aggregate (bulk, unsorted) Bennu material (representing a combined mass of  8 g) analyzed across four laboratories. Within analytical uncertainties, we find no statistically significant differences among aliquots of two aggregate samples that were homogenized into powders, or among subsamples (including two previously reported) of a non-homogenized aggregate sample. Nor do we find measurable variations among sample masses ranging from tens of milligrams to several grams. These findings indicate that the material returned from Bennu is well mixed at the aggregate scale. Combining all of the data, we derive an average bulk elemental composition that we term Best Bulk Bennu (B3). Comparison of B3 with an average CI chondrite composition shows excellent agreement within analytical uncertainties, confirming that terrestrial alteration of CI chondrites is effectively isochemical for non-highly volatile elements. B3 and the CI chondrite average composition compare similarly to solar photospheric composition. Thus, CI meteorites and returned samples of carbonaceous asteroids are the best available materials for estimating the bulk Solar System composition of non-highly volatile elements.

8. Semi-Analytic Predictions for Strong-Lensing Perturbers in Cold and Self-Interacting Dark Matter[2610.31881]
Abstract

Strong gravitational lensing is sensitive to the abundance and internal structure of dark matter halos, providing a probe of gravothermal evolution in self-interacting dark matter (SIDM). We combine semi-analytic cold dark matter (CDM) subhalo histories with a model for gravothermal evolution in SIDM to generate CDM and SIDM subhalo populations across SIDM parameter space. We predict their projected enclosed masses and deflection-based projected density slopes and compare them with three perturbers inferred through gravitational imaging in PJ011646, SDSS J0946+1006, and JVAS B1938+666. For SIDM model parameters $\sigma_0/m_\chi=147.1$ cm$^2$/g and $w=120$ km/s, SIDM subhalos reproduce all three perturbers more often than CDM subhalos. For PJ011646, we find  0.6 cored SIDM subhalos per host matching the perturber, compared with  0.002 in CDM, while core-collapsed subhalos are too steep. For SDSS J0946+1006, we find  3 core-collapsed and  0.004 cored SIDM subhalos per host, compared with none in 900 CDM realizations, although baryons, neglected here, may matter for such massive subhalos. For "detection V" in JVAS B1938+666, we find  220 core-collapsed and  20 cored SIDM subhalos per host within the $2\sigma$ posterior region, compared with  0.2 in CDM; the CDM candidates require exceptionally early accretion, at median redshift $z\sim13$. Simultaneously reproducing all three perturbers requires $w\gtrsim10^2$ km/s, so that the massive ( $5.6\times10^{10}M_\odot$) progenitors of SDSS J0946+1006 candidates can collapse, and is achieved for $\sigma_0/m_\chi\gtrsim10^2$ cm$^2$/g in the parameter sets studied. Our results show how semi-analytic models can quantify how often subhalos reproduce individual perturbers in different dark matter models, and our modular framework can be readily updated as models of tidal stripping and late-stage gravothermal evolution in SIDM improve. 9. Discovery of activity on Main Belt Asteroid 6993 (1995 BJ4)[2610.31910]
Abstract

We report the discovery of activity on asteroid 6993 (1995 BJ4) in the Zwicky Transient Facility (ZTF) alert stream data on UT 2026 August 5, with the help of machine learning. Visible activity can be seen as early as UT 2026 June 25. Activity on this outer-main belt object is likely not primarily driven by rotational instability, but rather is consistent with an impact event or sublimation based on several arguments. If this activity is sublimation driven, 6993 would be the largest known main belt comet (11.85 km), with the largest perihelion distance (2.853 au) and an unusually high geometric albedo of 0.166 $\pm$ 0.030. Asteroid 6993 should be monitored as it approaches perihelion in 2027 January to distinguish between activity mechanisms and understand its potential volatile inventory.

11. OGLE-2015-BLG-1395Lb: A Cold Sub-Saturn Mass Planet Orbiting an M Dwarf[2610.32020]
Abstract

We present an analysis of the high-magnification microlensing event OGLE-2015-BLG-1395 and report the discovery of a sub-Saturn mass companion to the lens. Photometric coverage of the planetary anomaly in the light curve was obtained from the University of Tasmania's Harlingten 1.27m telescope. Additional data were obtained following the peak from several other observatories, allowing photometric calibration and color measurement of the source. Fits to the light curve reveal a companion with mass ratio of q = 0.00062 +/- 0.00007 and two degenerate solutions for the projected separation, s = 1.7 +/-0.1 or 0.59 +/-0.04 Einstein ring radii. Source and lens parameters are further constrained by Subaru and Keck adaptive optics (AO) imaging in 2016. Finite source effects and AO flux constraints allow us to determine the planet and host star parameters to better than a factor of 2. The system is an  0.2 Jupiter-mass planet orbiting an  0.25 solar mass (early-mid M-dwarf) main-sequence star in the Galactic disk, at a distance of  2 kpc. The two degenerate solutions from the light curve both place the planet beyond the snow line. As a cold sub-Saturn around an M dwarf, OGLE-2015-BLG-1395Lb is in possible conflict with core accretion theory. Spitzer photometry from the time of the event is consistent with this conclusion, although highly uncertain due to faintness and crowding. The inferred relative proper motion of 9.4 mas/yr is consistent with a source star in the bulge and a lens in the disk, and predicts that the source and lens could be resolved a decade after the event.

12. A Unified Model for Radioactive Signatures and Source Identifiability in Neutron Star Mergers[2610.32047]
Abstract

Late light curves of neutron star mergers can contain substantial fission power while remaining compatible with different radioactive inventories. We develop a unified source attribution model in which a common bounded response formulation links nuclear heating, radiation transport and isotope identification. Within particle dependent deposition and gray photon escape, matched luminosity comparisons show how long alpha and fission responses become aligned. Explicit competing inventories then reproduce a light curve with a substantial $^{254}$Cf contribution, with smooth evolution of the beta luminosity response providing an important source of compensation. We derive optimization certificates and collective likelihood bounds that quantify the resulting limits on identification under specified source and ejecta constraints. We also introduce an energy conserving model with two radiation frequency groups to resolve the information carried by color at fixed total luminosity precision. Its exact response modes identify the source adjustments that absorb color contrast, while a residual color floor characterizes the contrast retained by the permitted family. Late observations of AT 2023vfi with the James Webb and Hubble Space Telescopes provide an observational benchmark: the reference and retained alternatives predict bolometric declines about 1.5 quoted uncertainties shallower than the reconstruction, while differences between inventories containing and excluding $^{254}$Cf remain within the reported decline uncertainty. The unified model provides a quantitative basis for connecting nucleosynthesis predictions, gravitational wave constraints and the temporal and spectral information in merger observations to radioactive source attribution.

14. A Framework for Analysing Nebular-Phase JWST Supernova Spectra with Application to Type Ic SN 2024aecx[2610.32323]
Abstract

We analyse what physical quantities of SN ejecta can be obtained from a single epoch of nebular JWST spectroscopy. We provide an analytical framework, which together with a grid of helium star \texttt{SUMO} spectral synthesis models allows the determination of these quantities for any stripped-envelope SN. Using the framework, we derive critical electron densities for major emission lines, finding values in the range $10^5-10^6$ cm$^{-3}$. This means formation under close to LTE conditions for 200 – 300 days. We obtain ionisation fractions of Ni and Co, as well as the temperature and filling factor of the Fe/He zone, the $^{56}$Ni and stable nickel masses, the Ni/Fe ratio, and the optical depths $\tau_{\mathrm{[Ni\,II]\, 6.636 \mu m}}$ and $\tau_{\mathrm{[Co\,II]\, 10.52 \mu m}}$. Besides these constraints from iron group elements, one can also obtain the filling factor of the carbon burning (O/Ne/Mg) zone from [Ne II] 12.81 $\mu$m, and the masses of Ar II and Cl I. We apply our methodology to a publicly available JWST spectrum of Type Ic SN 2024aecx. We find that this SN produced a chlorine mass of $M_{\mathrm{Cl\, I}} \simeq M_{\mathrm{Cl}} = 5.1^{+2.0}_{-1.6}\times 10^{-4}$ $M_{\odot}$, a factor a few larger than expected from stellar evolution modeling. Furthermore, we find that the SN experienced an unexpectedly small 'Ni-bubble' effect, with $\rho_{\mathrm{FeHe}} = 2.5^{+0.7}_{-0.4} \times 10^{-15}$ g cm$^{-3}$ (or an expansion factor of 1 – 3 for the Ni-bubble, depending on the adopted model) at 167d for the silicon burning material. We also determine $M_{\mathrm{Ar\, II}} = 4.9^{+2.2}_{-1.5} \times 10^{-4}$ $M_{\odot}$, which from ionisation fractions in our NLTE models yields a best estimate of $M_{\mathrm{Ar}} = 1.1 \pm 0.4 \times 10^{-3}$ $M_{\odot}$.

17. Introducing the GION Lightcone Simulation Suite for Multi-Wavelength Large-Scale Structure Cosmology[2610.32388]
Abstract

We present the GION (Ginkaku Lightcone Multi-wavelength Simulation Project) simulation suite: 10 full-sky high-resolution cosmological lightcone simulations and their associated products for multi-wavelength large-scale structure cosmology. We use the TreePM $N$-body code GINKAKU, which is highly optimized in the massively parallel environment, and integrate the on-the-fly lightcone construction technique. Each lightcone simulation is run with $6144^3$ particles in a volume of $(1.5 \, h^{-1} \, \mathrm{Gpc})^3$, which corresponds to a particle mass of $1.28 \times 10^9 \, h^{-1} \, \mathrm{M}_\odot$, and extends to $z = 5$. The fine mass resolution and the depth of the lightcone simulations allow us to resolve dark matter halos down to $10^{11} \, h^{-1} \, \mathrm{M}_\odot$ in the high-redshift Universe, which is crucial for applications to the Stage-IV cosmological surveys. The fundamental statistics of the simulations, including the matter power spectrum, halo power spectrum, and halo mass function, are validated against theoretical predictions. We also generate various large-scale structure observables from the lightcone outputs: the dark matter halo catalogs, mock galaxy samples, weak lensing convergence maps, and thermal and kinematic Sunyaev-Zel'dovich (tSZ and kSZ) maps. The angular power spectra of these observables, including the cross-correlation between weak lensing and the tSZ effect, are in good agreement with theoretical predictions, demonstrating that the suite provides mutually consistent multi-wavelength observables on the same lightcone. The GION simulation suite will be publicly available and can be used for various cosmological analyses to address the observational systematic effects and validate theoretical models.

19. Luminosity-weighted galaxy and gravitational wave cross-correlations[2610.32436]
Abstract

Cross-correlations of galaxies and gravitational waves (GW) can be used to extract cosmological parameters and the gravitational wave bias. In this work, we study how to include galaxy luminosities in these spatial cross-correlations, inspired by the standard approach of dark sirens line-of-sight redshift priors. We present a formalism to include luminosity information in galaxy-GW simulations and study the effects of assigning a hosting probability that depends on luminosity through a simple power law $p_{\rm{host}} \propto L^\beta$ with $\beta \in \{0,1,2\}$ corresponding to uniformly, linear and quadratically weighted in luminosity. This effectively changes the GW linear bias with respect to dark matter and, in principle, allows to gain signal-to-noise and a sharper measurement of cosmological parameters. For a realistic luminosity dependence of the galaxy bias taken from DESI data, the gain is marginal, making the method robust to different luminosity weighting prescriptions. We show that the cross-correlation inference is able to recover the injected GW bias within $10\%$ if there is a mismatch between the injected linear bias of host galaxies and observed galaxies, provided that one sticks to relatively large scales. We also show that the Hubble constant and matter energy density can be recovered to $0.5\%$ and $5\%$ respectively with a third generation network of GW detectors, regardless of the modeling of the GW bias.

21. Tracking the Evolution of Supermassive Black Hole Binaries with Pulsar Terms[2610.32736]
Abstract

Pulsar timing arrays will soon be able to resolve the loudest supermassive black hole binaries contributing to the stochastic gravitational wave background at nanohertz frequencies. While the few decades of observations are typically not enough to see these binaries evolve in frequency, the so-called pulsar terms offer a glimpse into the past and can help us map out the frequency evolution of these binaries over tens of thousands of years. Measuring this long-term frequency evolution can allow us to look for deviations from a vacuum binary evolution due to various environmental effects. We develop a Fisher-matrix approach to calculate how precisely the frequency can be tracked over time in this manner, and demonstrate its use on a realistic near-future pulsar timing array with a 30-year baseline. We find that the environmental effects predicted by 2-dimensional hydrodynamic simulations of circumbinary disks are unlikely to be detectable in the circular binaries we considered, however, in some cases they might result in biased chirp mass recovery if ignored. We also demonstrate how this approach allows one to test the orbital evolution predicted by general relativity in a purely gravitational-wave driven inspiral, and to put upper limits on additional generic environmental effects described by a power-law hardening model.

22. Probing the star-forming environment of GRB across cosmic ages with high-resolution X-ray spectroscopy: From \textit{XMM-Newton}/RGS and \textit{XRISM}/Resolve to \textit{NewAthena}/X-IFU[2610.32786]
Abstract

Optical and infrared GRB absorption spectroscopy provides valuable insight into host-galaxy properties across cosmic time. However, the star-forming region embedding the progenitor, on scales $\lesssim 100~\mathrm{pc}$ is heavily ionized by the GRB emission, making X-ray spectroscopy the sole probe of its physical and chemical conditions. We characterize the GRB-embedding medium as a function of progenitor type and cosmic epoch via high-resolution X-ray spectroscopy. We extend our study to high-redshift GRBs to evaluate how effectively X-ray absorption signatures can distinguish between pristine, Pop III-, and Pop II-enriched environments. Using the time-dependent photoionization code \texttt{TEPID}, we self-consistently calculate the ionization structure of the absorber, the energies and the strengths of the resulting absorption lines. We analyze a flux-selected sample of GRBs showing strong evidence for time-dependent photoionization, and search for predicted narrow features in the RGS spectra. We also assess the performance of \textit{XRISM}/Resolve and \textit{NewAthena}/X-IFU in detecting these features.
