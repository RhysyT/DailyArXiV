1. Direct inference of cosmological mass growth via timing of black hole - pulsar binaries[2611.24949]
Abstract

Cosmologically coupled black holes (CCBHs) respond to the expansion of the Universe, independently of conventional astrophysical processes. Recent CCBH solutions in General Relativity demonstrate evolution of gravitating mass $m_{\rm BH}\propto a^\kappa$, where $a$ is the cosmological scale factor. This can be tested through the orbital period timing of black hole (BH) pulsar (PSR) binaries. In contrast to previous methods used to constrain $\kappa$, pulsar timing measurement baselines span decades instead of gigayears, allowing sources of bias to be well-controlled. As a test case, we analyze the globular cluster BH+PSR candidate J0514–4002E (spin $P\sim 5.6~{\rm ms}$, orbital period $P_{\rm b}\sim 7~\rm days$) under the assumption that the companion is a CCBH. Taking the difference between the observed orbital and spin period derivatives removes all kinematic effects, leaving only the pulsar's intrinsic spin-down $\dot{P}_{\rm int}$ as unknown. Setting $\dot{P}_{\rm int} = 0$, its physical minimum, gives a robust upper bound $\kappa \leqslant 3.95$ at $95\%$ confidence. We estimate $\dot{P}_{\rm int}$ through the population of millisecond pulsars matched in spin and infer $\kappa = 0.82 \pm 1.67$ and $0.75 \pm 1.54$, using $H_0 = 67.6$ and $73.5\,{\rm km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}$, respectively. Adopting typical baseline scaling of timing precision, the value $\kappa = 3$, argued elsewhere to link energized vacuum BH interiors to accelerated cosmological expansion, can be excluded or detected for this $2.35^{+0.20}_{-0.18}\,M_\odot$ companion at $3\sigma$ and $5\sigma$ confidence by 2031 and 2036, respectively.

2. The OGLE Collection of Variable Stars. Over 1.3 Million Human-Verified Eclipsing and Ellipsoidal Binaries in the Milky Way[2611.25024]
Abstract

We present a new release of the OGLE collection of eclipsing and ellipsoidal binary systems in the Galactic bulge and disk. The catalog contains 1 330 024 objects, including 1 280 765 eclipsing binaries and 49 259 ellipsoidal variables, nearly three times as many systems as the previous editions of the OGLE collection. The selection and classification process involved expert visual inspection of the light curves. The catalog incorporates all available OGLE-II, OGLE-III, and OGLE-IV photometry, extending the observational baseline to nearly 28 years for some objects. The orbital periods and other observational parameters of previously known systems have been updated. We present basic statistics of the sample and highlight selected rare and unusual systems.

3. Simulation-based inference for gravitational-wave cosmology: parameter reconstruction of a first-order electroweak phase transition[2611.25036]
Abstract

The detection of a stochastic gravitational-wave background (SGWB) from a cosmological first- order phase transition would be unambiguous evidence for physics beyond the Standard Model, with a strong electroweak phase transition (EWPT) at LISA as a prime example. Reconstructing the underlying model from such a signal is challenging, since the map from the model parameters to the spectrum is degenerate and strong transitions occupy only small and sparse regions of parameter space. We show that simulation-based inference (SBI), a likelihood-free approach applicable to generic SGWB sources, provides a reliable parameter reconstruction despite these difficulties. We first apply SBI to the thermodynamic parameters characterizing the transition, namely its strength, inverse duration, temperature and bubble wall velocity. As a concrete application, we then consider a strong EWPT in a singlet extension of the Standard Model and reconstruct the model parameters from the resulting SGWB. We find that the SBI posteriors agree with those of a likelihood-based Markov chain Monte Carlo analysis, at negligible cost per data set once the network has been trained. Using the network posterior as the proposal for importance sampling with the exact likelihood further corrects its remaining deviations, at a sampling efficiency several orders of magnitude higher than sampling from the prior. SBI thus offers a fast and flexible framework for the cosmological science case of LISA

4. Spiral Pitch Angles in Black Hole Images and Movies[2611.25092]
Abstract

The Event Horizon Telescope (EHT) has produced images of M87* and Sgr A*, and will soon produce time sequences of images, or movies. General relativistic magnetohydrodynamics (GRMHD) models predict EHT sources will exhibit brightness fluctuations along the ring. The fluctuations are commonly connected to trailing spiral features. Here, we introduce a method to measure the pitch angle $\psi$ of radially extended features by cross-correlating brightness fluctuations sampled on rings at two distinct impact parameters. Pitch angle can be reliably measured from synthetic movies with BHEX resolution, and can be constrained even at EHT resolution. Single images enable noisy constraints of $\psi$. Pitch angle depends most on the viewing inclination and black hole spin. For constant inclination, the spiral winding direction depends on the sign of spin, with spirals winding more tightly in the direction of frame dragging near the horizon. The pitch angle will complement other observables to reliably constrain both the source inclination and spin in future analysis by EHT, and by expansions to the EHT such as ngEHT and BHEX.

5. Revealing galaxy assembly bias with luminosity-weighted marked correlations[2611.25093]
Abstract

We investigate whether luminosity-weighted marked correlation functions can reveal secondary halo information beyond that encoded by halo mass alone, as expected in scenarios of galaxy assembly bias. We propose a controlled null test in which galaxy luminosities follow the same mean luminosity–halo mass relation and scatter, while their residuals are either (A) uncorrelated with secondary halo properties or (B) correlated with a standardized secondary halo property, $s$. Both models have identical halo occupation, galaxy positions, luminosity function, and unweighted projected two-point correlation function, $w_p(r_p)$, so their distinction is encoded entirely in the luminosity marks. At leading order, the projected marked correlation function difference, $\Delta\mathcal{M}_p(r_p)$, scales as $\rho^2\sigma_{\log L}^2\xi_{ss}(r_p)$, where $\rho$ controls the coupling between the luminosity and secondary-property residuals, $\sigma_{\log L}$ is the scatter in log luminosity at fixed halo mass, and $\xi_{ss}$ describes the spatial correlation of the secondary property. We test this framework using a DESI-BGS mock catalogue over $0.1<z<0.4$. For the injected dependence, we detect a marked-clustering deviation over $0.11<r_p/(h^{-1}\,{\rm Mpc})<1.04$, obtaining $\chi^2=30.14$ relative to the null covariance. A one-parameter test rejects the mass-only hypothesis at greater than $5\sigma$ significance ($p=2.13\times10^{-7}$) for $|\rho|>0.63$. At fixed $\sigma_{\log L}=0.23$, correlations as weak as $|\rho|\simeq0.52$ remain detectable, with the results stable under variations in the HOD prescription and modelled galaxy selection effects. These results establish luminosity-weighted marked clustering as a sensitive null test for property-based galaxy assembly bias and secondary halo information hidden from unweighted two-point clustering.

6. On the High Eccentricity Orbit of 51 Eri b and Constraints on Interactions with a Second Companion[2611.25120]
Abstract

The eccentricity of an exoplanet's orbit is a key observational quantity that can be used to infer its dynamical past and predict its future orbital evolution. Planets are expected to emerge from their protoplanetary cradles with roughly circular eccentricities, and non-circular eccentricites to arise from subsequent dynamical processing in the absence of the gas disk. We present an updated orbit for 51 Eridani b based on 5 years of 0.2 mas precision optical interferometry from VLTI/GRAVITY(+). This planet is young (${\sim}20\,\mathrm{Myr}$ old), temperate ($T_{\rm eff}\sim650\,\mathrm{K}$), and on a wide orbit ($a{=}9.36\,\mathrm{au}$) with an uncertain mass ($M_{\rm b}\leq12\,M_{\rm J}$). We measured the planet's motion to an exceptionally high precision, confirming that its orbit is very eccentric ($e_b=0.574\pm0.003$). We observe residuals to the absolute astrometry of the host star based on a single planet orbit that could be attributable to reflex motion from a third body in the system. We construct a two companion orbit model that could explain the apparent reflex motion of 51 Eri A: this model predicts that the putative second companion has $M_c\in[1.0, 2900]_{95\%}\,M_{\rm Jup}$ and $a_c\in[0.3, 3.1]_{95\%}\,\mathrm{au}$. In this framework, a dynamical interaction between b and “c" could have kicked the exterior, lower mass b out onto its present, eccentric orbit. If this signal is astrophysical, and not due to a systematic error, this candidate should be confirmable with Gaia DR4 epoch astrometry in the near future.

7. Intra-day variability of the gamma-ray/optical emission of 3C 454.3: the complexity beyond the long-term correlation[2611.25125]
Abstract

Simultaneous $\gamma$-ray/optical observations probe particle acceleration and radiative processes in blazar jets. The blazar 3C 454.3 shows a significant long-term correlation between these bands, often interpreted as support for the canonical one-zone leptonic emission model. However, both bands show strong intra-day variability, and whether the correlation persists during individual flares remains unclear. We study the $\gamma$-ray/optical correlation and intra-day variability of 3C 454.3 over 17 years (2007–2024), on timescales $\leq 24$ h. We analyse AGILE and Fermi-LAT observations using light curves binned from 3 to 48 h, together with simultaneous $R$-band photometry from WEBT and public optical data sets. We examine the flux and spectral evolution of individual flares, estimate time-dependent correlation properties, and derive Compton dominance from optical and $\gamma$-ray luminosities. We confirm the long-term correlation, but find large intrinsic dispersion and a slope of $\eta \simeq 1.6$–$1.7$, inconsistent with a single canonical one-zone scenario. On shorter timescales, correlation strengths range from weak to strong, with slopes spanning $\eta \simeq 0.4$–$2.0$. We also find orphan flares in both bands, diverse flare morphologies, distinct Compton-dominance patterns with rapid excursions from $\sim 10$ to 120, and heterogeneous spectral trends. The shortest resolved variability timescales ($t_{\rm var} \lesssim 3$ h) indicate that localized emission regions are required by causality. The $\gamma$-ray/optical correlation in 3C 454.3 thus arises not from a single emission regime, but from multiple epoch-dependent physical states. Increasing temporal resolution reveals richer variability, highlighting the importance of systematic intra-day, multi-epoch studies of bright FSRQs.

8. Where Did the Angular Momentum Go? WASP-12 b May Have Circularized on a Retrograde Orbit[2611.25203]
Abstract

WASP-12 b is the only exoplanet with confirmed orbital decay. Angular momentum transferred from its decaying orbit should have realigned the star's thin convective layer, yet an earlier Rossiter–McLaughlin (RM) measurement suggested a $\sim$60$^\circ$ spin–orbit misalignment. We present two new Keck Planet Finder RM measurements. We measure a sky-projected obliquity of $\lambda=-1.3\pm8.7^\circ$; and the star's projected rotation velocity is $v\sin i_\star=1.3\pm0.1~\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$, an order of magnitude lower than expected for a star of its mass and age. With WASP-12's low chromospheric activity, $\log R^\prime_{\rm HK}=-5.5$, our analysis favors an aligned system in which $v\sin i_\star$ reflects slow surface rotation with $P_{\rm rot}\sim60$ days, rather than a projection effect. Our evolution models favor a late-main-sequence star rather than a subgiant, with a central convective core found in 99\% of posterior samples. This internal structure suppresses the central-focusing and nonlinear wave-breaking mechanisms for inward-propagating gravity waves. We propose that WASP-12 b was initially placed on a retrograde orbit after high-eccentricity migration, when its host rotated at $v\sin i_\star\approx14$ km s$^{-1}$ (or $P_{\rm rot}\sim$6 days). Tidal decay transferred orbital angular momentum to the convective envelope, aligning the surface. The retrograde interior acts as an angular-momentum reservoir, while interior–envelope coupling slows the surface rotation. A semi-empirical relation gives an envelope–interior angular-momentum exchange timescale of $\sim4$ Myr, similar to the observed orbital-decay timescale of $\sim3.25$ Myr. The angular-momentum budget also permits this initially retrograde evolution. In contrast, an initially prograde planet would have spun up the surface rotation to a rate substantially higher than observed.

9. Rediscussion of eclipsing binaries. Paper 33. The A-type system CW Canis Majoris in the PLATO southern field[2611.25217]
Abstract

CW CMa is a detached eclipsing binary containing two A0 V stars in a circular orbit of period 2.12 d. We analyse the light-curves of this system from the Transiting Exoplanet Survey Satellite (TESS) together with published radial velocity measurements to determine the masses and radii of the component stars to high precision, obtaining masses of 2.087 +/- 0.022 Msun and 1.972 +/- 0.024 Msun, and radii of 1.841 +/- 0.012 Rsun and 1.748 +/- 0.014 Rsun. We detect a changing orbital period over the four available TESS sectors indicative of the presence of a companion on a wider orbit. We develop a new method to determine the effective temperatures of the component stars using their measured radii, the Gaia parallax, and surface-brightness calibrations to reduce the degeneracy between temperature and extinction. The method gives temperatures of 9850 +/- 280 K and 9570 +/- 270 K and an interstellar extinction of A(V) = 0.32 +/- 0.06 mag. The properties of the system can be matched by theoretical models for a subsolar metal abundance (Z = 0.010) and an age of 400 +/- 20 Myr. CW CMa has been selected as a target for the PLATO satellite, with which it will be observed for at least two years.

10. The SPACE Program IV: Atmospheric Features on the Cold Transiting Sub-Neptune TOI-6478 b[2611.25230]
Abstract

Among the current population of exoplanets there are very few sub-Neptunes that have their atmospheric composition identified. This is primarily due to sub-Neptunes having high metallicity, clouds, and/or hazes obscuring absorption features. This often produces a spectrum that presents itself as a featureless spectrum especially in the near-IR. However, there are tentative trends that suggest sub-Neptunes with the lowest equilibrium temperatures ($\mathrm{T_{eq} < 400\:K}$) have detectable features, perhaps due to inefficient haze production or cloud sedimentation. TOI-6478 b is a low-density sub-Neptune exoplanet ($\rm <\mathrm{9.9\:M_{\oplus}}$ and $\rm \sim 4.5R_{\oplus}$) with an equilibrium temperature $\mathrm{T_{eq} = 220 \:K}$ assuming zero albedo. In this study, we present the highest S/N spectral feature ($\sim$6) of any sub-Neptune detected using HST/WFC3. We detect this spectral feature using a robust analysis that incorporates the Transit Light Source Effect (TLSE) to rule out false detections due to stellar activity. With the narrow bandpass of HST we were unable to distinguish whether the spectral feature at 1.4$\mu$m is from H$_2$O or CH$_4$ absorption. Meanwhile, self-consistent atmospheric models provide physical motivation for CH$_4$ to be the dominant absorber given that H$_2$O should condense or cold trap at higher pressures. Through our analysis we determine that HST/WFC3 alone cannot distinguish between major absorbers. However, a broader wavelength coverage by JWST will be able to measure the atmospheric composition in detail, due to the high S/N features for this target.

11. 3D Binary Evolution of a Merging Binary Black Hole Progenitor[2611.25310]
Abstract

Mass transfer is a highly dynamical 3D process that spans evolutionary timescales in binary stars. It is key to the production of gravitational waves through formation of binary black holes and other double compact objects. We integrate global 3D hydrodynamics into stellar evolutionary modeling to resolve mass transfer across nuclear timescales. We employ the shellular approximation in the stellar evolution code MESA to map a 3D spatial structure by equipotential shells in the Roche geometry to maintain hydrostatic equilibrium across tidally-distorted surfaces. We employ the method of time-incremented multiscale evolution to alternate between stellar evolution and 3D hydrodynamics to span large timescales with dynamical feedback. We employ this framework to examine a likely merging binary black hole progenitor system of a low-metallicity 30Msol donor star and 9.3Msol black hole from the end of the main sequence to core Carbon depletion. We modeled two complete mass transfer phases in 3D while self-consistently accounting for stellar evolution. These comprised a thermal-timescale early Case B phase and a nuclear-timescale late Case B phase. The prescription of Kolb & Ritter (1990) closely predicted the results of 3D evolution, except at the end of the first phase of mass transfer; our 3D model remained well-contained by the outer Lagrange point, despite 1D predictions of substantial L3 overflow during this stage. We find incorporating 3D hydrodynamics during Case B mass transfer did not significantly impact expected merger times. Our model never overflowed L3 as predicted by 1D prescriptions. L2/L3 outflows formed even while underflowing those equipotentials. Our model suggests these outflows can be neglected for the evolution of similar systems, but were dense enough to potentially impact X-ray observations.

12. First $Ω_{\rm m}-w$ Measurement Using Only Fast Radio Burst Observations[2611.25312]
Abstract

Though fast radio bursts (FRBs) are widely advocated as a dark energy probe, no valid $\Omega_m-w$ constraint has been obtained exclusively from FRB data to date. Benefiting from a rapidly growing localized sample and a novel statistical framework for modeling the dispersion measure (DM), we report the first simultaneous inference of the matter density $\Omega_m$ and dark energy equation of state parameter $w$ from FRBs alone, without external observational priors on these two parameters. We combine a multi-parameter emulator for the nonlinear matter power spectrum with a physically motivated electron prescription to construct a cosmology-dependent cosmic-DM distribution. Within this unified analysis framework, the mean and variance of DM respond self-consistently to the same underlying cosmology, directly connecting the FRB likelihood to both cosmic expansion and structure growth. Analyzing 125 localized FRBs at $0.105\leq z\leq2.148$, we obtain $\Omega_m = 0.33^{+0.10}_{-0.11}$ and $w = -0.80^{+0.28}_{-0.38}$ under the flat $w$CDM model. We find a new positive $\Omega_m-w$ degeneracy in the FRB posterior, opposite to the strong anticorrelation derived from Type Ia supernovae, offering a complementary test of dark energy properties and highlighting their potential in future precision cosmological studies.

13. FAST Burst-Cluster Catalog of FRB 20240114A[2611.25348]
Abstract

We present the complete FAST burst-cluster catalog of FRB 20240114A, covering its 495-day active episode from January 28, 2024 to June 5, 2025. The catalog contains 13261 burst clusters, 19037 bursts, and 29026 sub-bursts detected in 99 observing sessions. The data reveal distinct organization across temporal scales. Among drifting burst clusters, downward-drifting clusters outnumber upward-drifting ones by approximately $9.4:1$. Between separate burst clusters, previously reported frequency changes show no preferred direction, while our Weibull analysis finds their arrival times broadly consistent with Poisson statistics, suggesting that burst clusters may represent approximately independent emission episodes within observing sessions. The Parkes/Murriyang observations revealed a long-term modulation of the characteristic emission frequency, and the extended FAST measurements show a consistent evolution. Burst energetics increase with cluster multiplicity and activity level, although significant scatter remains. The manually refined dispersion measure increases at $1.29\pm0.11\,\mathrm{pc\,cm^{-3}\,yr^{-1}}$, enlarging the still limited but growing sample of repeaters with increasing DM. Overall, these results summarize the spectral–temporal evolution of FRB 20240114A and present a burst-cluster catalog linked to the existing BurstID measurements, providing a basis for future studies of repeating FRBs.

14. A Shallow Phase-resolved Gamma-Ray Luminosity-Cutoff Energy Relation in Globular Cluster Millisecond Pulsars[2611.25384]
Abstract

Globular clusters (GCs) provide dense stellar environments for investigating the high-energy emission of millisecond pulsars (MSPs). Using $\sim$17.3 yr of \textit{Fermi}-LAT Pass 8 data in the 0.1-500.0 GeV range, we study four MSPs that dominate, or contribute substantially to, the GeV $\gamma$-ray emission of their host GCs. Significant off-pulse $\gamma$-ray emission is detected from PSR J1823$-$3021A, J1824$-$2452A, and J1835$-$3259B. For these sources, the cutoff energy, $E_{\rm cut}$, varies systematically with rotational phase and is positively correlated with the phase-resolved energy flux. Using the phase-resolved isotropic $\gamma$-ray luminosity ($L_{\gamma}$), we find $L_{\gamma}\propto E_{\rm cut}^{\alpha}$ with $\alpha=0.74^{+0.16}_{-0.14}$. This slope is substantially shallower than that reported for field MSPs, $\alpha=2.31^{+0.22}_{-0.25}$, and the $\alpha=16/7\simeq2.29$ scaling expected for curvature radiation in the equatorial current sheet framework. The origin of the shallow relation remains uncertain, potentially reflecting cluster-associated $\gamma$-ray emission, differences in emission geometry and beaming, or limited photon statistics. Our results demonstrate that GC MSPs provide valuable targets for testing phase-resolved high-energy emission physics in dense stellar environments.

15. The Universality of Halo Growth and Merger Rates in Modified Gravity Theories[2611.25472]
Abstract

In the standard $\Lambda$CDM framework, the merger rate of dark matter haloes has been found to follow a nearly universal form, with only weak dependence on halo mass and redshift. We examine whether this universality is preserved in modified gravity (MG) models, where the growth of cosmic structure can deviate from that of general relativity (GR). We use the ELEPHANT suite of large $N$-body simulations to study halo merger rates and mass accretion histories in different modified gravity models, including two representative scenarios beyond-GR: the Hu–Sawicki chameleon $f(R)$ model and the normal branch of the Dvali–Gabadadze–Porrati model. For the mass accretion histories, the modified gravitational effect can produce systematic departures from GR, generally corresponding to more rapid halo growth. The deviations are relatively small when the MG effect is weak, while they become more pronounced in models with strong deviation from GR and can exceed $15\%$ in the extreme case. For the halo merger rate, we find that the nearly universal behavior is well preserved across all the gravity models, with only weak dependence on redshift and halo mass, reflecting the unbiased accretion nature of halo growth. However, apparent deviation from this universality can arise if the halo mass is not defined consistently with the modified gravity model.

16. Dependence of Dust Growth on Magnetic field Strength in Protoplanetary Disks[2611.25602]
Abstract

We performed one-dimensional calculations to investigate how the magnetorotational instability (MRI) affects dust growth in protoplanetary disks. We simultaneously solved the evolution of the dust size distribution and the vertical magnetic field. The dust size distribution is evolved taking into ac- count coagulation and fragmentation. For the magnetic field evolution, we considered both ambipolar diffusion and Ohmic dissipation. The MRI-driven turbulent viscosity was estimated self-consistently from the local magnetic field strength and non-ideal magnetohydrodynamics diffusivities. We found that MRI-driven turbulence becomes stronger for smaller initial plasma beta, and the enhanced tur- bulence promotes dust growth in MRI-active regions. However, dust growth is ultimately limited by radial drift, and the dependence of dust size on the magnetic field strength is significant only in the outer regions of the disk. In other words, over most of the disk, radial drift remains the dominant factor controlling the maximum dust size, and the magnetic field strength plays only a minor role in determining the overall dust size distribution.

17. False Periodicities in Repeating Fast Radio Bursts Caused by Burst Clustering[2611.25704]
Abstract

Fast radio bursts (FRBs) are millisecond-duration radio transients with high brightness temperatures, but their physical origin remains uncertain. The association of FRB 200428 with a Galactic magnetar provides compelling evidence that at least some FRBs originate from magnetar-like systems. Nevertheless, periodic modulation associated with neutron-star rotation has not been robustly detected in extragalactic FRBs. Motivated by the reported 1.7 s periodicity in the active repeater FRB 20201124A, we conduct a systematic search for short-timescale periodicity in four highly active repeating FRB sources. We employ a sliding-window method that analyzes temporally adjacent bursts within restricted time intervals, allowing transient or intermittent periodic signals to be identified. The search yields apparent period candidates with nominal Poisson-calibrated significances comparable to that reported for the 1.7 s signal, although the inferred periods vary substantially among sources and observing windows. We then perform a nonparametric waiting-time randomization test, in which the observed waiting-time distribution is preserved while the temporal ordering is destroyed. The randomized sequences reproduce the main candidate-period structures seen in the data, demonstrating that these apparent periodicities can arise from the non-Poissonian waiting-time statistics rather than from a coherent periodic process. Rotation-modulated epidemic-type aftershock sequence simulations provide a complementary parametric illustration of the same effect. We therefore find no robust evidence for a short intrinsic periodicity in these data, and the reported 1.7 s signal is not uniquely distinguished from clustering-induced candidates. Robust periodicity searches must evaluate significance against null models that account for the observed waiting-time statistics and the full search procedure.

18. New Candidate Activity Periods from FRB 20240209A and Repeating Fast Radio Burst Sources in the Second CHIME/FRB Catalog[2611.25797]
Abstract

Repeating fast radio burst (FRB) sources with periodic activity provide important constraints on FRB models invoking neutron-star precession, rotation, or orbital modulation in binary systems. Although a few FRB sources exhibit evidence of periodic behavior, it remains unclear whether periodic activity is a common underlying feature of repeating FRBs. Using a new FRB periodicity search pipeline introduced by A. B. Pearlman et al. (2026), which is optimized for sparse event-arrival-time data from FRB surveys, we search for activity periods in the burst arrival times from FRB 20240209A and repeating FRBs in the Second Canadian Hydrogen Intensity Mapping Experiment (CHIME)/FRB Catalog. For FRB 20240209A, we identify a new candidate activity period of 170.4 $\pm$ 4.7 days, with a trials-corrected statistical significance of 5.3$\sigma$. Our larger burst sample shows no evidence of the 128.5-day activity period previously reported for this source by A. Pal (2025). All statistical significances are evaluated against a homogeneous (constant-rate) Poisson null hypothesis, and we caution that they may decrease under null models that incorporate burst clustering. Collectively, our results constitute the most comprehensive search for periodic activity in the repeating FRB population to date.

19. The Atmospheric Circulation of Cloudy Hot and Ultra-hot Jupiters: the Need for Dissipation of Large-scale Flows to Reduce the Nightside Brightness Temperatures of Ultra-hot Jupiters[2611.25803]
Abstract

We perform general circulation modeling of hot and ultra-hot Jupiters across equilibrium temperatures of T_eq=1600 to 2800 K, incorporating cloud formation and its feedback on atmospheric dynamics. This study specifically examines whether clouds explain the systematically low nightside brightness observed in Spitzer thermal phase curves of ultra-hot Jupiters. We found that clouds generally increase phase-curve amplitudes and reduce eastward offsets, and these effects are most pronounced at relatively low T_eq and gradually weaken as T_eq increases. We discussed two mechanisms responsible for reducing the phase-curve offsets by clouds: the dynamical and geometric effects. Without explicit dissipation of large-scale winds, strong equatorial jets develop, maintaining a hot equatorial region and clearing clouds near the nightside equator at T_eq >= 2200 K, with thick clouds instead forming mainly at mid-to-high latitudes. These patchy nightside clouds do not sustain low nightside fluxes as temperatures rise, suggesting that clouds alone cannot explain the observed nightside brightness for ultra-hot Jupiters. Strong dissipation, parameterized as magnetic drags, suppresses equatorial jets and promotes nightside cloud coverage. In both clear and cloudy models, drag weakens the dependence of Spitzer nightside brightness temperature on equilibrium temperature; clouds further lower the nightside brightness temperature with the same drag prescription. Clouds also dampen amplitudes of thermal spectra on the nightside and, under small-particle conditions, can even produce emission features. Due to limited dayside cloud cover, our models yield negligible Bond albedos. Finally, interpreting simulated thermal phase curves with energy balance models in near-IR and IR bands tends to overestimate the Bond albedos of ultra-hot Jupiters by an order of magnitude or more.

20. Hard X-ray flux rope anchor points in the October 2024 X9 flare[2611.25810]
Abstract

In recent studies, signatures of non-thermal hard X-ray (HXR) emission at the anchor point of erupting flux ropes were reported, suggesting that accelerated electrons trapped in the erupting flux ropes can reach the chromosphere and produce Bremsstrahlung emission there. We investigated the HXR sources observed during the impulsive phase of the X9-class flare SOL2024-10-03T12:18 from AR 13842 and assessed whether any of the HXR sources are associated with the anchor points of the erupting flux rope. We analysed the evolution and location of the flare ribbon sources and coronal dimmings to diagnose the eruption process by using multi-vantage point data from the Solar Orbiter and Earth-orbiting instruments. We combined this with a pre-eruption non-linear force-free magnetic field extrapolation to reconstruct the initial magnetic connectivity and compare it to the observed flare ribbons and dimming locations. The magnetic field extrapolation shows two flux ropes before the flare onset. Tether-cutting reconnection during the impulsive phase of the flare results in a larger erupting flux rope formed by the reconnection of the two pre-existing flux ropes. The Spectrometer/Telescope for Imaging X-rays on Solar Orbiter observes at least four distinct HXR sources: two flare footpoints and two additional outer sources at the anchor points of the erupting flux rope. These observations corroborate earlier speculations that non-thermal electrons trapped in the erupting flux rope precipitate along the field lines into the denser chromosphere.

21. Square Kilometre Array Science Data Challenge 3b: EoR inference[2611.25823]
Abstract

We present and analyse the results of Science Data Challenge 3b (SDC3b; this https URL ), an Epoch of Reionization (EoR) inference exercise organised by the Square Kilometre Array Observatory (SKAO) using simulated SKA data. The nine-month challenge, running from October 2024 to May 2025, represents the first community-wide comparison of EoR inference pipelines on common simulated datasets. We consider multiple datasets designed to assess performance on both foreground-free and foreground-contaminated cylindrical power spectra and imaging products, adopting different reionization scenarios and EoR simulation models. We include results from 16 teams, which employ a range of methodologies to infer the posterior distribution of the hydrogen neutral fraction across three redshift intervals spanning z=8.41 to z=6.25. The results show significant inter-team variability of the astrophysical constraints even in the simpler case where only instrumental noise is added to the cosmological signal. Although individual methods can produce biased parameter estimates, the combined average of their estimates remains close to the input values, suggesting that independent approaches can offset method-specific biases.

22. Gamma-Ray Bursts from Primordial Black Hole-Induced Neutron Star Collapse[2611.25876]
Abstract

We show that the capture and subsequent endoparasitic consumption of a neutron star (NS) by a primordial black hole (PBH) can produce a short gamma-ray burst (GRB) without forming an accretion disk. During PBH-induced NS consumption, the NS spin is amplified through angular-momentum conservation and transfer from infalling matter, while flux freezing and a differential-rotation-driven dynamo amplify the magnetic field. We find that, for rapidly rotating millisecond pulsars (MSPs), these mechanisms can power short GRBs through magnetic dipole radiation and magnetic energy release. If asteroid-mass PBHs in the range $10^{-14}M_\odot$–$10^{-11}M_\odot$ constitute the dark matter, we estimate that searches of the Fermi-GBM archive and a future MeV gamma-ray mission such as COSI could detect these bursts. Collisionless shocks in the intracluster and intergalactic medium (ICM/IGM) are expected to energize both electrons and ions. While electron acceleration is revealed by prominent radio emission, $\gamma$-ray emission from hadronic interactions remains undetected, suggesting that high-$\beta$ (ratio of thermal to magnetic pressure), low-Mach-number shocks cannot accelerate protons efficiently. We present three-dimensional hybrid simulations of quasi-perpendicular shocks with sonic Mach numbers $M_s\sim{3.4{-}17.5}$, focusing primarily on high-$\beta$ conditions representative of cluster environments and including several lower-$\beta$ comparison runs. We find that weak shocks with $M_s\lesssim5$ are consistently inefficient, with $\varepsilon_{\rm CR}\lesssim0.1\%$, while inefficient cases can persist to substantially higher $M_s$ depending on $M_A$ and $\beta$. In contrast, stronger shocks ($M_s \gtrsim 10$) develop clear power-law tails with slopes $q \sim 4.0$ and reach $\varepsilon_{\rm CR} \sim 3\%$. These results suggest that weak, oblique ICM shocks are generally unlikely to accelerate protons efficiently. However, reducing $\vartheta_{B_n}$ to $\sim 45^\circ$ leads to substantially higher acceleration efficiencies, indicating that magnetic obliquity plays a critical role in determining proton acceleration. Our findings provide a microphysical framework for interpreting radio relic observations, whose polarization suggests that electrons are accelerated at oblique shocks, and the absence of cluster $\gamma$-ray detections.
