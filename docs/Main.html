1. Environmental dependence in the halo model informed by the FLAMINGO simulations[2610.23997]
Abstract

Galaxy-galaxy lensing (GGL) measurements around galaxy samples in different environments provide valuable information about the galaxy-halo connection. In such studies the halo model is commonly used to estimate physical parameters from the lensing signal, but the impact of environment is generally not considered. Our aim is to adapt the halo model to allow a density-split GGL analysis and to understand the environmental dependence of the elements of the model. We use the FLAMINGO suite of cosmological hydrodynamical simulations to construct samples of halos in underdense and overdense regions. Environment density is assigned to halos based on halo counts within an aperture, employing different aperture sizes to probe both local and large scales. We utilize spherical apertures with radii between $1 h^{-1} {\rm Mpc}$ and $10 h^{-1} {\rm Mpc}$. We find a strong dependence on environment for the halo mass function (HMF) and the halo bias. To account for this, we propose an empirical modification that can be applied to existing HMF prescriptions. We find that after a density split, the halo bias is approximately constant with mass. There is also an environmental dependence for the stellar-to-halo-mass relation (SHMR), the conditional stellar mass function, and the mass-concentration relation, all of which can be addressed with existing models. We evaluate the impact of overlooking environment on halo occupation distribution (HOD) parameter inference, finding that it leads to biased estimations of the SHMR and the satellite fraction. These results emphasize the need to review the assumptions adopted in the halo model when working with samples of galaxies binned by environment, and consequently, by galaxy properties that correlate with environment, such as morphology and color.

2. Multiline Spectropolarimetric Evidence for Photospheric Reconnection in Ellerman Bombs from DKIST[2610.24049]
Abstract

Ellerman bombs (EBs) are compact, transient brightenings observed in the wings of Balmer lines and are believed to result from magnetic reconnection in the lower solar atmosphere. However, their signatures have rarely been observed simultaneously in multiple spectral lines sensitive to the lower atmosphere. We present novel high-resolution multiline spectropolarimetric observations of EBs obtained with the Visible Spectro-Polarimeter (ViSP) on the Daniel K. Inouye Solar Telescope (DKIST). Simultaneous observations reveal remarkable H$\beta$ wing enhancements extending up to $\sim$4 Å from the line core and intensity enhancements in the \ion{Na}{1} D1 and \ion{Fe}{1} 6302 lines. Using multiline non-LTE inversion of the \ion{Na}{1} and \ion{Fe}{1} lines, we infer localized photospheric heating of up to $\Delta T\sim$800 K, accompanied by weak upflows and downflows at EB locations. Comparing with synthetic observables from Bifrost radiative-MHD simulations, we find that synthetic spectra reproduce the observed H$\beta$ and \ion{Na}{1} D wing enhancements and yield atmospheric stratification broadly consistent with those inferred from the non-LTE inversion. This study provides strong evidence that EBs are driven by photospheric magnetic reconnection below the temperature-minimum region, and places new constraints on the realistic EB models and small-scale energy release in the lower solar atmosphere.

3. A Two-Population von Mises–Fisher Distribution of the Magnetic Field Direction in the Young Solar Wind[2610.24057]
Abstract

The one-point distribution of the magnetic field direction in the young solar wind is found to be a superposition of two populations rather than a single exponential. Using Parker Solar Probe (PSP) Encounter 1 magnetometer data, we show that the distribution of the pitch-angle cosine mu(t) = bhat(t) . bhat_0 is well described by a two-component von Mises-Fisher (vMF) mixture: a broad, weakly confined component of concentration kappa_1   3.6 that accounts for the large-amplitude rotations and switchback tail, and a field-aligned component that reduces, in the small-angle limit, to a Gaussian in the polar angle theta of width sigma   15 degrees (concentration kappa_2   14). The concentration of the field-aligned component is not a free parameter: it is set by the level of the transverse Alfvenic fluctuation through kappa_2 = 2<|B|^2>/<delta B_perp^2> = 2/<1-mu^2>   16.2, in agreement with the fitted value to within  12%. The transverse fluctuations are weakly anisotropic (<delta B_1^2>/<delta B_2^2>   1.1), so the core is a weakly anisotropic Bingham distribution rather than a pure vMF. The broad component is exponential in mu, not in theta, confirming that switchbacks saturate toward mu = -1 rather than decaying away, consistent with the drift-diffusion framework of Bian et al. (2024, ApJ, 960, L15). This physics-based two-population framework connects macroscopic fluid parameters directly to those describing the directional statistics, offering a powerful diagnostic for probing the structural evolution of MHD turbulence and switchbacks in the inner heliosphere.

4. Consistently Reproducing Early Galaxy Observations and 21-cm Signal during the Epoch of Reionization[2610.24064]
Abstract

In the standard cosmological model, galaxies forming within dark matter haloes are the primary drivers of the Epoch of Reionization (EoR). The complex physics of galaxy formation introduces scatter in the relation between halo mass and star-formation activity, with implications for the production and escape of ionising photons. In this work, we implement a stochastic source model in our cosmological radiative transfer simulations. We calibrate our model against the high-redshift ultraviolet luminosity function observed by JWST and observational constraints on Lyman-continuum leakage to constrain the ionising escape fraction. We find that stochastic star formation redistributes ionising emissivity toward lower-mass haloes, producing a more fragmented ionisation topology and delaying the growth of the largest ionised regions relative to the deterministic, observationally calibrated model. However, when the two models match total ionising photon production, their global neutral fractions and 21-cm power spectrum remain nearly indistinguishable, despite small differences in the morphology of individual ionised regions. Additionally, we establish a unified framework that lets us apply source models derived from computationally expensive hydrodynamical simulations (SPICE, FIRE-2 and Thesan) and investigate their impact on large-volume reionisation simulations, extending their prescriptions to scales of >100 Mpc. We find that these prescriptions produce distinct ionisation topologies and 21-cm statistical signatures, even when their global reionisation histories are similar. Our results highlight the importance of combining physically motivated galaxy formation prescriptions from hydrodynamical simulations with large-volume radiative transfer simulations to interpret forthcoming 21-cm observations and constrain the sources of cosmic reionisation.

5. Multimessenger Probes of Primordial Black Holes with Gravitational Wave and 21 cm Observations[2610.24067]
Abstract

Primordial black holes (PBHs) formed from the collapse of curvature perturbations in the early universe are known to have an associated gravitational wave signal sourced by the same scalar perturbations. In this work, we show that there is a complementarity between these gravitational waves and modifications of the 21 cm global signal during the cosmic dark ages due to PBH evaporation. For PBHs in the range $ M_{\rm PBH} \sim 5 \times 10^{13} \, {\rm g} - 10^{17} \, {\rm g}$, there is a correlated signal in gravitational waves in the LISA, deci-Hz, and ground based regimes from $f_{\rm GW} \sim 10^{-3} \, {\rm Hz} - 20 \, {\rm Hz}$ and in the 21 cm global signal from $z \sim 300 - 30$. These observations are entirely independent of each other and within the reach of near-future experiments, making a combined measurement a novel multimessenger probe of primordial physics.

6. Mass-loss Rates of 74 Bow Shock OB Stars: No Evidence for Bi-stability Behavior[2610.24276]
Abstract

Accurate measurements of wind-driven mass-loss rates are essential for understanding the evolution and fate of massive stars. Established techniques for measuring $\dot{M}$ suffer from large dispersions and become insensitive in the regime of weak-winded late-O to early B stars. We employ a promising new technique to measure stellar mass loss for \numstars early type stars that produce infrared stellar bow shock nebulae. We use updated stellar parameters, improved parallactic distances and proper motions, and modern dust models to characterize the bow shock nebulae and obtain new measurements of mass-loss rates. $\mdot$ ranges from $2\times10^{-9}~\moperyear$ for weak-winded late-O and early B stars at $\log\left(\lstar/L_\odot\right)\approx4.5$ to $4\times10^{-6}~\moperyear$ for the most luminous stars in our sample at $\log\left(\lstar/L_\odot\right)\approx6.3$. Stellar space velocities relative to their local standard of rest are insufficient to accurately reflect the relative star-ISM velocities for the majority of our sample, which have speeds much less than canonical runaway thresholds, suggesting the pervasive influence of bulk interstellar flows in forming bow shock nebulae. Adopting a minimum relative velocity of $20~\kms$ is physically motivated and yields mass-loss rates in excellent agreement with the latest theoretical prescriptions while requiring no adjustable parameters. We find no enhancements in $\mdot$ for stars cooler than 25 kK, in agreement with modern mass-loss recipes that do not include bi-stability effects.

7. Intermittent turbulence as the driver of stochastic heating in the near-Sun solar wind[2610.24296]
Abstract

Understanding the mechanism by which the energy is dissipated at small scales to heat the outer corona and expanding solar wind, is a primary goal of the Parker Solar Probe (PSP) mission, and an outstanding question in heliophysics. Recent observational case studies have suggested that the dominant dissipation mechanism(s), stochastic heating (SH) or resonant heating (RH), may be modulated by the Alfvén `surface' ($M_A = 1$), while theoretical and simulation works predict that the mechanism should be modulated by the level of turbulence imbalance. We measure the local energy transfer (LET) rate from $9.8$ to $55$ solar radii from PSP observations, showing that the LET rate peaks at $M_A \sim 0.4 - 0.9$. We connect enhanced LET rates to signatures of intermittency, highlighting the key role that intermittent fluctuations play in heating the near-Sun solar wind. We compare the LET rate with estimates of the SH rate, finding that below $\sim 15$ solar radii the SH heating rate matches the LET rate provided intermittency is accounted for, regardless of $M_A$, while farther from the Sun, SH is unable to account for the observed LET rate. In contrast to predictions by recent theoretical works, this implies that `imbalanced' intermittent stochastic heating may be a viable mechanism to heat highly imbalanced, low-$\beta$ collisionless plasmas, and is likely an important mechanism of dissipation in the outer solar corona and near-Sun solar wind.

9. Numerical estimation of the capture ability of Neptunian mean motion resonances II. Influence of eccentricity and inclination[2610.24375]
Abstract

Evaluating the ability of Neptunian mean-motion resonances (MMRs) to capture small bodies is an important step in reconstructing the evolutionary history of the early Solar System. Building on a previous planar model, this study extends the investigation of MMR capture during Neptune's migration to the three-dimensional case, systematically analyzing the effects of the small objects' eccentricity, e, and inclination, i. Through numerical simulations with different migration rates, we obtain the distribution of capture probabilities for 12 MMRs on the e-i plane. The results show that increasing i narrows the “inevitable capture zone” (strong MMRs and/or slow migration) and raises the lower e threshold for capture (weak MMRs and/or fast migration), while it may also expand the upper e limit for capture. When the resonance order reaches three, the e-i plane further differentiates into several regions, which are closely related to the types of resonant angles. The emerging i-dominated region, along with its inevitable capture zone, makes third-order MMRs the most likely to benefit from higher inclination, as well as the most likely to host mixed-type resonators. Finally, using a hypothetical planetesimal distribution, we calculated the capture probability and efficiency for each MMR, finding that a cold disk favors strong MMRs, whereas a hot disk reduces differences among MMRs and across migration rates. In the future, a better determination of the population associated with different MMRs and different types of objects will help us understand the dynamical excitation state of the primordial disk in the early Solar System.

10. Critical magnetic Reynolds number of the weakly-collisional turbulent dynamo in supersonic plasmas[2610.24394]
Abstract

Weakly-collisional plasmas are found in several turbulent astrophysical environments, such as the Intra-galaxy-cluster medium (ICM) and the solar wind, where magnetic fields can be amplified by the turbulent dynamo. The onset of dynamo action is determined by the critical magnetic Reynolds number, ${\rm Rm}_{\rm crit}$, above which turbulent motions amplify magnetic energy. Although ${\rm Rm}_{\rm crit}$ has been studied extensively in collisional magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) turbulence, its value in supersonic weakly-collisional plasmas remains unknown. Here we determine ${\rm Rm}_{\rm crit}$ using three-dimensional hybrid particle-in-cell (HPIC) simulations of solenoidally driven turbulence with a sonic Mach number $\approx 2$. We vary the magnetic Reynolds number over ${\rm Rm}\approx31$-$1000$ and consider two initial plasma magnetisations, characterised by initial Larmor ratios $r_{\rm Larmor, 0}/L_{\rm box}=1$ and $100$. We find that the magnetic energy grows exponentially at sufficiently large ${\rm Rm}$ and decays when magnetic diffusion dominates at low ${\rm Rm}$. By fitting the magnetic-energy growth rate as a function of ${\rm Rm}$, we measure ${\rm Rm}_{\rm crit}=190^{+21}_{-17}$ and $198^{+16}_{-18}$ for the initially magnetised and unmagnetised plasma, respectively. These values are the same within their uncertainties, indicating that the initial plasma magnetisation does not significantly affect ${\rm Rm}_{\rm crit}$ in the supersonic regime. The measured values are higher than those previously obtained for the subsonic weakly-collisional dynamo, showing that increasing compressibility makes weakly-collisional turbulent dynamo action more difficult to excite. The measured values are also approximately 1.5 times higher than the ${\rm Rm}_{\rm crit}$ value reported for supersonic MHD turbulence.

11. Models of spectral bumps of primary cosmic rays and multi-messenger tests[2610.24412]
Abstract

The energy spectra of individual species of cosmic ray nuclei by recent direct detection experiments show complicated structures with spectral hardenings and softenings rather than featureless power laws, which challenge the traditional understanding of the production, acceleration, and propagation of cosmic rays. Particularly, the DAMPE measurements reveal charge-dependent softenings and reject mass-dependent softenings with a high significance. Taking into account the correlated structures at similar energies in the large-scale anisotropies of cosmic rays, one of the most natural interpretations of the spectral structures is the presence of a nearby cosmic ray source. In this case, the softening energies correspond to the acceleration upper limits of such a source, forming the so-called Peters cycle of the spectra. Alternative models such as the injection/acceleration and propagation models may also reproduce the spectral bumps, but show difficulty to explain the anisotropies. Fluxes of secondary nuclei and $\gamma$ rays offer additional tests of different models. Future multi-messenger observations are expected to be able to pin down the origin of cosmic rays.

12. Granulation in Color: The Chromatic Stellar Flicker of LTT-9779 and WASP-121[2610.24550]
Abstract

Granulation is a persistent feature of stars with outer convective layers and is the photospheric signature of near-surface convective energy transport. In disk-integrated observations, it produces stochastic brightness variations that contribute substantially to stellar variability on timescales of minutes to hours. High-precision, observations with JWST provide an opportunity to study the chromatic signatures of stellar granulation. This work aims to measure the amplitude and characteristic timescale of granulation-induced variability for solar-type stars and to investigate whether the granulation signal shows significant chromatic variation in the near-infrared. We accessed publicly available JWST/NIRISS SOSS time-series observations of LTT-9779 (G7V) and WASP-121 (F6V) and de-trended the light curves by fitting the transits, eclipses, and planetary phase curves. We computed power spectra from the de-trended light curves and fit them with a Harvey granulation profile. We first analyzed the broadband light curves to measure and fix the granulation timescale and then repeated the power-spectrum analysis in individual wavelength channels to measure the chromatic dependence of the granulation amplitude. For LTT-9779, we measure a broadband granulation amplitude of \sigma$=53.8^{+4.1}_{-4.0}$ppm at a timescale of \tau$=4.8^{+1.5}_{-1.3}$min. For WASP-121, we measure \sigma$=59.3\pm4.3$ppm and \tau$=6.3^{+2.1}_{-1.8}$min. We also measure peak oscillation frequencies of \nu$=3382^{+166}_{-191}\mu$Hz and $1649^{+55}_{-69}\mu$Hz in the bandpass-integrated power spectra of LTT-9779 and WASP-121, respectively. In the spectroscopic fits, we find $\sigma$ decreases with wavelength. Our results demonstrate a new observational window on short-timescale stellar variability and provides a way to characterize chromatic stellar noise in transit light curves.

13. Robustness of C/O and mean molecular weight under disequilibrium chemistry[2610.24583]
Abstract

Transmission spectroscopy increasingly reduces exoplanet spectra to derived compositional metrics, such as C/O, O/H, and mean molecular weight (MMW), but these quantities do not carry molecular abundance uncertainties in the same way. We assessed the reliability of these metrics for hot Jupiters and sub-Neptunes, by combining analytic uncertainty propagation with photochemical VULCAN forward models and representative published retrieval constraints. Propagating molecular abundance uncertainties produced substantially larger fractional uncertainties in elemental abundance ratios than in MMW. The fractional uncertainties in C/O and O/H were typically comparable to the inferred ratios, whereas the fractional uncertainty in MMW was more than two orders of magnitude smaller in H2/He-dominated atmospheres. Photochemistry and vertical mixing redistributed individual molecules but produced only minor changes in C/O and MMW at the pressures probed by transmission spectroscopy. C/O remains an important diagnostic of atmospheric chemistry and planetary formation history, but its interpretation requires careful uncertainty propagation. Mean molecular weight may provide a comparatively stable population-level diagnostic for linking observable atmospheres to bulk composition, especially across the sub-Neptune radius valley.

14. Gravitational filtering, magnetic-field inhomogeneity and Coulomb collisions in a kinetic model of a plasma atmosphere with stochastic heating at its base[2610.24678]
Abstract

We investigate the effects of stochastic heating, magnetic inhomogeneity, and Coulomb collisions in coronal loops using a reduced kinetic model. Coulomb collisions thermalize suprathermal populations generated by intermittent heating and populate velocity-space regions inaccessible from the lower boundary in the collisionless limit. We consider two regimes: when heating varies faster than the electron crossing time, the plasma develops a multitemperature nonthermal distribution; when it varies more slowly than the proton crossing time, individual heated loops approach an isothermal state. In the collisionless limit, conservation of the magnetic moment restricts the accessible velocity space, producing a strong temperature anisotropy, with parallel temperature exceeding perpendicular temperature, and additional density depletion. We find that Coulomb pitch-angle scattering efficiently suppresses these effects under typical coronal conditions. In the short-time-scale regime, density and temperature profiles approach those obtained with a constant magnetic field, while gravitational and collisional filtering sustain the temperature inversion. In the strongly collisional long-time-scale regime, magnetic-field-induced anisotropy is nearly eliminated, and individual loops remain approximately isotropic and isothermal. An increasing temperature profile then emerges only after averaging over unresolved flux tubes with different temperatures. Thus, Coulomb collisions largely suppress the macroscopic kinetic signatures of magnetic-field inhomogeneity in the transition region and low corona.

15. V347 Aurigae: from Peak to Quiescence[2610.24765]
Abstract

We present an analysis of eight epochs of high-resolution optical spectra of the YSO (young stellar object) V347 Aur. The source exhibits periodic brightness variations spanning $\sim$2 mag over several months, interpreted as cyclic accretion-burst and quiescent phases, making V347 Aur a useful probe of the broad range of accretion and outflow variability in YSOs. When faint, V347 Aur shows an M-type photospheric absorption spectrum; when bright, it exhibits a strong emission-line spectrum. Our Keck/HIRES spectroscopy was obtained during the decay of an accretion burst. The emission spectrum includes the strong H I, Ca II, and He I transitions commonly seen in YSOs, as well as numerous weaker metal lines, for which we provide a complete inventory. As the star fades, photospheric absorption lines deepen, consistent with decreasing veiling from the accretion continuum. H I and He I emission, which trace accretion, strengthen relative to the continuum as it fades, but show the expected decline in strength with decreasing accretion after correcting for the strong early veiling. Ca II behaves similarly, with line profiles that alternate between red- and blueshifted dominance, which we interpret as the rotation of the magnetospheric accretion flows. Narrow, slightly blueshifted absorption in Ca II, Na I, and K I indicates disk winds with localized density enhancements. Forbidden [O I] and [S II] emission is blueshifted, with broad shoulders and low- and high-velocity narrow peaks, consistent with a disk geometry that obscures much of the receding outflow. Their behavior as the continuum fades is similar to that of the broad accretion lines. The V347 Aur system is a useful proxy both for the ubiquitous short-term accretion variability observed in YSOs, and for their secular evolution on longer time scales.

16. Mid-infrared Imaging of SR 12 c with JWST/MIRI: Grain Growth in an Evolved Circumplanetary Disk[2610.24776]
Abstract

The physical properties of circumplanetary disks offer insights into the formation of giant planets and their satellites. The circumbinary planetary-mass companion SR 12 c (1200 au from SR 12 AB) hosts an isolated dust disk directly detected with ALMA. We present JWST/MIRI imaging of SR 12 c from 5.6 to 21 $\mu$m and analyze its spectral energy distribution from infrared to submillimeter wavelengths. Phenomenologically, the dust disk can be described by a two-component blackbody with large contrasts in characteristic temperatures and radii. Physically motivated models with varied geometries and heating mechanisms suggest a predominantly flat, irradiation-heated disk with a smaller contribution from viscous dissipation. The disk is inferred to be compact, with an inner radius of  3 $R_{\rm Jup}$. The outer radius is only loosely constrained, with a characteristic value of  3 au. The lack of strong 10 $\mu$m excess emission suggests significant grain growth and dust settling. We also find that SR 12 c is broadly consistent with the empirical star-disk scaling relations, providing context for its possible formation pathway. These results demonstrate the power of combining mid-infrared and submillimeter observations with disk modeling to better understand the physical properties of circumplanetary disks.

17. Long-term ultraviolet variability of Taurus T Tauri stars from the IUE archive[2610.24780]
Abstract

Ultraviolet (UV) radiation from Classical T Tauri stars, generated primarily by magnetospheric accretion, plays a fundamental role in circumstellar disk evolution. However, its long-term variability remains poorly characterized due to the scarcity of homogeneous time-series observations. We investigate the variability of the near-UV continuum and the Mg II resonance doublet to assess whether both diagnostics trace the same underlying accretion variability. We analyzed the complete archive of long-wavelength International Ultraviolet Explorer (IUE) observations of T Tauri stars in Taurus-Auriga, comprising 475 low-dispersion spectra of 36 objects collected over 18 years. We characterized continuum variability using three windows between 2600 and 3100 Angstroms and examined continuum-subtracted Mg II fluxes, UV-V colors, and Lomb-Scargle periodograms to investigate correlations and characteristic timescales. Continuum fluxes between 2675 and 3055 Angstroms are strongly correlated, with variability dominated by changes in continuum level rather than spectral shape. Near-UV and quasi-simultaneous optical fluxes are significantly correlated in six of the seven best-sampled sources, although their relative variability differs. The Mg II-continuum relationship shows substantial object-to-object diversity, ranging from correlated variations with different fractional responses to decoupled or anti-correlated behavior, indicating no universal temporal response. Lomb-Scargle periodograms reveal formally significant features in some well-sampled objects, including near 10 and 33 days. However, irregular and clustered sampling prevents their identification as reliable periodic or characteristic timescales.

18. Impact of Residual Gain Errors on the Detectability of Ionized Bubbles during Reionization with SKA 21-cm observations[2610.24843]
Abstract

Detecting ionized bubbles and characterizing their properties and the surrounding intergalactic medium (IGM) using the HI 21-cm signal provides direct probes of cosmic reionization. Here, we focus on the detection of individual ionized bubbles around luminous sources using a matched-filter technique. We investigate the impact of time- and frequency-correlated residual antenna gain errors on the detectability and characterization of individual ionized regions with SKA-Low. We simulate mock SKA-Low AA* observations that include the HI 21-cm signal, astrophysical foregrounds, residual gain errors, and system noise corresponding to 100 h of observing time. We subtract the foreground components from the gain-corrupted data and subsequently apply the matched-filter technique to the residual visibilities. We find that the matched-filter technique is able to detect the ionized bubble and constrain its properties in the presence of residual gain errors with the rms amplitudes up to $\sigma_A\sim1\%$ and rms phases up to $\sigma_\phi\sim1^\circ$. Residual gain errors at similar levels prevent the reliable identification of ionized regions in reconstructed HI 21-cm images and bias the estimated HI 21-cm power spectrum. Furthermore, we find that increasing the temporal correlation length increases the level of gain-induced artifacts in the reconstructed image. In contrast, increasing the frequency correlation length reduces these artifacts, as some fraction of the spectrally smooth gain-induced residuals is removed during foreground subtraction. We further find that the inferred bubble and IGM parameters remain largely unaffected by residual gain errors, although their uncertainties can increase. These results demonstrate the potential and robustness of the matched-filter technique for detecting and characterizing individual ionized bubbles in the presence of substantial residual calibration errors.

19. Tidal Disruption Events in Active Galactic Nuclei: Thermal Radiation and Jets[2610.24849]
Abstract

Over the past decade, dozens of Tidal Disruption Events (TDEs) have been identified, with many more expected from upcoming surveys. A small subset has been observed to launch transient, highly luminous relativistic jets, for which the origin of the requisite magnetic flux has not been identified. A non-negligible fraction of TDEs are expected in Active Galactic Nuclei (AGNs), where a magnetized accretion flow is already present. As shown first by Chan et al. (2019), the collision of a TDE debris stream with this flow can substantially alter both disk and debris dynamics, leading to a possibly Eddington-limited flare. Here, we use the Athena++ radiation magnetohydrodynamic (RMHD) code to simulate the time near peak fallback rate of a TDE in an AGN, studying the debris-disk interaction, the radiation emitted, and the accumulation of magnetic flux near the horizon. In the example simulated, the accretion rate climbs to $\sim 12\,\dot{M}_{ \rm Edd}$, while the escaping solid angle-integrated luminosity approaches $\sim L_{ \rm Edd}$. The photon luminosity is strongly asymmetric, brighter in the hemisphere from which the debris strikes the disk and dimmer in the hemisphere toward which the debris travels. The photon spectrum peaks in the EUV. The polar magnetic flux grows $\sim 33$-fold, enough to raise the Blandford–Znajek luminosity by a factor $\sim 10^3$, reaching a level sufficient to power observed TDE jets. The polar magnetic flux is also asymmetric, likely making any jet similarly asymmetric, but in the opposite sense to the photon flux.

20. Simulations of Particle Acceleration by an Interplanetary Shock Observed at 0.07 au and 0.7 au[2610.24995]
Abstract

Parker Solar Probe (PSP) observed a coronal mass ejection (CME)-driven shock at 0.07 au on 2022 September 5, and the same CME-driven interplanetary shock was subsequently observed by Solar Orbiter (SolO) at 0.7 au on 2022 September 6. Such a large difference in heliocentric distance between the two shock events enables our comparative study of particle acceleration in near-Sun and far-Sun regions. Using a backward-in-time test-particle approach in a slab-2D turbulent magnetic field model, we perform particle acceleration simulations for the 0.07 au PSP shock and 0.7 au SolO shock, covering the energy ranges measured by PSP/EPI-Lo (0.0697-8.06 MeV) and SolO/EPD (0.0495-5.83 MeV). With an initial kappa = 3 kappa distribution, the particle fluxes at the shock passage are successfully reproduced using effective local shock angles of 70 degrees for the PSP shock and 80 degrees for the SolO shock. Both the observed and simulated energy spectra exhibit a double power-law form. The 0.07 au PSP spectrum softens above 0.75 MeV, while the 0.7 au SolO spectrum hardens slightly above 0.51 MeV, revealing distinct near-Sun and far-Sun acceleration environments. Compared with the observations, the simulations yield harder energy spectra with higher break energies of 2 MeV at 0.07 au and 3 MeV at 0.7 au, indicating efficient particle acceleration under the initial kappa = 3 distribution. Both the observed and simulated spectral indices are harder than the standard diffusive shock acceleration (DSA) prediction, likely arising from non-local acceleration at the large-angle quasi-perpendicular shocks, efficient injection of pre-accelerated suprathermal particles, and the finite shock lifetime effect.

21. Beyond dust in local cosmography: perturbative Vlasov predictions[2610.25145]
Abstract

The dust approximation used in cosmography neglects the velocity dispersion generated during structure formation. This dispersion produces isotropic and anisotropic pressure, whose gradients accelerate the mean matter flow even though individual particles follow geodesics. The acceleration introduces an intrinsic dipole into the local distance-redshift relation. We calculate this dipole and the accompanying changes in expansion and shear using linear Vlasov perturbation theory about an FLRW background, with a mean dispersion history motivated by halo estimates. The assumed FLRW background prevents an independent test of the cosmological principle; the numerical predictions also depend on the adopted LCDM cosmology. Pressure also changes the growth of structure, modifying the shear and the spatial gradients that govern how the distance anisotropy varies with redshift. The largest correction at the lowest order in redshift is to the shear, while changes in the expansion gradient can produce a larger dipole at the next order. The resulting intrinsic dipole is far smaller than the observed low-redshift dipole, whose behaviour nearest the observer is dominated by motion between the matter and CMB frames.

22. Companions of Hot Jupiters: two decades of radial velocity monitoring with CORALIE[2610.25152]
Abstract

Hot Jupiters are thought to form through multiple pathways, some of which can leave observable signatures in the architecture of their planetary systems. Massive outer companions, in particular, can provide important constraints on the dynamical histories of hot Jupiters. We aim to characterize the occurrence rate and orbital properties of additional giant companions in hot Jupiter systems, and to investigate whether these companions provide evidence for the role of dynamical interactions in shaping the hot Jupiter population. However, the stochastic formalism was only recently formulated consistently within full General Relativity and has not yet been extended to more general theories of the early universe, which is the subject of this work. In order to find the stochastic sources for a wide class of fully nonlinear scalar-tensor theories, we apply our gauge-agnostic coarse-graining procedure to the linear equations of the associated effective field theory. Each theory can then be mapped to its own set of stochastic equations of motion by identifying the corresponding coefficients in the former. We illustrate this with a few concrete and, in most cases, unprecedented examples, including Gauss-Bonnet, generalized Brans-Dicke, Horndeski, and braiding theories. Finally, we discuss other natural extensions to provide a phenomenologically complete stochastic framework. For example, we showcase the coarse-graining of multifield inflation in full General Relativity and argue for the generality of our procedure and thus its potential applications beyond the realm of inflation.

90. Massive Graviton Dark Matter from a Gapped Continuum[2607.07295]
Abstract

We consider the possibility of dark matter in a warped extra-dimensional theory in the presence of a linear dilaton background, with a gapped continuum spectrum, in a brane-world cosmological scenario. Firstly, triggered by self-energy radiative corrections, we study the existence of an isolated resonance of massive gravitons, and its realization as a long-lived feebly interacting dark matter candidate, produced by the freeze-in mechanism. This massive graviton is proved to satisfy all theoretical and experimental constraints, in the sub-MeV mass range. We further consider the close relationship between the existence of this component of dark matter and the presence of an inflaton localized on the brane, with a mass around $10^{11}$ GeV and a sub-TeV reheating temperature, in a brane inflationary scenario that allows one to reproduce the most recent cosmological observables. Secondly, the gapped continuum of gravitons, a particular five-dimensional realization of the physics of unparticles, is identified as a holographic fluid which can play the role of holographic dark matter. The production of the holographic fluid goes by an ultraviolet freeze-in mechanism, with an abundance mainly depending on the reheating temperature. Depending on the values of the mass gap and the reheating temperature, one or both components of dark matter can be present.

91. Decoupling Limit of Quiver Theories and the Angular Spectra of Extreme C-metrics[2607.08747]
Abstract

We investigate the angular eigenvalue problem of the extreme charged C-metric. Starting from a constrained five-point Fuchsian description of the non-extremal angular equation, we obtain its extreme limit by decoupling a hypermultiplet in the dual four-dimensional $\mathcal{N}=2$, $\mathrm{SU(2)}\times \mathrm{SU(2)}$ linear quiver gauge theory. We derive the parameter dictionary and renormalized Matone relations, and connect the physical limit $Q\to M$ to the quiver decoupling by an explicit coordinate transformation. Regular endpoint conditions give an algebraic quantization condition. Eigenvalues obtained from the truncated instanton prepotential are tested against independent angular spectra near extremality and at larger acceleration. The axisymmetric sector recovers the known exact spectrum.

92. Multi-Band Constraints on Cosmic Strings: Unifying Harmonic and Burst Spectra[2610.02590]
Abstract

Cosmic-string loops generate a broadband gravitational-wave background (GWB) that is typically modeled via two distinct formalisms: a discrete harmonic expansion, commonly used in pulsar-timing-array (PTA) studies, and a continuous burst superposition, employed in ground-based GWB searches. In this work, we formally unify the two formalisms, showing that they provide discrete and continuous representations of the same underlying radiation process and converge on the scale-invariant spectrum plateau. On the rising low-frequency branch, where the first few harmonics dominate, the discrete nature becomes important, causing the two formalisms to diverge. Within this unified framework, the spectral calculation is more consistent across frequency bands, enabling multi-band inference. Leveraging this physical insight, we perform joint Bayesian inference across four representative loop-distribution models using the NANOGrav, EPTA, and LIGO–Virgo–KAGRA (LVK) datasets. By jointly sampling the string tension $G\mu$ alongside the average cusp and kink numbers $\{N_c, N_k\}$, the loop-radiation uncertainties are accounted for. We find that employing the harmonic expansion strengthens the LVK bounds by $21\%-31\%$ for three of these models. Additionally, the stringent LVK bounds preclude three of these models from explaining the observed PTA common signal. For the sole surviving model, our multi-band analysis improves the inferred limit on $G\mu$.
