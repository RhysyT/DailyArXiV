1. Thermal emission spectroscopy of the ultra-hot Jupiter HAT-P-70b: NIRPS constraints on its refractory and volatile content[2611.18835]
Abstract

Context. The chemical makeup of exoplanets provides insight into their origins and internal structures. High dayside temperatures on ultra-hot Jupiters vaporise refractory species and dissociate volatile molecules such as water. Cooler nightsides enable condensation (e.g. Fe) and cold-trapping (e.g. Ti), as compounds transition out of the gas phase. These contrasting conditions make dayside emission spectra a probe of both elemental inventories and atmospheric chemistry in the gas. Aim. We characterise the dayside atmosphere of the ultra-hot Jupiter HAT-P-70b via thermal emission spectroscopy, constraining its refractory and volatile budget and assessing thermal dissociation and cold-trapping. We focus on the abundances of rock-forming elements (Fe, Si, and Mg) and the depletion or survival of Ti and H2O. Methods. We analyse secondary-eclipse observations of HAT-P-70b acquired with NIRPS. From the emission spectrum, we infer the planet's atmospheric thermal structure and composition using a well-mixed retrieval framework. Results. We detect emission signatures from Fe, Mg, V, Si, and OH, but no significant H2O signal. The presence of OH and absence of water indicate strong thermal dissociation, with water likely depleted by more than 90% in the probed layers. The retrieved temperature profile shows a pronounced thermal inversion. Elemental abundance ratios (Fe/Si, Mg/Si, V/Si) are broadly consistent with solar-like composition, while titanium appears sub-solar, possibly due to partial condensation and nightside cold-trapping. Conclusions. HAT-P-70b's dayside atmosphere is dominated by refractory species and strong thermal processes. The detection of OH and absence of H2O indicate efficient water dissociation, while sub-solar titanium suggests nightside cold-trapping. Refractory element ratios are consistent with solar values, supporting a link between planetary and stellar compositions.

2. Searching for Anisotropic Cosmic Birefringence with SPT-3G[2611.18876]
Abstract

We search for anisotropic cosmic birefringence using two years of data from the SPT-3G instrument on the South Pole Telescope. Anisotropic cosmic birefringence refers to a theorized dependence of the refractive index of the vacuum on the polarization of light that varies across the Universe in a statistically isotropic manner. Such an effect would manifest as a direction-dependent mixing of the $E$ and $B$ polarization modes of the cosmic microwave background (CMB), caused by rotation of the polarization angle as photons travel from the CMB last scattering surface to us. We present a measurement of the power spectrum of the rotation angle $\alpha$ in six bins across the angular multipole range $20 < L < 2048$, finding no evidence for cosmic birefringence with the spectrum consistent with zero. At 95% confidence, we set a new best upper limit on the amplitude of a scale-invariant birefringence power spectrum of $A_{\rm CB}<0.027$. Additionally, we place upper limits on a scale-dependent white noise model. We translate the scale-invariant upper limit to constraints on the axion coupling constant and primordial magnetic field strength, finding 95% confidence limits of $g_{\alpha\gamma} < 1.7\times 10^{-2}/H_I$ and $B_{\rm 1\,Mpc} < 9.6\,{\rm nG}$ respectively. We expect these limits to improve several-fold with the complete SPT-3G survey.

3. On the Prospects of Using Cyclic Spectroscopy on Canonical Pulsars[2611.18882]
Abstract

We use the cyclic figure of merit to determine the likelihood of achieving cyclic deconvolution for 2609 canonical pulsars, a population for which this technique has not previously been used. These pulsars have spin periods between 0.1 and 10 seconds and are surveyed across 15 different telescope-observing frequency combinations. We conclude that cyclic deconvolution will only be a viable method for pulse broadening function or phase retrieval recovery in a handful of canonical pulsars. Few, if any, pulsars pass the conservative threshold for a given telescope-frequency combination. We believe that the cyclic figure of merit does not accurately reflect our ability to use cyclic deconvolution on this population, instead showing a shortcoming of the measurement when observing canonicals at low observing frequencies. Still, it is reliable for MSPs and for canonicals at high observing frequencies. More promising is the prospect of using this technique to recover scintles for canonical pulsars; DSA and FAST are best-suited for this as judged by the number of sources where scintles may be resolved and S/N is sufficient. We suspect there may be more sources that would have large figures of cyclic merit, but insufficient S/N, at very low frequencies. We advocate for expansions of real-time cyclic spectroscopy backends for current and upcoming low frequency telescopes.

4. Carbon dioxide and amorphous water ices in the debris disk around $β$ Pictoris[2611.18918]
Abstract

The star $\beta$ Pictoris is orbited by a dusty debris disk, within which planet formation is occurring. The disk extends far from the star such that molecules in the outer disk should freeze. We observed the $\beta$ Pictoris disk in the near-infrared using the James Webb Space Telescope. The spectrum shows features due to amorphous water ice (<7% crystallinity), and carbon dioxide ice in both the $^{12}$CO$_2$ and $^{13}$CO$_2$ isotopologues. We infer that the CO$_2$ ice is intimately mixed with the water ice, at a ratio of 1:10. This implies that the ices formed in cold conditions, likely in the interstellar medium during a phase of amorphous H$_2$O-dominated formation. Frequent collisions within the debris disk are required to explain the observed uniform distribution of icy dust grains.

5. 3D Radiative Transfer Simulations for Characterization of the Optically Thick DART Ejecta Plume[2611.18984]
Abstract

The Hyperion package is a three-dimensional Monte-Carlo radiative transfer approach to modeling dust ejecta. Because it has proven to be a powerful tool for simulating optically thick environments with complicated structure, we are applying the routines to observations of the DART impact on asteroid Dimorphos. Our analysis focuses on the LICIACube LUKE red-filter image acquired 175 s after impact, when the near-surface plume remained dense and not strongly structured. The model incorporates the updated Dimorphos shape model, spacecraft and solar geometry derived, and size-dependent scattering properties of realistic dust particles. We adopt an axisymmetric conical ejecta geometry and iteratively adjust the particle size distribution (PSD) and vertical number density profile to reproduce observed brightness profiles extracted from the LUKE image. We find that a power-law PSD with slope k = 3.0 and a number density peak located at approximately 120 m above the surface best reproduces the brightness contrast between the illuminated surface, the bright ejecta plume, and the adjacent dark band produced by shadowing within the optically thick material. The resulting best-fit model implies a total ejecta mass of 1.25 x 10^6 kg within 500 m of the impact site.

6. High-Resolution DKIST/ViSP Observation Reveals Small-scale Photospheric Magnetic Flux beneath a Coronal Hole Region[2611.18985]
Abstract

The magnetic unipolarity of photospheric regions beneath coronal holes is well established at spatial resolutions of about 1 arcsecond, but the effect of smaller-scale magnetic structures remains uncertain. We compare co-aligned observations from the Helioseismic and Magnetic Imager (HMI) aboard the Solar Dynamics Observatory and the Visible Spectro-Polarimeter (ViSP) at the Daniel K. Inouye Solar Telescope (DKIST). HMI observes the Fe I 6173 Å line, while ViSP observes the Fe I 6302.5 Å line; both form at similar photospheric heights. We derive the line-of-sight magnetic field (B_LOS) from ViSP spectropolarimetric data using a Milne-Eddington inversion and compare it with the HMI field derived from vector magnetograms over a common coronal-hole region. ViSP detects smaller magnetic structures with stronger fields than HMI. The absolute mean field and mean absolute field are higher by factors of 1.30 and 1.37, respectively. HMI measures approximately 28% less unsigned magnetic flux and 24% less net signed magnetic flux than ViSP. A comparison of spatially degraded ViSP data with HMI indicates that differences in effective resolution and associated spatial averaging contribute to these discrepancies. Nevertheless, the magnetic flux imbalance remains similar: 69% for HMI and 65% for ViSP. Thus, the additional small-scale flux detected by ViSP does not substantially change the measured unipolarity in this region. Because ViSP has a limited field of view, observations covering entire coronal holes are needed to test whether this result holds more generally.

7. Cosmography with gravitational waves II: Padé approximants in an extended redshift regime[2611.19006]
Abstract

We evaluate the capability of next-generation gravitational-wave detectors, including LISA, the Einstein Telescope, and DECIGO, to probe cosmographic parameters using electromagnetically bright standard sirens. Adopting Padé approximants, we extend the cosmographic analysis to substantially higher redshifts and constrain the parameters $H_0$, $q_0$, $j_0$, $s_0$, and $l_0$. We examine the constraints primarily as a function of the number of detections and, in a complementary analysis, across correlated SNR–redshift regimes. LISA provides percent-level constraints on $H_0$, while the Einstein Telescope and DECIGO reach subpercent precision as the catalogue size increases. The parameter $q_0$ can be constrained at the tens-of-percent level, whereas the higher-order parameters $j_0$, $s_0$, and $l_0$ remain progressively less determined. Compared with the low-redshift Taylor analysis, the Padé approach extends the usable redshift range, increases the accessible event population and enables a higher-order reconstruction of the expansion history.

8. The Stability Frontier of Interacting Binary Stars[2611.19026]
Abstract

We derive a conservation based dynamical stability frontier for interacting binary stars that distinguishes stationary mass transfer from the restoration of a perturbed contact state. We apply the formalism to conservative white dwarf binaries with direct impact accretion, coupling Roche overflow, stream angular momentum, gravitational radiation, and both stellar spin reservoirs. From the contact equations, we obtain the required orbital return torque, and an exact Jacobian identity yields the restoring gradient and an oscillatory stability boundary in the computed white dwarf domain. A dimensionally normalized quadrupolar tide reveals separate equilibrium fold and oscillatory thresholds, including coexisting stable and unstable stationary branches. Finite donor synchronization introduces an additional oscillatory stability frontier. The model recovers the classical Roche and gravitational radiation limits, while the calculated orbital period derivatives are consistent with published binary timing measurements within their combined observational and model uncertainties. These results specify the torque amplitude, restoring stiffness, and synchronization rate needed for local stability, providing quantitative targets for stellar tidal calculations and the interpretation of interacting binaries in the millihertz gravitational wave band.

9. Early Spectroscopy of the Type II SN 2024phv Suggests Short-Lived Circumstellar Interaction[2611.19031]
Abstract

We present the high-cadence light curve and rapid spectroscopy of the Type II SN 2024phv, discovered just 16.8 hours after the estimated explosion time. The early light curve exhibits a rapid 2 mag rise over the first day of observation, and spectra at 1.7 and 2.6 days reveal weak, narrow emission lines, suggesting moderate interaction with circumstellar material during the first few days after explosion. Models of shock-cooling emission provide a reasonable fit to the light curve after day 2, implying minimal interaction at later epochs, and yield a best-fit progenitor radius of $R=390\pm90\,R_{\odot}$. We also compare our earliest spectra with radiation hydrodynamical models of exploding red supergiants to measure circumstellar densities of ${\sim}10^{-12}$ g cm$^{-3}$ within a few stellar radii of the progenitor, followed by a sharp dropoff in the density profile. This rapidly decreasing profile leads us to conclude that the progenitor of SN 2024phv had an extended atmosphere and/or closely confined circumstellar material, likely from rapidly increasing or eruptive mass loss prior to explosion. In the context of other supernovae discovered at similarly early phases, SN 2024phv points to a diversity in the mass-loss rates of red supergiant stars in their final years.

10. Detection of CH$_2$NH on Titan[2611.19063]
Abstract

Saturn's moon, Titan, presents a chemical environment where abiotic synthesis of organics essential for life – including amino acids and nucleotide bases – may naturally occur. The assessment of Titan's organic content begins with characterizing its atmospheric chemical inventory, where many predicted molecular species remain unidentified. Here, we present the definitive spectroscopic detection of CH$_2$NH (methanimine) on Titan from observations with the Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array. CH$_2$NH represents the first stable imine (C=N) to be detected on Titan, and may be integral for further biomolecule formation as a precursor to glycine and adenine. We find abundances of CH$_2$NH at the 0.1–1 ppb level in Titan's mesosphere through multiple valid vertical profile configurations, yielding total column densities ranging from $\sim0.9$–$3\times10^{13}$ cm$^{-2}$. Middle-atmospheric abundances are commensurate with recent photochemical model predictions. However, models with parameterized galactic cosmic ray (GCR) fluxes, which exhibit a secondary, stratospheric formation site, are shown to provide poor fits to the data. Thus, our results provide confirmation of predicted formation and destruction pathways for CH$_2$NH in the upper atmosphere, and demonstrate secondary formation mechanisms in the lower atmosphere require adjustments. The latter presents an important barrier for fully estimating Titan's lower atmospheric composition, as enhanced production by GCR is predicted for additional N-bearing species, such as NH$_3$ and CH$_3$NH$_2$ (methylamine). The detection of CH$_2$NH provides the motivation to search for additional species fundamental for prebiotic molecule formation, which will dramatically improve our understanding of complex molecular synthesis of relevance to astrobiology in CH$_4$-rich environments.

11. ASASSN-15af: An Eclipsing Cataclysmic Variable above the Period Gap Showing Both Positive and Negative Superhump-like Signals[2611.19093]
Abstract

We present a photometric study of ASASSN-15af (TIC 231050794), combining TESS full-frame-image photometry from the TEQUILA project with 2-minute SPOC light curves and archival ASAS-SN and ATLAS monitoring over 4400 d. The long-term light curve shows recurrent dwarf-nova outbursts, establishing ASASSN-15af as a high-inclination eclipsing dwarf nova above the canonical period gap. From 391 quiescent eclipses, we derive the working ephemeris $T(E)=2139.76108(3)+0.14982063(2)\,E$ (BTJD), corresponding to $P_{\rm orb}=3.596$ hr. A quadratic fit is formally better, but the timings sample only three widely separated epochs, so we regard the apparent O–C curvature as tentative. In quiescence, the out-of-eclipse waveform is dominated by a stable $2f_{\rm orb}$ component, consistent with a substantial ellipsoidal contribution from the donor; during the Sector 96 outburst, its fractional amplitude decreases by $\simeq66\%$, consistent with dilution by the brightened disk. The outburst-only signals occur at periods $\sim1.6\%$ shorter than $P_{\rm orb}$ in Sector 2 and $\sim3.1\%$ longer in Sector 96, consistent with negative- and positive-superhump-like modulations, respectively. Because each is detected in only one outburst, their recurrence remains to be established. Their period-offset ratio, $\phi\simeq0.51$, is consistent with empirical trends among systems showing both modes, while the inferred superhump-based mass ratios span $q\simeq0.06$–$0.24$. During the Sector 96 outburst, the eclipse deepens by $\Delta D=0.196\pm0.028$ and its centroid shifts earlier by $\Delta t_{\rm c}=-66.6\pm7.3$ s, indicating a redistribution of accretion-powered light. These results identify ASASSN-15af as an above-gap eclipsing dwarf nova with a dynamically variable accretion disk; spectroscopy is needed to constrain the mass ratio.

12. Testing General Relativity by Reconstructing Dark Energy and the Growth Index with Physics-Informed Neural Networks[2611.19117]
Abstract

We extend the Cosmo-PINN, a physics-informed neural network for model-independent cosmological reconstruction, to reconstruct the dark energy equation of state parameter $w_{\mathrm{DE}}(z)$ and the growth index $\gamma (z)$ of matter perturbations. The reconstruction is performed through three modules: the background module, the matter growth module, and the growth index module. The background module learns the dark energy density $\rho _{\mathrm{DE}}(z)$ from cosmological observations, and $w_{\mathrm{DE}}(z)$ is reconstructed through the residual of the conservation equation. The reconstructed background is then passed to the other two modules, which reconstruct the observables $f(z)$ and $f\sigma _{8}(z)$ either directly from the linear perturbation equation of General Relativity or through an independently reconstructed growth index $\gamma (z)$. Comparing the two reconstructions on the same background provides a consistency test of the underlying physical framework. The network is trained on the DESI DR2 Baryon Acoustic Oscillations, Cosmic Chronometers, Type Ia Supernovae, and redshift-space distortion data. Posterior uncertainties for the reconstructed cosmological parameters and functions are obtained through Hamiltonian Monte Carlo sampling. For the different data combinations, the posterior median of the present-day dark energy equation of state lies within the range $w_{\mathrm{DE,0}}\in \lbrack -0.930,-0.867]$, while the phantom divide crossing takes place at $z\simeq 0.41$–$0.53$. The posterior median of the present-day growth index reconstructed from the data lies within $\gamma _{0}\simeq 0.60$–$0.62$, indicating suppression of the growth of cosmic structure.

13. Initial Conditions Dictate Gas-Stellar Counter-Rotation in Galaxies[2611.19294]
Abstract

We present a physical origin of gas-stellar counter-rotation in galaxies. Scale-dependent partitions of tidal torques from cosmic primordial perturbations source angular momentum for distinct baryonic components. This scale dependence maps to radial hierarchy in Lagrangian space clustering: stellar components, assembled at earlier times from the inner protohalo, retain persistent memory of small-scale tidal torques, whereas gas, accreted later from outer Lagrangian regions, is governed by large-scale perturbations and can develop counter-rotation. This mechanism is observationally testable and offers a direct probe of the hierarchical cold dark matter (CDM) paradigm. Unlike conventional late-time scenarios that only explain the existence of counter-rotation, our framework further predicts its component-resolved direction, enabling component-resolved angular momentum to serve as a new probe of primordial density perturbations and improving cosmological initial condition reconstruction.

15. Constraining neutrino masses with localized fast radio bursts: Current bounds and future prospects[2611.19416]
Abstract

The dispersion-measure (DM)–redshift relation of fast radio bursts (FRBs) provides independent low-redshift cosmological information for neutrino-mass inference. In this work, we perform joint analyses with cosmic microwave background (CMB) data, using 120 localized FRBs with reliable host-galaxy redshifts and larger mock samples, to investigate the effects of sample size and the dark-energy model on constraints on the sum of neutrino masses, $\sum m_\nu$. In the $\Lambda$CDM+$\sum m_\nu$ model, CMB+FRB gives $\sum m_\nu<0.21\,\mathrm{eV}$, substantially relaxed relative to the current CMB+DESI result. With 1000 simulated events, CMB+FRB1(mock) gives an upper limit of $0.12\,\mathrm{eV}$, which decreases to $0.035\,\mathrm{eV}$ for CMB+FRB2(mock) with 10000 events. The latter is more stringent than the CMB+DESI limit, demonstrating the potential of large localized FRB samples to constrain neutrino mass. When the model is extended to $w_0w_a$CDM+$\sum m_\nu$, both the observed and mock samples yield upper limits of approximately $0.3$–$0.4\,\mathrm{eV}$, indicating that the assumed dark-energy model remains a dominant factor in these constraints. As localized FRB samples grow and the modeling of the individual DM contributions becomes more accurate, FRBs may provide further independent low-redshift information for constraining neutrino mass.

16. Radial Evolution of a Corotating Interaction Region from a High-Resolution MHD Simulation[2611.19438]
Abstract

Corotating interaction regions (CIRs) are large-scale structures formed by fast–slow solar wind interaction, playing a crucial role in heliospheric dynamics and space weather. However, the evolution of individual CIRs and their full three-dimensional morphology remain poorly understood. We investigate the radial evolution of a single CIR using a high-resolution simulation combined with a backmapping method. The simulation reproduces the major plasma and magnetic signatures observed by Wind at 1 AU and by MAVEN and Tianwen-1 at $\sim$1.6 AU. The CIR exhibits a warped, shell-like spiral structure tilted relative to the heliospheric current sheet, consistent with theoretical predictions. During its developing stage, geometric changes are modest, and the radial scalings of velocity ($V_r \propto r^{0.02}$), density ($N \propto r^{-2.00}$), magnetic field ($B_{\rm total} \propto r^{-1.74}$), and temperature ($T \propto r^{-0.97}$) are comparable to those of the background solar wind. In the mature stage, the CIR drifts in longitude and expands toward high latitudes, while its longitudinal and latitudinal widths decrease with distance. The full-domain scalings change markedly to $V_r \propto r^{0.13}$, $N \propto r^{-1.91}$, $B_{\rm total} \propto r^{0.35}$, and $T \propto r^{-0.52}$, differing from both the background wind and the ecliptic-plane CIR. This study quantifies the radial evolution of a CIR from formation to maturity and highlights the importance of its three-dimensional structure in characterizing its morphology and dynamics.

17. Disentangling Magnetospheric and Colliding-Wind X-ray and Radio Emission in the Post-Mass-Transfer Magnetic Massive Binary Plaskett's Star[2611.19567]
Abstract

Plaskett's star is an O+O binary system harboring a kG-order magnetic field associated with the broad-lined secondary. It is the only O-type star believed to host a centrifugally supported magnetosphere. We present orbital- and rotational-phase resolved X-ray and low-frequency radio observations of Plaskett's star. X-ray observations were taken with Chandra using the High Energy Transmission Grating Spectrometer (HETGS), while the radio observations come from uGMRT and VLA. The X-ray light curve shows rotational modulation according to the magnetic component's rotation period, with maximum flux near magnetic minima. We confirm the decline in flux near orbital phase 0.5 where the slow rotator primary is in front of its magnetic companion. We used the $f/i$ ratios of Si XIII and Mg XI from high-resolution X-ray spectroscopy to infer the location of the plasma emitting X-rays. We find no statistically significant variation in line widths or centroid shifts with either orbital or rotational phase; however, given instrumental limitations, this non-detection may partly reflect observational constraints, while still suggesting a complex and extended X-ray emitting plasma distribution. We could not detect the star in the low-frequency radio bands (0.6–1.5 GHz). Re-analysis of existing Very Large Array data reveals an additional detection at 3 GHz, consistent with a nonthermal contribution predicted by magnetospheric models, although no direct signature was obtained. At low frequencies, nonthermal emission from the magnetosphere and the colliding-wind region is likely suppressed by free-free absorption in the winds of one or both components.

18. High-inclination Centaur or Captured Halley-Type Comet? Observational and Dynamical Characterization of C/2024 L1 (PANSTARRS)[2611.19581]
Abstract

The high-inclination (`high-i') Centaurs of the middle and outer Solar System are of uncertain origin. While many have been captured directly from the Oort Cloud, making them less-evolved analogues to the Halley-Type Comets, this process alone cannot produce the number of observed objects. The most discussed alternate origin is direct excitation of trans-Neptunian Objects into high-i orbits by the action of some unfound massive planet beyond Neptune. It is thus critical to find ways to discern the origins of these objects to provide new constraints on the various unfound planet hypotheses. This work assesses the only known active high-i Centaur: C/2024 L1 (PANSTARRS). Our dynamical investigations show that L1 has not decreased its semimajor axis in at least the past 1810 years, unlike all known TNO-derived active Centaurs at lower-i whose activity initiates after orbital changes. We also imaged L1 on five nights in the year before its April 2025 perihelion and found its brightness and optical colors to be stable and that its tail grew steadily. While this is like many Halley-type comets, few active low-i Centaurs have such stable activity. We thus propose that L1 is a captured Oort Cloud comet analogous to lower perihelion Halley-types and is thus a highly attractive target for large aperture follow up. The number and active fraction of these high-i objects should help us discern their origins and survey telescopes like Roman and Rubin could help us figure out where in the Outer Solar System the mystery lies.

19. Discovery of Ultra Fast Outflows in the Narrow Line Seyfert 1 Galaxy Mrk 110 with XMM-Newton and NuSTAR[2611.19612]
Abstract

We present an X-ray analysis of XMM-Newton and NuSTAR spectra of the Narrow-Line Seyfert 1 (NLS1) galaxy Mrk 110. Our analysis reveals the intriguing presence of a multi-component ultra fast outflow (UFO) both in the grating spectrum and the NuSTAR data. The moderate UFO significance (3-4$\sigma$), in the grating and CCD data indicates a shallow absorber, consistently with the absence of previous such claims in this source. The two components (UFO1 and UFO2) in the grating spectrum show a fairly wide range of velocities (0.05-0.06c), as their respective ionization state (Log U = -1.43 and -1.82), however they share the same column density (Log N$_H$ $\sim$ 19.7). In the NuSTAR spectrum, we found a much faster wind component with a velocity of $\sim$ 0.21c (UFO3). We show that, in an energy-driven wind scenario, UFO3 might carry enough energy to produce significant feedback on its host galaxy. Considering its known radio properties, Mrk 110 is therefore another Narrow-Line Seyfert 1 with stratified ultra-fast outflows co-existent with a pc-scale jet/outflow radio structure.

20. A new chemical network to explore the disequilibrium chemistry of silicates and iron in hot gas giants[2611.19638]
Abstract

Chemical equilibrium modeling of gas giant planet atmospheres has shown that the production and condensation of silicates plays an important role in shaping their final composition. However, no chemical kinetics network for these species are available, preventing the opportunity to analyze the impact of chemical disequilibrium processes. In this paper we present \texttt{SILANE}, a new chemical network that include Mg, Si, Fe and 15 carriers of these elements, including MgSiO$_3$ and Mg$_2$SiO$_4$, ready for use in the VULCAN code. We compare the results of our network with a chemical equilibrium model and discuss them in relation to previous results from literature. We then apply our new network to the analysis of a vertically mixed, Solar-composition atmosphere with equilibrium temperatures $T_{eq}$ in the [700, 1600] K range, finding that (i) rock-forming silicates disappears from the upper atmosphere already at 1100 K, (ii) SiH$_4$ reaches concentrations of $10^{-7}$ in the upper ($P\le 1.0$ bar) atmosphere for planets with $T_{eq}\in$ [800, 1100] K and (iii) the amount of oxygen in refractories decrease from 21\% at $T_{eq}$=700 K to essentially zero already at 1000 K, while in the classical equilibrium framework it remains constant up to $T_{eq}$=1300 K. By applying our network to the case of the young hot Jupiter YSES-1 c and comparing both the mixing ratios of volatile species and the enstatite fraction in the silicate mix, we find evidence of a well-mixed atmosphere, possibly with an average $K_{zz} \ge 10^{10}$ cm$^{2}$s$^{-1}$ up to the $10^{-4}$ bar level.

21. Magnetogenesis in Low-Scale Inflation: Viability for Astrophysical Magnetic Fields[2611.19721]
Abstract

The indirect evidence for magnetic fields in the intergalactic medium motivates the study of their possible primordial origin, including generation during inflation. One mechanism for inflationary magnetogenesis involves a pseudoscalar inflaton coupled to a gauge field through the interaction $g_{\phi\gamma}\phi F_{\mu\nu}\tilde{F}^{\mu\nu}$. In this work, we study the viability of this mechanism in the context of low scale inflation, particularly on inflationary energy scales near the electroweak (EW) scale. We find that the post inflationary amplification of the gauge fields can produce astrophysically relevant magnetic fields for $g_{\phi\gamma}\ge 360~m_{\rm pl}^{-1}$ in the absence of Schwinger backreaction for an inflationary scale around the EW scale. We then include the backreaction of the charged current generated through the Schwinger mechanism. We find that the induced current suppresses the gauge field amplification and leads to a saturation of the gauge field energy density at a value of order $m_e^4$, where $m_e$ is the electron mass. Assuming this saturation level, we find that magnetic fields above the lower bound inferred from the non detection of secondary GeV photons from blazars can be obtained when reheating occurs at temperatures below $\sim 0.3$ GeV. For an inflationary energy scale of $100~{\rm GeV}$, achieving this saturation until a reheating temperature, $T\le 0.3~{\rm GeV}$, requires a coupling strength $g_{\phi\gamma}\ge 20000~m_{\rm pl}^{-1}$. We obtain a present-day magnetic field strength of $B_0 \sim 2\times10^{-13}$ G with a coherence length of $L_{0}\sim 5\times10^{-3}$ kpc for the reheating temperature of $100$ MeV.

23. Polarization of Fast Radio Bursts due to Propagation in Magnetized Plasma[2611.19811]
Abstract

Polarization measurements of radio transients, particularly their frequency dependence, shed light on the magnetic environments of these sources. We extend the analytical solution of radiative transfer of the polarization properties of such transients to both a cold magnetized plasmas beyond the high-frequency approximation and a relativistic plasma with synchrotron emission. The Faraday mixing coefficients are enhanced makes Stokes parameters $Q$, $U$, and $V$ to exhibit a more rapid and complex frequency-dependent evolution. A relativistic plasma medium can also generate both Faraday roataion and conversion with a smaller magnetic field. Abstract

We study the low-frequency scattering of gravitational and pseudoscalar waves off a non-rotating black hole in dynamical Chern-Simons (dCS) gravity, where the axial (odd-parity) gravitational sector is irreducibly coupled to the dynamical pseudoscalar while the polar sector remains general relativistic. Working to first order in the dimensionless coupling zeta = (l_CS/M)^4 and using matched asymptotic expansions, we obtain, for the quadrupole (l=2) channel, an exact closed form for the graviton-to-pseudoscalar conversion probability and a high-precision analytic reconstruction of the absorption (greybody) asymmetry. Both reduce to combinations of pi^4, pi^2, and rational numbers, with no ln2 or odd-zeta terms in the reconstructed basis; the transmission-phase sector, by contrast, is governed by an analytically derived static integral involving Apery's constant zeta(3), with its matching prefactor identified numerically. The parity-violating reflection-phase asymmetry is strongly suppressed at low frequency – well below the parity-even general-relativistic reflection phase – so that any polarization rotation imprinted on the reflected wave is negligible, while a subleading O(omega*zeta) asymmetry remains in the transmitted phase. These effects are intrinsically strong-field and low-frequency: the normalized flux asymmetries are consistent with a 1/(M*omega)^2 falloff in the geometric-optics limit, where the leading remaining flux effect is conversion loss. The matched-asymptotic results are checked against direct two-channel integration, and the integral determining the absorption asymmetry is evaluated to high precision within an analytically controlled transcendental basis. The coefficients provide analytic targets linking dCS parity violation in absorption and mode conversion to the axial/polar quasinormal-mode splitting.
