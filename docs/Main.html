1. A Sub-Parsec Origin for Rapid Accretion-State Transitions in Radio-Loud AGN[2611.08497]
Abstract

I investigate the physical origin and timescale of accretion-state transitions in radio-loud active galactic nuclei, focusing on the evolution from FRII high-excitation radio galaxies (HERGs) to FRII low-excitation radio galaxies (LERGs) within the framework of Garofalo, Evans & Sambruna (2010). I show that the required transition timescale of about 4 million yr arises naturally if the accretion rate is regulated at sub-parsec radii (about 0.1-0.3 pc), corresponding to viscous evolution at about one thousand to ten thousand R_g for black hole masses 100 million to 1 billion solar masses. These radii coincide with the broad-line region and inner dusty torus, where multiphase gas dynamics regulate the effective inward mass flux on Myr timescales. I argue that powerful FRII jet feedback heats and perturbs the surrounding medium, reducing the effective mass supply reaching the inner disk. Once the accretion rate falls below the critical threshold m_crit about 0.01, the inner flow rapidly transitions to an advection-dominated accretion flow (ADAF). Observations of molecular gas reservoirs in radio galaxies are compatible with this picture because the transition requires suppression of inward fueling rather than complete removal of cold gas. I conclude that rapid, internally driven evolution provides a physically consistent basis for the FRII HERG -> FRII LERG transition while linking radio morphology, accretion state, and cosmic evolution.

2. Primordial or Post-Inflationary: Distinguishing the Origin of Scale-Dependent Features in the CMB[2611.08585]
Abstract

Physics beyond the Standard Model, whether of primordial or post-inflationary origin, can leave scale-dependent signals in the anisotropies of the Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB). In this work, we study how distinguishable these broad classes of signals are and quantify their level of degeneracy. In this initial investigation, for primordial features, we limit ourselves to those of the “sharp” and “resonant” kinds; for post-inflationary features, we consider two qualitatively distinct models: exotic energy injection, which deposits energy into the primordial plasma and modifies the global thermodynamic history, and early dark energy (EDE), which affects the CMB through the background expansion and gravitational perturbations. By studying representative examples, we find that while primordial features can leave oscillatory signatures in the CMB power spectrum with essentially arbitrary frequency and running, the post-inflationary models considered here produce qualitatively different patterns that cannot generically reproduce the characteristic running of primordial feature oscillations. We verify these findings first with a principal component analysis, and show using only the most significant principal components that there are no significant degeneracies between the primordial and post-inflationary features considered here, and secondly with a full likelihood analysis, which shows that the resulting posteriors do not change significantly when considering individual classes of models or combinations of primordial/post-inflationary features. While the two post-inflationary models probe distinct physical mechanisms and both are implemented in a simplified, phenomenological fashion, the methods used in this work can be generalized to include more realistic signals or other classes of signals.

3. Anisotropic three-point statistics of the peculiar velocity and galaxy density fields: modelling in Fourier and configuration space[2611.08589]
Abstract

By the equivalence principle, large-scale peculiar velocities provide a kinematical probe of the matter distribution independent of the biased galaxy distribution. Their three-point correlation with galaxies assesses the nonlinear coupling between cosmic flows and the surrounding density field. We develop a tree-level model of the anisotropic correlation between one line-of-sight peculiar velocity and two galaxy densities in redshift space, including quadratic and tidal galaxy bias. Starting from the corresponding velocity-divergence bispectrum, we derive predictions in both Fourier and configuration space through a spherical-harmonic expansion. We test the Fourier-space calculation against a closed-form expression and assess the numerical convergence of the configuration-space predictions. For a fiducial model, the signal is dominated by a dipolar dependence on triangle orientation relative to the line of sight, while subdominant multipoles distinguish the responses to growth and galaxy bias. In configuration space, the baryon acoustic oscillation (BAO) feature depends on which triangle side crosses the acoustic scale and on the velocity projection. We also relate the model to the density-weighted statistic measured by peculiar-velocity catalogues and formulate estimators for its observable multipoles. This framework supports future measurements and covariance analyses needed to establish the additional cosmological information carried by velocity-density three-point correlations.

4. Hydrodynamic Natal Kicks and Formation of Black Hole Binaries[2611.08604]
Abstract

The observation of over 300 Gravitational Wave (GW) events by the LIGO-Virgo-KAGRA collaboration, has yielded information of the merger of compact object binaries, such as their individual masses and spins. However, the effective spin parameter obtained from the GW signal provides important information that could be used to discern the binary's formation channel. Overall, binaries formed from dynamic interactions in dense stellar environments are expected to have a random distribution of effective spins between -1 and 1. Meanwhile, binaries formed from the evolution of isolated stellar binaries are expected to have positive effective spins unless there is a large enough natal kick during the compact object formation that could significantly alter the orientation of the individual spins with respect to the binary's angular momentum. We study the viability of obtaining large natal kicks during the formation of massive Black Holes by studying the asymmetric mild explosion of $13\text{M}_{\odot}$, $21\text{M}_{\odot}$, $28\text{M}_{\odot}$ and $34\text{M}_{\odot}$ pre-supernova stellar models, by means of 3D Smooth Particle Hydrodynamics simulations. Our results show that, even kicks in the order of $\sim$ 1000 km/s, which are among the highest values observed for natal kicks, can't consistently change the orientation of the orbit. Physical setups where the orientation of the orbit changes require the kick to be directed correctly, and they would also require for the supernova explosion energy to be quite high.

5. VENUS: Strong Lens Mass Models and Einstein Radii of 60 Galaxy Clusters[2611.08630]
Abstract

We present the strong lens mass modeling result of 60 galaxy clusters targeted by Vast Exploration for Nascent, Unexplored Sources (VENUS), the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) multi-epoch Treasury program. We obtain refined mass models for the 60 clusters with the glafic software by fitting positions of $\sim 4500$ multiple images, $\sim 1150$ of which are newly identified multiple images with help of multi-band JWST images. Each cluster contains typically $\sim 50$ multiple images, thanks to the selection of target clusters based on their high lensing strengths. We validate our models with the Jackknife method. The median Einstein radius is $\sim 30''$ and the distribution of the Einstein radii extends out to $\sim 60''$ for the source redshift of 9, which confirms that their lensing strengths are indeed among the highest known to date. For the source redshift of 9, we identify MACS J0717.5+3745, MACS J0600.1$-$2008, and PLCK G287.0+32.9 as clusters with the largest Einstein radii as well as the largest areas of the high-magnification ($|\mu|>10$) regions. We find that the observed Einstein radii of VENUS clusters are fully compatible with the standard cold dark matter model.

6. Short-term optical monitoring of quadruply imaged quasars[2611.08633]
Abstract

We present two case studies to demonstrate that short-term optical monitoring campaigns of quadruply imaged quasars (quads) reveal their astrophysical potential. Using the Liverpool Telescope and the third facility of the Two-meter Twin Telescope (TTT3), we monitored the quads QSO 2237+0305 and PS J0147+4630 in the gr bands between July 2025 and January 2026. For QSO 2237+0305, the resulting light curves revealed microlensing variability over a timescale of  150 days. The g-band difference light curve between images A and B was then used to reliably constrain the half-light radius of the g-band source within the 3.5-16 lt-day range (1{\sigma} confidence level). For PS J0147+4630, we derived new constraints on the AB and AC time delays from the r-band light curves of the ABC images in an arc-like configuration, obtaining 2{\sigma} uncertainties of  2 days. These constraints were used to shed light on the lensing mass distribution. Overall, our results validate the design of short-term optical monitoring campaigns of quads as valuable astrophysical tools in the era of large-scale surveys. The proof of concept relies on observations with ground-based 2mclass telescopes that lack sophisticated systems to correct for optical distortions caused by atmospheric turbulence, and we indicate the astro-photometric properties of the targets and the monitoring strategies needed to achieve success with these telescopes. Additionally, this work represents the first validation of the TTT3 facility for precision photometric monitoring of gravitationally lensed quasars.

7. Nonstandard Dark Matter at Nonlinear Scales[2611.08636]
Abstract

We investigate how two extensions of the standard $\Lambda$CDM scenario affect the evolution of the matter density and velocity divergence fields, from the linear regime to deeply nonlinear scales, using $N$-body simulations. We consider two well-motivated scenarios in which the canonical properties of dark matter (DM) are modified. In the first scenario, DM interacts with dark energy (DM–DE interaction). In the second scenario, we allow the DM equation-of-state parameter to deviate from the canonical pressureless limit, $w_{\rm DM}=0$. For the coupled DM–DE scenario, we find significant and scale-dependent deviations from $\Lambda$CDM of order $50$–$100\%$ in the matter power spectrum, persisting throughout the nonlinear regime, with the velocity divergence spectra providing complementary and partially independent sensitivity. For the $w_{\rm DM} \neq 0$ scenario, we find systematic but remarkably small deviations confined within $5\%$ across all scales probed. Our results identify which scales carry the greatest discriminatory power between these nonstandard scenarios and $\Lambda$CDM. We show that testing these models with current and forthcoming LSS surveys, through the analysis of density and/or velocity fields, will require a deeper theoretical understanding and accurate nonlinear modelling before robust constraints can be derived from observational data.

8. Testing influence of tides on gravitational wave emission with SPH code PHANTOM[2611.08642]
Abstract

In this article, we demonstrate how a Newtonian SPH (Smoothed Particle Hydrodynamics) code PHANTOM can be used to estimate the tidal contribution to gravitational wave (GW) waveforms. Using a binary neutron star (NS+NS) system with typical masses of 1.5 + 1.5 Msun as an example, we show - by comparing our results with waveforms calibrated against full numerical General Relativity (GR) simulations - that the tidal contribution to GWs can be reliably extracted using as few as about 10$^4$ smoothed particles. Naturally, one must account for the difference in neutron star compactness between Newtonian gravity and general relativity. At separations greater than 100 km, the relative tidal contribution falls below the level of numerical noise inherent in SPH; however, at such distances, GW waveforms for point-like sources are sufficient for detection purposes. For a white dwarf-neutron star (WD+NS) binary with typical masses of 0.6 Msun and 1.5 Msun respectively, a much larger number of smoothed particles is required, along with a separation on the order of tens of thousands of kilometers, as tidal mass transfer and the disruption of the white dwarf begin quite rapidly. Nevertheless, these challenges can be overcome, but the approach is not capable of yielding useful results due to the richness of Newtonian effects. In all cases, it is crucial to perform a hydrostatic/hydrodynamic relaxation (HPR) of the initial models; otherwise, the entire GW signal is lost in the numerical noise.

9. TitanMPAS - a new global and regional Titan atmospheric circulation model. Part I: Global model results[2611.08700]
Abstract

We developed TitanMPAS, a new Titan global and regional climate model, to simulate atmospheric dynamics and methane cycles. It integrates the non-hydrostatic MPAS dynamical core and TitanWRF physics parameterizations, with corrections to relax the thin-atmosphere assumptions on the gravity and curvature terms. TitanMPAS improves upon TitanWRF by incorporating a better moist convection parameterization and the observed surface properties. Long-term simulations ($>$100 Titan years) with a high resolution of $1^\circ$ and the realistic topography show that TitanMPAS is able to produce the stratospheric superrotation with typical equatorial wind speed over $\rm 120\,ms^{-1}$ and the maximum zonal wind speed exceeding $\rm 170\,ms^{-1}$ . Besides the good match of temperature profiles to the Cassini CIRS observations, the simulated wind, temperature and methane abundance in the troposphere match well with Huygens probe in-situ measurements. TitanMPAS also captures the major features of seasonal methane precipitation distribution patterns inferred from the observed cloud activities, as well as the vertical and latitudinal variations of methane abundance in the reanalysis of Cassini VIMS data. Resolution sensitivity studies show that higher resolution produces faster stratospheric superrotation, as well as stronger near-surface wind due to better resolved terrain variations. We further demonstrate that, despite the weak solar insolation, the near-surface temperature, pressure, wind magnitude and methane abundance all exhibit diurnal variations. Lastly, we show potential impacts of topography on methane transport and eddy momentum flux in both troposphere and stratosphere, which should be investigated in a future study.

10. Modified Newtonian Dynamics and galaxy clusters[2611.08702]
Abstract

First proposed by Milgrom in 1983 as an alternative to the standard Cold Dark Matter (CDM) paradigm, Modified Newtonian Dynamics (MOND) has gained renewed momentum in light of persistent non-detections of exotic dark matter particles and growing tensions facing CDM on galactic scales. While MOND perfectly reproduces galactic rotation curves, it encounters notable challenges in explaining the equilibrium of galaxy clusters. Although it reduces the overall dark matter budget in these massive systems, an unidentified mass component remains required. This paper investigates the hypothesis that this missing mass is baryonic: composed of non-radiating ordinary matter such as stellar remnants, gas too diffuse to initiate star formation, or compact, ultra-dense clouds of cold molecular gas with low volume filling factors. Using a sample of 106 clusters mapped in X-rays, emitted by the hot intra-cluster medium (ICM) composing most of the visible mass, we derive first the total mass within the Newtonian dynamics, assuming hydrostatic equilibrium and spherical symmetry. Then, with the modified Poisson equation, we reconstruct the spatial distribution of the invisible missing matter within the MOND framework. In the sample, the relaxed systems harboring cooling flows, known as cool-core clusters, have the highest peaks of dark missing mass. Its density profile reveals a pronounced central concentration bounded by a steep truncation immediately beyond a core radius of 20-30 kpc, confirming previous studies. MOND reduces the necessary dynamical mass by a factor of 4 to 10. The residual missing mass is proportional to the total gas mass, being roughly twice lower.

11. Coherent radio emission at 45 MHz from the nearby planet-hosting star GJ 725A[2611.08719]
Abstract

Ultra-low-frequency (<100 MHz) coherent radio emission from exoplanet-hosting stellar systems carries information about planetary and stellar magnetic fields, as well as the space weather environment - information that is difficult to obtain at other wavelengths. We carried out the circularly polarized LOFAR LBA Sky Survey, the most sensitive circularly polarized survey in this frequency range, using the LOFAR low-band antenna system. As part of this untargeted search, we detected a signal in a 1h observation that is coincident with the quiescent, planet-hosting, solar-neighborhood M dwarf GJ 725A at a distance of 3.5 pc. The signal has a high circular polarization fraction of $74\pm16$% and a peak brightness of $B=135\pm17$ mJy/beam between 42 MHz and 50 MHz. It was undetected in the 50-66 MHz range, with a 3$\sigma$ upper limit of $B<48$ mJy/beam, corresponding to a drop of at least a factor 3 across 12 MHz, and indicating a strong spectral feature or cutoff around 50 MHz. Our detection represents the lowest-frequency confirmed detection of a stellar system. Based on the high estimated brightness temperature ($\sim10^{14}$ K), transient character and polarization fraction, the emission is most plausibly attributed to the electron cyclotron maser instability. The available observations are not sufficient to identify the astrophysical origin of the emission. We consider three plausible scenarios: auroral emission of a yet undetected wide-orbit giant planet with a magnetic field $B\geq18$ G, star-planet interaction with the known 11 d orbit terrestrial planet GJ 725Ab, or an origin due to stellar magnetic activity. Ultra-low-frequency monitoring, combined with new, preferably simultaneous observations in the X-rays, UV, and H$\alpha$, and a search for a wide-orbit (thousands of days) giant planet across the full allowed parameter space will allow us to distinguish between these scenarios.

12. Constraining the Merger Fraction and the Upper Mass Limit of Ultramassive White Dwarfs[2611.08736]
Abstract

We performed a spectroscopic survey of all known white dwarfs with $M \geq 1.3~M_\odot$ and $T_{\rm eff}\geq11,000$ K identified through Gaia DR3 astrometry from the Gentile-Fusillo et al. (2021) catalog. Of the 293 white dwarf candidates that met our criteria, 259 had no optical spectroscopy or spectral classification available in the literature. We obtained follow-up spectroscopy on 97% of this sample and performed a detailed model atmosphere analysis to constrain the atmospheric composition and mass for each object. For a selection of magnetic white dwarfs, we use offset dipole models to constrain the magnetic field strength and geometry and calculate the magnetic fraction as a function of mass. We use magnetism, unusual atmospheric composition, kinematics, and rapid rotation rates to identify merger remnants, and constrain the merger fraction to be $53_{-6}^{+7}$% for white dwarfs more massive than $1.3~M_\odot$. Hence, roughly half of the most massive white dwarfs likely form through binary mergers. We also use our sample to derive an empirical upper mass limit for white dwarfs in the solar neighborhood. Including the General Relativistic effects, the most massive white dwarf in our sample, J0318+7923, has $M=1.33-1.35~M_\odot$ depending on the unknown core composition. Furthermore, because our temperature criteria overlap with the ZZ Ceti instability strip, we find a new selection of ultramassive pulsator candidates that may provide an excellent opportunity to probe the interiors of crystallized CO or ONe-core white dwarfs.

13. Neutrino pair annihilation in curved spacetime: a fast moment method and the geometry of flavour conversion[2611.08742]
Abstract

Neutrino-antineutrino annihilation above black-hole accretion discs is shaped both by general-relativistic transport and by neutrino flavour conversion. Whether these effects can be treated independently depends on how gravity weights the different flavour populations. We investigate this interplay using backward ray tracing in Schwarzschild and Kerr spacetimes, combined with an exact angular-moment formulation of the annihilation kernel that we validate against the analytic spherical solution and published disc calculations. We first establish that flavour-conversion corrections are independent of the spacetime geometry when conversion is uniform and the initial heavy-lepton neutrino emission is negligible. For uniform conversion, this separability remains approximately valid when the flavour-dependent emitting regions overlap: compared with the same discs in flat space, moving with the same local velocities, gravity enhances the deposited energy by factors of 1.4-5, while changing the relative conversion effect by no more than 6% for most temperature profiles considered; conversion that varies across the disc raises this to 22% around a rapidly spinning hole. A qualitatively different regime emerges when heavy-lepton neutrinos originate from an inner ring separated from the electron-flavour emitting regions. Relativistic bending and energy shifts preferentially enhance annihilations involving inner-ring neutrinos, producing relativistic conversion factors up to 2.4 times the flat-space ones. In one configuration, flavour equipartition reduces the deposition by 54% in flat space but increases it by 11% in Kerr spacetime. These results identify the spatial arrangement of flavour-dependent neutrinospheres as a key determinant, in the models considered, of whether flavour conversion and relativistic transport can be treated separately.

14. The Complex Magnetic Anatomy of a White Dwarf Chromosphere[2611.08766]
Abstract

This study presents phase-resolved spectroscopy and spectropolarimetry of the DAHe white dwarf LP 705-64. Here, two apparently discrete chromospheric regions are observed to alternate in visibility and magnetic polarity with rotation, with field strengths of 9.5 and 22 MG based on the measured Zeeman splitting in H{\alpha}. As in the class prototype GD 356, emission line equivalent widths vary in antiphase with the broadband light curve. The 9.5 MG field is visible for 60 per cent of the stellar rotation and stays nearly constant, whereas the 22 MG field increases modestly during the remaining phases. Notably, the time-series transitions between magnetic regions are abrupt and transit-like in morphology, requiring at least one magnetic region to be highly localised and significantly smaller than the areas inferred for the dark surface spots. Both the intensity and the Stokes V/I spectra indicate a field configuration significantly more complex than an offset dipole, but higher-cadence data are necessary to distinguish between a global field and localised magnetic regions. All data are consistent with magnetic, chromospheric, and dark surface regions that are co-located and thus physically related. These findings support a model of chromospheric emission that is intrinsic and localised, consistent with predictions of dynamo-driven electrical currents that heat the upper atmosphere near magnetic poles.

15. Bicoherence States from QPO-Accretion Flow Coupling in Black Hole X-ray Binaries[2611.08769]
Abstract

We calculate the bispectrum of a black hole X-ray binary light curve model where lognormal fluctuations associated with the inner flow are multiplicatively modulated by a quasi-periodic oscillation (QPO) and its harmonic. The bispectrum is a third-order Fourier statistic which measures the strength of nonlinear relationships between frequencies in a timeseries. When applied to black hole X-ray binaries, the bispectrum reveals nonlinear coupling between QPOs and the broadband noise. These couplings are sorted into a small set of states and X-ray binaries are observed to transition between these states as they undergo spectral transitions. We show that the model we consider is able to reproduce all of the canonical bispectral states analytically. We consider other sources of incoherent variability which dilute this model light curve and find that changes in this dilution, together with changes in the QPO amplitude, drive the transitions between the bispectral states. We also show that the larger QPO amplitudes of high-inclination sources favor the cross state and the weaker QPOs of low-inclination sources favor the hypotenuse state, which can explain the empirically observed correlation. Additionally, any dilution by equatorial winds, which are seen mainly at high inclination, would push high-inclination sources further toward the cross state.

16. Modeling of the X-ray Flux and Polarization Energy Spectra of Black Hole X-Ray Binaries with the KerrC Code[2611.08882]
Abstract

In this chapter, we introduce the kerrC model, an advanced framework designed for the analysis of combined spectral and polarimetric X-ray emission from black hole systems. The model is built upon an extensive dataset of ray-tracing Monte Carlo simulations that compute the radiative transfer of X-rays in the strongly curved spacetime near the event horizon of a Kerr black hole. These simulations incorporate thermal seed emission from a geometrically thin, optically thick accretion disk, along with the subsequent Comptonization processes within a hot corona of variable geometry and physical properties. Crucially, the reflection of both primary disk and Comptonized photons is treated self-consistently utilizing the xillver reflection tables, and the polarization 4-vectors of the photons are rigorously transported throughout the simulation to preserve full polarization information. The code tracks photons forward in time, allowing for multiple photon scatterings in the corona and off the accretion disk. We detail the underlying mechanics of the ray-tracing simulations and the fitting models, and discuss the applications of kerrC in leveraging X-ray polarization observations to constrain black hole spin and coronal geometry.

17. Probing Dark Matter via the Migdal Effect in LUX-ZEPLIN Data[2611.09106]
Abstract

Sub-GeV dark matter (DM) particles deposit little energy in conventional direct-detection experiments and are therefore difficult to probe, but the Migdal effect provides an electronic signal from atomic ionization accompanying the recoil. Using the new results of the LUX-ZEPLIN experiment with a 4.2 tonne-year exposure, we evaluate the sensitivity of the Migdal effect to the spin-independent DM–nucleon scattering cross section. For masses between $350~\mathrm{MeV}$ and $10~\mathrm{GeV}$, the projected 90\% upper limits range from $6.5\times10^{-37}$ to $9.6\times10^{-42}~\mathrm{cm^2}$. These limits are then used to constrain the minimal scalar- and vector-mediator DM models. For the light-mediator DM model, the constraint on the coupling reaches $\alpha\simeq 10^{-12}$, which is stronger than or comparable to the constraints from kaon decays, stellar cooling, and SN1987A.

18. Chromospherically Active Stars in the MeerKAT Galactic Plane Survey[2611.09211]
Abstract

Stellar magnetic activity has long been established to occur in subsets of late-type main-sequence stars and manifests itself in the form of chromospheric and coronal activity. This activity is driven by magnetic fields generated by dynamo mechanisms associated with stellar rotation and convection, which channel and dissipate magnetic energy to the outer atmospheric layers. Optical spectroscopic follow-up was carried out for 99 radio-X-ray coronally active stars of G-, K-, and M-spectral types in the SARAO MeerKAT Galactic plane survey point sources. There are 46 stars that exhibited chromospheric activity. Specifically, 39 stars show Ca II H&K emission lines, whereas 18 show Ca II IRT, 11 show both H&K and IRT lines. Of the 39 stars displaying H&K emission, 37 show activity levels greater than the Solar minimum activity. Their X-ray to bolometric luminosity ratios are within the coronal thresholds of > -4.5. In addition, we explored optical photometry to investigate the rotation information and found that these stars show short rotation periods; in comparison with activity levels, the activity levels dampen as the rotation periods increase. We also analysed optical light curves for a subset of stars, and they show flaring behaviour at the same epoch as enhanced chromospheric activity, indicating the flares and chromospheric emission are manifestations of the same underlying magnetic activity process.

19. Quasi-spherical fallback in binary neutron-star mergers: fuel and obstacle for extended emission[2611.09216]
Abstract

Extended emission (EE) lasting hundreds of seconds has been observed in a fraction of short gamma-ray bursts (sGRB) containing both nonthermal and thermal components, and representing a significant puzzle for models involving binary neutron stars (BNSs). Recent GRMHD simulations of BNS mergers found that the late-time fallback is approximately quasi-spherical and motivated an r-process powered thermal model for EE. However, the available radioactive-heating power may be insufficient to sustain the luminosity inferred by such a model. We therefore propose a fallback-fed secondary-jet scenario in which a fraction of the fallback material circularizes on the black-hole equatorial plane and powers the jet, while polar fallback impedes its propagation. Using a semi-analytic toy model, we follow the propagation of the jet-head through the fallback envelope and identify jet-dominated, cocoon-dominated, and choked scenarios. The boundaries of these regimes depend primarily on the circularization fraction $f_{\rm circ}$, on the jet half-opening angle $\theta_j$, and on the polar refilling factor $\xi_{\rm pol}$, with the approximate scaling $\widetilde{L}\propto f_{\rm circ}/(\xi_{\rm pol}\theta_j^2)$. By post-processing data from BNS-merger simulations we find that the fraction of bound fallback material that avoids prompt capture is $f_{\rm npc}\simeq0.52$, thus providing an upper bound on the circularizable fallback supply. Under suitable prescriptions for nonthermal jet emission and thermal emission from the jet head, the estimated isotropic-equivalent $0.3$–$10$ keV energies reproduce well both the observed EE distribution for the jet-dominated branch and its low-energy end for the cocoon-dominated branch. Overall, these results suggest that fallback-fed secondary jets represent a dynamically feasible and energetically consistent explanation of the EE in sGRBs.

20. Successive Decimetric Bursts Associated with Breakout Magnetic Reconnection during the Decay Phase of an M-Class Flare[2611.09332]
Abstract

Radio imaging spectroscopy diagnoses coronal electron acceleration and propagation by tracking energetic electron beams and locating energy-release sites. Using MUSER (0.4-2 GHz) and SDO data, we investigate decimetric radio emission during an eruption with an M1.1 flare on 17 December 2014. The eruption involves a twisted coronal structure evolving through three stages: a violent internal eruption, a rise-and-expansion phase, and a breakout eruption. The flare peaks at 01:50 UT during the internal eruption, while the cluster of successive type III-like decimetric bursts occurs in the decay phase, linked to the breakout eruption. The radio source coincides with a high-squashing-factor Q region from nonlinear force-free field (NLFFF) extrapolations, indicating a quasi-separatrix layer (QSL). We interpret the radio emission as consistent with plasma emission driven by nonthermal electrons accelerated by breakout reconnection at this QSL, between the outer envelope of the erupting structure and the surrounding corona. The successive bursts occur in the decay phase because breakout reconnection involves a larger, more energetic twisted structure at higher altitude, where an extended high-Q region, favorable acceleration geometry, and repeated sub-eruptions sustain efficient electron acceleration. Missing soft X-ray enhancement suggests most accelerated electrons escape upward or lose energy before reaching the chromosphere. The concentrated source positions of the successive radio bursts suggest pseudostreamers may act as particle accelerators, trapping electrons and directing them along a narrow layer. These results establish decimetric radio imaging as a powerful diagnostic of breakout reconnection and particle acceleration in the solar corona, with implications for energy release in pseudostreamer environments.

21. GW230729\_082317 and GW231118\_005626: A Candidate of Hierarchical Triple Mergers[2611.09338]
Abstract

Hierarchical triple mergers denote a sequential merger process in which the black hole remnant from an initial binary merger subsequently merges with a third black hole. In this study, we identify a related pair of binary black hole mergers, GW230729\_082317 and GW231118\_005626, which exhibit consistent sky localization and luminosity distances; the latter is identified as a candidate hierarchical merger binary back holes. The Bayes factor in favor of a hierarchical triple merger origin over the non-association hypothesis is $110$. However, after accounting for the priors odds, the corresponding odds ratio decreases to $0.1$, indicating that the current evidence is insufficient to favor the hierarchical triple merger scenario. GW230720\_0823 show no detectable signatures of orbit eccentricity or line-of-sight acceleration. The inferred properties of these two gravitational wave events suggest a dynamical formation channel, such as few-body scattering or gas-assisted mergers, while a primordial triple origin is disfavored. Among the proposed dynamical environments, the accretion disk of an active galactic nucleus provides the most plausible site. We further derive constraints on the local rate of hierarchical triple mergers rate under both the null and candidate scenarios. Under the assumption of no detected hierarchical triple mergers, the most stringent $90\%$ confidence upper limit is $4.2\,{\rm Gpc^{-3}yr^{-1}}$ for $t_{\rm max}=10$ years using the GWTC-4 catalog.

22. Activity of the ejected galactic nuclei and cosmic rays[2611.09343]
Abstract

The double and triple approaches of supermassive central black holes are a fairly common process in the hierarchical picture of galaxy formation. In this case, the ejection of a black hole is possible; it may carry the accretion disk and some amount of stars with it. The activity of the galactic nucleus can be restored after the ejection. One consequence of this activity is an ultrafast gas outflow, accompanied by a shock wave. We show that the acceleration of cosmic rays at such a shock is more effective than in the case of a stationary galactic nucleus due to the lower density of the surrounding gas and the higher velocity of the shock front. In this case, proton acceleration can occur up to energies of  10^{19} eV, which is an order of magnitude higher than in an ordinary nucleus in the centers of galaxies.

23. Existence of Distinct Strong and Weak Shear Regimes of Self-Generated Turbulent Magnetic Reconnection[2611.09354]
Abstract

Magnetic reconnection can become fast in three-dimensional turbulent layers. However, the parameters and mechanisms that control the fast state are not yet fully clear. Existing guide-field simulations of self-generated turbulent reconnection show locally coherent flux-rope structures embedded in a stochastic layer, motivating a flux-rope-mediated picture, with reconnection rates of order $V_{\rm rec}/V_A \sim 0.01$ in MHD. Here, we test whether and how magnetic shear changes the reconnection regime, using 3D resistive-MHD simulations at $S_L=10^5$. We vary the guide field while keeping the initial reconnecting field strength and rate normalization fixed, comparing a guide-field, weaker-shear configuration with the anti-parallel, strong-shear limit. We find that the strong-shear regime is qualitatively distinct from the flux-rope-mediated guide-field regime. Rather than retaining a narrow layer and locally coherent flux-rope structures, the current layer develops short-wavelength corrugations that broaden it substantially, and the reconnection layer electric field is sustained by fluctuation EMF. The reconnection rate increases to $V_{\rm rec}/V_A \sim 0.03$, about three times the guide-field value. These results indicate that magnetic shear can select the mechanism controlling fast 3D turbulent reconnection, shifting the system from a guide-field regime consistent with flux-rope mediation toward a very fast turbulence-broadened regime. This transition provides a novel explanation for the sudden switch-on of fast reconnection in eruptive solar flares.

24. Primordial non-Gaussianity as a large-scale cosmic-web bias[2611.09362]
Abstract

Primordial non-Gaussianity is a unique probe of the physics of inflation, but its large-scale-structure signature is difficult to measure because it appears on the largest, most cosmic-variance-limited modes. Abstract

74. Challenging the Weak Cosmic Censorship with Phantom Fields[2603.13210]
Abstract

Penrose's weak cosmic censorship conjecture asserts that spacetime singularities produced by gravitational collapse are generically hidden behind event horizons, thus preventing them from causally influencing distant observers and preserving the predictability of the exterior region. In this work, we probe this conjecture in a setup that deliberately violates one of its central assumptions - the dominant energy condition - by considering the spherical collapse of a phantom scalar field with negative energy density. In principle, such a field could produce a Schwarzschild geometry with negative mass and therefore no event horizon. Our aim is to assess whether, once the dominant energy condition is abandoned, the fully coupled evolution of matter and geometry can dynamically generate or expose naked singularities, thereby probing the robustness of cosmic censorship. To this end, we perform high-accuracy numerical relativity simulations based on fourth-order finite-difference schemes. Starting from smooth, asymptotically flat initial data representing regular phantom scalar wave packets, we follow their fully nonlinear evolution through collapse or dispersion. While an ordinary (positive-energy) scalar field exhibits the standard Choptuik critical behavior at the threshold of black-hole formation, the phantom field displays qualitatively different dynamics. For all amplitudes considered, we find no evidence for trapped surfaces, naked singularities, or alternative stationary end states. Instead, the phantom scalar field disperses in all the configurations studied. Within the parameter space explored, we find no dynamical violation of cosmic censorship.

75. Relativistic mode sums for neutron-star tidal response[2605.08569]
Abstract

Time-dependent tidal interactions during the late inspiral of binary neutron stars encode valuable information about neutron-star structure, but systematically extending the familiar Newtonian mode-sum picture into full general relativity is nontrivial. In this paper, we develop a practical relativistic implementation of mode-sum tidal response for non-rotating neutron stars in Regge-Wheeler gauge. Using near-zone boundary conditions, we systematically define the interior tidal field, the relativistic overlap integrals, and the corresponding mode amplitudes. The good is that the dominant f-mode contribution is remarkably robust, reproducing the direct matching calculation to within $\sim 3$\% across the equations of state we consider. The bad is that the operator governing mode inner product is not positive definite on the full Regge-Wheeler-gauge function space, so the relativistic mode sum truncated at ${\cal{O}}(\omega^2)$ is not expected to strictly converge to the direct matching solution. The subtle is that the tidal field inside the star is not unique, although this ambiguity has only a limited impact on the dominant f-mode response for the classes of extensions studied here. Our results establish the practical utility of relativistic mode-sum approximations, while making clear that their predictive power comes from a controlled low-mode description, rather than from a formally convergent strong-field expansion.

Abstract

Abstract

Newborn proto-neutron stars (PNS) are potential targets of gravitational wave asteroseismology, the study of the (inner) structure of stars via their oscillation modes. To prepare for the eventual detection of such modes by current and future gravitational wave observatories, theoretical studies have obtained the possible spectrum of oscillations. However, there has been disagreement when it comes to identifying and classifying specific modes according to the physical mechanism that excites them. In this paper, we present a novel scheme to classify the oscillation modes of a newly born PNS surrounded by a stalled accretion shock in core-collapse supernovae (CCSNe). Our classification is physically motivated, as it is based on the energy of the restoring forces of the mode. We investigate the nature of the modes by considering the different regions of the CCSN that they stem from. We apply this scheme to a set of 28 non-rotating 1D, 2D and 3D CCSN simulations performed with two different numerical codes and using different progenitors and equations of state. We also find that the dominant high-frequency emission mode is the f-mode of the PNS, associated with the strong density gradient at its surface. Abstract

Detecting quadratic quasi-normal modes in black hole ringdowns would provide evidence for nonlinear gravitational dynamics, while measuring their properties would enable tests of the corresponding predictions of general relativity. Specifically, second-order black hole perturbation theory predicts that their amplitudes scale with the product of the amplitudes of their parent linear modes, with a coupling coefficient that depends on the spin of the remnant black hole. This mode-specific coupling coefficient has not yet been directly measured from gravitational wave data. Here, we use Bayesian inference on the GW250114 ringdown, modeled with the $220$, $221$, and $220\times220$ modes, to infer the amplitude-coupling coefficient of the $220\times220$ mode.
