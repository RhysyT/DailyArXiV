1. De-lensing cosmic birefringence with joint reconstruction[2611.03761]
Abstract

Reconstruction of anisotropic cosmic birefringence from the CMB is limited by lensing $B$-mode sample variance and partially degenerate with the lensing curl. We study the joint reconstruction of lensing (gradient and curl) and birefringence at the maximum-a-posteriori point, whose spectra are near-optimal statistics. We predict the joint response and noise analytically, validate them on simulations, and show unbiased inference of its amplitude. Errors will shrink $5$-fold relative to quadratic estimators at SPT-3G+ depths, promising much tighter constraints on parity-violating new physics, owing to the removal of the lensing $B$-polarization sample variance.

2. The formation of the first cosmic structures in hybrid Milgromian cosmology[2611.03770]
Abstract

Tackling cosmology with Milgromian Dynamics (MOND) has been a challenge ever since its theoretical inception. In a hybrid MOND scheme, a hypothesized light sterile neutrino of $\approx$ 11 eV/$c^2$ rest-mass is able to provide realistic initial conditions, fitting the third peak of the Planck CMB power spectrum as a Hot Dark Matter (HDM) component. At the same time, MOND governs the gravitational dynamics of density perturbations, while the background expansion follows the standard FLRW metric. We conduct and analyze hydrodynamical simulations of the fiducial $\Lambda$CDM model ($H_0 = 67.8$ km/s/Mpc, $\Omega_m \approx 0.31$, $\Omega_{\text{CDM}} \approx 0.26$), the progenitor $\nu$HDM model ($H_0 = 67.64$ km/s/Mpc, $\Omega_m = 0.3, \Omega_{\nu}\approx 0.26$), and its optimized version (opt-$\nu$HDM model, $H_0 \approx 55.6$ km/s/Mpc, $\Omega_m \approx 0.49$, $\Omega_{\nu} \approx 0.42$), utilizing the publicly available codes RAMSES and Phantom of RAMSES. We compare for the first time the total matter power spectrum $P(k)$ of the hydrodynamical simulations at various redshifts, examining the systematic differences of the three investigated cosmologies. The redshift evolution of the power spectrum of the $\Lambda$CDM model compared to the $\nu$HDM variants' counterpart offers important insight about the onset of structure formation. The combination of a light sterile neutrino and MOND gravity exceeds the $\Lambda$CDM model's matter $P(k)$ over all collapsing scales at late times ($z\lesssim 1$). The density and velocity fields reveal a distinct difference between the underlying laws of gravity. Regardless of the modeled dependence in the jointly estimated data from Planck, DES Y1, SDSS, and eBOSS, the $\nu$HDM hybrid models seem to produce an excess of power at associated resolution scales due to their cosmological design.

3. 3D dark matter map of the JWST COSMOS-Web field using diffusion-based generative priors[2611.03786]
Abstract

Mapping the three-dimensional distribution of dark matter is key to tracing the growth of cosmic structure. Two-dimensional weak lensing mass maps recover its projected distribution, but they blend structures at different distances along the line of sight. We reconstruct the three-dimensional matter distribution in the 0.54 deg$^2$ COSMOS-Web field using deep James Webb Space Telescope imaging, with an effective source density of 121 galaxies arcmin$^{-2}$ We combine a physical weak lensing forward model with a diffusion-based Bayesian generative prior learned from cosmological simulations, yielding a posterior distribution of the matter field across 20 lens planes to $z \simeq 2$, doubling the redshift reach of previous Hubble Space Telescope-based 3D reconstructions and approaching cosmic noon, an epoch of rapid galaxy growth. The reconstruction separates structures that overlap in projection and recovers non-Gaussian matter structure across a range of spatial scales, including over-densities associated with independently identified galaxy clusters that were not detected in earlier weak lensing maps. Posterior sampling quantifies the range of three-dimensional matter distributions allowed by the data, enabling a probabilistic visualization of the cosmic web while propagating uncertainties in its inferred structure. Null and simulation-based tests show that the recovered structures are supported by the lensing signal and are consistent with expectations from the fiducial $\Lambda$CDM cosmology. These results extend three-dimensional weak lensing mapping into the high-redshift, non-Gaussian regime and establish a route towards probabilistic mapping of the evolving cosmic web with current wide-field lensing surveys.

4. The Hot Neptune Initiative (HONEI): IV. TOI-4129 b, an Adolescent Neptune in the Ridge[2611.03810]
Abstract

Recent exoplanet demographics studies have uncovered the Neptune ridge: an overdensity of intermediate-size planets at orbital periods between 3–6 d. The origin of this pile-up remains unclear, but it could be a consequence of high-eccentricity migration (HEM). Here, we confirm TOI-4129 b, a planet that may help to reveal the origin of the Neptune ridge. Using radial velocities from HARPS-N and transit photometry from TESS and four ground-based telescopes, we found that TOI-4129 b has a relatively low density ($R_p = 5.7 \pm 0.2\, R_\oplus$, $M_p = 26\pm 6 \,M_\oplus$, $\rho_p = 0.8 \pm 0.2 ~\textrm{g}~\textrm{cm}^{-3}$) and orbits a $V = 12.9$ K dwarf star every 3.27 d, placing it at the inner edge of the Neptune ridge. We detect the stellar rotation period at 12.7 d in the photometry, implying a fairly young gyrochronal age of $1.20^{+0.16}_{-0.30}~$Gyr for the system. With a Transmission Spectroscopy Metric of $112\pm 30$, this adolescent Neptune is a good target for atmospheric observations with \textit{JWST} and \textit{Ariel}, and we also find that it is amenable to future constraints on the (three-dimensional) stellar obliquity. TOI-4129 b will therefore be an important testing ground for the idea that planets in the Neptune ridge underwent HEM to arrive at their current locations.

5. Towards Self-consistent Modeling of Quasi-Stars and their NLTE Spectra: Implications for Little Red Dots[2611.03819]
Abstract

Quasi-stars, massive envelopes surrounding rapidly accreting black hole (BH) seeds, are promising candidates for explaining the rapid formation of supermassive black holes in the early Universe and may account for some of the JWST-discovered Little Red Dots (LRDs). We present a self-consistent framework combining stellar evolution calculations with non-local thermodynamic equilibrium (NLTE) spectral synthesis to test this connection. Our models drop the polytropic assumptions of previous work: the envelope is described entirely by mixing-length theory, with saturated convection and radiative energy setting the luminosity at an inner boundary a few hundred Schwarzschild radii from the BH. This removes the BH-mass limits of earlier models, and we follow the evolution up to BH-to-total mass ratios approaching unity. We then use TLUSTY to compute the first converged NLTE atmospheric models for the surface conditions and primordial hydrogen-helium composition expected in quasi-stars. Our models produce cool photospheres, prominent Balmer breaks, and Balmer and Paschen absorption features. NLTE effects substantially modify line strengths, increasing Balmer absorption depths by $\sim 35-55\%$ relative to LTE models, while having a minor effect on the continuum. Comparison with observed LRD spectral energy distributions shows broad agreement in continuum shape, although our models do not reproduce their commonly observed strong emission lines. Under the quasi-star interpretation, comparisons with individual LRDs suggest characteristic quasi-star masses of approximately $10^5-10^7 M_\odot$. These results establish a physically motivated connection between quasi-star evolution and observable spectra, providing a framework for testing quasi-stars as progenitors of LRDs while identifying limitations of current atmospheric models.

6. XMM-Newton and NuSTAR quasi-simultaneous follow-up of the very high energy flare of PKS 1725+123 in August 2025[2611.03829]
Abstract

We report the results of the spectral timing analysis of the quasi-simultaneous X-ray follow-up with XMM-Newton ($50\,\rm ks$) and NuSTAR ($180\,\rm ks$) of the very high energy flare of the blazar PKS 1725+123 ($z=0.586$) in August of 2025. The X-ray monitoring started $\sim3$ days after the announcement of the VHE flare by LST-1, MAGIC and H.E.S.S. and lasted for almost 5 days. The high sensitivity of XMM-Newton reveals two spectral components, a soft ($\Gamma_{\rm X}>2$) and a hard ($\Gamma_{\rm X}<2$) power law. While the soft component dominates at $E<2\,\rm keV$, the hard component dominates at $E>2\,\rm keV$, likely marking the transition between the synchrotron and the high-energy emission, the "valley" of this blazar's spectral energy distribution. Within the XMM-Newton pointing ($\sim14$ hours) we witness the shift of the synchrotron component out of the X-ray band. In the following $\sim 4$ days, NuSTAR further reveals the brightening by a factor of 1.5 of the hard component, following the shift of the high-energy part of the SED into the X-ray band. Overall, within a week (observed frame) of the VHE flare, PKS 1725+123 went from temporarily being an intermediate synchrotron peaked blazar to transitioning back to its `normal' state of a low synchrotron peaked one. This is the first time that the evolution of a VHE flare from a blazar jet has been followed continuously in X-rays over such an extended period and our results highlight the importance of long-term quasi-simultaneous X-ray follow-up of VHE blazar flares.

7. SS 433 Eastern Jet Turbulence Revealed by Spatially-Resolved IXPE Data Analysis[2611.03846]
Abstract

The extended X-ray jet (EXJ) of SS 433/W50 shows hard X-ray and very-high-energy gamma-ray emission, indicating strong particle acceleration, likely sensitive to the EXJ magnetic field structure and turbulence. To study this field, we re-analyze the 2023 Imaging X-ray Polarimetry Explorer (IXPE) observation of the eastern EXJ using the publicly available polarization fitting package LeakageLib. Compared with standard PCUBE and spectropolarimetric analyses, this approach improves the polarization constraints and enables spatially resolved polarization measurements. Dividing the EXJ into head, spine, and sheath regions with independent polarization properties, we find significant (>3 sigma) improvement over a single-zone model. The sheath exhibits lower polarization than the head and spine, while the electric vector position angles are consistent across all regions, indicating magnetic fields aligned with the jet axis. The measurements imply enhanced magnetic turbulence at the jet boundary, potentially driven by Kelvin-Helmholtz instabilities.

8. Shadowing the Sun I: Testing Synthetic Granulation Spectra with NEID Solar Eclipse Observations[2611.03898]
Abstract

The subtle Doppler radial velocity (RV) signature of an Earth-mass exoplanet in the Habitable Zone around a nearby Sun-like star is difficult to detect due to stellar variability. While multiple techniques aim to mitigate rotationally-modulated stellar activity, granulation, introducing 0.1-1 m s$^{-1}$ RV shifts, currently sets the floor for detecting Earth-mass RV signals for low-activity stars. Observations of the Rossiter-McLaughlin (RM) effect during a solar eclipse, with a peak-to-trough amplitude of $\sim$1.4 km s$^{-1}$, are a rigorous test of granulation and convective blueshift in the solar spectrum. Using NEID solar feed data of the October 2023 solar eclipse and GRASS-E (a geometry-modified version of GRASS), we compare the observed RVs for 22 lines with the simulated RVs from the synthetic granulation-driven spectra. Residuals of the GRASS-E Fe I 5434 angstrom model show an RMS of 13.47 m s$^{-1}$ over the RM signal. We find that a model with an optimized convective blueshift polynomial for Fe I 5434 significantly outperforms GRASS-E with an RMS of only 2.90 m s$^{-1}$, suggesting that GRASS-E is currently limited by sparse center-to-limb observations. By combining a set of spectral lines, we achieve a weighted RMS of 5.2 m s$^{-1}$ over the RM signal ($\sim$0.37\%), a level of accuracy well beyond the reach of current exoplanet transit observations. In all, we demonstrate that solar eclipse observations allow us to recover the center-to-limb variation of the convective blueshift for individual spectral lines.

9. Protostellar evolution of intermediate-mass stars: implications for the survival of fossil magnetic fields[2611.03919]
Abstract

The detection of strong magnetic fields in radiative stars, particularly chemically peculiar Ap/Bp stars, has motivated various scenarios regarding their origin, including the fossil field hypothesis. Because field survival depends strongly on stellar internal structure, determining when protostars transition from convective to radiative states is essential to assess whether fossil fields persist. We determine the characteristic mass at which intermediate-mass protostars transition from convective to radiative interiors, exploring how this transition favors magnetic field survival. Using the 1D stellar evolution code MESA, we model the pre-main-sequence evolution of intermediate-mass protostars from convective initial seeds across different metallicities, average accretion rates, and accretion histories. The transition mass from predominantly convective to radiative states depends on both accretion rate and metallicity, occurring between $\sim 1.3\,M_\odot$ and $2.8\,M_\odot$ for $1\,M_\odot$ reference models (and up to $\sim 4\,M_\odot$ for other initial masses at high accretion rates). Accretion history details play a secondary role compared to the average rate and metallicity. Lower metallicities shift the transition to lower masses. For $Z \ge 0.01$ and accretion rates above $4 \times 10^{-6}\,M_\odot\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}$, models retain convective envelopes up to the end of accretion, potentially dissipating accreted magnetic flux. Because this transition typically occurs above $\sim 1.3\,M_\odot$, our results help explain the preferential survival of fossil fields in intermediate-mass stars, provided the radiative structure persists post-accretion. We also predict an increased incidence of strongly magnetized radiative stars at metallicities below $Z=0.01$.

10. A Wolf-Rayet nebula in its disruption phase: high-resolution radio observations of PCG 11[2611.03954]
Abstract

(abridged abstract) Wolf-Rayet stars have high mass-loss rates and fast stellar winds. Their interaction with circumstellar material from previous evolutionary phases can produce complex nebular morphologies. Hydrodynamic instabilities can fragment wind-swept material, producing condensations, filaments, and gaps that alter the confinement of stellar wind and ionizing radiation. The role of fragmentation in the late evolution and dispersal of WR nebulae remains poorly constrained. PCG 11, with its fragmented morphology and regular scalloped rim, offers an opportunity to investigate these processes. We aim to characterize the structure of PCG 11 and investigate its ring-like morphology and scalloped rim. We constrain its three-dimensional geometry and determine whether the optical fragmentation pattern is also traced by ionized gas. We obtained high-resolution radio continuum images with MeerKAT at 2.6 and 3.1 GHz and ATCA at 5.5 and 9.0 GHz. The radio emission reveals a hollow, equatorially enhanced structure viewed close to its symmetry axis. The images resolve the rim into condensations, low-brightness gaps, and finger-like protrusions. We quantify the fragmentation scale and relate it to the nebular geometry. We also detect an unresolved source coincident with WR 75ab, whose rising radio spectrum is consistent with thermal wind emission. The nebula processes only a small fraction of the stellar ionizing radiation, indicating substantial photon leakage. PCG 11 captures a poorly observed stage in massive-star bubble evolution: the swept-up shell has developed a coherent instability pattern and become fragmented and porous, while remaining dynamically coupled to the stellar wind. It provides a clear view of how fragmentation and erosion reshape the circumstellar environment during a transitional evolutionary phase.

11. Quasi-normal Modes and Instabilities of Rotating Relativistic Stars[2611.03986]
Abstract

We derive the basic equations suitable for the coupled fluid–spacetime eigenvalue problem from the second-order action in full general relativity and accurately determine quasi-normal modes of a variety of rapidly rotating stars with outgoing-wave boundary conditions. For MIT-bag strange stars, the spectrum realizes Chandrasekhar's instability picture, with distinct Chandrasekhar–Friedman–Schutz and Jacobi-like neutral conditions and the transition to dynamical bar-mode growth. For neutron stars, we determine the $f$-, $r$-, and other inertial-mode spectra and their radiative damping or growth times. Interactions between $f$- and inertial modes reveal avoided crossings and exchanges for radiative damping or growth rates. We locate quasiradial collapse thresholds through a static stability analysis and independently verify them by tracking the fundamental $F$-mode frequency to zero, clarifying that the central density at the onset of the dynamical instability is always larger than that of the secular instability for uniform rotation. Our formalism and numerical implementation can also resolve highly damped $w$-modes.

12. Carbon-Reaction Constraints on Stellar Evolution: White-Dwarf Heating, Carbon-Burning Products, and Black-Hole Masses[2611.04197]
Abstract

The effects of ${}^{12}\mathrm{C}+{}^{12}\mathrm{C}$ fusion and ${}^{12}\mathrm{C}(\alpha,\gamma){}^{16}\mathrm{O}$ capture on stellar heating, composition, and black-hole masses are examined. Rates from a published Bayesian analysis of carbon fusion are used in calculations with MESA. In an accreting carbon–oxygen white dwarf, the Bayesian rates move the point at which nuclear heating equals combined neutrino and surface photon losses to slightly larger stellar masses and higher central temperatures and densities. At an accretion rate of $10^{-7}\,M_\odot\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}$, the Bayesian median gives a central temperature approximately $2.57\%$ higher and a central density $18.9\%$ higher than the calculation using the standard CF88 rate. A separate calculation follows carbon burning in an initially $20\,M_\odot$, $Z=0.0142$ star. Relative to the CF88 rate with MESA's standard division between proton and alpha channels, the Bayesian-median rate with the proton-to-alpha ratio of Yang et al. increases the neon-20 mass remaining at central carbon depletion by $10.8\%$ and decreases the sodium-23 mass by $28.7\%$. For helium capture on carbon, the nuclear constraint $S_{\rm tot}(300\,\mathrm{keV})=118\pm17\,\mathrm{keV\,b}$ is applied to the published $Z=10^{-5}$ stellar models of Farag et al. Linear interpolation gives a maximum black-hole mass below the pair-instability gap of approximately $61.5$–$77.4\,M_\odot$ within the assumptions of those models. New MESA calculations at $Z=0.00142$ give smaller bound masses before collapse. Differences in the stellar assumptions prevent attributing the difference between the two curves to metallicity alone.

13. Fermi-LAT Detection of Extended GeV Emission toward the Middle-aged Supernova Remnant G156.2+05.7[2611.04227]
Abstract

We analyse 17 years of Fermi-LAT $\gamma$-ray data toward the middle-aged supernova remnant (SNR) G156.2+05.7, supplemented by radio and CO observations and by published X-ray constraints. An extended $\gamma$-ray source is detected, well described by a 2D Gaussian template ($R_{68} \sim 0.84^\circ$). This description is preferred over a foreground CO template, whereas the Gaussian and a uniform disc are statistically indistinguishable under the AIC. The spectrum shows evidence for curvature, with a log-parabola preferred over a power law at the $\approx 3.4\sigma$ level. Multi-wavelength spectral energy distribution (SED) modelling, under the adopted assumptions, suggests a leptonic–hadronic joint scenario consistent with the data, with the Fermi-LAT spectrum favouring a hadronic contribution over a purely leptonic interpretation. However, the proton-dominated alternative remains competitive, so the partition of the GeV emission is model-dependent. In the adopted leptonic–hadronic model, the electron population accounts for the radio synchrotron emission and a leptonic $\gamma$-ray component, while a steep proton population contributes the remaining hadronic $\gamma$-rays. Adopting a reference distance $d = 2.7$ kpc, the fitted electron cutoff ($E_{e,\mathrm{cut}}\approx 1.2$ TeV) is compatible with the thermal-dominated X-ray emission, while the proton cutoff is fixed by assumption at $1$ TeV. The northeast and southwest quadrants show enhanced $\gamma$-ray emission, which may be related to a clumpy or asymmetric surrounding medium. A correspondence with the directions of optical H$\alpha$ filaments is suggested by the quadrant orientation but is not yet established. No significant long-term flux variability is found, consistent with steady emission from an extended source.

14. Measuring the ultraviolet background at $z\simeq3$ from the quasar proximity effect in DESI DR1[2611.04316]
Abstract

Ionizing radiation from quasars reduces the abundance of neutral hydrogen in the surrounding intergalactic medium (IGM), suppressing Ly$\alpha$ absorption. This absorption deficit, known as the quasar proximity effect, can be used to measure the ultraviolet background (UVBG). We measure the hydrogen photoionization rate of the UVBG in Data Release 1 of the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI). Our sample contains 6,245 quasars over $2.75\leq z<3.25$, selected with rest-frame absolute magnitudes at 1450 Angstroms of $M_{1450}\leq-25.5$ and signal-to-noise ratios of at least 3 measured redward of Ly$\alpha$. We reconstruct quasar continua with principal component analysis trained on Hubble Space Telescope spectra and stack the resulting transmitted-flux profiles. We fit the resulting stacked profile with a halo model that accounts for reduced absorption from quasar radiation and enhanced absorption in the surrounding overdense environment. We constrain the environmental contribution using independent measurements of quasar clustering bias, while the transmission profile constrains the UVBG. To validate our method, we construct mock spectra matched to the luminosity and noise properties of our DESI sample using the Astrid hydrodynamical simulation at $z=3$. We apply the same analysis pipeline to the mock spectra and DESI data, testing the recovery of known input photoionization rates and using the results to calibrate the final UVBG measurement. We obtain $\Gamma_{\mathrm{HI}}=(1.376^{+0.095}_{-0.076})\times10^{-12}\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$ at $z\simeq3$. The statistical errors are obtained by bootstrap resampling the DESI sightlines. We also estimate a systematic uncertainty of $^{+0.837}_{-0.521}\times10^{-12}\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$.

15. How Fast do Black Holes Spin?[2611.04361]
Abstract

We present a systematic study of the spin evolution of accreting black holes. Using the BHAC code for general relativistic magnetohydrodynamic simulations, we conduct a total of 500 high resolution simulations of magnetized accretion disks around Kerr BHs with spin parameters ranging from $\chi=0$ to $\chi = 0.99$. The baseline configuration follows the default Fishbone–Moncrief torus setup in BHAC. We further explore the effects of: I) introducing multiple poloidal magnetic loops, II) the initial position of the torus, III) magnetic field strength and IV) radial loop modulation. From each simulation we extract the mass flux and angular momentum flux through the horizon to compute the time-averaged spin evolution rate after the system becomes quasi-stationary. The equilibrium spin ranges from $\chi_{\rm eq}\simeq0.88$ in the fiducial model to $\chi_{\rm eq}\simeq0.98$ for weak-flux model. Applied to Sgr A$^*$, even an optimistic estimate for rapid spin evolution gives a timescale longer than $10^{13}$ yr, indicating an earlier growth or merger history. These trends are consistent with the picture that larger net magnetic flux threading the horizon enhances jet-torques and reduces the equilibrium spins, whereas weak net flux yields near-maximal spins. Our results provide a pathway to interpret the high spins reported for X-ray binaries and active galactic nuclei and they sharpen the astrophysical viability of near-horizon high-energy processes that rely on near-extremal black holes.

16. Stress-Testing Small-Scale Cosmological Inference With Empirical Galaxy–Halo Connection Models[2611.04419]
Abstract

Small-scale galaxy clustering contains substantial cosmological information, but extracting it reliably requires robust modeling of the galaxy–halo connection. In this work, we compare two widely adopted families of empirical models, subhalo abundance matching (SHAM) and the halo occupation distribution (HOD), testing their predictions for galaxy clustering and their recovery of the input cosmology. Using Aemulus-$\beta$, a new multi-cosmology simulation suite with substructure merger trees, we fit multiple SHAM and HOD models to the small-scale clustering of a stellar-mass-selected mock galaxy catalog generated with the more physical semi-empirical model UniverseMachine. We then use cosmological evidence modeling to infer the structure growth parameter $S_8$. Our results show that the SHAM model, regardless of the treatment of numerically disrupted subhalos, is able to offer tight, unbiased constraints on $S_8$ with  2-3% precision, using simulations of relatively small volumes that span a limited number of cosmologies. In contrast, HOD models, even when accounting for assembly bias, yield weaker and potentially biased cosmological constraints that are sensitive to implementation choices: the inferred $S_8$ degrades in precision to  6-11% and can be offset by  1$\sigma$. This suggests that the excessive parametric flexibility and model misspecification of most commonly adopted HOD models can degrade and bias the resulting cosmological constraints. We further evaluate each model's performance in predicting higher-order statistics not used in the fit and find that SHAM generally reproduces them more accurately than the tested HOD models. These results highlight the potential of leveraging predictive subhalo-level information from simulations to enable robust cosmological inference on small scales.

17. Structural axes of sources in the SUMSS survey and their alignments[2611.04484]
Abstract

Coherent orientations of extragalactic radio sources have been reported on large angular scales, but their statistical significance and susceptibility to observational systematics remain debated. Searches based on very long baseline interferometry (VLBI) also leave the far southern sky comparatively sparsely sampled. We test for local alignments of kiloparsec-scale radio source structural axes in the Sydney University Molonglo Sky Survey (SUMSS), providing a complementary probe in the undersampled southern sky. We constructed a catalog of structural position angles for 4087 sources from 629 SUMSS mosaics. A convolutional neural network supplied initial angle estimates, which were corrected for image-to-sky coordinate distortion and visually vetted. We searched for local alignments using a coordinate-invariant parallel transport statistic on HEALPix tessellations, then refined candidate regions with a genetic algorithm. We calibrated the significance of the full search against mock skies that preserve the declination-dependent position angle distribution and undergo the same search procedure. Several regions show apparently strong local alignment, but none remains statistically significant after accounting for the look-elsewhere effect. The candidate signals are consistent with chance fluctuations in the full search. We find no statistically significant evidence for alignment of structural axes of radio sources in SUMSS images. The resulting southern sky position angle catalog provides a useful resource for future studies of radio source morphology and population properties.

18. The Survival Paradox of Close-In Planets Orbiting Red Clump Giants[2611.04544]
Abstract

Close-in planets orbiting core-helium-burning red-clump (RC) giants directly challenge tidal-engulfment theory, which predicts that planets within about 1 au of Sun-like stars should be engulfed during red-giant-branch (RGB) expansion. The existence of such survivors would imply missing physical processes in the late stages of stellar and planetary evolution. Yet only one such "forbidden" planet had been identified to date. Here we report three systems-24 Boo b, HD 12648 b, and TYC 4282-605-1 b-whose host stars are confirmed red-clump giants through asteroseismology, showing that each has already undergone dramatic expansion and contraction following helium ignition. Their planets now reside at orbital separations smaller than the maximum stellar radii attained during the RGB phase, implying that they should not have survived. Among them, 24 Boo b orbits at only 0.183 au with a 30-day period, making it the closest planet known around a red-clump giant. These discoveries demonstrate that this paradox is not an isolated anomaly but signals the emergence of a new class of evolved planetary systems. Their survival defies current models of stellar-planetary coevolution and points to unexplored physics shaping planetary architectures around post-RGB stars.

19. Up in smoke: carbon-destruction in the terrestrial planet-forming region of a young Class I disk[2611.04557]
Abstract

Earth's surface and deep mantle are strongly depleted in carbon compared to the Sun. Sublimation of solid carbonaceous material in the inner midplanes of young disks could cause this depletion. We study such thermal processing in HOPS-68, a Class I protostar with a rich hydrocarbon spectrum. A companion paper has shown that it is recondensing refractory dust from a hot inner disk region, and that oxygen-bearing species are tracing a warm (300-500 K) molecular layer entrained in a disk-wind. In this work, we analyze the carbon species in the JWST spectrum of this source using local thermal equilibrium slab models. We detect warm ( 400 K) HCN and C2H2 at the base of a disk wind, as well as colder ( 40 K) C4H2, CH3, and CS in the outer disk or outflow cavity wall. Additionally, 13CCH2, CH4, and C6H2 are detected, while ALMA observations reveal C2H tracing the outflow cavity walls. We suggest that in a "refractory carbon destruction zone" in the inner disk, carbon-bearing species are released from the grains, possibly contributing to the observed carbon depletion of Earth. The disk wind then redistributes carbon carried by HCN and C2H2 from the inner disk to the colder outer disk, where UV-induced gas-phase reactions with C2H in the outflow cavity walls could produce the aliphatic carbon chains seen. This result implies that the Warm Carbon Chain Chemistry (WCCC, e.g. N. Sakai & S. Yamamoto 2013) might trace outflows rather than infall. The formation of aliphatic chains in the disk wind could promote the abundant aliphatic organics found in meteorites and asteroids.

20. A Detailed Polarimetric Imaging Study of Type II Solar Radio Burst with the MWA: Detection of Linear Polarization[2611.04567]
Abstract

Type II radio bursts, produced by magnetohydrodynamic shocks propagating through the solar corona and interplanetary medium, serve as powerful diagnostics of shock properties and ambient coronal magnetic fields. While circular polarization in type II bursts has been extensively studied, most investigations rely on dynamic spectra (DS), providing only spatially integrated measurements. DS obscures spatial variations of the polarization sign and magnitude, potentially causing cancellation effects and misrepresenting true polarization levels. We present a full-polarimetric imaging study of a metric type II burst observed with the Murchison Widefield Array at 78–133 MHz. Remarkably, we report the first detection of linear polarization in a metric type II burst, reaching a polarization fraction of $\sim12\%$ with significant spectro-temporal variability across observing bands. The spectro-temporal, spatial, and morphological properties found in this study establish linear polarization as a new diagnostic of large-scale coronal and interplanetary magnetic field structure through full-Stokes imaging of metric solar radio bursts – challenging conventional paradigms predicting negligible linear polarization from coherent plasma emission processes.

21. MAGIC's interferometric diameters of $θ$ Ophiuchi and $β$ CMa and comparison with asteroseismic modelling[2611.04650]
Abstract

$\beta$ Cephei type stars are massive ($8-25$M$_{\odot}$) and known to have several pulsation modes with periods typically of a few hours. Despite a better understanding of their physics since 1992, the inner structure of these stars involves key stellar physics processes that are still uncertain. Asteroseismology offers insights into these variable stars by analysing pulsation modes, helping to probe their inner layers and understand their structure and mixing processes. We measure the angular diameter of two $\beta$ Cephei stars, $\theta$ Oph and $\beta$ CMa, using the MAGIC Stellar Intensity Interferometer and compare them, together with the Gaia and Hipparcos parallaxes, to the predictions of the asteroseismic model. The latter are based on Levenberg-Marquardt local minimisation of stellar evolution model parameters to fit observed pulsation modes. In the case of $\theta$ Oph, the observed angular diameters are compatible with the asteroseismic model. For $\beta$ CMa, the radius derived from the angular diameter and the Hipparcos parallax is in tension, at the $2.5\sigma$ level, with the asteroseismic prediction. We show that this tension mirrors a pre-existing conflict between independent spectroscopic determinations of the surface gravity of the star, that percent-level systematics of the stellar models are quantitatively insufficient to explain it, and that it most likely points to a bias of the Hipparcos parallax. In the frame of this paper, the statistical uncertainty on the interferometric results is similar to the systematic uncertainty on the parallaxes. With the already implemented addition in early 2024 of the first Large-Sized Telescope (CTAO-North LST-1) to the setup, the uncertainty in the angular diameter of these stars is expected to decrease by an order of magnitude, allowing to further constrain stellar evolution models and parallaxes.

22. Outer pressure bumps beyond the dust disc: Missing Outer Rings in the exoALMA Sample: Physical Mechanisms Suppressing Dust Ring Formation Despite Strong Pressure Bumps[2611.04676]
Abstract
