1. Wolf-Rayet Stars in M33: The Impact of Stellar Crowding on Spectral Classification and Mass-Loss Diagnostics[2610.33163]
Abstract

The metallicity dependence of Wolf-Rayet (WR) mass-loss rates is a critical input for massive star evolutionary models, chemical yield predictions, and ionizing radiation budgets of young stellar populations. The Triangulum Galaxy (M33), with 213 spectroscopically confirmed WR stars spanning a $\sim$0.5 dex radial metallicity gradient, is the premier laboratory for this calibration. However, the ground-based fiber spectra on which the current census rests blend each target with neighboring OB stars, diluting diagnostic emission-line equivalent widths and biasing spectral classifications, inferred mass-loss rates, and surface hydrogen abundances. Using $0\farcs1$-resolution HST/PHATTER survey imaging, we quantify this contamination for 123 WR stars within the survey footprint, finding that $\sim$27% suffer moderate-to-severe dilution from unresolved neighbors with WR continuum flux contributions $F_{\rm WR} < 0.85$. We provide crowding classifications, $F_{\rm WR}$ fractions, and six-band photometry in the F275W, F336W, F475W, F814W, F110W, and F160W bands for each identified counterpart. We also present Keck/DEIMOS slit spectra of five WR stars, which are substantially less contaminated than the discovery MMT/Hectospec fiber spectra. A direct equivalent-width comparison of these targets confirms the predicted dilution. The slit spectra yield blue-edge velocities of He I absorption ($v_{\rm blue} \approx 700$–$1500$ km s$^{-1}$), comparable to WR stars at similar metallicity in the LMC. The $F_{\rm WR}$ correction factors, together with the slit-spectrum benchmarks, provide the inputs needed to place M33 on the $\dot{M}$–$Z$ relation as a calibration anchor between the LMC and the Galaxy.

2. Empirical Brightening Model for Distant Long-Period Comets[2610.33167]
Abstract

Recent studies have shown the traditional comet brightening model, $Hn$, with a constant activity index $n$ (i.e., single slope in $\log_{10}(r_{\rm h})$), fails to accurately describe heliocentric lightcurves at large distances, where water-ice sublimation is inefficient. We present a new empirical model, $Hab$, for distant comet brightening in which the effective brightening slope varies with heliocentric distance, $r_{\rm h}$. Using a multi-year pre-perihelion dataset from uniform instrumentation for 23 comets, we found that $Hab$ provides the best match to long inbound arcs spanning a wide range in $r_{\rm h}$. The best-fit coefficients $a$ and $b$ cluster tightly for our best-observed comets, suggesting a common brightening behavior; we therefore also tested one- and two-parameter $Hab$ variants in which $a$ and/or $b$ are fixed to anchor values derived from these comets ($A_1 = 0.40 \pm 0.09$, $B_1 = -1.78 \pm 0.68$), either individually, jointly, or via a linear relation. The three-parameter $Hab$ model is strongly favored by the Bayesian information criterion (BIC) for the full lightcurve, justifying its added complexity for characterization. We also tested predictive performance against $Hn$. For brightness forecasting from limited initial data ($\Delta r_{\rm h} \lesssim 2.5$ au, the heliocentric distance range spanned by observations), $Hab$ itself predicts poorly, whereas a one-parameter reduction, with both coefficients fixed, predicts more reliably, including outperforming $Hn$ with a fixed $n \sim 2-4$. Two-parameter reductions with only one coefficient fixed typically reach predictions within 1 mag of the observed brightness after only $\Delta r_{\rm h} \sim 1.0$ au of inbound coverage.

3. Collisional Evolution around A-type Stars with Strong Silicate Features[2610.33242]
Abstract

Around luminous A-type stars, retaining micrometer-sized dust grains is difficult owing to the high radiation pressure that repels particles with sizes below the blowout limit. Yet such particles have been evidenced in the terrestrial zone around those stars, via pronounced silicate spectral features. An in situ planetesimal belt that collisionally replenishes the dust is expected to deplete quickly, limiting the lifetime of observable features. However, modeling of such systems often relies on a power-law description of the grain size distribution that has a lower size limit owing to blowout. Using more complex, collisional simulations instead, we investigated the temporal evolution of hypothetical planetesimal belts in the terrestrial zone around five A-type stars showing strong spectral silicate features. A collisional cascade that is modified by radiation pressure results in dust distributions that replicate observed spectral features without requiring other physical processes. While features can be altered by particles below the blowout limit, their magnitude is dominated by the emission of larger grains. Using the systems' ages, we investigated the origin of the planetesimal belt. For the three younger systems of our sample, a planetesimal belt that emerged from the protoplanetary disk phase can explain the observations. This is even possible for two older systems if the planetesimals are located at slightly larger distances than the dust, e.g., if the dust drifted inward owing to the Poynting-Robertson drag. Alternatively, a recent production of the planetesimals, for instance, in a giant collision event, could have happened only several million years ago.

4. Trapped sausage modes in magnetic pores: existence criteria and viscous damping[2610.33251]
Abstract

The conditions governing the existence and evolution of trapped sausage waves in photospheric magnetic pores are investigated, with particular emphasis on the limitations of analytical cut-off criteria and the role of viscous dissipation. A pore is modelled as a cylindrical magnetic flux tube with smoothly varying radial profiles of magnetic field and density, subject to the constraint that its temperature is lower than that of the surrounding atmosphere. For finite plasma-$\beta$, the linearised equations governing sausage-mode perturbations are derived, and an initial-value problem is solved numerically to obtain the full spatio-temporal evolution of the waves. The applicability of the analytical cut-off relation derived for a flux tube with step-function equilibrium profiles is examined, revealing that its predictions depend on the equilibrium parameters. The simulations reveal distinct wave behaviours across different parameter regimes, including the simultaneous excitation of trapped fast and slow sausage waves for a representative photospheric parameter set. Viscous damping is further investigated through the temporal evolution of the wave energy. Energy dissipation increases with decreasing viscous Reynolds number, increasing longitudinal wavenumber, and increasing radial magnetic-field contrast. These trends are consistent with enhanced viscous damping associated with increasingly fine spatial gradients generated through phase mixing. The findings highlight the importance of radial equilibrium structure in assessing sausage-wave trapping and demonstrate the sensitivity of wave-energy dissipation to the physical parameters of photospheric magnetic pores.

5. Saturn's Formation at the Carbon Dioxide Iceline[2610.33257]
Abstract

Saturn's bulk metallicity and atmospheric volatile enrichments exceed those of Jupiter. Combined with their similar heavy-element masses, this suggests that Saturn formed farther out in a protoplanetary disk whose metallicity increased with heliocentric distance. Numerous models argue that Jupiter formed near the H$_2$O iceline; Saturn's composition is therefore expected to reflect accretion beyond this region. We test whether Saturn's elemental enrichments can be reproduced by formation near the CO$_2$ or CO icelines using a one-dimensional viscous $\alpha$-disk model that tracks the transport, sublimation, and recondensation of 13 volatile carriers. We compute time-dependent radial distributions of solids and vapors for $\alpha = 5\times10^{-4}$–$5\times10^{-3}$ and varied CO/CO$_2$ abundance ratios, and compare the resulting metallicities and enrichments with Saturn's observed composition. Strong carbon and oxygen enrichment peaks result from the cold-finger effect, most prominently at the CO$_2$ iceline, where supersolar metallicities comparable to Saturn's envelope are achieved only for $\alpha \le 10^{-3}$. Smaller C and O enrichment peaks also occur at the CO iceline, but the local protosolar nebula metallicity there never reaches Saturn's inferred value. At $\sim$0.2–0.3 Myr, carbon and phosphorus enrichments in this region match Saturn's atmospheric values, while noble-gas abundances remain near protosolar owing to their long diffusion times. These results identify material originating near the CO$_2$ iceline as the most likely source of Saturn's heavy-element inventory. For the conditions adopted in our model, our results imply that Saturn accreted this material early ($\lesssim0.3$ Myr), in a weakly turbulent disk ($\alpha\lesssim10^{-3}$) with a CO$_2$-rich volatile inventory.

6. Accretion Geometry and Variability in QS Telescopii from multi-epoch Observations[2610.33324]
Abstract

We present phase-resolved photometry, spectroscopy, photopolarimetry, and circular spectropolarimetry of the polar QS Telescopii, obtained between 2017 and 2025 using the South African Astronomical Observatory (SAAO) 1.0-m and 1.9-m telescopes, the Transiting Exoplanet Survey Satellite (TESS), Asteroid Terrestrial-impact Last Alert System (ATLAS) and the Southern African Large Telescope (SALT). From ground-based photometry, we report the first detection of quasi-periodic oscillations (QPOs) in QS Tel, with timescales ranging between 2 and 11 minutes. This makes QS Tel one of only seven polars to show QPOs. The average spectra of QS Tel show broad and strong Balmer ($\rm H\delta$, $\rm H\gamma$, $\rm H\beta$ and $\rm H\alpha$) and He\textsc{ii} 4686 Å emission lines, along with weaker emission from He\textsc{i} at (6678 Å, 5017 Å, 4921 Å, 4712 Å, 4471 Å, and 4387 Å) and the Bowen fluorescence complex C\textsc{iii}/N\textsc{iii} at 4650 Å. For the first time detailed Doppler tomography of the $\rm H\alpha$ and He\textsc{ii} 4686 Å lines are presented which reveal emission from the irradiated face of the secondary star, the ballistic stream, the threading region and complex magnetically confined flows. The magnetically confined accretion stream splits into two distinct components coming from the same stream. One component appears near the threading region and the other component is seen along the high velocity in the magnetically confined accretion stream closer to the White Dwarf. Broad band photopolarimetry reveals circular polarisation reaching levels of $+8\%$, however more detailed circular spectropolarimetry shows polarisation ranging from $-10\%$ to $+25\%$, associated with two broad, phase-dependent cyclotron humps centred at $\sim$4800 Å and $\sim$6300 Å, consistent with a magnetic field strength of $\sim$53 MG.

7. Constraining the Inner Dark Matter Profiles of Strong-Lensing Galaxy Clusters[2610.33370]
Abstract

We present mass modeling of three strong lensing galaxy clusters found in the Dark Energy Survey utilizing Hubble Space Telescope imaging for lens modeling, and complementary constraints from Chandra X-ray observations. All three clusters are relaxed and have giant arc or ring lens structures ideal for constraints on the dark matter distribution. The strong lens modeling and X-ray temperatures yield consistent total cluster mass estimates. We find these clusters have best-fit X-ray temperatures of $2.7 - 6.2$ keV and total masses of M$_{200} = (1-6) \times 10^{14} M_{\odot}$. Our lens models parameterize dark matter distribution with a generalized Navarro-Frenk-White (GNFW) density profile. While the GNFW profile allows the inner dark matter slope to vary, we find in all cases that the inner slopes are consistent with the standard NFW density profile $\gamma^{DM} = 1$. The X-ray surface brightness distributions for all three clusters are generally symmetric with no significant substructure, supporting the conclusion that these are relaxed clusters. In addition, we find generally low values of the external shear magnitude with median values ranging $|\gamma^{ext}| = 0.04-0.09$ and consistent with zero for two of the clusters.

8. Proxima Centauri's Angular Diameter Revisited[2610.33373]
Abstract

Proxima Centauri is the nearest known exoplanet-hosting star and a benchmark for low-mass stellar models. Published diameters differ by several percent, affecting its inferred radius and temperature. Model-dependent near-infrared limb darkening introduces an additional 2-5% correction to the inferred radius. We re-reduced two previously published VLTI/PIONIER interferometric epochs from 2014 and combined them with three previously unpublished archival epochs from 2017 and 2019. We fit all five data sets jointly with uniform-disk and atmosphere-grid-based center-to-limb models. The joint fit gives a uniform-disk angular diameter, $\theta_{\rm UD}=1.117\pm0.024$ mas. Using the PHOENIX model and adopted bolometric flux, we obtain a PHOENIX limb-darkened angular diameter, $\theta_{\rm LD,PHX}=1.154\pm0.026$ mas, a stellar radius, $R_\star=0.162\pm0.004\,R_\odot$, and a Stefan–Boltzmann effective temperature, $T_{\rm eff,SB}=2833\pm49$ K. No significant close companion is detected across the five epochs over the searched separation and contrast range.

9. Gravitational Waves from Magnetically Confined Mountains on Accreting White Dwarfs[2610.33409]
Abstract

We propose fast-spinning, magnetic white dwarfs in binary systems as promising sources of continuous gravitational waves for future space-based detectors. The aim of this work is to determine the maximum ellipticities and gravitational wave amplitudes achievable by magnetically confined mountains accreted on the star's magnetic poles, and to assess their detectability against predicted sensitivity curves. Self-consistent magnetohydrodynamic equilibrium simulations are used to characterize the structure of the accreted mountain. A parametric search is then performed on these simulations to determine the maximum mass values that can be confined by the star's magnetic field. The stellar ellipticity $\varepsilon$ grows nearly linearly with accreted mass $M_{\rm a}$ up to a maximum, and was found to scale as $\varepsilon_{\rm max} \simeq 1.6\times10^{-2}T_5^{-2} B_{*,8}^{2}$, while the maximum gravitational wave amplitude as $h_{\rm 0, max} \simeq 1.2\times10^{-23}T_5^{-2} B_{*,8}^{2}$, where $T_5$ represents the temperature of the mountain in units of $10^5 \mathrm{K}$ and $B_{*,8}$ represents the star's polar magnetic field intensity in units of $10^8 \mathrm{G}$. Across a representative range of source inclinations, distances and spin periods, magnetically confined mountains on white dwarfs can generate continuous gravitational waves detectable by future space-based interferometers. BBO and DECIGO can probe diverse configurations, whereas LISA, TianQin, and Taiji require highly magnetized systems. Applications to known fast-spinning white dwarfs indicate that cooler accreted mountains favor detectability.

10. Fast Outflow Driven from the Magnetically Arrested Disc[2610.33443]
Abstract

Fast outflows are commonly observed in accreting black hole systems, yet their launching mechanism remains debated, especially when the gas is highly ionized and radiation line driving becomes inefficient. We investigate magnetically driven outflows from magnetically arrested discs (MADs) using a steady, axisymmetric magnetocentrifugal outflow model. The outflow equations follow the classical trans-magnetosonic MHD framework, while the disc structural scalings and outflow-base conditions are motivated by general relativistic magnetohydrodynamics (GRMHD) simulations. We solve for the trans-magnetosonic outflow solutions along prescribed large-scale poloidal magnetic field lines. The outflows launched from the inner disc are strongly accelerated and the formal Newtonian solutions can enter the mildly relativistic regime. The outflow velocity decreases systematically with increasing footpoint radius, producing a radially stratified outflow in which inner streamlines reach UFO-like velocities while outer streamlines form slower components. We also find that the MAD solutions have a large magnetic lever arm $\tilde{l}=(R_{\rm A}/R)^2$ is typically several tens and can reach $\sim10^2$, which implies that the outflow can carry a large amount of the specific angular momentum of the disc material at its footpoint, and we find that about $55\%$ of the angular-momentum estimated between the outer and inner disc boundaries is removed by the magnetic outflow. The total mass-loss rate in the outflow is about $14\%$ of the disc mass-accretion rate in our fiducial model. These results suggest that the MADs provide a natural environment for producing both fast, radially stratified disc outflow and efficient disc angular-momentum removal. MAD-driven outflow may therefore provide a magnetic origin for ultra-fast absorbers in low-luminosity active galactic nuclei (AGNs) and X-ray binaries (XRBs).

11. Apparent magnetic field decay in a solar flare from Zeeman splitting of the Mg 12.32 microns observations[2610.33502]
Abstract

The magnetic field is the most fundamental observable in solar physics, while solar flares are the most violent, rapid magnetic energy-release events in the solar atmosphere. Decades of observations and theory have sketched the framework of flare evolution and magnetic-field-related physical process, yet the actual magnetic field variations during flares remained poorly understood. The main obstacle is that magnetic-diagnostic spectral lines are distorted by transient flare activity, making traditional spectro-polarimetry inversions complicated. Using a mid-infrared solar telescope, we investigate spectroscopic evolution of the Mg 12.32 microns line during an X1.5-class flare. Owing to the exceptionally large Zeeman sensitivity of this line, magnetic field strength can be directly determined from the measured line separation. Our analysis reveals apparent magnetic field weakening beneath flare ribbons in the impulsive phase. Radiative-hydrodynamic simulations and atmospheric inversions confirm that the formation height of the Mg 12.32 microns line increases during the flare. We suggest this apparent field change probably originates either from elevated line-formation altitude or magnetic-field redistribution. Additional observations and modeling are needed to clarify these two contributions. This study demonstrates the capability of mid-infrared wavelengths to trace magnetic-field evolution during flares.

12. PeV Diffuse Neutrinos from Magnetically Arrested Disks in Low-Luminosity Active Galactic Nuclei[2610.33548]
Abstract

Radiatively inefficient accretion flows (RIAFs) in low-luminosity active galactic nuclei (LLAGNs) are promising candidates for the PeV astrophysical diffuse neutrino background (DNB), but how the magnetically arrested disk (MAD) state affects their neutrino output and what fraction of LLAGNs are in this state remain unclear. We present a comprehensive study of diffuse neutrino emission from the inner accretion flows of LLAGNs in the MAD state, including all three flavors and both hadronuclear ($pp$) and photohadronic ($p\gamma$) production channels. Assuming that $\sim 5\%$ of the accretion power is transferred to nonthermal protons, current IceCube data allow at most $2.3\times10^{-3}$ of LLAGNs to have their inner accretion flows in a MAD state that can produce high-energy neutrinos. A lower fraction of the accretion power transferred to nonthermal protons would allow a proportionally larger fraction of such LLAGNs. Assuming that the comoving number density of low-excitation radio galaxies (LERGs) approximately matches that of LLAGNs in the MAD state, we also estimate a MAD fraction of $\simeq 1.6\times10^{-3}$. The corresponding diffuse intensity is compatible with current IceCube data and testable by IceCube-Gen2. For this MAD fraction, the all-flavor diffuse intensity peaks at $E_\nu\simeq1.5\,{\rm PeV}$ with $E_\nu^2\Phi_\nu\simeq2.6\times10^{-9}\,{\rm GeV\,cm^{-2}\,s^{-1}\,sr^{-1}}$. Current IceCube data are beginning to challenge several predictions from previous LLAGN RIAF models, and the MAD component can become the dominant contribution from LLAGN accretion flows beyond the cutoffs of most earlier RIAF spectra considered here.%%%This constraint suggests that only a small fraction of LLAGNs are supplied with sufficient coherent net poloidal magnetic flux.

13. A Global Magnetohydrodynamic Simulation of the Birth and Cannibalism of Homologous Coronal Mass Ejections[2610.33614]
Abstract

Homologous coronal mass ejections (CMEs) are sequential eruptions from the same active region. They are of particular space weather interest because they can interact and coalesce into compound ejecta, potentially amplifying their geomagnetic impact. Yet the full chain from recurrent initiation to magnetic merging has not been captured self-consistently in a single global simulation. We present a three-dimensional magnetohydrodynamic model that generates three homologous CMEs from a bipolar active region by recurrent photospheric shearing. All three eruptions follow the same initiation mechanism by tether-cutting reconnection within a recurrently formed sigmoid, while the later CMEs propagate significantly faster than the first. We demonstrate that this speed hierarchy arises from progressive coronal preconditioning; preceding eruptions evacuate the overlying corona, reducing both mass loading and ambient drag. The faster later CMEs catch up with their predecessors, and the compression between them forms inter-CME current sheets. Reconnection within these current sheets then progressively merges the interacting flux systems, completing the cannibalistic process. Synthetic coronagraph images reproduce the apparent coalescence of multiple white-light fronts. This simulation provides the first self-consistent global view of the birth, preconditioning, and cannibalistic interaction of homologous CMEs, bridging recurrent eruption initiation and inter-CME magnetic merging in the near-Sun corona.

14. Spatial variability of CH4-to-CO conversion pathways in irradiated gas giant atmospheres: implications for chemical relaxation and 1D quench schemes[2610.33643]
Abstract

Close-in gas giant atmospheres are in chemical disequilibrium. Solving 3D kinetics to capture one source of this disequilibrium, transport-induced quenching, is computationally demanding, so simplifications are used. General circulation models (GCMs) relax abundances on the timescale of a single conversion pathway. 1D models place a quench level and hold abundances vertically uniform above it. We test both simplifications on CH4 against aerosol-free, solar-composition GCM simulations with thermochemical kinetics for HAT-P-11b, HD 189733b, HD 209458b and WASP-17b. We find ten CH4-to-CO conversion pathways, varying with depth, longitude and planet. A single pathway controls CH4 quenching on the three cooler planets; on WASP-17b two compete. The CH4 quench level is 3D: where the atmosphere is hotter, or a faster pathway takes over, the conversion timescale shortens, the quench level rises and the quench-level abundance falls. On WASP-17b the pathway switch lifts the quench level over the substellar tropics, deepening equatorial CH4 depletion. A 1D quench treatment with the Zahnle & Marley timescale typically locates the quench level within a factor of two in pressure for CH4 and about four for CO on every planet. It fails above the quench level, where every quench scheme holds abundances vertically uniform. The minor carbon carrier, CH4 or CO depending on the planet, is wrong by up to two orders of magnitude, on HD 189733b in opposite directions at different latitudes. Quenching sets an abundance at the quench level but does not fix it thereafter; even without photochemistry, transport and conversion keep that abundance spatially structured.

15. Far Ultraviolet Variability on Timescales of a Few hours in the Recurrent Nova M31N 2008-12a: Origin and Implications[2610.33844]
Abstract

The fate of the accretion disk during nova eruptions remains an open question, particularly in rapidly recurring novae, where high accretion rates and low ejecta masses may allow the disk to survive the eruption. M31N 2008-12a, the most rapidly recurring nova currently known, provides an ideal laboratory for investigating this process. We present high-cadence AstroSat/UVIT observations obtained during eruptions between 2019 and 2023. Complementary multiwavelength observations of the 2023-2025 eruptions are also presented, which are broadly consistent with prior outbursts. The main result is the early onset of intrinsic far-ultraviolet variability on a timescale of a few hours, approximately 4-5 days after eruption, which is of astrophysical origin. A periodicity search suggests a tentative signal at 4.60 (+0.25,-0.15) hr at 2.3 sigma confidence, detected in high-cadence UVIT/FUV observations. We show that a Keplerian orbit of this timescale lies not only within the Roche lobe of the white dwarf in M31N 2008-12a, but also within the circularisation radius. A disk-related origin is, thus, physically plausible. The presence of this UV variability so soon after eruption suggests that accretion structures may already exist during the early post-eruption phase, supporting the disk-survival hypotheses. If confirmed by future observations, this method may provide a new dynamical probe of accretion disk survival in rapidly recurring novae.

16. Magnetoelastic avalanches in magnetar crusts with plastic islands[2610.33865]
Abstract

High-energy outbursts from magnetars may be associated with crustal failures or quakes. The magnetoelastic modes of the star can be excited to large amplitudes in such a framework, providing a natural explanation for the quasi-periodic oscillations observed in the X-ray tails of some events. However, previous calculations of torsional modes have been carried out assuming an intact crust without accounting for the failure itself. We develop a code in this paper that solves the axial sector of the linearised Einstein-Maxwell-Euler equations in a cavity that hosts inelastic regions or 'plastic islands'. As crustal elements are displaced, shear stresses are tracked as a function of time to allow cells to dynamically switch from elastic to inelastic states. The presence of islands in the crust promotes phase mixing and, when reflected waves mix constructively, large sections fail collectively ('avalanche') if the initial kick is strong enough. Transient spectral features are observed as a result of the progressive loss of elastic support, which may explain the temperamental nature of the oscillations observed following giant flares and magnetar storms.

17. Finite-temperature ab-initio-Informed Nuclear Equation-of-State Tables (FINEST): Nuclear matter uncertainties in core-collapse supernovae[2610.33879]
Abstract

We construct new finite-temperature, composition-dependent equation of state (EOS) tables for astrophysical simulations by implementing a recently developed energy-density functional. The functional is constrained by ab initio nuclear matter calculations and neutron-star observations. A distinguishing feature of this functional is a non-monotonic density dependence of the nucleon effective mass, with an increase beyond saturation density. Using these EOS tables, we investigate the impact of current uncertainties in nuclear matter properties on core-collapse supernova (CCSN) evolution with spherically symmetric simulations employing an effective treatment of convective mixing. The magnitude of the EOS effects depends on the progenitor compactness. Within current constraints, variations in incompressibility, symmetry energy, and high-density pressure produce only modest differences during the early post-bounce evolution. In contrast, the effective mass dependence has a much stronger influence on proto-neutron star contraction and the explosion dynamics in a stellar model with high core compactness. Its non-monotonic density dependence leads to a qualitatively different contraction history and can alter the explosion outcome compared to mean-field effective-mass behaviors. Therefore, among the nuclear matter properties considered, the density dependence of the nucleon effective masses has by far the largest impact on CCSN dynamics. This highlights the importance of realistic effective-mass parameterizations in nuclear EOSs used in astrophysical simulations.

18. The Fate of Ice Sheets on Near-Synchronously Rotating Land Planets[2610.33884]
Abstract

Rocky exoplanets orbiting M dwarf stars are often assumed to be synchronously rotating, such that water-trapping in ice sheets on the permanent nightside may pose a threat to habitability. However, in compact M dwarf systems, gravitational interactions between planets may cause the substellar point to librate or circulate on millennial timescales and sporadically change direction or speed. Here, we use a 3D global climate model to quantify, for the first time, the water-trapping capabilities of these "near"-synchronously rotating exoplanets, with TRAPPIST-1e as our test case. Across a range of synodic day lengths, we find that there is a permanent polar ice reservoir and an increasingly large build-up of lower-latitude transient nightside ice for longer days. A persistent ice-free region develops on the dayside where surface conditions can sustain liquid water. However, this steady-state ice distribution may never be realized since the accumulation timescale may be slower than changes in the spin regime. Additionally, we revise the nightside ice build-up on a synchronously rotating exoplanet using a coupled atmosphere+ice numerical model that accounts for atmospheric lapse rate and isostatic adjustment feedbacks. We find that accounting for this ice-elevation feedback reduces the predicted ice accumulation by a factor of  3. Taken together, our study suggests that water-trapping in ice sheets is less severe than previously thought for land planets orbiting M dwarf stars.

19. Core-Collapse Supernovae from Single and Binary-Stripped Massive Stars - I. Explodability and Nucleosynthesis[2610.33954]
Abstract

Binary interaction can substantially alter massive-star evolution and pre-supernova structure, changing core-collapse outcomes and nucleosynthetic yields. We compare explodability and nucleosynthesis in 66 non-rotating, solar-metallicity single-star and binary-stripped progenitors spanning zero-age main-sequence (ZAMS) masses of $11$-$45\,{\rm M}_\odot$, using the spherically symmetric PUSH framework and nucleosynthesis post-processing. Both populations explode up to $22\,{\rm M}_\odot$, with binary stripping altering explodability at higher ZAMS masses. Successful explosions span similar energies ($\sim$0.4-1.3$\,$B) and remnant masses ($\sim$1.4-2.0$\,{\rm M}_\odot$). The largest nucleosynthetic differences occur at low ZAMS masses, particularly for material undergoing explosive O and Si burning. The $^{56}$Ni yields and iron-group abundances broadly agree with observations of core-collapse supernovae and the metal-poor star HD 84937. When compared with calculations using time-dependent mixing-length theory and a two-parameter explodability criterion for the same progenitors, our results reveal substantial differences in explodability, with agreement on successful explosions for only 5 cases across all three approaches. Nevertheless, elemental yield patterns can remain broadly similar for models that explode successfully across treatments. Salpeter initial-mass-function-weighted yields of most elements differ by $\sim$20% between populations, with K enhanced by a factor of $\sim$3.4 in the binary-stripped population, driven by increased production in the lowest-mass progenitors. Binary-stripped models show closer agreement with empirical core-collapse S, K, and Ca abundance ratios relative to Mg inferred from APOGEE, while Na remains overproduced in both populations. Together, our results highlight the need to account for binarity when interpreting supernova abundance patterns.

20. Halo formation and distribution around cosmic web[2610.34042]
Abstract

We use the ELUCID simulation to study how the local environment affects the formation of dark matter halos and their spatial distribution around the cosmic web. We describe the environment by local density and cosmic-web type. At fixed web type, the halo mass function (HMF) depends strongly on the local density, whereas at fixed density it depends only weakly, though significantly, on the web type; the character of both dependencies changes around the mean density. The halo formation redshift is primarily driven by local density rather than cosmic web. We then introduce the three-dimensional cosmic-web cross-correlation function (3D-WCCF), which expresses the distribution of halos around cosmic web in the local tidal eigenframe. The 3D-WCCF provides numerous anisotropic characteristics. Around all web types, halos are most strongly correlated along the axis of weakest compression, $\mathbf{e}_3$, and least along the axis of strongest compression, $\mathbf{e}_1$. Nearly all cluster-sized halos reside in knots and lie preferentially in the plane perpendicular to ${\bf e}_1$. The 3D-WCCF also provides important information on the geometry and size of filaments, sheets and voids. Moreover, early-forming halos are more strongly correlated with the web than late-forming ones, and this assembly bias is anisotropic around filaments, sheets, and voids, being strongest along $\mathbf{e}_3$, but nearly isotropic around knots.

21. Planetesimal Delivery to Giant Planet Circumplanetary Disks: Bulk Ice Enrichment and an Ice-rich Iapetus[2610.34081]
Abstract

The regular satellites of Jupiter and Saturn are substantially enriched in ice relative to the rock-rich solids of the outer Solar System. In addition, the satellites interior to Titan and distant Iapetus are icier than Titan itself. Collisional processes and subsequent migration can readily alter compositions deep in the planetary potential well, but producing a distant, ice-rich satellite with Iapetus's radius of about 700 km is a challenge \citep{mosqueira2026a}. We show that differential ablation and capture of icy and rocky fragments from differentiated outer Solar System bodies can explain the bulk Jovian and Kronian satellite ice enrichment and supply the substantial ice-rich inventory needed for Iapetus. For 1 m–1 km fragments with the canonical size-distribution index \(p=3.5\) and a source containing 30\% ice by mass, the fixed radial distance Saturn calculation deposits material with ice fractions of 0.66 (delivery of vapor-only rock) or 0.50 (with retained rock melt) at 20 Saturn radii and nearly pure ice at 60 Saturn radii. Following three-dimensional trajectories over repeated passages through the pressure-supported gas gives ice fractions of about 0.4 near 20 Saturn radii and 0.9 near 60 Saturn radii. The matching Jupiter calculation yields a similar bulk ice enrichment, with a corresponding compositional gradient across the Galilean satellite region. These delivery profiles supply ice-enriched material for satellite formation. Yet satellitesimal inward migration subsequently modifies the material incorporated into satellites and thus alters their bulk compositions. We briefly discuss the implications for the PDS 70 planetary system.

22. Survey of small amplitude waves in solar prominences[2610.34226]
Abstract

In particular, we are interested in the lensed signal from a binary (the source) near a massive black hole (the lens). We present a new formalism for obtaining low frequency corrections to sources being lensed in the strong field regime of gravity by leveraging the fact that null geodesics obey the Hamilton–Jacobi equations. In doing so, we are able to construct a time delay surface without relying on the thin lens approximation, and solve the diffraction integral for the resulting surface directly. For a stationary source at $10M$, deep in the strong field regime, when comparing the results with a relativistic wave scattering model, we find $6.5\%$ and $0.3\%$ agreement in the peak amplification and first diffractive fringe, respectively. Furthermore, we find a $< 1\%$ agreement with the amplitude of the point mass lens with a source at $10^4 M$ in the weak field, but noticeable dephasing between the peaks of the amplification function at higher frequencies. This work allows for clean interpolation between otherwise separate regimes, valid for sources from $\sim 6M \to \infty$. It provides a transparent, physically motivated framework for computing beyond-geometric optics lensing effects in the strong field regime.
