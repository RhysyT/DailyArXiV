1. Beyond Point Photo-$z$ Estimates: Normalizing Flows for Tomographic $N(z)$ Reconstruction with DESI DR1[2611.00121]
Abstract

Accurate calibration of tomographic redshift distributions, $N(z)$ is essential for cosmological analysis of weak lensing data. We investigate conditional normalizing flows (NFs) and compare their performance with random forest (RF) and quantile random forest (QRF) models. We consider DESI galaxy and quasar targets from DESI DR1 spectroscopic data and with the photometry from Legacy Surveys DR10. We train on 1.1 million sources and test on 2.0 million sources in a non-overlapping sky region ($0<z_{\rm spec}<4.5$). For point estimation, the NF achieves the lowest scatter and outlier fraction at $0<z_{\rm spec}<1.6$ and outperforms both baselines across all three metrics at $z_{\rm spec}>1.6$. By introducing distribution-level metrics like Wasserstein distance and JS divergence, we find that stacking NF posterior PDFs provides more accurate tomographic $N(z)$ reconstruction than QRF, particularly at high redshifts. For $z_{\rm median}>2.5$, the NF achieves a smaller absolute mean-redshift bias ($0.060\pm0.031$ versus $0.318$) and higher probability overlap ($0.905\pm0.008$ versus $0.787$) than QRF. We apply redshift-dependent post-hoc PDF calibration derived from a held-out validation sample. After calibration, the absolute mean-redshift biases in four bins spanning $0.2<z_{\rm median}<1.0$ range from $0.0016$ to $0.0019$, with central values below referring benchmark $0.002(1+z)$. In the highest-redshift bin, calibration further reduces the bias to $0.016\pm0.011$. Our results demonstrate the potential of NF PDFs for tomographic $N(z)$ reconstruction while highlighting challenges in transferring models across galaxy populations.

2. Lightest and heaviest nuclei as keys to the origin of ultrahigh energy cosmic rays[2611.00205]
Abstract

The origin of ultrahigh energy cosmic rays (UHECRs) has been a long-standing mystery. A unique feature of UHECRs originating in binary neutron star mergers (BNSMs) is two distinct populations of light elements – those directly accelerated in the jets, and photo-disintegration products of massive nuclei accelerated in the bulk outflow. We show that Pierre Auger Observatory spectrum and composition data confirm the existence of the predicted two populations at $\geq \!7.2\sigma$ significance, having rigidity ($R\equiv E/Ze$) cutoffs agreeing with prior estimates. The data also favor 5-10% of UHECRs originating as r-process nuclei, a signature of a BNSM origin. Our results provide strong evidence that UHECRs are produced by merging neutron stars, and show how UHECRs and UHE-neutrinos elucidate otherwise inaccessible aspects of neutron star mergers.

3. Probing the geometry and outflows of the accreting millisecond pulsar SAX J1808.4-3658 with multi-band polarimetry[2611.00221]
Abstract

We present VLT/FORS2 R- and I-band imaging polarimetry of the accreting millisecond X-ray pulsar SAXJ1808.4-3658 during its 2025 outburst, coordinated with the first X-ray polarimetric observations of this source with IXPE. We detect significant optical linear polarisation at every epoch (polarisation degree P 0.3-1.5%, angle Phi   8-48°), consistent with the only prior optical polarimetric campaign of this source, obtained during a previous outburst. The average optical polarisation angle is offset by  16-24° from the IXPE angle of the persistent X-ray emission, which pulse-profile modelling shows to trace the spin axis. A fast-timing sequence reveals the optical polarisation angle swinging by up to  40° within minutes, disfavouring scattering in the disc. A colour-magnitude diagram and the dependence of the optical polarisation on the red colour excess over the disc emission, which we attribute to the jet, instead point to a jet origin during the main outburst, consistent with contemporaneous radio detections. The offset, with the same mean optical angle as in the previous outburst, can arise from emission near a wide jet base, where the Doppler-boosted side of the cone dominates. At the onset of the reflaring stage the optical polarisation degree is highest and its angle coincides with the IXPE one, while the disc likely dominates the optical flux. We discuss a polarisation flare of the fading jet, scattering in an outflow, and synchrotron emission at the disc-magnetosphere boundary as possible origins, tentatively favouring the last.

4. Slowly rotating white dwarfs in f (R, T ) = R + 2λT gravity: structure and stationary tidal response[2611.00329]
Abstract

We calculate the structure and stationary tidal response of cold carbon fluid white dwarfs in $f(R,T)=R+2\lambda T$ gravity through second order in uniform rotation. Equilibria are compared at fixed baryon number, with angular momentum also conserved during each aligned electric quadrupolar tidal perturbation. Negative coupling increases radii and dimensional tidal deformabilities over the common mass range. For the baryon sequence studied, background readjustment accounts for 95.8–97.5\% of the rotational enhancement at 40\% of each model's Kepler reference frequency. The explicit mixed rotational tidal contribution is positive and numerically resolved, but subdominant. At that spin, the enhancement relative to each coupling's static model decreases from 107.4\% in general relativity to 14.2\% at the most negative coupling, despite its larger absolute deformability. This reduction reflects less relative background expansion and a different Love-number variation.

5. The Valence and Inner Shell Ionization Properties of Sr I - III by Non-Thermal Electrons in Kilonovae[2611.00335]
Abstract

The presence of strontium (Z = 38) was identified from spectra of the first multi-messenger kilonova transient, AT2017gfo, with the evolution of its spectral signature indicating a rapidly changing ionization balance. Interpretation of spectra at later times requires a more detailed understanding of recombination and ionization processes, including ionization by `non-thermal' electrons produced by beta decay. Published measurements of the ionization cross sections of Sr I and Sr II show complex structures likely arising from excitation autoionization. Using the Flexible Atomic Code we report calculations of the valence and inner shell ionization cross sections for Sr I, Sr II, and Sr III, and recover similar structures in the ionization cross sections. Though no measurements exist for Sr III, the calculations suggest negligible contributions from excitation autoionization. These new data are incorporated into Spencer-Fano models of non-thermal electron degradation and coupled to an ionization balance solver. The effects of excitation autoionization, treated as excitation with the additional production of an Auger electron, on the degradation spectra of non-thermal are explored. Calculations at both level- and configuration-average resolution return ion fractions that agree within 5-20%. The calculations probe the impact of including final-charge-state-resolved branching ratios for inner shell ionization which result in multi-ionization events from successive Auger cascades. Omission of these branching ratios is shown to have large impacts on the ionization balance. The calculated cross sections will be provided on Zenodo.

6. HD 210058 b and BD+37 4838 b: A precessing eccentric hot Jupiter and a misaligned substellar companion around two A-type stars[2611.00378]
Abstract

The number of A- and F-type stars known to host exoplanets is relatively low, as is the number of planets with measured spin-orbit angle, which is the angle between the planet's orbital axis and the star's rotational axis. Expanding the sample of such systems is important for constraining the formation and migration mechanisms of close-in giant planets around early-type stars. We aim to measure the sky-projected spin-orbit angles of HD 210058b (TOI-1355b) and BD+37 4838b (TOI-1414b), two objects still listed as candidate exoplanets, and to assess the planetary nature of these systems. Both of their host stars are fast rotators. We analyzed TESS photometry together with transit time-series of HARPS-N spectra. For both objects, we created cross-correlation functions from the spectra to analyze Doppler tomography and detect and model the Doppler shadow, while we also used the photometric observations to refine a number of previously poorly constrained photometric and spectroscopic parameters of both the host stars and the companions. When possible, we used the derived results to constrain the companion masses. We verified the planetary and substellar nature for both objects respectively. We report HD 210058 b to be a giant hot Jupiter with a mass of 10 M_Jup, residing on a slightly misaligned orbit with the sky-projected spin-orbit angle {\lambda}=-32 -6 +4 deg. Archival photometry reveals continuously changing inclination and impact parameter, pointing to a nodal precession with period of 860 yr, and giving availability of only about six upcoming years for any further transit observations. The second target, BD+37 4838 b, orbits a hybrid {\gamma} Dor-{\delta} Sct pulsator on a misaligned orbit with {\lambda}=56.9 +1.4 -2.3 deg. Since we could not reliably recover its mass, we only report an upper limit of approx. 24.3 M_Jup, leaving the possible planetary nature of this object unresolved.

7. A multiprobe $Λ$CDM consistency analysis with gravitational-wave standard sirens[2611.00415]
Abstract

Multi-messenger cosmology offers an independent path to stress-test the $\Lambda$CDM model as gravitational wave (GW) catalogs grow in size. We present the first multiprobe assessment of $\Lambda$CDM tensions incorporating GW standard sirens: LIGO-Virgo-KAGRA Collaboration (LVK) fifth Gravitational-Wave Transient Catalog (GWTC-5.0) standard sirens are combined with Dark Energy Survey (DES) Year 6 weak lensing and galaxy clustering (3$\times$2pt), baryon acoustic oscillations (BAO), and type Ia supernovae (SNe Ia), together with Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI) BAO, and confronted with primary cosmic microwave background (CMB) measurements from Planck, the Atacama Cosmology Telescope (ACT), and the South Pole Telescope (SPT). All probes are analyzed under homogeneous priors and combined via normalizing flows, and we quantify the $S_8$ discrepancy and $H_0$ tension with respect to CMB. Adding standard sirens to the 3$\times$2pt analysis improves the constraints in the $\Omega_m$-$S_8$ plane by $\sim$30%, while constraining $H_0$ to 6.2%. We present cosmological constraints from the low-redshift probes including standard sirens, yielding $S_8 = 0.793 \pm 0.010$, $\Omega_m = 0.308^{+0.007}_{-0.006}$, and $H_0 = 69.4^{+4.1}_{-4.4}$ km s$^{-1}$ Mpc$^{-1}$. At current GW sensitivity, however, both the $S_8$ discrepancy and the $H_0$ tension remain essentially unchanged. Our analysis nonetheless delivers the first assessment of $\Lambda$CDM tensions computed with standard sirens included among the probes, and establishes them as a mature cosmological probe – ready to be combined on equal footing with established datasets in forthcoming surveys such as the Legacy Survey of Space and Time (LSST), Euclid, and Roman.

8. A temporally clustered gravitational wave pair and a Type IIb supernova[2611.00440]
Abstract

Compact object mergers are generally expected long after the stellar collapse that forms their remnants. Here we report two marginal gravitational wave (GW) candidates, S190910d and S190910h, separated by 7.1 hr, whose three-dimensional localizations overlap at the position and distance of the Type IIb supernova SN 2019rsq. The GW pair lies in the extreme tail of the joint distribution of temporal separation and localization overlap, with a pairwise null probability of $7.6 \times 10^{-5}$ under our Monte Carlo null test. Independent re-analyses recover both as signal-like candidates. Under a common-origin hypothesis, S190910h remains consistent with a low-mass compact binary with source-frame masses near $2.5 + 1.3\,M_\odot$, whereas S190910d favours a strongly asymmetric $\sim 34 + 3\,M_\odot$ system whose observed amplitude is compatible with the supernova distance. SN 2019rsq is a rapidly evolving stripped-envelope Type IIb explosion whose light curve is consistent with stellar collapse occurring near the GW activity. Although the supernova coincidence alone is not statistically rare, its timing and physical properties provide a stellar collapse anchor for the unusual GW pair. A fragmenting collapsar disk provides a plausible framework that can accommodate the component masses, hour-scale delays and supernova properties, raising the possibility that multiple compact object mergers can accompany a single stellar collapse episode.

9. Double White Dwarfs with Circumbinary Disks: Evolution, Population Properties, and Gravitational Wave Signatures[2611.00475]
Abstract

Double white dwarfs (DWDs) are important multi-messenger sources and powerful probes of binary interaction physics. Close DWDs formed through isolated binary evolution are expected to have negligible eccentricities. However, recent observations have revealed that some eclipsing DWDs exhibit eccentricities of order $10^{-3}$ or larger. In this work, we investigate the interaction between a circumbinary disk (CBD) and the central DWD, where the CBD is assumed to form from a recent common-envelope (CE) ejection. Our models are the first to include CBD-DWD interaction in DWD population synthesis and to constrain disk parameters such as disk mass and lifetime. Our population models predict a correlation between the eccentricity distribution and the orbital period distribution of DWDs, which can be tested by future observations. Furthermore, we discuss the influence of CBD on the gravitational-wave (GW) signal. The CBD-DWD interaction can shrink the orbit and excite eccentricity, extending the time for which the system is individually resolvable by a future space-borne GW detector. Our results suggest that CBD interaction should be accounted for in population synthesis of Galactic DWD GW sources and the stochastic GW foreground.

10. Gravitational Capture of Large Interstellar Planetesimals by Collapsing Prestellar Cores[2611.00537]
Abstract

We investigate the capture of free-floating planetesimals by the time-varying gravitational potential of a collapsing prestellar core. We use one-dimensional collapse models to estimate the volume of space from which planetesimals are captured. We combine this calculation with an estimate of the size distribution and velocity dispersion of the free-floating planetesimal population to determine the largest planetesimal that is typically captured, as well as the total mass of all planetesimals captured by a given protostar. We consider two cases: one in which the free-floating planetesimal population comes from the birth cluster of the star, and one in which the planetesimals are captured from the Galactic population of free-floating planetesimals. We find that the details of the collapse matter substantially, with the predicted diameter of the largest captured body from the Galactic population ranging from $2.8$ km to $6.4$ km depending on collapse model, and the largest body from our model birth cluster environment ranging from $70$ km to $1200$ km, for assumed velocity dispersions ranging from 0.7 to 8.5 km/s. The total solid mass captured by this mechanism does not exceed 0.07 Earth masses in all cases. We discuss the effect of these captured objects on the planet formation process, and argue that while the total solid mass captured is likely not significant, the largest captured bodies may grow to planet size via accretion of pebbles present in their host disks. This process may accelerate planet formation, or enable it in disks that have difficulty in forming their own planetesimals.

11. A Unified Mass-Loading Model for ASKAP J1745-5051: Linking Long-Period Radio Transients to Magnetic Cataclysmic Variables[2611.00581]
Abstract

Long-period radio transients (LPTs) in white dwarf binaries require sufficiently tenuous plasma to sustain coherent radio emission, posing a challenge in systems with dense accretion flows. ASKAP J1745-5051 (J1745) combines LPT-like radio pulses recurring approximately every 82 min with magnetic accretion and X-ray modulation at a consistent period. We investigate this coexistence using a unified mass-loading framework for Roche-lobe overflow and a tenuous donor wind in the binary magnetic field. The dense stream supplies the accretion curtain and near-white-dwarf X-ray sources, while the tenuous wind feeds flux tubes of $(1-4)\times10^7\,{\rm cm^{-3}}$, satisfying the local density requirement for electron-cyclotron maser emission. A fiducial geometry places the X-ray maximum near orbital phase $0.886$ and produces a radio-visibility window at phases $0.22-0.33$, consistent with the primary observed pulse window. Geometrical modulation and curtain absorption produce stronger soft-band than hard-band X-ray variability. Long-term phase-connected radio monitoring and simultaneous X-ray observations can test this interpretation through recurring radio phase changes and correlated changes in X-ray modulation. These results extend the proposed connection between detached binary LPTs and magnetic cataclysmic variables (mCVs). J1745 may represent an evolved mCV near the orbital-period minimum, suggesting that LPT-like activity can occur at a late accreting stage.

12. Two Isolated Microlensing Events Near the Upper Shore of Einstein Desert[2611.00754]
Abstract

The Einstein desert is a deficit in the angular Einstein-radius ($\theta_{\rm E}$) distribution of isolated objects over the range $9 {\rm \mu as} < \theta_{\rm E}<26 {\rm \mu as}$. Although this interval contained no events when the desert was first identified, several subsequent detections have fallen within it, raising questions about its detailed structure and requiring a larger detection sample. In this paper, we report the observations and analyses of two microlensing events possibly caused by isolated giant planets or brown dwarfs whose $ \theta_{\rm E}$ lie near the upper shore of the desert. MOA-2021-BLG-145 has $ \theta_{\rm E} = 23.1\pm2.0 ~{\rm \mu as}$, placing it just inside the upper boundary, and KMT-2026-BLG-0357 has $ \theta_{\rm E} = 36.5\pm 3.7~{\rm \mu as}$, placing it above the desert. We also compare the $ \theta_{\rm E}$ distributions of isolated objects and bound planetary events. The bound population shows no corresponding deficit over the same range, suggesting that the Einstein desert may be associated with the formation or subsequent evolution of the isolated population. Furthermore, some FFPs may lie above the upper shore of the Einstein desert, indicating that a more complete understanding of the FFP population requires direct lens-mass measurements.

13. How tightly can PBH abundance information constrain scalar-induced gravitational waves?[2611.00873]
Abstract

Primordial black hole (PBH) abundances and scalar-induced gravitational waves (SIGWs) probe the same primordial fluctuations through different physical weights. We examine the predictive power of precise synthetic information on absolute PBH abundances over a finite stellar-mass interval, together with an exact total abundance and external population bounds. For Gaussian perturbations under a fixed quantum chromodynamics (QCD) formation prescription, two smooth, symmetric lognormal spectra satisfy the same abundance conditions while producing nonproportional leading-order SIGW spectra. The two spectra differ by about 20% when averaged over the lower of two adjacent nanohertz windows but by nearly a factor of 60 in the higher-frequency window, so the difference cannot be represented by a single rescaling of the gravitational-wave background. Peak position and width compensate with amplitude in the abundance calculation, while the higher-frequency window draws almost all its energy from scalar-mode pairs with both wavenumbers above the primordial peak. Under the principal abundance benchmark, local finite-width components yield larger contrasts at a fixed nanohertz frequency through distinct mode couplings under both the QCD and stochastic peak-curvature formation prescriptions. For a separately generated population, extending mass information over most of its abundance restricts the lognormal peak-position and width compensation and reduces the higher-window contrast, while the unequal window responses persist. These results show why joint PBH-SIGW inference must propagate primordial shapes compatible with the PBH abundance information rather than assign one tensor template to a single abundance fit.

14. Cometary dust activity as a proxy for refractory organic material release[2611.00936]
Abstract

Cometary dust is an important reservoir of refractory organic matter and may contribute to the redistribution of carbon-rich material throughout planetary systems. We investigate whether the widely used dust-activity parameter $Af\rho$ can serve as a first-order observational proxy for the release of refractory organic material. A sample of 27 active small bodies representing active asteroids, main-belt comets, quasi-Hilda comets, quasi-Trojan objects, Jupiter-family comets, Centaurs, and long-period comets was compiled from published photometric studies. Representative near-perihelion, phase-corrected $A(0)f\rho$ values span more than three orders of magnitude, from a few centimetres for weakly active asteroids and main-belt comets to $\sim 10^4$ cm for the most active long-period comets. The large dispersion within individual dynamical classes shows that orbital classification alone does not determine the level of dust activity. Direct compositional measurements of cometary dust, including the high refractory-organic fraction measured for 67P/Churyumov-Gerasimenko, support the interpretation of dust activity as an indirect indicator of potential organic-material release. However, a universal conversion of $Af\rho$ into dust mass-loss rate is not possible because of uncertainties in grain-size distribution, density, porosity, velocity, and optical properties. We therefore suggest that $Af\rho$ may be used as a comparative, first-order proxy for refractory-organic material release.

15. A JWST/NIRSpec Catalog of Low-Mass Candidates toward NGC 2024 from Multi-component Spectral Modeling[2611.00970]
Abstract

Young low-mass objects in crowded, highly obscured star-forming regions are difficult to characterize. Deriving reliable physical parameters requires separating photospheric emission from complex extinction and circumstellar emission. We apply a multi-component spectral-modeling framework to JWST/NIRSpec PRISM spectra toward NGC 2024, obtaining adopted fits for 78 sources. The framework incorporates atmospheric models, dust and ice opacities, and optional thermal emission. Most spectra favor additional large-H$_2$O-ice opacity over the baseline small-grain model, while a smaller subset requires additional gray opacity, potentially arising from large refractory grains. Some spectra also require a thermal component to reproduce the long-wavelength continuum. The parameters inferred with this framework, together with the NGC 2024 distance, yield radii and bolometric luminosities. Comparison with evolutionary models for young low-mass and substellar objects provides mass estimates and identifies 46 candidate members, 27 sources classified as background, and five uncertain sources. Candidate members show lower fitted surface gravities than likely background stars. Of the candidate members, 23 lie above the youngest available isochrones, plausibly reflecting the cluster's youth and uncertainties in model initial conditions. The resulting catalog provides a uniform characterization of the NGC 2024 sample and establishes a framework for treating complex extinction and circumstellar emission in JWST spectra of other obscured star-forming regions.

16. Probing the nature of Galactic microquasars with diffuse gamma rays and neutrinos: low-mass versus high-mass X-ray binaries[2611.01000]
Abstract

The observations of sub-PeV gamma-ray emission have provided strong evidence for a Galactic origin of the cosmic-ray "knee" region. Meanwhile, mounting observations have ruled out most supernova remnants as sources of PeV cosmic rays, i.e., the so-called PeVatrons. Recently, a new type of PeVatron candidate, microquasars, has received much attention. Studies indicate that microquasars could be the dominant contributor to PeV cosmic rays. However, the spatial distribution of microquasars is not known. They could belong to either LMXBs or HMXBs, depending on the mass of the optical companion star. In this work, we study which type could be the dominant by means of very high energy gamma rays and neutrinos. We find that the diffuse gamma-ray distributions along the Galactic longitude are quite different due to the spatial distributions of these two kinds of sources. If the very high energy gamma rays are dominated by the distribution of LMXBs, the gamma-ray distribution would be more concentrated toward the direction of the Galactic center. Furthermore, we also find that HMXB distribution cannot account for the latest observations of Galactic diffuse neutrinos. Higher-precision measurements of the Galactic diffuse gamma-ray and neutrino fluxes with LHAASO, HAWC, IceCube, and KM3NeT will allow the spatial distribution of the microquasar population to be determined and the origin of the knee to be tested.

18. exoALMA XXV: A Statistical Analysis of Gaseous Substructure in Low-Inclination Disks[2611.01209]
Abstract

Protoplanetary disks exhibit complex structure in both their gas dynamics and their temperature. Characterizing it has proven challenging, owing to the mismatch between the observed complexity and the relatively simple semi-analytical models typically used to interpret it. To augment these analyses, we apply the second-order structure function to the velocity and temperature perturbations of four low-inclination sources from the exoALMA program, each measured against a noise-only null built from the data themselves. All exhibit correlated structure. Its radial correlation length is limited by the beam in every case, which excludes non-axisymmetric structure on radial scales larger than  1.5 pressure scale heights. In three of the four disks the structure is instead resolved and elongated in azimuth, by factors of 1.6-2.3, as expected of small-scale perturbations sheared out by Keplerian rotation. We further introduce two statistics that characterize spiral structure without tracing an arm crest: a modal power spectrum, measuring the periodicity of the azimuthal structure, and a winding contrast, measuring the radial dependence of its phase. The velocity residuals of three disks are significantly wound. MWC 758 is the only source in which the winding is coherent across the disk and shared by the temperature field. Its trailing m=1 temperature structure is consistent with the illumination of a warp, while its velocity field carries small-scale, sheared structure that could redistribute angular momentum. Because neither statistic requires a crest, both extend a quantitative description of winding to the flocculent regime, where a spiral cannot be parameterized.

19. Cuscuton Fifth Force: Implications of Non-Dynamical Scalars for Structure Growth[2611.01228]
Abstract

The cuscuton is a scalar field that obeys a constraint rather than an evolution equation, so it modifies gravity without adding a propagating scalar degree of freedom. We study the cosmology of effective cuscuton theory when matter is minimally coupled in the Jordan frame, and therefore conformally coupled to the cuscuton in the Einstein-frame. We derive the background and linear perturbation equations and show that the algebraic cuscuton response mediates a fifth force even though the scalar itself does not propagate. The same conformal factor also changes Einstein-frame particle masses and the friction of matter perturbations, tying the strength and sign of the force to the background evolution rather than to an independent coupling. We study a power-law family of models which contain both tracking solutions and sign-switching solutions; in the latter the effective cuscuton density runs from negative during matter domination to a positive, cosmological-constant-like value today. Using a modified version of CLASS, including the induced particle-mass dependence of recombination, we illustrate the corresponding changes to CMB temperature and linear matter power spectra for benchmark models, without fitting to data. In the strongest benchmarks the fifth force changes the effective gravitational coupling on sub-horizon scales by about one percent, smaller than the accompanying changes to the matter power spectrum and the acoustic scale. For the sign-switching solutions this change is negative over most of cosmic history, so a non-propagating field can weaken rather than strengthen gravitational attraction.

20. Roughness-Dependent Radiative Effects on Dimorphos within Hera's Reach[2611.01235]
Abstract

Following the Double Asteroid Redirection Test (DART) impact, radiative recoil, tides, and other dissipative processes drive the dynamical evolution of the Didymos-Dimorphos binary, yet the effect of unresolved surface roughness on radiative migration remains poorly quantified. We incorporate this roughness into a binary thermophysical model coupled to planar spin-orbit dynamics. During the 6-month investigation by ESA's Hera mission, surface roughness significantly reduces the radiative contribution to the mean orbital contraction rate by 28-39% relative to smooth surfaces for two post-impact shape models of Dimorphos in synchronous principal-axis rotation. With surface roughness included, planar-libration calculations still yield centimeter-scale radiation-driven orbital contraction that increases mean motion, contributing a mean of 40-85 deg to orbital-phase advance from DART to Hera's arrival. During Hera's investigation, radiative recoil in these calculations contributes a mean orbital-phase advance of 0.7-2.0 deg, which could be detectable given Hera's projected 0.05 deg formal orbital-longitude uncertainty. In the modeled tumbling state, the net radiative contribution to the mean orbital contraction rate over the same interval is much smaller, with surface roughness reducing it by 16-18%. These results identify thermal roughness as a key factor in radiative migration estimates that, combined with Hera's orbital and rotational observations, can constrain post-impact dissipation and rubble-pile mechanical properties relevant to planetary defense. Refining the thermophysical treatment of roughness with Hera's thermal and shape measurements can improve binary YORP estimates from remote infrared observations of other synchronous binaries, helping infer tidal response from measured orbital migration to probe asteroid interiors and advance understanding of binary evolution and lifetimes.

22. Bayesian and frequentist constraints on dark energy parameterizations using non-CMB data[2611.01505]
Abstract

Data from the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI) BAO combined with the cosmic microwave background (CMB) have provided evidence for dynamical dark energy (DDE) with a phantom crossing ($w(z) < -1$) when constraining $w_0-w_a$ parameterizations. Given the tension between BAO and CMB data within $\Lambda$CDM, we use only non-CMB data: Type Ia Supernovae (SNe) from the DES-Dovekie compilation, DESI BAO DR2, Gamma-Ray Bursts (GRBs) and Redshift-Space-Distortion (RSD) measurements. We use the dataset combinations GRB$+$BAO, BAO$+$SNe and GRB$+$BAO$+$SNe, each with RSD. We perform both Bayesian and frequentist analyses of two quintessence-inspired thawing dark energy parameterizations: the thawing quintessence parameterization (TQD) and the Algebraic Thawing parameterization (AT), together with the Chevallier-Polarski-Linder (CPL) and the Generalized Emergent Dark Energy parameterizations (GEDE). This difference is consistent with the Occam penalty from the prior volume in the Bayes factor. GEDE, with the DE density restricted to be suppressed at high redshifts is disfavored by all three metrics. Hence, the preference for DDE depends on the parameterization, the dataset and the comparison metric used.

23. foraois: Fast, Flexible Extended Press-Schechter Merger Trees with a General First-Crossing Solver[2611.01580]

117. Stability analysis and double critical phenomenon in the Einstein-Maxwell-scalar theory[2604.00960]
Abstract

We investigate the dynamical stability and phase transition behavior in a holographic superfluid model incorporating higher-order self-interaction terms $\lambda |\psi|^4$, $\tau|\psi|^6$, and a non-minimal coupling $h(\psi)=e^{\alpha|\psi|^2}$. Thermodynamic and dynamical stability analyzes show that the thermodynamic stability and dynamical stability of the system are consistent. Phase diagram analysis reveals rich critical and supercritical phenomena. 118. Probing Cosmic-Ray-Boosted and Supernova-Sourced Sub-GeV Dark Matter with Paleo-Detectors[2604.17869]
Abstract

Astrophysical dark matter particles with masses well below GeV-scale can be difficult to detect using conventional nuclear recoil experiments due to their low velocities in our Milky Way halo. Elastic scattering with high-energy cosmic rays or thermal production inside core-collapse supernovae can accelerate sub-GeV DM to (semi-)relativistic velocities, producing nuclear recoil energies above the keV threshold that paleo-detectors can record over geological timescales. Using olivine as the target with 100$\,$g$\cdot$Gyr exposure, we compute track length distributions from such (semi-)relativistic dark matter fluxes, incorporating all major backgrounds (neutrinos, uranium-chain neutrons, thorium recoils) with a statistical analysis on an Asimov dataset. Our results show that paleo-detectors are able to probe large parameter regions that are not covered by current and near-future experiments designed to detect dark matter and neutrinos. In particular, paleo-detectors offer a unique ability to record the dark matter flux from Galactic supernova events over geological times. Such cumulative exposure enables sensitivity gains of a few orders of magnitude compared to conventional experiments.

120. Relativistic and Newtonian Proca Stars: A Tale of Two Limits[2606.13785]
Abstract

We investigate a representative set of static solitonic solutions of the Einstein-Proca theory in the Newtonian regime, where the field frequency approaches the particle mass, $\omega \to \mu$, and compare them with the corresponding solutions of the spin-1 Schrödinger-Poisson system, which provides the effective description in this limit. While this correspondence is relatively straightforward in the Einstein-Klein-Gordon case, the vector nature of the Proca field, combined with the enhanced $U(3)$ symmetry of the nonrelativistic spin-1 regime, gives rise to several nontrivial features that require careful analysis. We establish a mapping between the two descriptions by identifying $\ell=0$ electric Proca stars with radially polarized (hedgehog) configurations and $\ell=1$ electric Proca stars with linearly polarized configurations. 121. Ultra-High-Energy Cosmic Ray Boosted Relic Neutrinos[2606.18088]
Abstract

Ultra-high-energy cosmic rays (UHECRs) can boost relic neutrinos to high energies through Standard Model (SM) neutral-current interactions, providing an indirect probe of the cosmic neutrino background (C$\nu$B). In this work, we perform a systematic study of the diffuse UHECR-boosted C$\nu$B flux including elastic neutrino–nucleon scattering (ES), coherent elastic neutrino–nucleus scattering (COH), incoherent neutrino–nucleus scattering (INCOH), baryon-resonance production (RES), and deep inelastic scattering (DIS). For the UHECR flux, we use mixed-composition spectra obtained from the UHECR propagation code PriNCe and from the H3a and H4a implementations of the Hillas model, together with SFR, QSO, and GRB source evolution models. We find a clear hierarchy of scattering channels as a function of boosted neutrino energy. The coherent scattering dominates at low-energy neutrino flux for heavy nuclear components, while ES and INCOH become important once individual nucleons are resolved. The RES channel gives a non-negligible contribution in the high-energy region, and DIS appears only at the highest energies and is most visible for the H4a case. Using current IceCube and Pierre Auger Observatory data, we obtain upper limits on the C$\nu$B overdensity. Our results show that reliable predictions of the UHECR-boosted C$\nu$B signal require a combined treatment of the relevant SM scattering channels, UHECR composition, source evolution, and the neutrino mass spectrum.

122. Internal heating in rapidly rotating convection is not a shortcut to geostrophic turbulence[2608.28756]
Abstract

Convective turbulence in planets and stars is often driven by internal heating. This forcing mechanism has also been proposed as a means of accessing the diffusion-free scalings of the geostrophic turbulence (GT) regime at modest forcing. We test this with the asymptotically reduced quasi-geostrophic model, which is formally valid in the limit of vanishing Ekman number, $Ek \rightarrow 0$, and retains the Prandtl number as an independent parameter. We find no such shortcut: the Nusselt and Reynolds numbers are no closer to their diffusion-free predictions than in the boundary-heated case; the predicted $Pr^{-1/2}$ collapse fails over the range surveyed; and the prefactor depends on the form of the heating. Our no-slip/stress-free cases track the radiatively driven simulations of Hadjerci, Bouillaut, Miquel & Gallet (J. Fluid Mech., vol. 998, 2024, A9) case by case. Those simulations span only the narrow range of forcing over which the compensated Nusselt number is near its maximum within their geostrophic-turbulence criterion; across the wider range accessible to the reduced model it rises to that maximum and then falls by more than an order of magnitude while the flow remains rotationally constrained. We argue that bulk transport scalings are incomplete diagnostics of GT, whereas the saturation of the interior mean temperature gradient and of the vertical velocity kurtosis remain reliable indicators.
