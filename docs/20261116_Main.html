1. Holographically motivated Hubble-running dark energy: Phantom-divide crossing in VCDM and observational constraints[2611.17374]
Abstract

Motivated by power-law dependences on the expansion scale in holographic dark-energy constructions, we study the Hubble-running equation of state $w_{\rm DE}=w_0(H/H_0)^q$ (HoloEoS), where $H$ is the Hubble expansion rate, $H_0$ and $w_0$ are the present values of $H$ and $w_{\rm DE}$, and $q$ is a constant dimensionless exponent controlling the running. We embed this history in VCDM, a type-II minimally modified gravity theory that propagates only the two tensor modes of general relativity. For regular expanding histories with standard matter, we reconstruct the potential $V(\phi)$ of its nondynamical auxiliary scalar field, providing a Lagrangian description of both the homogeneous background and cosmological perturbations. This framework allows HoloEoS to realize a smooth late-time crossing of the phantom divide $w_{\rm DE}=-1$. We confront this VCDM realization with SPT, Planck, and ACT CMB data, updated DESI DR2 distances, and optionally DESY5 supernovae with Dovekie recalibration, comparing it with $\Lambda$CDM and the Chevallier–Polarski–Linder parameterization embedded in the same gravitational framework (CPL-VCDM). For the full data combination, the posterior medians and central $68\%$ credible intervals are $w_0=-0.881^{+0.040}_{-0.038}$ and $q=0.453^{+0.143}_{-0.133}$, with a crossing redshift $z_\times=0.476^{+0.106}_{-0.081}$ conditional on $0<z_\times<6$. For this data combination, physical-likelihood optimization by Metropolis–Hastings (MH) simulated annealing gives Akaike information criterion differences of $\Delta\mathrm{AIC}\simeq-3.40$ for HoloEoS and $-4.10$ for CPL-VCDM relative to $\Lambda$CDM. Thus, a Hubble-scale-motivated dark-energy history realized within a specified gravitational theory achieves fitting performance comparable to CPL-VCDM while naturally allowing phantom-divide crossing.

2. Full-shape analysis of the Lyman-alpha forest power spectrum and bispectrum: validation on simulations[2611.17405]
Abstract

The Lyman-$\alpha$ (LyA) forest of neutral hydrogen provides a large-volume, high-resolution map of cosmological fluctuations from optical spectra at high redshift. To extract more information from the currently observing Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI), we derive an effective field theory (EFT) model for joint full-shape analyses of the LyA forest data. It combines the one-loop power spectrum of the LyA forest (FF), and of its cross-correlation with dark-matter halos, used as proxies for high-redshift quasars (FQ), with the tree-level bispectrum of the LyA forest (FFF). Importantly, the bispectrum breaks the LyA-specific degeneracy between the mean flux and the amplitude of the power spectrum. We validate our theory framework on idealized large-volume LyA forest mocks painted onto the AbacusSummit N-body simulation suite at redshift z=2.5. Fitting scales up to $k_\mathrm{max}^{FF\,(FQ)}=0.6\,(0.3)\,h\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-1}$ in the power spectrum and $k_\mathrm{max}^{FFF}=0.1\,h\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-1}$ in the bispectrum, our full-shape analysis yields unbiased constraints on the cosmological parameters in simulations. For a fully sampled flux field in a simulated volume of $V=8\,h^{-3}\,{\rm Gpc}^3$, we measure the Hubble constant, $H_0$, to $0.6\%$, the matter density, $\Omega_{\rm m}$, to $1.6\%$ and the amplitude of matter clustering, $\sigma_8$, to $3.5\%$. Adding the bispectrum improves the constraints on $\sigma_8$ by $28\%$. We also demonstrate that the inclusion of the quasar one-loop power spectrum (QQ) and tree-level bispectrum (QQQ) further improves cosmological constraints by $\approx 35\%$ using $k_\mathrm{max}^{QQ,\,(QQQ)}=0.2,\,(0.08)\,h\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-1}$. Our theory framework enables full-shape analyses of high-redshift data from DESI and its successor DESI-II, enhancing knowledge of the growth of structure of the Universe during matter-domination.

3. The Full-Shape Analysis of the Fading Dark Sector Model[2611.17422]
Abstract

We analyze the Fading Dark Sector (FDS) model, which is motivated by string theory and in which the fading of the dark sector is driven by a scalar field. The model is specified by two $O(1)$ numbers $(c,c')$ that parametrize the logarithmic derivatives of the dark energy and dark matter densities with respect to the field in Planck units. In previous work, we showed that this model provides a good fit to the DESI BAO, CMB, and SN datasets. Here, we extend that analysis to include the DESI DR1 full-shape galaxy power spectrum, which adds information about the time evolution of cosmic structure and provides an independent consistency check of the FDS model. We find that, when the DESI DR1 full-shape data are combined with CMB+DESI DR2 BAO+DES-Dovekie, the FDS model outperforms the Chevallier-Polarski-Linder (CPL) $w_0$-$w_a$ parameterization by $\Delta \chi^2_{\rm MAP}=-4$ at equal parameter count and is preferred over $\Lambda$CDM at $4.1\sigma$. The inferred values of $(c,c')$ with the full-shape data are unchanged from our previous analysis, so this improvement indicates that FDS makes a prediction for the growth of structure that captures the observed growth history better than the $w_0$-$w_a$ parameterization.

4. Exoplanets are predominately aligned with exo-Kuiper Belts[2611.17423]
Abstract

The architectures of planetary systems encode their formation and dynamical histories. In the Solar System, the near alignment of planetary orbits, the Kuiper Belt, and the Sun's equator supports planet formation in a rotating disk of gas and dust. However, some exoplanet systems show planets on orbits misaligned with their host stars or other planets, raising the question of whether misalignments are primordial or produced by post-formation dynamics. We present the first population-level constraints on the mutual inclinations of 32 planets in 16 systems relative to debris disks. We find that planets across orbital distances from 0.1 to 100 AU are predominantly aligned with the debris disks within 10 deg (30/32 planets). The alignment holds across systems spanning tens of million years to a few gigayears in age. These results indicate that the aligned architecture of the Solar System is representative of the birth conditions of exoplanet systems.

5. Augmenting Galaxy Catalogues using Machine Learning in the FLAMINGO Simulations[2611.17425]
Abstract

Hydrodynamic simulations are instrumental in the analysis of observations such as galaxy clustering used for the estimation of cosmological parameters. However, for these purposes, the simulations need both a large box size and high resolution, which is computationally prohibitive. In this work we use the connection between galaxies and their host dark matter halos to populate dark-matter-only simulations, using data from the FLAMINGO simulations suite. We employ the Extremely Randomized Trees algorithm to model the connection between the mass profile, history and environment of dark matter halos and the probability distribution of the stellar mass of the galaxy contained in the halo. We use halos matched between a dark-matter-only simulation and the corresponding hydrodynamical simulation to train a model for centrals. For satellites, we employ a Gaussian Processes emulator that simultaneously optimises the hyperparameters of a model and selects for the dark matter features that are predictive of the stellar mass while being insensitive to the differences between the hydrodynamic and dark-matter-only simulations, then train a model on only these features. We then apply these models to produce a new galaxy catalogue for the 2.8 Gpc FLAMINGO-10k dark-matter-only simulation as a product of this work. We demonstrate that this catalogue reproduces the statistics of the galaxy-halo connection predicted by the smaller volume hydrodynamical simulation on which the model was trained, including the stellar mass function, stellar mass-dark matter mass relation, 2-point correlation functions and correlations between stellar mass and dark matter concentration, half mass radius and dark matter mass within 3 Mpc.

6. Gravitational-wave hierarchical inference from population samples[2611.17428]
Abstract

Standard approaches to gravitational-wave population inference require evaluating the probability density of the population model. While appropriate for models composed of exact functional forms, this approach is not compatible with state-of-the-art astrophysical predictions from population-synthesis codes, which simulate samples from astrophysical models whose densities are unknown. We present an approach that solely requires samples and never density evaluations of the targeted population model. Key technical ingredients include efficient surrogates for the single-event likelihoods and detection probability, together with an efficient sampling strategy. Using detections up to GWTC-5.0, we validate the method against standard inference with a parametric model, showcase its flexibility on a simulated population including hierarchical mergers, and present a realistic application to a grid of astrophysical population-synthesis simulations with five formation channels. This work paves the way toward a direct comparison of astrophysical models and gravitational-wave data.

7. Searching for magnetic field changes in the central compact object 1E 1207.4-5209 in two decades of X-ray spectra[2611.17430]
Abstract

The central compact object (CCO) 1E 1207.4-5209 is remarkable in that its X-ray spectrum shows clear absorption features at  0.7 and 1.4 keV. Attributing these features to electron cyclotron resonance, the implied surface magnetic field is B (7-8)x10^10 G, which agrees approximately with the field inferred from the spin properties of the pulsar. Here we examine all XMM-Newton EPIC pn spectra from more than 30 observations of 1E 1207.4-5209 from 2001-2020. We model the rotation phase-averaged and phase-resolved spectra with a two blackbody and multiple absorption line model, focusing on potential changes in the absorption lines which would correspond to changes in the magnetic field that could be related to the pulsar's possible spin glitch in 2015 and recent detection in radio. We find the phase-averaged spectral parameters, and by implication the magnetic field, are constant across nearly 20 years of data, while some parameters characterizing the phase-resolved spectra vary with rotation phase. The spectral line energies and widths (e.g., E_1 0.66 keV and full width of 0.34 keV) imply the magnetic field distribution across the X-ray emitting regions spans the range B (6-11)x10^10 G, with the highest fields being visible during the declining phase of the pulsations of 1E 1207.4-5209.

8. Euclid DR1 Legacy Science on Ultracool Dwarfs: New URLD candidates in Euclid and SPHEREx[2611.17519]
Abstract

Unusually red L dwarfs (URLDs) probe extreme atmospheric conditions in the substellar regime, where enhanced dust opacity, cloud structure, and surface gravity effects challenge standard brown dwarf models. As free-floating analogues of young giant exoplanets, they provide key benchmarks for interpreting low-resolution infrared spectra from current and future wide-field space missions. The goal is to identify new red L dwarf candidates both photometrically and spectroscopically in the first data release (DR1) of the Euclid space mission. The area covered is about 1,900 and 50 square degrees in the wide and deep survey, respectively. We used colour cuts in multiple colour-colour diagrams combined with point-source and quality criteria to identify candidate red L dwarfs in both the Euclid wide and deep survey fields. We cross-matched photometric candidates with the SPHEREx mission and Euclid red grism spectra to confirm spectroscopic properties and evaluate bolometric magnitudes and colours. We identified 1202 and 38 red L dwarf candidates in the Euclid wide and deep surveys, respectively. Spectroscopic follow-up and archival cross-matching confirm a substantial new population of red L dwarfs. Distance-independent diagnostics show that these objects occupy distinct loci characterised by enhanced condensate opacity and significant redness. Euclid vastly expands the census of unusually red L dwarfs. While preliminary spectral classifications suggest many of these new candidates are intermediate-age (Pleiades-like) objects, a subset exhibits extremely red signatures indicative of very young, low-gravity (<=10 Myr) atmospheres, providing vital benchmarks for young exoplanet research.

9. Note on quantum optimal measurements of CMB spectral distortions[2611.17552]
Abstract

As quantum sensing techniques continue to push the frontier of detection of ultra-weak signals, one may wonder if a sophisticated quantum strategy could be used to enable measurement of spectral distortions in the cosmic microwave background. Using quantum Fisher information techniques, we prove that the optimal strategy is simple frequency-binned intensity measurement. This proof strategy also enables us to provide numerical estimates for the information-theoretic minimum observation time required to observe the $\mu$- and $y$-distortions expected from $\Lambda$CDM cosmology. As case studies, we compare these results to the PIXIE and SPECTER mission concepts, and show that they are within a few orders of magnitude of this fundamental limit.

10. The Eclipsing $δ$ Scuti Binary HIP 7666: Absolute Dimensions and Pulsation Properties[2611.17590]
Abstract

We present a comprehensive analysis of the pulsating eclipsing binary HIP 7666 (TIC 308953703), containing a $\delta$ Sct-type component, based on 120-s TESS photometry and 63 newly acquired high-resolution echelle spectra. While previous orbital solutions were limited by sparse spectroscopic sampling, our extensive radial velocity (RV) measurements enable a robust determination of the system's physical parameters. The primary has an effective temperature of $T_{\rm eff,A}$ = 7376$\pm$72 K and a projected rotational velocity of $v_{\rm A}\sin i$ = 56.6$\pm$5.3 km s$^{-1}$. Joint modeling of the light and RV curves shows that HIP 7666 is a detached system in a circular orbit, with Roche-lobe filling factors of $f_{\rm A}$ = 57 \% and $f_{\rm B}$ = 44 \%. The masses and radii are determined to better than 2 \% precision: $M_{\rm A}$ = 1.822$\pm$0.029 $M_\odot$, $R_{\rm A}$ = 2.154$\pm$0.017 $R_\odot$ for the primary, and $M_{\rm B}$ = 1.246$\pm$0.020 $M_\odot$, $R_{\rm B}$ = 1.170$\pm$0.015 $R_\odot$ for the secondary. The binary-based distance (316$\pm$8 pc) agrees well with the Gaia DR3 parallax-based distance (313$\pm$1 pc), validating the derived system parameters. Frequency analysis of the light-curve residuals reveals 15 significant signals, predominantly in the $\delta$ Sct pulsation frequency range. PARSEC evolutionary tracks indicate an age of $\sim$0.9 Gyr and a supersolar metallicity ($Z=0.03$), with the primary on the main sequence and the secondary located close to the zero-age main sequence. Galactic kinematics place HIP 7666 in the thin disk.

11. Polarization of strongly lensed gravitational waves as a probe of the lens-induced orientations of electromagnetic counterparts[2611.17637]
Abstract

Strong gravitational lensing is a well-established phenomenon in electromagnetic (EM) observations, where the lensed images and arcs are resolved on the two-dimensional sky and constrain the mass distribution of the lens. Strongly lensed gravitational waves (GWs) are also expected to be detected routinely by next-generation detectors, but their multiple images are recorded only as one-dimensional time series without positions. Among them, lensed binary neutron star mergers can be accompanied by EM counterparts with resolvable structures, such as relativistic jets. In EM lensing, the lens-induced change in the apparent orientation of such a structure can be measured only relative to other images or statistically, because its intrinsic orientation is not directly observable. In this work, we show that the GW polarization, which the lens leaves unchanged, provides this missing reference, i.e., a compass, so that the orientation offset of each image can be measured in absolute terms. We place this orientation layer on the EM image plane together with image positions from the lensed host arcs and Fermat-potential heights from GW time delays and magnifications. Using a mock population of such mergers observable by the Einstein Telescope and Cosmic Explorer, we find that the predicted image positions and arrival times are calibrated once the detection selection is included. The lens-induced orientation offsets reach tens of degrees and are detectable in up to a third of the events, at viewing angles above about $40^\circ$ for quadrupole-dominated signals.

12. DART ejecta reveal the recoil momentum budget of a kinetic impact[2611.17649]
Abstract

Double Asteroid Redirection Test (DART) demonstrated that ejecta recoil enhances asteroid deflection, but the ejecta processes generating and distributing this recoil remained unclear. We reconstruct three-dimensional ejecta evolution from time-resolved morphology and brightness observations, recovering previously missing constraints on momentum transfer and material transport. Integrating reconstructed momentum yields momentum enhancement factors $\beta_\mathrm{D}=2.13 \pm 0.19 (1{\sigma})$ for Dimorphos and $\beta_\mathrm{sys}=2.04 \pm 0.19 (1{\sigma})$ for the Didymos system. Combined with the measured deflection, the reconstructed recoil constrains Dimorphos's bulk density to $1435.18_{-153.71}^{+112.59} \mathrm{kg m^{-3}}$ without assuming its mass. By linking observed orbital deflection to the released material, this reconstruction provides ejecta-based constraints for assessing kinetic-impact effectiveness and investigating how natural collisions drive mass loss and debris transport among small Solar System bodies.

13. On the Existence of a Third Planet and an Apsidal Resonance Chain in the Kepler-396 System through TESS Data[2611.17667]
Abstract

The possible existence of a third planet in the Kepler-396 system has been previously proposed. Given that the planetary mass is poorly constrained and the orbit could be either interior or exterior to planet Kepler-396 c, that proposal is not clearly known in the community. In order to address this and further investigate Kepler-396 system's configuration, new TESS data are employed to form a long baseline of transit timing. Using the pixel-level decorrelation technique, the mid-transit times are determined. Through long baseline transit-timing-variation technique, we perform a comprehensive exploration of the parameter space of the third planet. Two best-fit solutions, derived from different priors of orbital inclinations, have led to the reduced chi-square values as 2.24 and 2.34 respectively, compared to 12.97 for the two-planet model. This result strongly suggests the existence of the third planet, i.e. Kepler-396 d. Based on these two best-fit solutions, the masses of Kepler-396 b, c, d are updated to be around 9, 4, 4 Earth Mass, and the corresponding orbital periods are 43, 89, 144 days, respectively. With these updated masses, Kepler-396 b is on the border between rocky or volatile-rich, Kepler-396 c is confirmed to be a volatile-rich super-puff planet, and both planets lie in the Neptune savanna. Dynamical simulations with three planets are performed, showing that they are stable for a timescale of $10^6$ years. The examination on resonant angles leads to the conclusion that planets Kepler-396 b, c, d form a three-body apsidal resonance chain.

14. Predicting Individual Dark Matter Halo Concentrations from Their Assembly Histories[2611.17676]
Abstract

We present an empirical model for predicting concentration parameters of individual dark matter halos in cold and warm dark matter (WDM) cosmologies over z=0-5. Constructed using a large suite of cosmological N-body simulations, our model separately incorporates cosmic expansion (linear growth factor), mass scale (linear density fluctuations), halo mass assembly histories via the Extended Press-Schechter (EPS) scaled formation variable, plus a WDM damping term tied to the half-mode mass. It works for both virial and 200 $\rho_{crit}$ halo definitions. Across all tested cosmologies and redshifts, our global logarithmic fit gives concentration scatter  0.09 dex for relaxed halos and  0.09 to 0.10 dex for all halos. While relaxed halos maintain nearly constant scatter near 0.09 dex, the scatter of all halos at z>3 increases from 0.10 to 0.18 dex, revealing substantial violent-relaxation impacts on high-redshift halo structure. At fixed mass, concentration scatter is dominated by assembly history. We extract the scaled formation variable's probability distribution function (PDF) from merger trees and find departures from standard EPS theory. This PDF is narrower in WDM than LCDM due to suppressed small-scale perturbations and rapid, delayed monolithic collapse near the WDM truncation scale. Monte Carlo realizations with our formulae reproduce concentration distributions and match our prior peak-concentration model, whereas standard EPS underestimates concentrations from delayed-formation bias. Our framework offers a transparent, computationally lightweight tool to predict individual halo concentrations across diverse cosmological models.

15. Axion quintessence with a negative cosmological constant: Bayesian constraints from current cosmological data[2611.17725]
Abstract

We constrain canonical axion quintessence with a negative cosmological constant using DES Year 5 supernovae, DESI DR2 BAO, compressed CMB information, and geometry-corrected Gold-2017 growth data. We solve the background and linear-growth equations and sample with PolyChord. Our joint prior is logarithmic in the decay constant over $0.005\leq f/M_{\rm Pl}\leq10$ and uniform in the initial angle $\theta_i=\phi_i/f\in[0,\pi]$, conditioned on $\pi-\theta_i\geq H_{\rm inf}/(2\pi f)$ with $H_{\rm inf}=10^{13}$ GeV and reduced Planck mass $M_{\rm Pl}$. The broad-prior model favors recent thawing, with median $w_{{\rm DE},0}=-0.865^{+0.068}_{-0.053}$, $m_\phi\in[0.91,4.39]\times10^{-33}$ eV, and $f/M_{\rm Pl}\in[0.589,4.899]$ (central 68\% credible intervals). Restricting $f\leq M_{\rm Pl}$ gives $f/M_{\rm Pl}\in[0.262,0.822]$ at 68\% credibility and Bayes factors 0.233 and 32.4. Relative to the zero-vacuum axion model, the broad-prior Bayes factors are 0.620 and 1.65, providing no compelling evidence for a negative cosmological constant. Local likelihood optimization gives an Akaike information criterion difference $\Delta{\rm AIC}\simeq-10.87$ for the zero-vacuum model relative to flat $\Lambda$CDM. For this model, assuming that the instanton generating $m_\phi^2f^2=\Lambda_{\rm UV}^4e^{-S_{\rm inst}}$ satisfies the axionic Weak Gravity Conjecture, the one-sided 95\% posterior lower limit on the minimum compatible coefficient $c_{\min}$ rises from 37 to 55 as the ultraviolet scale $\Lambda_{\rm UV}$ increases from $10^{-10}M_{\rm Pl}$ to $M_{\rm Pl}$, in tension with an order-one coefficient under these assumptions.

16. Periodic Modulation of Linear Polarization Fraction in Repeating FRB 20240114A[2611.17805]
Abstract

Long-term polarization changes in repeating fast radio bursts (FRBs) can probe their emission geometry and propagation environment. We searched for periodic variation in the degree of linear polarization (DOL) of FRB 20240114A using the polarization catalog from the Five-hundred-meter Aperture Spherical radio Telescope. The sample contains 6,099 bursts detected on 95 observing days over $\sim 488$ days. The daily mean DOL series was analyzed using phase distance correlation and Lomb–Scargle periodograms over trial periods of 10–240 days. The strongest peaks occur at $119.91^{+2.67}_{-2.04}$ and $122.31^{+2.63}_{-2.82}$ days, respectively. The quoted ranges correspond to the period intervals above the global $3\sigma$ thresholds. Global false-alarm probabilities were estimated from \(10^7\) random permutations of the daily values among the fixed observing dates, with the full period search repeated for each permutation. As a supplementary check, the same analysis using daily median DOL yielded similar peaks near 120 days. A descriptive sinusoidal fit to all 95 daily mean DOL measurements at a fixed period of 119.91 days has a semi-amplitude of 3.23\%. Possible explanations include precession-driven changes in viewing geometry and variations in propagation conditions within a binary system.

17. Squeezing Drives Southward Magnetic Field Enhancement During 10 May 2024 CME-CME Interaction[2611.17807]
Abstract

The southward magnetic field carried by coronal mass ejections (CMEs) is a key driver of space weather events, yet how interacting CMEs amplify this field remains debated. The 10 May 2024 event, which produced the strongest geomagnetic storm since 2003, offers an ideal testbed for this question. Combining multi-spacecraft observations with high-resolution controlled numerical experiments, we track the CMEs continuously from their origin near the solar surface to beyond Earth, and find that inherent squeezing during CME-CME interactions generates the sustained enhancement of the southward field, exceeding 60 nT in magnitude near Earth. First driven by the dynamic pressure of the trailing CME and later maintained by magnetic and thermal pressures at the CME-CME interface, squeezing transforms the preceding CME from a bulky cloud into a flattened "pancake" structure, prevents expansion-induced field decay, and maintains an amplified southward field over long distances. The strength of this enhancement scales with the intensity of the squeezing. Our findings establish a direct link between CME-CME interaction and strong southward fields, laying a physics-based foundation for understanding and forecasting the geoeffectiveness of interacting CMEs.

18. Probing the Activity History of Repeating Fast Radio Bursts with Cluster Lensing[2611.17833]
Abstract

Fast radio bursts (FRBs) are millisecond duration coherent radio transients, some of whose sources repeat, but the nature and active lifetimes of their engines remain uncertain. Strong lensing by galaxy clusters provides a physical lever on this lifetime problem: a short campaign can sample emission epochs of a repeating FRB separated by years, and distinct bursts in different lensed images, given a known relative lens delay, set a lower bound on its active lifetime, whereas delayed copies of a single burst only encode the lens delay. Based on the cluster-lens model of MACS J0717.5+3745, we quantify the accessible range of emission epochs for finite activity episodes, observing windows, and discovery selection. For a source-frame episode of duration \(100~{\rm yr}\) at \(z_s=2\) and a source-frame rate of \(0.1\,{\rm h}_{\rm src}^{-1}\), a five year campaign conditional on discovery yields a \(95\%\) gain threshold of \(m_{95}\simeq25.6\), corresponding to a source-frame span of \(L_{95}\simeq42.6~{\rm yr}\). At the population level, combining distinct burst associations with same burst echoes provides a way to test specified activity durations with a finite lens catalogue, e.g., activity durations differing by an order of magnitude can be distinguished with expected Bayes factor above $20$. More broadly, these lensed source frame epochs enable applications beyond activity-duration tests, e.g., linking burst activity to the evolution of the engine and its environment. Galaxy-cluster lensing therefore provides a promising future probe for accessing otherwise unobserved source history and for distinguishing between different activity duration models.

19. High-resolution Observations of Physical Properties of Dynamic Fibrils Around a Sunspot[2611.17838]
Abstract

Understanding the formation and evolution of dynamic fibrils (DFs) in active regions is essential for uncovering the physical processes operating in the highly structured and magnetized solar atmosphere. High-resolution chromospheric observations from the New Vacuum Solar Telescope (NVST) show DFs with periodic up-and-down motions. We carry out a statistical analysis of 176 DFs around a sunspot. The mean values of deceleration, maximum velocity, maximum length, and duration of the DFs are $120 \boldsymbol{\pm 43}~\mathrm{m~s^{-2}}$, $15.9 \boldsymbol{\pm 4.5}~\mathrm{km~s^{-1}}$, $1.13 \boldsymbol{\pm 0.48}~\mathrm{Mm}$, and $270 \boldsymbol{\pm 63}~\mathrm{s}$, respectively. 20. What constrains the neutron-star crust? Present data, future measurements, and EOS doppelgängers[2611.17920]
Abstract

The neutron-star crust plays a central role in pulsar glitches, thermal evolution, and stellar oscillations, yet its properties remain difficult to constrain. We use Bayesian inference with a unified crust-core equation of state to assess what present nuclear and astrophysical data tell us about the crust and what future stellar measurements can add. Within this model, present crust constraints are dominated by chiral effective field theory, with laboratory data providing additional information and current stellar observations having a smaller effect. The adopted theoretical bands determine how tightly the crust properties are constrained, while the treatment of neutron-rich surface energy affects their inferred values. Forecasts centred on a single reference equation of state show that more precise measurements of radius, tidal deformability, and total moment of inertia mainly constrain crust thickness. They provide little additional information about the crust-core transition, crust mass, or crustal moment of inertia and do not recover the precision lost when the chiral effective field theory constraint is removed. Two models with nearly identical global stellar observables can have crust masses and crustal moments of inertia differing by about a factor of two, yet remain indistinguishable at the assumed precision. Precise global measurements therefore need not uniquely determine the crust. Observations that directly probe the crust are needed to distinguish these models.

21. Numerical experiments on sunspot penumbral flows[2611.17946]
Abstract

We present 3D radiative magnetohydrodynamic simulations of sunspots with a self-consistently generated penumbra. We analyze the impact of resolution, surrounding opposite-polarity fields and subsurface large-scale flows on the structure and stability of the simulated penumbra. Extended penumbral filaments are obtained for a subset of the simulations. These were run at 25 km (15 km) horizontal (vertical) resolution and with initial conditions favoring fields with strong horizontal components in the penumbral region. The surface velocities in these simulations are consistent with observations of Evershed flow. The main driving mechanism remains the same as illustrated previously in Rempel (2012). The most successful simulations produce extended penumbrae using a potential magnetic field top boundary, in contrast to previous simulations of extended penumbra with an artificial enhancement of horizontal magnetic field at the top boundary.

Abstract

We expand the analysis of the spin distribution with an enlarged sample of 39 accreting neutron stars and investigate whether the high-frequency objects preferentially occur in binaries with longer orbital periods and have higher quiescent surface temperatures. Methods. We compare several empirical models for the spin distribution. We use stochastic drift-diffusion estimates and simple braking models to determine whether short-timescale torque fluctuations or secular spin-down can account for the observed population widths. We explore six families of models to describe the heterogeneous spatial distribution of radiogenic elements in the Earth's mantle. Each model contains anomalous bodies or reservoirs carrying an excess of heat-producing elements over a depleted mantle (DM) background, with total inventory set by bulk-silicate-Earth mass balance and by a geochemical Th/U constraint. For each model, we compute the geo-response at each detector site and fit the resulting signals to the four measurements. A total of 968,274 individual model configurations are defined and tested. The results of $\sim$3 million distinct fits show that no plausible mantle or lithosphere model applied uniformly to all four sites reproduces the difference between the inferred mantle signals at the Jiangmen and Kamioka sites. Only local, site-specific prescriptions are able to reach the observed difference, suggesting that the Kamioka-Jiangmen tension is due to a local effect or statistical scatter, rather than large-scale mantle structure.
