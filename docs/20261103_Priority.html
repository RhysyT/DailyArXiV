1. SEAMLESS Survey: A Closer Look at Seven Local Volume Dwarf Galaxies in Diverse Environments[2610.40765]
Abstract

We present Hubble Space Telescope (HST) imaging of six Local Volume dwarf galaxies identified by the SEAMLESS survey - Cetus B, Hydrus A, Ursa Major C, Hydra B, Grapes, and M83-dw14 - along with Centaurus C (Cen C), discovered serendipitously in the M83-dw14 field. We report the discovery of M83-dw14 and Cen C for the first time. Our HST imaging resolves each system into individual stars, enabling measurements of tip of the red giant branch (TRGB) distances (3.6-6.5 Mpc), structural parameters, and star formation histories (SFHs). We complement these data with new HI observations from the Very Large Array for Ursa Major C, Hydra B, and M83-dw14 (also covering Cen C), and from the Green Bank Telescope for Grapes, together with existing HI limits for Cetus B and Hydrus A. With accurate TRGB distances, we place each target in its environmental context, ranging from a confirmed satellite (M83-dw14) to isolated systems (Hydrus A, Hydra B, Grapes, Cen C). Cen C ($M_V=-7.96$) is among the faintest isolated dwarfs known. It matches the size and luminosity of Leo T, the lowest mass gas-rich galaxy currently known, yet unlike Leo T it shows neither detected HI ($\log(M_{\rm HI}/M_{\odot})<5.3$) nor recent star formation. Ursa Major C shows strongly asymmetric, spatially offset HI, suggesting that feedback may efficiently displace gas in such low-mass systems. Overall, our sample exhibits a broad range of SFHs and quenching states that does not track simply with present-day environment. This diversity is consistent with recent evidence that similarly varied SFHs occur even among isolated low-mass dwarfs, pointing to the important roles of inhomogeneous reionization, stochastic mass assembly, and internal feedback alongside environmental effects.

2. Chaos and loss: reevaluating the loss-cone formalism in the context of chaotic diving orbits in flattened potentials[2610.40767]
Abstract

The region close to a massive black hole is one of the few truly spherical geometries in galactic dynamics. Stellar structures, which dominate from pc to kpc scales, are generally at least flattened (axisymmetric) if not more irregular and time evolving. Here we show that orbits that pass between these diverse geometries are commonly chaotic, especially those which dive - visiting their minimum possible periapse. We show that these chaotic diving orbits are common across a wide range of potentials, and can lead to large variation in angular momentum orbit to orbit. We characterise the transition from regular to chaotic orbits with a notional critical angular momentum, $j_d$. We then model the flux of orbits into the loss cone including the effects of these diving orbits combined with evolution through two-body scattering. Systems which cross into the chaotic region are quickly advected away, and thus there is a large net flux across the boundary. We formulate the problem as a probability density, rather than a number density, allowing us to find a true equilibrium state. We find that the rate of disruptions when including diving orbits is boosted by a multiplicative factor of $1+\ln(j_d/j_t)$, where $j_t$ is the angular momentum corresponding to a disruption. This boost can increase disruption rates by an order of magnitude compared to the classical loss cone formalism.

3. When precise inference is wrong: Diagnosing model misspecification with applications to cosmic dawn and reionisation[2610.40770]
Abstract

As observations of the cosmic dawn (CD) and epoch of reionisation (EoR) rapidly improve in precision, the approximate forward models used to interpret them might struggle to keep pace. Bayesian inference with a misspecified model, one that cannot represent the processes generating the observations, can yield tightly constrained, yet misleading and biased astrophysics, with different parts of the observations favouring incompatible parameter values. Model misspecification can evade a $\chi^2$ goodness-of-fit test, and Bayesian model selection requires a better model, which is not always available. We therefore apply the suspiciousness, a statistic developed to quantify tensions between datasets, to CD/EoR inference as a model criticism tool that tests whether these parts can be explained by the same parameters. We first apply it to HERA 21-cm power spectrum (PS) upper limits, which are too weak to reveal misspecification, as expected, and to HST and JWST UV luminosity functions (LFs). Under our galaxy model, the UV LFs at $z\leq10$ and $z>10$ are in tension at $5.0\sigma$ in the Gaussian limit, a failure visible in the fits, although doubling the uncertainties of the $z>10$ observations reduces it to $2.1\sigma$. Finally, we analyse a mock SKA-Low AA$^\ast$ 1080 h 21-cm PS with the model that generated it and with a simpler, misspecified model. We find that the misspecified model silently fails: it fits the PS well, passing visual inspection and, for most noise realisations, a $\chi^2$ test, yet its inferred astrophysics is wrong. The Bayesian evidence disfavours it, but only relative to the true model, which is unavailable for real observations. The suspiciousness finds no tension for the well-specified model, but a $\sim3.4\sigma$ tension for the misspecified one, strongly ruling it out without requiring a more adequate candidate model.

4. Bridging Scales: Multi-Zone GRMHD-Informed Anisotropic AGN Feedback in Galaxies[2610.40784]
Abstract

We present a galaxy-scale black hole (BH) accretion and feedback model incorporating results from multi-zone general relativistic magnetohydrodynamic (GRMHD) simulations spanning the event horizon to the Bondi radius. The model replaces a fixed suppression of the Bondi rate with a nuclear-environment-dependent factor, $\kappa^{\rm hot}_{\rm cho}$, set by the local ratio of Bondi to gravitational radius, and distributes outflow mass, kinetic energy, and thermal energy according to the angular structure measured in the large-dynamic-range GRMHD calculations. We apply it to idealized isolated simulations of an M87*-like massive elliptical and a Milky Way/Sgr A*-like galaxy, spanning over three orders of magnitude in BH mass without tuning accretion or feedback efficiencies to match observables. In the M87*-like system, the GRMHD-informed angle-dependent feedback model suppresses late-time stellar mass growth more effectively than the isotropic reference model, despite similar long-term BH accretion rates. Varying the uncertain mass loading of the collimated polar component has a modest impact on BH growth and star formation, indicating that feedback anisotropy matters more than its detailed mass loading. We find that $\kappa^{\rm hot}_{\rm cho}$ varies with nuclear environment and time, yielding BH growth histories markedly different from fixed-suppression models. For Sgr A*, this variable factor yields an accretion rate more consistent with Event Horizon Telescope (EHT) constraints than a fixed, suppressed Bondi model. These results show that nuclear-environment-dependent accretion and feedback angular structure affect BH-galaxy evolution, requiring subgrid models to include more than a universal Bondi suppression factor and a scalar feedback efficiency.

5. How to feed your dragon: the role of turbulence in AGN activity[2610.40788]
Abstract

Active galactic nuclei (AGN) are thought to be important drivers of galaxy evolution. However, the way AGN jets couple their energy to the interstellar medium (ISM) remains poorly understood. Recent observations have revealed $\sim$kpc-scale jets in galaxies of different morphological types. Here, we use idealized turbulent box simulations with accreting black holes to investigate how jet-ISM interactions and feedback shape the evolution of jets. We show that a turbulent ISM is a key ingredient in both jet launching and jet propagation. Without turbulence, jet feedback initially shuts off the cold gas supply to the black hole and the system enters a steady state with jet power $\leq 10^{39}\,\mathrm{erg\,s^{-1}}$ for a $10^{6}\,\mathrm{M}_\odot$ black hole. In a turbulent medium, jets fill voids with hot diffuse material which the turbulence then carries away, replenishing the black hole's fuel supply. However, above a critical jet efficiency factor, the jet begins to significantly deplete its fuel supply and enters a self-limiting accretion regime. This critical factor depends on the ISM Mach number, with lower turbulence yielding a lower threshold. Moreover, while the jet power depends on the ambient gas density in the vicinity of the black hole, we find that, at fixed turbulent structure, the morphology and extent of jet propagation are largely insensitive to the average background density; turbulence in the ISM dominates over mean density in setting how far jets propagate, although denser media experience higher total energy injection and stronger heating.

6. Learning Galaxy Kinematics from Spatially Unresolved Spectra[2610.40789]
Abstract

Galaxy kinematics encode information about the assembly histories of galaxies and the black holes at their centers, motivating the measurement of kinematic parameters for large galaxy populations. In particular, such parameters have been shown to help identify galaxies that host supermassive black hole binaries, a major outstanding question in multi-messenger astrophysics. Yet, inferring kinematic properties from spatially unresolved observations, where distant galaxies appear as point sources, remains a challenge. Spectroscopy can address this challenge because Doppler effects encode internal motions into spectral line profiles. Exploiting this encoding requires knowing how spatial structures map onto the line profiles but this cannot be derived from first principles for complex systems such as galaxies. We present a neural network trained on spatially resolved spectra that infers kinematic properties from spatially integrated spectra, offering two advantages over classical approaches: it learns the kinematic encoding directly from data without requiring spatial resolution and remains effective even when spectral resolution only marginally resolves the lines. This enables recovering the kinematic properties for millions of unresolved galaxies in existing and future surveys.

7. The assembly of dusty galaxies at $z \geq 4$: grain growth and the evolutionary origin of the dust-to-stellar-mass scatter[2610.40790]
Abstract

Understanding dust evolution is essential for interpreting the properties of galaxies across cosmic time. Nearby galaxies have revealed a detailed picture of dust enrichment, but whether the same picture applies in the early Universe remains unclear. Here we explore the origin of interstellar dust in simulated galaxies at $z \sim 5$. Starting from the dust-to-stellar mass relation, we select five simulated star-forming galaxies with $SFR> 1 M_\odot yr^{-1}$ and $ log(M_\star/M_\odot) > 8$. We focus on three low-mass systems with comparable stellar masses ($log(M_\star/M_\odot) \sim 8.7$) but significantly different dust masses ($log(M_{\rm dust}/M_\odot) \sim 5.1-6.2$), to investigate the origin of the scatter in the relation. Our analysis combines outputs from the cosmological hydrodynamical simulation dustyGadget, a semi-analytical model for stellar dust production, and radiative-transfer post-processing with SKIRT. We confirm that stellar dust production alone reproduces only the low-dust branch of the relation. Grain growth is required to recover both its S-shape and the observed scatter, as established for nearby galaxies. The transition to efficient grain growth occurs at lower metallicity than locally, consistent with the denser interstellar medium of high-redshift systems. Differences in dust content reflect different stages of galaxy assembly. Progressively longer star formation histories lead to greater chemical enrichment and denser ISM, promoting increasingly efficient grain growth. We therefore identify the assembly stage as the key regulator of dust growth at high redshift. Mock observations show that JWST multi-band imaging can reveal the structural diversity of galaxies within the scatter, although the reconstruction of clumpy morphologies may remain incomplete. Their dust continuum emission remains below the sensitivity of current ALMA continuum surveys.

8. Kinematic Decomposition of the BCG and ICL in Abell S1063 and MACS J1206[2610.40792]
Abstract

The brightest cluster galaxy (BCG) and the intracluster light (ICL) overlap in space and merge smoothly in surface brightness. Beyond the nearest clusters, measurements of the ICL have therefore relied on photometric definitions. We present a spectral decomposition of the light in cluster cores into BCG and ICL, using deep VLT/MUSE integral-field spectroscopy of Abell S1063 (z=0.35) and MACS J1206.2$-$0847 (z=0.44). Spectral templates of the two components are derived iteratively from the BCG center and the ICL-dominated outskirts, and their weighted sum is fitted to the spectrum of every spatial bin. This enables us to measure the kinematics and the light of the BCG and ICL separately without assuming a boundary or a functional form for their light profiles. The velocity dispersions of the BCG and ICL are $316\pm5$ and $753\pm25\ \mathrm{km\ s^{-1}}$, respectively, in AS1063, whereas we obtain $313\pm5$ and $695^{+47}_{-45}\ \mathrm{km\ s^{-1}}$ in MACS1206. The ICL is blueshifted relative to the BCG by $75\pm22$ and $148\pm38\ \mathrm{km\ s^{-1}}$, respectively, and is  0.7 dex more metal-poor, at a combined statistical significance of  $2\sigma$. Defining the ICL as the light fainter than $\mu_{B}=25\ \mathrm{mag\ arcsec^{-2}}$ assigns two-thirds to three-quarters of the kinematically defined ICL to the BCG, while a double Sersic model fits the total profiles well but leaves the division between the two components poorly constrained given the depth and resolution of the current MUSE data. Our kinematic decomposition provides a direct, physically motivated reference for photometric definitions of the ICL.

9. NGDEEP: Anchoring the $0.5 < z < 3.5$ Star-Forming Main Sequence at Low Stellar Masses with Ultradeep JWST/NIRISS WFSS[2610.40796]
Abstract

Here we present deep JWST/NIRISS wide-field slitless spectroscopy (WFSS) that covers $\mathrm{H\alpha}$ and $\mathrm{H\beta}$ for galaxies at $0.5 < z < 3.5$ in the NGDEEP survey. The NGDEEP NIRISS observations detect emission line fluxes from galaxies in the Hubble Ultra Deep Field to $\mathrm{m_{F150W}} \lesssim$ 28 mag, enabling studies of star-formation based on $\mathrm{H\alpha}$ (or $\mathrm{H\beta}$, if necessary) in low-mass galaxies with $\mathrm{\log(M_*/M_{\odot}) \gtrsim 7}$ at z = 0.5 and $\mathrm{\log(M_*/M_{\odot}) \gtrsim 8}$ at z = 3.5. For 284 NGDEEP galaxies with $>3\sigma$ detections of $\mathrm{H\alpha}$ or $\mathrm{H\beta}$, we determine dust-corrected star-formation rates, $\mathrm{SFR_{H\alpha}}$, via flux measurements of these Balmer lines and compare them to the galaxies' stellar masses to investigate the properties of the star-forming main sequence (SFMS) on short, $\sim10$ Myr timescales across three redshift bins. When we contrast the intrinsic scatter in the $\mathrm{H\alpha}$–based SFMS to that of a SFMS comprised of UV–based SFRs, which trace star formation within the past $\sim 100$ Myr, we find that the \Ha–based SFMS's intrinsic scatter ($\approx0.35-0.5$ dex over $0.5 < z < 3.5$) is $\approx0.2-0.4$ dex higher than the UV–based SFMS's intrinsic scatter at $z < 2.5$. We argue that brief ($\lesssim10$ Myr) bursts of star formation likely account for this disparity in SFMS scatter. Theoretical predictions from the FIRE simulations underestimate the scatter we observe in the $\mathrm{H\alpha}$–based SFMS at $1.5 < z < 2.5$ and $\mathrm{\log(M_*/M_{\odot}) < 9}$ by up to $\approx0.25$ dex, which suggests that low-mass galaxies have burstier star-formation histories than expected on $\lesssim10$ Myr timescales.

10. From Quantum Field Fluctuations to High-Redshift AGN: Semiclassical Origins of Halo Mass Function Constraints in the JWST Era[2610.40846]
Abstract

We develop a continuous framework connecting quantum field fluctuations in the early Universe to the abundance and observable properties of galaxies and active galactic nuclei (AGN) at cosmic dawn. Beginning with quantum fields in curved Friedmann Lemaitre Robertson Walker spacetime, we follow inflationary vacuum fluctuations into the primordial curvature spectrum, linear matter power spectrum, mass variance $\sigma(M,z)$, mass peak height $\nu(M,z)$, and the statistics of nonlinear halo formation. The Press-Schechter and Sheth-Tormen formalisms provide an analytic description of halo abundance and its dependence on peak rarity. A complementary high redshift halo mass function calibrated with the GUREFT simulations of Yung et al. (2024) provides a numerical description specifically constructed for the cosmic dawn regime probed by JWST. The comparison between these analytic and simulation calibrated prescriptions provides a direct measure of the sensitivity of predicted halo populations to the observations. We separate this cosmological supply of halos from the astrophysical and observational mappings that produce stars, ultraviolet luminosities, and active galactic nuclei. Controlled numerical experiments show that a percent level perturbation in the primordial fluctuation amplitude produces only a modest change in $\sigma$ and $\nu$, but can strongly amplify the exponentially suppressed high $\nu$ tail of the Halo Mass Function. This sensitivity increases toward high halo mass and high redshift, identifying rare cosmic dawn populations as potentially powerful probes of primordial statistics while simultaneously exposing the importance of uncertainties in halo abundance. High redshift AGN population statistics and the Ultraviolet Luminosity Function provide additional constraints on the primordial power spectrum and dynamics of primordial inflation theory.

11. FOMO-Z. Optimal galaxy classification, photometric redshifts, and reconstruction of population distributions[2610.40875]
Abstract

We present FOMO-Z (Flexible Optimizer of Multi-dimensional Operators and photo-$z$s), a set of statistical methods implemented in Python for galaxy classification and probabilistic inference from photometry. The methods use a reference sample with known galaxy properties. They rely on explicit, physically interpretable criteria and make no assumptions about the functional form of the color distributions. Binary selections are calibrated using either linear color conditions, particularly suited to background galaxy selection, or flexible color–color grids, seeking to maximize completeness given a threshold on purity. For probabilistic inference, the code identifies reference objects with compatible colors within photometric uncertainties to estimate each test galaxy's probability of satisfying user-defined criteria, as well as probability density functions of continuous properties such as redshift. The code also reconstructs property distributions of selected galaxy populations using photometrically similar galaxies in the reference sample. This framework supports prior weights and reference redshift probability distributions. A built-in feature-selection method identifying informative colors is also introduced. We demonstrate applications to background galaxy selection, galaxy population classification, tomographic redshift calibration, and photometric redshift estimation. The code achieves results consistent with those obtained through machine-learning approaches, such as self-organizing maps, while remaining computationally efficient, making it suitable for large photometric surveys and future Stage IV cosmological analyses. 12. The Size-Mass Relation and Inside-Out Quenching of Massive Quiescent Galaxies at $2<z<3$ with JWST PRIMER and MINERVA[2610.40937]
Abstract

We investigate the morphology, size–mass relations, and spatially resolved star formation histories of 26 massive quiescent galaxies at cosmic noon ($2 < z < 3$) in the Ultra-Deep Survey field using deep JWST NIRCam imaging from the PRIMER and MINERVA surveys. Combining 16 broadband and medium-band filters with empirical point spread function matching, we perform single-Sérsic modeling and bulge–disk decompositions with pysersic across six filters (F115W to F444W), alongside Bayesian size–mass fitting with dynesty. In the rest-frame optical (F200W), the size–mass relation exhibits a steep slope of $\alpha = 0.59 \pm 0.12$ and effective radius of $A = 0.83_{-0.07}^{+0.08}\ \mathrm{kpc}$ at $M_* = 5 \times 10^{10}\ M_\odot$. In the rest-frame near-infrared (F356W), we derive $\alpha = 0.56 \pm 0.13$ and a more compact radius of $A = 0.70_{-0.06}^{+0.07}\ \mathrm{kpc}$. Galaxy sizes shrink by $\approx 22\%$ from F115W to F444W, reflecting negative color gradients and centrally concentrated mass profiles. Two-thirds of the sample are bulge-dominated ($B/T \ge 0.5$), whereas one-third are disk-dominated. Central bulges are ultra-compact ($R_{e,\mathrm{Bulge}} \approx 0.49\ \mathrm{kpc}$) with negligible mass dependence, whereas outer disks extend to $R_{e,\mathrm{Disk}} \approx 1.4\text{--}1.5\ \mathrm{kpc}$. Resolved spectral energy distribution modeling with piXedfit demonstrates positive radial specific star formation rate gradients and inner regions that formed at least $\approx 0.7\ \mathrm{Gyr}$ earlier and quenched faster than their outskirts. These results confirm inside-out quenching, consistent with dissipative gas compaction assembling the dense core, active galactic nucleus feedback halting central star formation, and the compact bulge dynamically stabilizing the surviving disk against fragmentation.

13. Stellar Bars Reshape the Spatial Distribution of UV-bright Clumps in Nearby Galaxies[2610.41355]
Abstract

Stellar bars redistribute gas and angular momentum in disk galaxies, but whether they mainly change the amount or the spatial organization of recent star formation remains uncertain. We use archival HST/WFC3 F275W imaging to study 70 barred galaxies and 95 unbarred control galaxies matched in stellar mass, optical color, redshift, and F275W background noise. A uniform dendrogram-based procedure identifies 95,486 UV-bright star-forming clumps. We compare their radial and azimuthal distributions, central F275W emission, and luminosity and size distribution functions. Relative to their controls, barred galaxies show a deficit of clumps at intermediate radii broadly corresponding to the bar region, followed by a local recovery near the bar ends. Their clumps are also preferentially concentrated along the bar major axis. These spatial differences are stronger in high-mass galaxies. Normalized bar length is more closely associated with the radial redistribution and central F275W concentration than bar ellipticity, although its relation to the azimuthal distribution is less uniform. When galactocentric radius is normalized by bar length, the recovery occurs near $R/R_{\rm bar}\sim1$, indicating that the bar sets a characteristic scale for the redistribution. Despite these spatial differences, barred and control galaxies have broadly similar clump luminosity and size distribution functions. Our results suggest that stellar bars mainly reorganize where recent star formation occurs, rather than substantially altering the observed luminosities and sizes of individual UV clumps, and that this effect depends on galaxy mass and bar extent.

14. Kinematic Map of the Galactic Bulge Using HST Proper Motions[2610.41395]
Abstract

Constraining the Galactic bulge's non-axisymmetric dynamics requires proper motions in deep fields across many sightlines, yet crowding and extinction limit such measurements. Using multi-epoch observations of 27 fields in the southern Galactic bulge (-6$^\circ$ < l < 4$^\circ$, -5$^\circ$ < b < -1$^\circ$) from the ultraviolet-visible (UVIS) channel of Wide Field Camera 3 (WFC3) on the Hubble Space Telescope (HST) with time baselines of 3 to 13 years, and combining them with the absolute proper motion reference frame provided by Gaia Data Release 3 (DR3), we measure absolute proper motions for   400,000 stars. Colour-magnitude diagrams are used to separate bulge stars from foreground disc stars, identify red clump stars and correct for interstellar extinction and reddening. Statistical analysis shows that the mean proper motion in Galactic latitude is consistent with that of the central supermassive black hole Sgr A*, while the mean proper motion in Galactic longitude is affected by disc stars but agrees with Sgr A* after extrapolation to the Galactic plane. Proper motion dispersions increase towards the Galactic centre and are asymmetric across Galactic longitude, with a steeper gradient for l < 0$^\circ$, revealing the bulge's non-axisymmetric bar structure. The velocity distribution shows clear anisotropy ($\sigma_l$/$\sigma_b$ = 1.131 $\pm$ 0.005). The normalised covariance is negative in the l > 0$^\circ$ region (  -0.085) and close to zero in the l < 0$^\circ$ region. The proper motion distributions are non-Gaussian in both Galactic longitude and latitude, mainly due to contamination by foreground disc stars.

15. Resolving the Complex Line-Locking Structure in Quasar J004613+010425 with VLT/X-shooter Spectroscopy[2610.41427]
Abstract

Line-locking (LL) signatures in quasar absorption-line systems provide direct observational evidence for radiatively driven outflows. We present a detailed investigation of LL phenomena in the quasar SDSS J004613.54+010425.7 ($z_{\rm em} = 2.150$) using medium-resolution VLT/X-shooter spectra, as part of the TOLL project. Compared with previous studies based on Sloan Digital Sky Survey spectroscopy, the X-shooter data resolve the absorption complex into twelve narrow absorption line (NAL) components spanning $\sim$$2700$ km/s in the C IV, N V, Si IV, and Ly$\alpha$ transitions, a factor of $\sim$2.4 more components than identified from lower resolution spectra. We detect fourteen distinct LL pairs across three ionic species, forming one of the largest line-locking webs known to date: three Si IV pairs connecting components (2, 10), (4, 11), and (5, 12); six N V pairs organized into four groups; and five C IV pairs organized into three groups. Monte Carlo simulations show that the probability of this twelve-component LL web arising by chance is $< 10^{-5}$. Photoionization modeling with Cloudy yields ionization parameters of $\log U \approx -2.4$ to $-0.6$ and total hydrogen column densities of $\log N({\rm H}) \approx 18.4$-$20.6$ for the twelve components. A comparison of the properties of shadowed and shadowing clouds shows that ion column densities and ionization parameters typically agree within a factor of $\sim$3, broadly supporting the standard radiative acceleration picture, although several outliers suggest that multi-ion locking and cloud-cloud interactions may also regulate LL stability. These results demonstrate that medium- and high-resolution spectroscopy is essential for resolving the full complexity of line-locked quasar outflows.

16. OASIS I: Spectroscopy of Mini-Quenched and Lulling Galaxies Constrains the Quiet Side of Bursty Star Formation at z > 4[2610.41489]
Abstract

JWST has revealed that star formation in early galaxies is bursty, including episodes of violent suppression, so-called mini-quenching. But these were largely serendipitous discoveries in samples of UV-bright, emission-line-dominated galaxies, so that the quiet side of the burst cycle has remained poorly explored. With the JWST Cycle 3 program OASIS (GO 5997), we searched for such galaxies deliberately, using deep 28-hour NIRSpec/PRISM spectroscopy across the full burst cycle at z>4. We identify three mini-quenched galaxies with log(M*/Msun) = 8.3-8.9 and no detectable Balmer emission, at least 1.2-1.7 dex below the star-forming main sequence (SFMS), five lulling galaxies with weak Balmer emission above the quenching threshold, and 17 nearly-main-sequence galaxies just below the SFMS. Finding them required spectroscopy: seven of our ten photometric candidates for suppressed (mini-quenched and lulling) galaxies turned out to be nearly-MS or above the SFMS, and photometric fits overestimate their current star-formation rates and cannot distinguish lulling from mini-quenched ones. The suppressed galaxies are common: five of the eight come from tiers not selected on star-formation state, where they make up 29% of the galaxies at z = 4-5.5 and log(M*/Msun) = 8-9.5, a number density of  1.6x10^-3 Mpc^-3 dex^-1. Simulations predict them to be an order of magnitude rarer at these masses and to occur mainly at lower masses. The suppressed galaxies are also old: Balmer-break strength and stellar age both increase as galaxies fall further below the SFMS, with light-weighted ages of 5 Myr for galaxies above it,  30 Myr for the lulling galaxies and above 100 Myr for the mini-quenched ones. They show little stellar reddening (E(B-V) < 0.2), consistent with depleted gas reservoirs. Suppressed phases at z>4 are therefore neither rare nor brief, but a common and lasting part of early galaxy growth.

17. OASIS II: The Ionized Gas Traces Feedback-Driven Declines into the Quiet Side of Bursty Star Formation at z > 4[2610.41490]
Abstract

Models of bursty star formation in low-mass (10^8-10^9.5 Msun) galaxies at z>4 differ most in the suppressed phases: what drives the decline, how fast star formation falls, and how long it stays low. OASIS Paper I showed that such quiet galaxies are common and old; here we measure how they got there and how their ionized gas responds. We compare Balmer-line star-formation rates (SFRs) with 100-Myr averages from the stellar continuum, in NIRSpec/PRISM spectra of 54 OASIS galaxies at z>4, including three mini-quenched galaxies below the quenching threshold and five lulling galaxies at least 0.6 dex below the star-forming main sequence (SFMS) but still star-forming. Galaxies above the SFMS rise and fade equally often, whereas those below it are mostly fading: the lulling galaxies formed little more than a tenth of their mass in the last 100 Myr, and their decline began tens to  100 Myr before observation. Suppressed phases thus last of order 100 Myr, as long as or longer than in most simulations, whereas recovery is much faster: few galaxies below the SFMS are rising. The ionized gas tracks these changes in star formation. [OIII]/[OII] falls from galaxies above the SFMS to the lulling ones, even at fixed mass and after dust correction, and follows the recent change in SFR (SFR_10/SFR_100) more closely than the position relative to the SFMS: two rising galaxies below the SFMS are highly ionized. This is expected if fewer and softer ionizing photons than in strongly star-forming galaxies meet gas depleted by earlier star formation. Lulling galaxies show ISM conditions distinct from those of strongly star-forming galaxies. The declines are faster than passive gas exhaustion predicts, favoring feedback that expels the gas or keeps it from forming stars. Quiet phases are thus long, feedback-driven episodes, and the ionized gas traces where a galaxy is in its burst cycle.

18. OASIS III: Bursty star formation drives observed galaxy sizes at $z\geq4$[2610.41491]
Abstract

We employ JWST/NIRSpec MSA PRISM spectroscopy together with NIRCam imaging to investigate the impact of star-formation variability on galaxy sizes for a representative sample of 51 galaxies at $z\geq4$. The OASIS survey enables us to probe low-star-formation-rate (SFR) lulling and mini-quenched galaxies down to $\log_{10}(M_\star/M_\odot)=8.3$ and $\log_{10}(\mathrm{SFR}/M_\odot\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1})<-1.6$, i.e. more than one order of magnitude below the star-forming main sequence (MS). This enables us to probe objects that are often missed by shallower spectroscopic surveys, in addition to the MS and starburst systems, yielding an SFR- and mass-representative sample at high redshift. We derive galaxy half-light sizes using ForcePho, enabling us to resolve the highly compact nature of these sources, and we analyse their star-formation histories and interstellar medium (ISM) properties with the full-spectral-fitting code ppxf. We find a clear anti-correlation between burstiness, as traced by the ratio of the SFR from H$\alpha$ compared to that from non-parametric spectral fitting averaged over 100 Myr, and galaxy UV/optical half-light radii as measured with ForcePho from a combination of well-sampled NIRCam photometry. Using partial correlation coefficients we show that the anti-correlation between burstiness (as traced by the SFR ratio) and size cannot simply be explained by secondary dependencies on stellar mass or redshift. We interpret this relation as the effect of central starbursts in SFR-rising (bursting) systems, where intense central star formation reduces measured galaxy half-light sizes. This burstiness then regulates galaxy sizes at high redshift. Should the same mechanism be present at $z>10$, this could explain the size bimodality seen in the UV luminous galaxies as simply stemming from a more fundamental relationship between burstiness and size.

19. As a matter of reconstruction – varying the magnetic Prandtl number in implicit large eddy simulations[2610.41493]
Abstract

The magnetic Prandtl number ($\mathrm{Pm}$), the ratio of viscosity to magnetic diffusivity, spans many orders of magnitude in astrophysical plasmas. It shapes how turbulence amplifies and structures magnetic fields. In direct numerical simulations (DNS) the viscosity and magnetic diffusivity can be controlled explicitly and independently, whereas in implicit large eddy simulations (ILES) they are set by the numerical scheme. We test whether, in a finite-volume code, $\mathrm{Pm}$ can be varied through the reconstruction scheme by applying different methods to the hydro and magnetic variables. As a proof of concept, we simulate driven, subsonic, homogeneous magnetohydrodynamic turbulence using five pairs of hydrodynamic and magnetic reconstruction schemes at varying resolutions. We measure the effective viscosity and resistivity from the energy transfer between scales. Reconstructing the magnetic field at higher order than the fluid raises the effective magnetic Prandtl number and vice versa. We demonstrate that a mix of second- and third-order reconstruction alone results in a $\gtrsim20\times$ change in $\mathrm{Pm}$. A comparison of DNS with matched explicit dissipation at these values agrees with the ILES. Mixed reconstruction thus offers a simple and efficient way to explore $\mathrm{Pm} \not\simeq 1$ in ILES without adding explicit dissipation.

20. Dark Halos, Intermediate-Mass BH and Nuclear Stellar Clusters in Dwarf Spheroidals[2610.41522]

65. A Framework for Evaluating Forward Contamination Risk for Bio-ISRU Microorganisms[2609.12015]
Abstract

NASA planetary protection policy seeks to avoid inadvertent forward contamination of celestial bodies and backward contamination of Earth during space exploration. For Mars missions, it has historically focused on sterilization of landers and rovers. Current policy provides no framework for assessing the forward contamination risk of missions that deliberately include living organisms and must not be sterilized, such as a human crew, agriculture, or biotechnology for manufacturing and life support. We propose the PRIM (Propagation Restricted, Inert on Mars) framework to evaluate the forward-contamination risk posed by known organisms. Building on the biocontainment literature and probability-of-contamination model, PRIM bounds the ability of an organism released from a worst-case off-nominal event to sustain growth on the Martian surface at a probability under P_c <= 10^-4 using independent single-stressor propagation assays. We apply the PRIM framework to two engineered Mars biological in situ resource utilization chassis organisms, using low water activity and carbon starvation to qualify them for low forward contamination risk even at high bioburden, and outline how to extend the assays to phototrophs and anaerobes. PRIM offers a path to flight-qualify living organisms for Mars based on laboratory assays rather than bioburden alone. This enables biological life support infrastructure for a sustained presence on Mars while keeping forward contamination risk demonstrably low.

66. Searching for Ultra-Light Axions with Twisted Cavity Resonators of Anyon Rotational Symmetry with Bulk Modes of Non-Zero Helicity[2208.01640]
Abstract

Möbius-ring resonators stem from a well-studied geometrical structure with a one-sided topology, the Möbius strip, and have been shown to exhibit fermion rotational symmetry with respect to an untwisted ring resonator, which exhibits boson rotational symmetry [1]. Here, we present twisted hollow resonators with equilateral triangular cross-sections, leading to cavities with anyon rotational symmetry. Unlike conventional cavity resonators, these structures permit bulk resonant modes with a non-zero complex electromagnetic overlap $\mathcal C_p=\int_V\mathbf{E}_p\cdot\mathbf{B}_p^*\,d\tau$. The real and imaginary parts of this overlap couple to different observable quadratures. Although the negative-frequency component of a real axion field is immaterial for haloscopes employing a static DC magnetic field, it must be retained for a pumped resonator because the complex forms of Maxwell's equations differ for positive and negative frequencies and produce distinct upper and lower mixing branches. Retaining both branches shows that the real overlap produces amplitude-modulated sidebands, whereas the imaginary helicity of the modes studied here produces phase-modulated sidebands through the axion-photon chiral anomaly. A sensitive ultra-light dark-matter experiment may therefore be realised with an ultra-stable oscillator and a phase-sensitive readout. The sensitivity curves calculated here require the assumed noise floor to be interpreted as phase-modulation noise rather than amplitude-modulation noise, and consequently require sufficiently low residual oscillator phase noise at the axion Fourier frequency. The scheme removes the usual requirement for an external magnetic field and permits superconducting resonators with reduced surface loss and enhanced axion sensitivity.

67. Bayesian nonparametric estimation of correlated gravitational wave detector network noise using matrix-gamma process priors[2607.26619]
Abstract

This paper addresses the important problem of estimating the noise spectral density of next-generation gravitational-wave detectors, such as LISA and the Einstein Telescope (ET), where cross-channel correlations must be accounted for to avoid biased parameter estimation of gravitational-wave signals. Unlike approaches that estimate test-mass and optical-metrology-system noise separately at the single-link level and then map them to the Time-Delay Interferometry (TDI) channels through known transfer functions, we develop a Bayesian nonparametric method that directly estimates the spectral density matrix of the XYZ channels, thereby accommodating additional sources of uncertainty. Our approach combines a flexible matrix-gamma process prior on the matrix-valued coefficients of a Bernstein polynomial basis expansion with a blocked multivariate Whittle likelihood. The prior guarantees Hermitian positive definiteness of the spectral estimate at every frequency. To avoid reversible-jump methods, we use an adaptive Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) algorithm for posterior sampling. The proposed framework can also be used to correct misspecified parametric noise models. Results from a simulation study and simulated correlated-noise data for both LISA and ET demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed method.

68. cWB-space: A time-frequency unmodelled transient-search pipeline for LISA[2609.12986]
Abstract

The Laser Interferometer Space Antenna (LISA) will observe massive-black-hole mergers and other transient phenomena alongside many long-lived gravitational-wave signals. Some transients may be incompletely modeled or have waveforms that have not been anticipated. We present \texttt{cWB-space}, a pipeline that combines an unmodeled transient search, waveform reconstruction, and a time-frequency glitch classifier. The search identifies concentrations of signal power in time and frequency and reconstructs candidate waveforms without requiring a source template. The glitch classifier compares how candidates appear in different combinations of LISA's laser measurements, with the background estimated from the observed data. We test the pipeline with simulated black-hole binaries with aligned and precessing spins, burst signals with different shapes, and instrumental glitches. The response comparisons help assess a candidate's astrophysical or instrumental origin, while also revealing correlated glitches that can resemble gravitational waves. In the Sangria simulated LISA data, the search recovers all six injected massive-black-hole binaries as its six highest-ranked candidates. Their local reconstructions recover the main features of the injected waveforms over the intervals studied. These results demonstrate how an unmodeled search can connect transient identification with investigation of a candidate's origin and waveform recovery.

69. Searching for gravitational wave echo with group equivariant neural posterior estimation[2609.18337]
Abstract

Current methods for the gravitational-wave (GW) echo searches generally require substantial computational time and resources. Recent advances in group equivariant neural posterior estimation (GNPE) have demonstrated the potential to substantially accelerate posterior inference.
