1. Efficient and Optimal Analysis of Galaxy Surveys on Large Scales[2611.21718]
Abstract

On sufficiently large scales the galaxy overdensity field is approximately Gaussian, so its information can be losslessly compressed into the power spectrum. Standard power spectrum estimators are nevertheless suboptimal on the largest scales, where the survey window induces significant coupling between different modes. Optimal power spectrum analysis typically requires an inverse of the covariance matrix, which can be overly expensive. Here we develop an alternative field level approach, which is significantly faster. We assess its impact on constraining primordial non-Gaussianity of the local type, which is particularly sensitive to these largest scales. Relative to the standard power spectrum analysis, our field level likelihood forecasts a $14\%$ improvement in constraining power. For the DESI DR2 LRG and QSO samples, our approach forecasts $\sigma(f_{\rm NL})=4.1$, compared to $\sigma(f_{\rm NL})=4.8$ from the power spectrum based analysis. The field level approach also enables efficient implementations of additional non-cosmological contributions to the signal. As an example, we present a novel non-parametric method to mitigate angular systematics, enabling efficient deprojection of contaminated modes.

2. Velocity Reconstruction from kSZ with SPT-3G and DESI-LS[2611.21721]
Abstract

We reconstruct the large-scale radial velocity field from the kinetic Sunyaev-Zel'dovich effect for the first time with the South Pole Telescope, and measure the galaxy-velocity correlation and the velocity power spectrum. We cross-correlate the SPT-3G D1 maps at 95, 150, and 220 GHz with luminous red galaxies from the DESI Legacy Imaging Surveys over the SPT-3G Main field. For the extended galaxy sample, we detect the galaxy-velocity correlation at signal-to-noise ratio $11.2$ and the velocity power spectrum at $8.1$, combining the three frequencies. We detect the reconstructed velocities for the first time at 220 GHz with signal-to-noise ratios of 4.2 and 3.1 from the galaxy-velocity correlation dipole and monopole, and at 3.8 from the velocity power spectrum. We model and estimate foreground contributions to the monopole, detecting both the thermal Sunyaev-Zel'dovich effect and the cosmic infrared background contribution separately. On the around five percent of sky jointly covered by SPT-3G and DESI Legacy Imaging Surveys, reconstructed velocities give new constraints on local-type primordial non-Gaussianity at $f_{\rm NL}=-38^{+61}_{-61}$, and adding galaxy clustering gives $f_{\rm NL}=-20^{+40}_{-46}$. The galaxy-velocity correlation is consistent between the three frequencies.

3. Baryonification V: Thermal Sunyaev-Zel'dovich maps from dark-matter-only lightcones[2611.21733]
Abstract

Baryonic feedback is a major theoretical uncertainty for Stage-IV weak lensing (WL) surveys. The thermal Sunyaev-Zel'dovich (tSZ) effect traces the hot gas redistributed by feedback, so combining it with WL can calibrate feedback and improve cosmological constraints. Doing so with simulation-based inference requires correlated lensing and Compton-$y$ maps across many cosmologies and feedback scenarios. We present two methods to generate Compton-$y$ maps from dark-matter-only $N$-body light cones using the component-wise baryonification (BFC) model, which produces separate gas and stellar fields and computes the gas pressure from hydrostatic equilibrium. The two methods, painting pressure profiles onto halos or redistributing the signal along the baryonified gas, agree to within a few per cent. We calibrate the model to the fiducial and strongest AGN feedback FLAMINGO simulations by jointly fitting gas mass fractions and thermal pressure profiles, measuring the non-thermal pressure from the simulated gas velocities rather than treating it as a free parameter. The predicted Compton-$y$ profiles match FLAMINGO within halo-to-halo scatter, and when painted onto the FLAMINGO halos we reproduce the tSZ power spectrum of both feedback models to $\sim5\%$ for $500\lesssim\ell\lesssim3000$. Applied to a \emph{CosmoGridV1-like} $N$-body simulation, our maps agree with FLAMINGO within $\sim10\%$ for $300\lesssim\ell\lesssim8000$; the remaining differences come from the few hundred most massive clusters, and removing them from both halo catalogs brings the two into agreement. Our method thus enables accurate and efficient tSZ forward models from $N$-body simulation suites such as CosmoGridV1, an important step towards joint WL and tSZ simulation-based inference for Euclid, LSST, and the Simons Observatory.

4. Flash UV Spectroscopy: Decoding the Ultraviolet Diversity of Young Core-Collapse Supernovae with the Hubble Space Telescope[2611.21742]
Abstract

We present multi-wavelength observations and modeling of the largest sample to date of core-collapse supernovae (SNe) with Hubble Space Telescope (HST) ultraviolet (UV) spectroscopy within < 2 weeks of first light. Our total sample contains early-time UV spectroscopy from 12 Type II SNe (SNe II), 3 Type IIb SNe (SNe IIb), 3 Type IIn SNe (SNe IIn) and 1 Type I super-luminous SN (SLSN-I). This sample includes 11 objects with unpublished UV spectroscopy from HST Cycles 31 and 32 "Flexible Thursdays" target of opportunity programs, increasing the number of core-collapse SNe with early-time HST UV spectra by a factor of 2.4. The early-time near-UV spectra of most sample SNe II show similar Doppler-broadened P-Cygni profiles of metals (Mg, Fe, Ti, Cr), though with notable diversity in line velocities and absorption strengths. Comparison to non-Local Thermodynamic Equilibrium (nLTE) radiative transfer model grids at similar phases indicates that most sample SNe II are consistent with low circumstellar material (CSM) densities at the time of UV observations (e.g., $\dot M = 10^{-6}$ M$_{\odot}$ yr$^{-1}$, $v_w = 50$ km/s) and standard kinetic energies (e.g., $E_k = 0.6-1.2$ B), while two objects, SNe 2024faq and 2023ixf, show UV/optical evidence for interaction with more confined, higher-density CSM. SN 2023ixf and sample SNe IIn exhibit relatively featureless early-time UV spectra despite displaying prominent optical Lorentzian emission lines from electron scattering in optically thick CSM – this being attributed to differing properties of UV resonance versus optical recombination transitions as well as intrinsic CSM asymmetries. These results demonstrate the potential of early-time UV spectroscopy to constrain the outermost SN ejecta structure, explosion energetics/composition, and progenitor mass-loss histories of core-collapse SNe.

5. From active nests to fast wind gusts: Nested flux emergence as a precursor of persistent non-axisymmetric coronal holes[2611.21746]
Abstract

Recurring flux emergence within active nests reshapes the Sun's large-scale magnetic field and creates unipolar magnetic regions that can support coronal holes. The fast solar wind emerging from coronal holes drives space weather across the heliosphere. We investigated how the emergence of active nests at the peak of solar cycle 25 drove changes in global magnetic topology, coronal hole formation, and solar wind speed. Coronal holes were identified from composite SDO/AIA maps and their magnetic field properties were extracted from SDO/HMI magnetograms. Spherical harmonic decomposition quantified how magnetic flux was distributed at different spatial scales and was used to extrapolate the coronal field. We compared coronal structure with SOHO/LASCO-C2 observations and the solar wind measured at 1 au. Emerging magnetic flux within active nests rapidly surpassed the pre-existing magnetic flux in 2024, strengthening the non-axisymmetric field and shifting the corona into a tilted-dipole state. This dipolarisation stabilised the large-scale magnetic field, with the heliospheric current sheet anchored above active nests. Unipolar magnetic regions from the edges of active nests sustained non-axisymmetric coronal holes covering 6 to 14% of the solar surface in 2025, comparable to the 12% covered by polar coronal holes at solar minimum. Flux emergence near the tilted-dipole's negative pole returned the corona to a quadrupole-dominated state in 2026. Active nests helped stabilise the coronal magnetic field, while the decay and transport of unipolar magnetic flux sustained persistent, non-axisymmetric coronal holes. Differential rotation between unipolar regions triggered episodic expansion and contraction of the largest coronal holes. Early identification of specific active nest configurations, combined with the solar cycle phase, could improve the forecasting of coronal holes and fast solar wind.

6. Mapping the multimessenger horizon for $γ$-ray counterparts of cosmic neutrinos[2611.21752]
Abstract

The joint detection of high-energy neutrinos and $\gamma$-rays offers a powerful probe of the most energetic astrophysical accelerators, linking hadronic interactions to observable signatures across cosmic distances. Understanding the conditions under which these two messengers can be simultaneously observed is essential for identifying and characterizing the sources of cosmic neutrinos. In this work, we examine the detectability of $\gamma$-ray cascades associated with neutrino production in source environments that are gamma-ray transparent, focusing on how electromagnetic cascades evolve as $\gamma$-rays propagate through intergalactic space. By modeling photon interactions with the extragalactic background light and deflections in intergalactic magnetic fields, we determine the minimum neutrino fluence for transient emission and neutrino flux for steady emission required to produce a $\gamma$-ray counterpart detectable by $\gamma$-ray observatories, such as Fermi-LAT, HAWC, or LHAASO. This defines a “multimessenger horizon" – the region in flux-distance parameter space where neutrinos and $\gamma$-rays are jointly observable. We consider both energy-binned injection tied to IceCube's sensitivity and a continuous power-law spectrum extending from TeV to PeV energies. Our results reveal that detectability depends strongly on redshift, magnetic field strength, and photon energy, with higher-energy cascades suffering greater attenuation inside filamentary structures. The framework provides new constraints on source opacity, baryon loading, and neutrino production mechanisms, offering a predictive tool for interpreting current events and guiding future multimessenger campaigns.

7. NEMESIS: Exoplanet TraNsit SurvEy of Nearby M-dwarfs in TESS FFIS II - 262 Transiting Planet Candidates from the TESS Primary Mission[2611.21758]
Abstract

We present NEMESIS II, a blind transit search of nearby M-dwarfs in TESS Primary Mission full-frame images (Sectors 1-26), including stars lacking 2-minute observations. Using the open-source NEMESIS pipeline, we extracted, detrended, and searched 213,658 single-sector light curves of 113,047 M-dwarfs within 100 pc, yielding 23,339 threshold crossing events. Automated LEO-Vetter vetting, applied identically to the survey and its injection-recovery and orbit-scrambling controls, retains 558 detections corresponding to 525 distinct signals with converged transit fits and periods of 0.51-9.10 days. Of these, 482 lie on stars without TOI, CTOI, or NASA Exoplanet Archive transiting-planet entries. Orbit-scrambling predicts 329 false alarms among the 558 detections, all but 14 of them among the 408 below Multiple Event Statistic 8. Assuming an astrophysical signal, TRICERATOPS favors a planetary origin for 262 of 525 candidates (232 new), which we classify as planet candidates; it also excludes resolved-neighbor eclipses for 105. Known planets and TOI candidates survive vetting at 1.01 +/- 0.07 of the injection-predicted rate. Correcting for each light curve's sensitivity, false alarms, and astrophysical false positives, we infer 0.81 +/- 0.44 planets per star for periods between 1.0-10.0 days and planet radii between 0.5-11.0 Earth radii. This is a lower limit, because our injections recover few or no planets near and below 1 Earth radii and assume central transits. Light curves, catalogs, vetting reports, and injection-recovery results will be released as a High Level Science Product at MAST upon publication. 8. Cross-correlating the SPHEREx extragalactic background light with CMB lensing I: theoretical predictions and forecasts[2611.21779]
Abstract

The near-infrared extragalactic background light (EBL), which SPHEREx maps in 102 spectral channels, holds the emission lines of galaxies on top of a much brighter continuum from the integrated galaxy light (IGL) and the intrahalo light (IHL). Cross-correlating these maps with the lensing of the cosmic microwave background (CMB) isolates the emission that traces the matter, unbiased by uncorrelated foregrounds. The lines face two obstacles. Filtering along the line of sight removes the long radial modes that carry the cross-correlation, and the extragalactic continuum, a signal in its own right, is a correlated foreground for the lines. We forecast the detectability of all EBL components, in a common halo-model framework, for the SPHEREx deep fields and all-sky survey with lensing maps at the sensitivity level of Planck and the Simons Observatory (SO). Without filtering, and with all amplitudes fitted jointly, the cross-correlation with Planck lensing detects the IGL at $9\sigma$ in the deep fields, but not the lines. In the all-sky survey, it measures the IGL and IHL amplitudes to $1.0\%$ and $3\%$ and detects the five lines, fitted as one template, at $4.6\sigma$. With SO lensing, these become $0.6\%$, $1.6\%$ and $7.4\sigma$. In these joint fits, a filter removing radial modes longer than about $600$ Mpc costs every component $90\%$ or more of its signal-to-noise ratio, so the lines are reached by fitting the continuum, not by filtering it. These values assume a known spectral shape of the continuum. Calibrating it with the continuum's auto-spectra keeps the lines at $7.3$–$7.4\sigma$; fitting it from the cross-spectrum alone leaves $1.4$–$2.3\sigma$. The line detections vary by a factor of seven to eleven across line models. The cross-correlation is thus a robust probe of the IGL and a viable route to the emission lines, provided the continuum is modeled across the channels.

9. Cross-correlating the SPHEREx extragalactic background light with CMB lensing II: theoretical predictions confronted with lightcone simulations[2611.21780]
Abstract

Cross-correlating intensity maps with the lensing of the cosmic microwave background (CMB) rejects the foregrounds and noise that do not trace the dark matter. In the near infrared, the remaining signal combines the integrated galaxy light (IGL), the intrahalo light (IHL) and several emission lines. Accurate modeling of all three is essential to forecast detectability and interpret measurements. We present simulated SPHEREx intensity maps of all three on a lightcone, painted on halos from the Hidden Valley $N$-body simulation, and the CMB-lensing convergence of the same lightcone, over $100\,{\rm deg}^2$ to $z \approx 6$. We assign each halo its lines and continuum by stellar-population synthesis, in two models whose metallicities are fixed or follow its stellar mass. Halo-model predictions from the simulation's luminosity-halo mass relations and measured clustering, without free parameters, match the auto-spectra of the IGL and lines, and their cross-spectra with lensing, across the band: at $2.2\,\mu$m, to within $5\%$ above $\ell \approx 400$ and $7\%$ above $\ell = 200$, respectively. The agreement requires nonlinear clustering: linear theory describes the cross-spectrum of a line up to $\ell \approx 1000$-$1600$ at $2.2\,\mu$m, but fails for the IGL and the convergence at multipoles of a few hundred. With the noise of a SPHEREx deep field and of Simons Observatory lensing, the unfiltered cross-spectra, each component taken alone, reach signal-to-noise ratios of $9$-$21$ for the lines, $20$ for the IGL and $15$ for the IHL. A filter that removes radial modes longer than about $600$ Mpc keeps $62\%$ of the IGL's signal-to-noise and at most $20\%$ of the lines', with fitted amplitudes within about $1\sigma$ of the predictions before and after filtering. The emission model changes a channel's intensity by up to a factor of three, but the line detectability only by tens of percent.

10. Connecting Calcium Depletion and Dust Evolution in the Rapid Accretor T28[2611.21786]
Abstract

We present a multi-wavelength case study of the star T28 in the Chamaeleon I star-forming region and investigate the dust evolution of its highly Ca-depleted protoplanetary disk. We combine new observations from the MIKE spectrograph on the Magellan Clay Telescope with archival IRS spectrum and photometry. We constrain the optical extinction using the hydrogen column density of the region, and apply ROTFIT to obtain stellar parameters. We estimate the mass accretion rate by fitting the excess flux above the photosphere with an accretion shock model. We model the line profiles of Ca II K and H$\alpha$ with magnetospheric accretion models to infer Ca/H abundance, while the infrared excess is modeled with irradiated accretion disk models to get disk properties. We find that T28 is a high accretor with significant Ca depletion in its accretion flows. We find that the disk is best fit with a pre-transitional model with a disk mass of 0.12 $M_{\odot}$, where two optically thick disks are separated by a gap filled with optically thin dust extending to 16 au. This gap would not be resolved in the existing observations. T28 follows the trend of increasing depletion with increasing mass accretion rate. This trend can potentially be explained by preferential and early giant planet formation in massive disks, as suggested by recent planet formation simulations, leading to enhanced capture of refractory material in the massive disks of high accretors and thus depletion in the material reaching the star.

11. Long-Term Spectral and Timing Variability of PKS 2155$-$304 from {\it Chandra} and {\it NuSTAR} Observations[2611.21792]
Abstract

Blazars exhibit strong variability across the electromagnetic spectrum, on timescales from minutes to decades. We present a study of the spectral and timing variability of the TeV blazar PKS 2155$-$304 using {\it Chandra} and {\it NuSTAR} observations spanning 25 years (1999$-$2024). Timing analysis reveals fractional variability amplitudes of up to $F_{\rm var}\approx 24\%$ in the hard X-ray band and $F_{\rm var}\lesssim 7.5\%$ in the soft X-ray band, along with variable power-spectral indices across observations. The minimum variability timescale, $\tau_{\rm var} = 1.15\pm0.26$ ks, limits the size of the X-ray emitting region to $R \lesssim 7.7 \times 10^{14}$ cm, consistent with synchrotron emission from a compact region in the jet. We fitted the spectra using power-law (PL), broken power-law (BPL), log-parabola (LP), and bremsstrahlung plus power-law (BR+PL) models, the latter used only as a phenomenological description of the spectral curvature. The BPL model provides the best statistical description for most {\it Chandra} spectra. An F-test confirms a curved model over PL for all {\it Chandra} spectra, whereas most {\it NuSTAR} spectra are consistent with a single PL. Hardness ratio analysis shows a harder-when-brighter trend in the {\it Chandra} band. Joint fitting of the simultaneous 2013 {\it Chandra} and {\it NuSTAR} observations shows that the broadband 0.4$-$79 keV curvature is confined to the soft X-ray band, with the spectrum steepening above $\sim$2 keV and a steep hard X-ray power law ($\Gamma \simeq 2.7$). Our results reveal energy-dependent X-ray variability over more than two decades, and show that broadband X-ray coverage is needed to locate the curvature of the synchrotron tail in high-synchrotron-peaked blazars.

12. Brown dwarfs and stars are dynamically indistinguishable as companions in AU-separation binaries[2611.21800]
Abstract

Brown dwarfs occupy the mass range between stars and planets, populations whose dominant formation channels differ, which leave measurable differences in the orbital eccentricity and host-star metallicity distributions. We assemble a sample of astrometric binaries with main sequence primaries and brown dwarf companions at $\mathcal{O}$(AU) separations from $\textit{Gaia}$ DR3. The observable photocenter motions for such binaries are degenerate between a genuinely low-mass companion and a near-equal-mass binary. We break this degeneracy using $\textit{Gaia}$ XP spectrophotometry and follow-up FEROS spectroscopy, which removes removes ${\sim}70\%$ of initial low-mass-function candidates as likely near-twin binaries and leaves residual contamination of only a few percent. The resulting 368 systems, with masses and metallicities determined from the XP spectra and a selection function forward-modelled with $\texttt{Gaiamock}$, form the largest homogeneous sample of brown dwarf companions with astrometric orbits. We find the eccentricity distribution to be well described by a normal distribution. This is comparable to eccentricities of stellar companions at the same periods and higher than those of giant planets, with no significant dependence on companion mass ($\alpha=-0.13\pm0.09$) or period ($\beta=0.10\pm0.05$). The host-star metallicity distribution is likewise stellar-like, extending to [M/H]$=-0.5$, in contrast to the predominantly super-solar metallicity hosts of giant planets. After accounting for selection, the companion-mass distribution over all brown dwarf masses is consistent with an extension of stellar-binary demographics. Brown dwarf companions at AU separations down to at least $20\,M_J$ therefore appear to form predominantly through a binary-star-like channel. A possible transition to planet-like formation at lower masses cannot be located with the present sample.

14. A Forecast for Testing the Decreasing $H_0$ Trend in Time-Delay Cosmography[2611.21821]
Abstract

In 2019 the H0LiCOW/TDCOSMO collaboration reported a decreasing Hubble constant $H_0$ trend with lens redshift in the flat $\Lambda$CDM model at low statistical significance ($< 2 \sigma$). The signal is expected if physics is missing from the $\Lambda$CDM model in the late universe. In the wake of recent TDCOSMO 2026 results, we revisit the feature. Although the statistical significance of the trend has increased in a like-for-like comparison with earlier studies, propagating mass-sheet transformation (MST) uncertainties reduces the significance to $\sim 1 \sigma$. As a result, Akaike Information Criterion (AIC) currently favours a constant $H_0$ over a decreasing $H_0$ in the ratio 2.4:1. Nevertheless, we highlight good agreement on a negative slope with independent observables and forecast that approximately 40 and 90 lenses will be required to substantiate the trend at $\sim 2 \sigma$ and $\sim 3 \sigma$ significance, respectively, if the feature is physical and current uncertainties are representative. Even now, it is evident that the TDCOSMO 2026 $H_0$ constraint $H_0 \approx 71$ km/s/Mpc reflects an average over the lens redshift distribution and is naturally anchored at intermediate lens redshifts.

15. Three Dimensional Structure of Jovian Polar Cyclones Revealed by Juno Microwave Radiometer[2611.21841]
Abstract

Jupiter's circumpolar cyclones (CPCs) represent one of the most extreme dynamical regimes of the Solar System, where rotation, stratification, and convection interact to organize a stable polygonal cyclone lattice at each pole. Yet the depth and vertical structure of these cyclones remain unresolved. Here, using Juno Microwave Radiometer observations acquired between May 2023 and May 2024, we retrieve the 3-D thermal/compositional structure of Jupiter's northern CPCs at depths beyond those detectable by Earth-based telescopes and spacecraft that have ever visited. The eight CPCs, appearing with enhanced microwave brightness, show as modest ammonia depletion near the water lifting-condensation level ($\sim$5 bar) and/or, equivalently, warm-core structures analogous to terrestrial tropical cyclones. Five of them show substantially stronger and deeper anomalies than the remaining three, with approximately twice the inferred ammonia depletion and localized heating. In contrast, the central polar cyclone exhibits little microwave brightness contrast relative to its surroundings, despite its substantially cloudier and visually distinct appearance in visible and infrared observations. Previous dynamical models have treated the CPCs and the central polar cyclone as dynamically similar structures arising from the self-organization of turbulent flows. The new observations, however, suggest that the CPCs and the central polar cyclone may have distinct origins and operate under different dynamical regimes.

Abstract

SIPS J2045-6332 is an unusual low-mass object previously identified by its infrared excess and possible signatures of youth and unresolved multiplicity. Earlier work suggested that it is relatively young and may host a mid-L companion. Here we reassess the system using low-resolution 0.75-5 micron spectroscopy from SPHEREx. 17. Theoretical Instability Models of Quasi-Periodic Eruptions[2611.22015]
Abstract

The nature of the Quasi-Periodic Eruptions (QPE) is still not firmly established while the phenomenon probes the innermost part of the accretion flow undergoing dramatic changes. In this chapter we review the possibility that the observed rapid variability is caused by the accretion disk instability. We summarise the defining observational properties of QPE sources: time scales of recurrence, the amplitudes of soft X-ray flares, high coherence over many cycles, and comparatively modest time-averaged luminosities. We then translate these into constraints on the characteristic radius, accretion rate, and energy reservoir of the inner disk. We review the main classes of disk instabilities relevant to supermassive black holes: radiation pressure induced thermal/viscous limit cycles, magnetically mediated inner disk transitions, and coupling to disk winds that can produce the oscillatory behavior. For each scenario we discuss the required parameter space (black hole mass, Eddington ratio, viscosity prescription, and truncation radius), the predicted spectral and timing signatures, and the degree of fine tuning needed to reproduce the observed duty cycles and flare morphologies. We highlight key tensions, including the extremely soft spectra, short recurrence times compared with standard viscous times at tens of gravitational radii, and the weak or absent contemporaneous optical/UV variability in several systems. Finally, we outline observational tests that may help distinguish between instability driven and geometrical or orbital models: phase resolved spectroscopy to track the temperature evolution, searches for hysteresis in hardness-intensity diagrams, reverberation and absorption variability tied to winds, and long baseline monitoring to measure drift in period and energy output.

18. On Modelling the Far-Side Unsigned Magnetic Flux Evolution of Long-Lived NOAA Active Regions[2611.22051]
Abstract

The evolution of magnetic flux in solar active regions is central to space-weather forecasting and solar-cycle studies. Direct measurements of the far-side magnetic field, however, remain unavailable with current solar instrumentation. We develop a method to estimate the far-side evolution of active-region magnetic flux using 304 Å (He II) emission as a quantitative proxy. Using SDO/AIA and SDO/HMI observations of all four near-side passages of a long-lived active region (NOAA AR 12192), we fit a single relation that gives mean per-pixel unsigned magnetic flux from mean per-pixel 304 Å intensity. The fit uses only disk-centre, flare-free intervals, and flux is the dependent variable, so the relation can be applied directly to intensity measurements. The magnetogram flux is deprojected to the radial direction before fitting, and we report the line-of-sight version for comparison. We first test the relation by fitting it on the first two passages and predicting the two later passages, which were not used in that fit; the relation adopted for the reconstruction is then fitted to all four passages. We then apply the same relation to STEREO-A/EUVI 304 Å observations, scaling the EUVI intensities to AIA-equivalent boundary intensities inferred from the adjacent near-side flux measurements, and so reconstruct the unsigned flux history while the region was out of view from Earth. The test on the later passages gives a median log-space residual of +0.052 dex, a root-mean-square error of 0.083 dex, and a median absolute fractional error of 14% over 272 samples that were not used in the fit. Because a single cross-instrument scale factor cannot match both ends of a far-side interval, the absolute level of each reconstructed segment carries an uncertainty of several tens of per cent; it is the shape of the far-side curve, rather than its level, that the EUVI observations constrain.

19. 3D and 2D Radiation Non-ideal MHD modeling of the decay of FUor outbursts[2611.22225]
Abstract

FU Orionis (FUor) outbursts are high-accretion events in protoplanetary disks thought to be driven by the magnetorotational instability (MRI). Although these outbursts span a wide range of observed durations and can thermally process and potentially transport disk material, the physical processes governing their decay remain poorly understood. We present a three-dimensional radiative non-ideal magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) simulation of the decay of an MRI-driven FUor outburst, complemented by a suite of two-dimensional models that explore its dependence on disk properties. During the decay, rings and gaps form naturally within the inner disk. Magnetic flux preferentially concentrates in the gaps, while the rings remain comparatively weakly magnetized. We show that MRI-active rings act as “magnetic dams,” confining magnetic flux within adjacent gaps. As the rings cool below the MRI activation temperature, non-ideal MHD effects decouple the gas from the magnetic field, allowing the trapped flux to diffuse outward. This outward diffusion can sometimes transiently reactivate MRI in neighboring rings, producing localized reheating events that we term “Reactivation.” By comparing otherwise identical two- and three-dimensional simulations, we find that MRI heating is systematically less efficient in two dimensions, leading to outburst durations shorter by a factor of $\sim3$–4. Our model suite further shows that the decay timescale depends primarily on the disk surface density and distance from the central star, with only a weak dependence on the magnetic field strength. We further derive an empirical relation linking the outburst duration to these quantities.

20. The Lives and Deaths of Red Supergiants[2611.22233]
Abstract

Red supergiants (RSGs) represent a brief yet pivotal phase in the evolution of massive stars, encompassing the final stages of nuclear burning for many core-collapse supernova progenitors. Having expanded to 100 times larger than their main-sequence sizes, they occupy the cool, luminous extreme of the Hertzsprung–Russell diagram. Characterised by extended convective envelopes, complex and dynamic atmospheres, and, sometimes, substantial mass loss, RSGs play a critical role in shaping both their own evolutionary outcomes and the circumstellar environments into which they ultimately explode.
