1. Characterizing Gravitational Wave Electromagnetic Counterparts with the Schmidt Observatory System[2611.05461]
Abstract

The emergence of gravitational wave (GW) multimessenger astronomy, marked by the detection of GW170817 and its associated kilonova, has opened a new window into the physics of compact binary mergers. Building a statistical population of kilonovae remains a challenge, limited by the low neutron star merger detection rates and the rapid evolution of these faint transients. The former problem will be overcome with upcoming LIGO/Virgo/KAGRA (LVK) sensitivity improvements leading to the fifth Observing Run (O5), while the latter will be addressed by the Schmidt Observatory System (including the Argus Array, the Deep Synoptic Array (DSA), and the Lazuli Space Observatory), in addition to ongoing and upcoming surveys (including the Rubin Observatory Legacy Survey of Space and Time). Using simulations of expected kilonovae at LVK O5 sensitivity, we find that Argus and Rubin could detect binary neutron star kilonovae at a rate of 0.3-4.2 yr$^{-1}$ and 0.6-7.0 yr$^{-1}$ respectively. For neutron star-black hole mergers, Rubin could detect up to 1 kilonova per year. Lazuli will be able to characterize this kilonova population down to $g \sim 26$ mag as early as 12 hours post merger. Additionally, $\sim35\%$ of these events are expected to exhibit a jet afterglow that will be detectable by DSA. We further characterize kilonova spectra to determine observable features of heavy element production. Finally, we develop an observing strategy that combines high cadence imaging and spectral monitoring, maximizing Lazuli's ability to constrain ejecta properties and composition of GW counterparts with reasonable observing time.

2. CHEX-MATE: Density profile reconstruction in galaxy clusters[2611.05476]
Abstract

We investigated the reconstruction of the intra-cluster medium radial density profiles in galaxy clusters, focusing on the impact of cluster morphology, dynamical state, and methodological choices, within the framework of the CHEX-MATE project. Using a sample of 109 simulated galaxy clusters analysed along 40 independent lines of sight, we compared azimuthal mean and median techniques for deprojection. We find that azimuthal median-based density profiles recover the true gas density with negligible bias over the radial range $[0.15-1.3] R_{500}$. Mean based profiles are affected by the presence of substructures. While an accurate masking of clumps or substructures can reduce the bias to $< 5\%$, the discrepancy increases substantially in disturbed systems or when substructures remain unresolved. The mean-to-median density ratio statistically traces projected gas inhomogeneities, although intrinsic scatter and projection effects limit its use for individual clusters. The complementary use of mean and median profiles therefore provides the most complete approach for large X-ray cluster samples.

3. Gravitational waves flooding the Universe after wall domination[2611.05504]
Abstract

We discuss a possible early Universe scenario, where radiation era is interrupted by the epoch of domination of domain walls eventually annihilating into particles. During this period the Universe undergoes accelerated expansion, while the comoving correlation length of the domain wall network remains constant. The network serves as a source of tensor modes, which get frozen upon exiting the horizon similarly to the case of inflationary fluctuations. The modes re-enter the horizon during the subsequent stage of radiation domination and since then contribute to the gravitational wave background. The spectral shape of gravitational waves predicted in this scenario is markedly different compared to the case of domain walls remaining sub-dominant all the way till annihilation. These gravitational waves are strong enough to be observed with present-day and future facilities in a broad range of frequencies. In particular, their energy density is sufficient to produce a significant impact on the effective number of neutrino species, unless the duration of wall domination is suitably constrained.

4. An obstruction to cosmic acceleration from shift-symmetric dark sector interactions[2611.05560]
Abstract

Using the effective field theory of cosmological media, we show that derivative interactions in the dark sector between a perfect fluid and a superfluid cannot support slow-roll expansion while remaining free from instabilities. Furthermore, decomposing the dark sector medium into effective dark matter and dark energy components, we find that the stability conditions exclude a phantom-to-non-phantom crossing. Our approach gives a clear route for constructing theoretically viable dark sector models, and highlights the fundamental constraints that stability requirements impose on their cosmological dynamics.

5. PrimordialPy: A Python library for single-field inflation, primordial black holes, and scalar-induced gravitational waves[2611.05566]
Abstract

We present \texttt{PrimordialPy}, an open-source Python library designed to compute the cosmological predictions of arbitrary canonical single-field inflationary models. By numerically solving the background dynamics and the curvature perturbation equation, the package evaluates the primordial power spectrum of scalar and tensor perturbations at all relevant scales. Furthermore, it automatically computes the resulting mass fraction and abundance of Primordial Black Holes (PBHs), as well as the associated spectrum of scalar-induced gravitational waves (SIGWs). Users can seamlessly implement any canonical single-field model by simply specifying the inflaton potential $V(\phi)$ and its parameters. Designed to be modular, efficient, and highly accessible, \texttt{PrimordialPy} comes with comprehensive documentation, validation benchmarks, and practical examples, making it a versatile tool for both research and education in early-universe cosmology.

6. One gravitational function across matter phases: compatibility and interface constraints in static $f(R,T)$ gravity[2611.05657]
Abstract

Conservative $f(R,T)=R+h(T)$ gravity fixes $h$ by demanding that the matter stress tensor be conserved, and the reconstruction is normally carried out for one equation of state at a time. We ask whether one function $h(T)$ can serve two matter phases separated by a sharp transition. The analysis is static and local: spherically symmetric backgrounds, the $\mathcal{L}_m=\rho$ closure, cold barotropic perfect fluids, and a Maxwell interface without a surface layer. A common $h$ must satisfy two conditions that single-phase reconstructions do not impose. It must solve both reconstruction equations at every trace value occupied by both phases, and it must make the effective normal pressure continuous across the interface; equality of material pressure and chemical potential does not secure the latter. For a hadronic polytrope ($K>0$, $\Gamma>1$) and an affine quark equation of state, an open overlap of the occupied trace ranges forces $h$ to be constant on both. When the ranges are separated, both conditions can be met by a nonconstant $C^2$ function, which we construct explicitly at a single interface. Two further results bound this local construction. If the same polytrope extends to zero density, regularity of $h$ at $T=0$ removes its nonconstant part there; and wherever $h'(T)\neq0$, separate conservation forbids any change in the density of a fluid element.

7. The spatial structure of solar magnetoconvection at scales up to $60$ Mm[2611.05674]
Abstract

While convective motions at the scale of granules on the solar surface are well understood, those of mesogranular and supergranulation are not. At still larger scales, the lack of giant cells is a major mystery. We aim to slightly improve understanding of the radial and horizontal flows associated at mesogranular scales and supergranulation. We perform a deep MURaM small-scale dynamo simulation, extending from $50$ Mm below the solar surface and with a horizontal size of 60 Mm and sufficient resolution to begin to properly resolve the magnetic bright points in intergranular lanes. We determine the structure of the convective modes from the simulations at different scales, and compare the power spectra to those which have been previously observed. At all scales which are resolved by the simulation the simulated flows agree with the amplitude implied by Doppler measurements and local correlation tracking (LCT) measurements. The power in the vertical Doppler velocities at supergranular and larger scales is shown to be influenced by density perturbations near the surface. The structure of the modes at depth is simple as expected from the previous study of \cite{LordCameron2014} except the horizontal forcing scale scales like $H_\rho^{2/3}$ instead of $H\rho$. The simulations reproduce the observations up to scales of $60$ Mm. Within the simulated range, $l>=70$, we do not see the excess power associated with the convective conundrum.

8. A high-frequency turnover in the persistent radio source of FRB20201124A[2611.05694]
Abstract

Persistent radio sources (PRSs) associated with repeating fast radio bursts probe the immediate magneto-ionic environment of their engine and thereby constrain the nature and evolutionary stage of the progenitor. The persistent radio source of FRB20201124A is the most extreme in the current sample, with a spectrum that remains flat or inverted up to 22 GHz. We aim to constrain the centimetre-to-millimetre spectral energy distribution of this source and to test whether its properties are consistent with a young, rotation-powered magnetar wind nebula. We analysed archival ALMA Band 3 observations at $\sim100$ GHz and combined them with existing VLA measurements at 6, 15 and 22 GHz. The resulting spectrum was fitted with the Granot–Sari broken power law in the slow-cooling regime using Bayesian nested sampling. The two-parameter model yields a peak frequency $\nu_m = 63^{+100}_{-33}$ GHz and a peak flux $F_m = 28\pm9$ $\mu$Jy. This solution is consistent with theoretical expectations for a young magnetar wind nebula, which predict a peak frequency in the same range and a self-absorption frequency below $\sim0.5$ GHz. With its inverted spectrum extending to tens of gigahertz and the highest peak frequency among known PRSs, the source associated with FRB20201124A is the most extreme in the current sample. Its properties match those expected for a rotation-powered magnetar wind nebula, making it a strong candidate for the youngest ($t\lesssim20$ yr) magnetar associated with a repeating FRB to date.

9. Observational Test of the Maximum-Entropy Conjecture for Black-Hole Mergers with GW250114[2611.05718]
Abstract

The remnants of binary black-hole (BBH) mergers are accurately described by numerical relativity, but the physical principle governing the selection of the final states remains unclear. A recently proposed maximum-entropy conjecture suggests that a BBH merger produces the Kerr BH that maximizes the Bekenstein-Hawking entropy, subject to the post-Newtonian formalism or balance laws for energy and angular momentum. In this work, we test this conjecture with the event GW250114, inferring the progenitor and remnant properties from the observed pre-merge and post-merge signals, separately. We construct the surrogate Kerr entropy associated with candidate remnant states along the inferred energy-angular-momentum trajectory. We then compare the maximum-entropy state with the independently inferred mass and dimensionless spin of the final BH. We find that the conjecture predicts a remnant mass of $M_f=70.0^{+0.35}_{-0.21}\,M_\odot$ and spin of $\chi_f=0.70^{+0.00}_{-0.01}$, compared with the ringdown estimates $M_f={72.3}^{+3.6}_{-3.7}\,M_\odot$ and $\chi_f=0.74^{+0.05}_{-0.07}$. The entropy deviation is consistent with zero within the $68\%$ credible interval for our fiducial analysis, and variations in the pre-merger truncation time and quasi-normal modes (QNM) content reveal no compelling discrepancy with the conjecture.

10. Constraining Ganymede's Interior Structure and Bulk Composition from Its Present-Day Magnetic Field Strength[2611.05727]
Abstract

Ganymede is the only Solar System satellite with a global magnetic field driven by a core dynamo. Although its small moment-of-inertia (MoI) factor indicates a differentiated structure of a metallic core, rocky mantle, and outer hydrosphere, its exact bulk composition and layer thicknesses remain highly uncertain. We developed a comprehensive, long-term thermochemical evolution model that couples thermal history with magnetic field generation. Building upon previous theoretical frameworks, this study systematically evaluates how diverse chondritic precursor compositions constrain Ganymede's internal thermochemical evolution and core solidification processes. Based on interior structures initially permitted by a wide range of chondritic compositions and geodetic constraints, we applied the observed equatorial surface magnetic field strength ($\sim$719 nT) as a dynamic filter. Our evolutionary simulations demonstrate that while magnetic dynamos can operate under entire melting, Fe-snow, and FeS-layer regimes, the requirement to sustain the observed field strength strongly restricts the viable interior models. Satisfying this constraint preferentially requires a relatively large core radius and a specific, non-eutectic bulk core sulfur concentration. Consequently, this coupled thermochemical approach highlights the main novelty of this study: characterizing the hydrosphere's density serves as an independent constraint to break the structural degeneracy of Ganymede's bulk composition. Our model indicates that L and CI chondrites yield parameter spaces capable of sustaining comparable field strengths under a low-density hydrosphere scenario (1,200 kg m-3), whereas the H chondrite composition can satisfy this criterion under a high-density scenario (1,300 kg m-3). These predictions provide a critical framework to be tested by the JUICE mission arriving in 2031.

11. The impact of M dwarf stellar convection on exoplanetary atmospheric signatures. A 3D radiation-hydrodynamics simulation analysis for high-resolution spectroscopy[2611.05740]
Abstract

M dwarf stars represent prime targets for the detection and characterization of small, temperate exoplanets; however, their complex activity and convective surfaces pose significant challenges for high-resolution spectroscopic studies. This work aims to investigate the impact of stellar convection on high-resolution spectroscopic observations of M dwarf stars and develop an integrated approach combining 3D radiative hydrodynamical simulations, spectroscopic modeling, and transit analysis to mitigate stellar noise. We employ 3D radiative hydrodynamical simulations of M dwarf stellar convection to generate synthetic spectra. We compare the synthetic spectra to actual observations of GJ 3470. We model a high-resolution transit sequence of the Neptune-size GJ 3470 b, including centre-to-limb variations (CLVs) and the Rossiter-McLaughlin effect (RME), and process the sequence via principal component analysis (PCA) to de-trend stellar effects. Comparison of synthetic spectra with actual observations shows that current H2O line lists remain inaccurate, with modest agreement in line strength and position for the chosen K-band interval. During the transit of GJ 3470 b, the amplitude of CLVs+RME is comparable to exoplanet signals. Current PCA can in principle de-trend such effects, but its behaviour is highly dependent on the copresence of telluric and planetary signals. While we are able to correctly detect the exoplanet with no evident bias from stellar residuals, this result is limited to a narrow spectral interval and a specific orbital configuration. This study demonstrates that 3D radiative hydrodynamical simulations are essential for accurately modeling convection-induced spectral variability in M dwarfs. Our integrated framework establishes a foundation to obtain unbiased measurements from future ELT observations of exoplanets orbiting M dwarfs.

12. Insights on the mass composition of ultrahigh-energy cosmic rays from small-scale anisotropy with large-scale structure simulations[2611.05783]
Abstract

Ultra-high-energy cosmic rays (UHECRs) above 100 EeV provide important clues to the most energetic processes in the Universe. At the highest energies, it is expected that UHECRs originate only from nearby sources because of energy losses during propagation. In such a regime, small-scale anisotropies in arrival directions could appear if the magnetic deflections are sufficiently small, for example, for a limited fraction of UHECRs with relatively low electric charge. Using the Pierre Auger Observatory open data, we confirm there is no significant small-scale anisotropy above 100 EeV within 3 degrees, indicating an almost isotropic sky distribution. To interpret this, we consider that heavier nuclei have higher photodisintegration thresholds due to their smaller Lorentz factors, resulting in longer propagation lengths. Combination with the nearly homogeneous distribution of galaxies at such distances can explain the absence of anisotropies above 100 EeV. Based on this idea, we perform mock observations of UHECRs above 100 EeV assuming pure-proton, iron, and uranium compositions. We predict the expected UHECR distributions using the Millennium Run semi-analytic galaxy catalog and turbulent extragalactic magnetic fields. By varying the source density and magnetic field strength, we evaluate the anisotropy for each case. Without magnetic fields, the observed isotropy above 100 EeV excludes the pure-proton and pure-iron cases. The pure-uranium case can reproduce the observed isotropy when the source density is high and the magnetic field is turbulent. When the turbulent field is strong, both the iron and uranium cases remain consistent with the data, whereas almost no parameter set can explain the observation for a pure-proton composition. Our results constrain the possible combinations of source density and magnetic field strength across different UHECR compositions.

13. Unsupervised Neural Operators for Rotating Relativistic Stars: High-Fidelity Equation of State Inference and Pulsar Spin Evolution at Scale[2611.05790]
Abstract

Observed populations of rotating neutron stars (NSs) with millisecond-order periods are powerful probes of fundamental physics. Mass-radius measurements inform poorly constrained properties of degenerate nuclear matter, whereas high-fidelity population synthesis connects unknowns in NS formation and binary evolution to large radio-timing surveys. However, repeated structure calculations are prohibitive due to the computational cost of solving the highly coupled partial differential equations of axisymmetric general relativistic hydrostatic equilibrium that govern rotating NS interiors. Non-rotating calculations or approximate rotational corrections can bias equation of state inference and limit the fidelity of population synthesis in tracing the evolutionary history of progenitors and propagating equation of state uncertainties. In this \textit{Letter}, we develop physics-informed neural operators that can solve the axisymmetric problem with high accuracy and negligible computational cost. \textit{Our solvers are trained in a fully unsupervised setting, with the training objective informed solely by the partial differential equations, and achieve a $10^3\times$ speed-up with negligible accuracy loss}. These solvers enable scalable EoS inference that self-consistently accounts for rotation and repeated stellar-structure calculations during binary evolution, making spin predictions that account for rotational structure and EoS uncertainties computationally accessible to population synthesis.

14. A Laplace-Transform Green's Function for Two-Zone Diffusion with Energy Losses: Application to the Geminga Pulsar Halo[2611.05808]
Abstract

Extended TeV gamma-ray halos around pulsars are commonly interpreted as evidence for slow diffusion of high-energy electrons and positrons near their sources, with diffusion coefficients about two orders of magnitude below the Galactic average inferred from B/C measurements. A two-zone diffusion picture offers a natural phenomenological resolution: particles are temporarily confined in a slow-diffusion region around the source and subsequently propagate through the outer region with a diffusion coefficient close to the Galactic average. Using a Laplace-transform method, we derive an analytical Green's-function solution to the spherically symmetric two-zone diffusion equation, in which the diffusion coefficient changes discontinuously at r_b. The solution satisfies continuity of both the particle density and the diffusive flux at the boundary and reduces to the standard Gaussian kernel in the one-zone limit. For the parameter set used in our comparison, the spatial kernel and the positron flux at Earth agree numerically with an independent integral solution of the same two-zone problem. Applied to Geminga, the two-zone models examined here produce a more extended outer particle distribution and broader positron spectra than the homogeneous diffusion model, with higher positron fluxes in the TeV range. The adopted injection model further affects the height and shape of the high-energy spectral peak. The predicted gamma-ray surface-brightness profile also reproduces the main radial decline observed by HAWC.

15. Photometric Study of a Distant SU UMa-type Dwarf Nova KSP-OT-201803a[2611.05833]
Abstract

We present the discovery and photometric study of a distant dwarf nova KSP-OT-201803a using BVI-band observation data obtained by using the KMTNet Supernova Program. One superoutburst (SO) and three normal outbursts are identified from two years of observations (2018-2019), which show that KSP-OT-201803a is an SU UMa-type dwarf nova (DN). Applying the phase dispersion minimization method to the superhump data during the plateau phase of the SO yields a superhump period of P_sh = 127.4 minutes (=2.123 hr =0.08847 days). By stacking ten images of the quiescent phase, we find the quiescent phase magnitude of V = 22.59 +/- 0.18 mag. The SO shows quiescent-to-peak amplitude of 4.97 +/- 0.18 mag and duration of 16.59 days which are typical for the SU UMa-type DNe. Using the superhump period-orbital period relation of SU UMa stars, we obtain the approximate distance of 3.7 +/- 0.3 kpc to KSP-OT-201803a and Galactocentric distance of 9.4 kpc and height from the Galactic plane of +1.2 kpc. Stars at these large distances may belong to the Galactic halo or thick disk, which contain old, metal-poor Population II stars; therefore, these systems may have undergone a different evolutionary process than Population I objects. The orbital period gap of DNe spans 2.45-3.18 hours. Assuming that the orbital period of KSP-OT-201803a is a few percent shorter than its superhump period, KSP-OT-201803a may have just emerged from the period gap and recently resumed mass transfer after crossing its lower boundary.

16. Frequent Optical Flares in an M-type Detached Eclipsing Binary TIC 349005337[2611.05881]
Abstract

A comprehensive photometric and spectroscopic analysis of TIC 349005337, a highly active M-type detached eclipsing binary with an orbital period of 2.6079133 days, is presented utilizing high-cadence and long-term monitoring photometry from the Transiting Exoplanet Survey Satellite (TESS) and medium-resolution spectroscopy from the Large Sky Area Multi-Object Fiber Spectroscopic Telescope (LAMOST). By integrating radial velocity curves with light curves, we determined the fundamental physical parameters for both components. The system consists of an M-dwarf primary ($M_1 = 0.517 \pm 0.009 M_\odot$, $R_1 = 0.518 \pm 0.003 R_\odot$) and a fully convective secondary ($M_2 = 0.298 \pm 0.004 M_\odot$, $R_2 = 0.357 \pm 0.003 R_\odot$). As the secondary component falls below the fully convective boundary, these energetic flares provide critical empirical constraints for distributed dynamo models in the low-mass regime. Our results highlight TIC 349005337 as an ideal laboratory for investigating the interplay between binary dynamics, magnetic reconnection, and the resulting impact on the atmospheric stability of potential orbiting exoplanets.

17. Collective Characterization of Polar Crown Filament Eruptions[2611.05902]
Abstract

Polar crown filaments (PCFs) are crucially related to the structure of solar polar magnetic fields. They are physically significant because of the “rush-to-the-pole” motion, which is an indicator of the poleward drift of magnetic fields from decayed active regions. The formation and evolution of PCFs offer important insights into the Sun's magnetic activity at high latitudes and the solar cycle variation. In this study, we present a collective analysis of 50 PCF disappearance events covering solar cycle 24 and 25. Among them, 24 show rotational motions, and nine have sufficiently clear magnetic configurations for comparison with numerical model predictions. In all nine events, the observed rotation directions agree with the model predictions, suggesting possible magnetic-flux-rope-like structures in these events. All except one of the PCFs in our sample disappeared through eruption rather than thermal disappearance, and most associated CMEs were slow ($<800$ km s$^{-1}$). For events accompanied by transient coronal holes, the estimated magnetic flux associated with the dimming regions is on the order of $10^{20}$–$10^{21}$ Mx. Time–distance analysis identifies 22 events with a clear slow-rise to fast-rise transition, with onset heights ranging from several tens to about 160 Mm and a mean onset velocity of about 2.8 km s$^{-1}$ after excluding the highest-velocity event. Together, these findings characterize the magnetic structure and dynamics of PCF eruptions.

18. Tiny Quiet-Sun EUV Ejections Revealed by High-resolution Multiwavelength Observations[2611.05934]
Abstract

We investigate two successive tiny quiet-Sun EUV ejections on 2022 October 29 using coordinated Solar Orbiter/EUI, IRIS, SDO/AIA, and Goode Solar Telescope observations. We examine their low-lying geometry and the chromospheric and transition-region response before and between the releases. A curved H-alpha thread is present beforehand, and Mg ii and Si iv spectra reveal a compact multithermal precursor 2.6–2.7 min before the first EUV brightening. EUI–AIA stereoscopy places the recurrent compact EUV kernel near 2.2 Mm and the sampled emission at approximately 0.6–4.2 Mm along inclined, locally arched structures. The first ejection shows multithreaded, helical-looking EUV strands; the second shows straighter strands and a decrease in the cross-ridge separation of redshifted and blueshifted Mg ii k3 samples from 1.47” to 0.89”. Nevertheless, the two peaks have comparable emission-measure increments and estimated event-associated thermal contents of a few 10^26 erg under the same assumptions. Before the second ejection, the regional Si iv and k3 blueshift signals remain enhanced, while the Mg ii central reversal along the activated ridge remains shallower than before the event. Component reconnection between the curved structure and the surrounding field offers a possible explanation. The observations show that jet-like EUV emission can occur in low-lying structures. The second ejection begins before the chromospheric and transition-region spectra have returned to their pre-event states. Its spectral signatures may therefore depend on the atmospheric changes associated with the first ejection as well as on the additional energy input.

19. Gravitational-Wave Microlensing as a Probe of Compact Object Population[2611.05947]
Abstract

The abundance of compact objects ranging from stellar to intermediate masses ($\sim 1 - 10^{5}M_{\odot}$) remains poorly constrained, particularly for populations such as primordial black holes and dark matter subhalos that emit little or no electromagnetic radiation. Gravitational-wave (GW) microlensing provides a complementary way to probe these populations through wave-optics effects imprinted on the GW signals, with implications for astrophysical and cosmological inference. We develop a hierarchical Bayesian framework to infer the population properties of microlenses from an ensemble of microlensed GW events, constraining the slope of the power-law lens mass function ($\alpha_{\rm ML}$), the lens number density ($n_0$), and the fraction of dark matter in compact lenses ($f_{\rm DM}$). Using simulated microlensed GW signals drawn from a realistic binary black hole population, we find that $\sim 100$ detected microlensed events are able to constrain both $\alpha_{\rm ML}$ and $n_0$, although a degeneracy between the two remains. For a low-mass-dominated lens population ($\alpha_{\rm ML}=2$) with $n_0=10^6~\mathrm{Mpc}^{-3}$, a three-year observation at the O4 sensitivity yields $\sim 130$ detectable microlensed events and recovers the injected dark-matter fraction, $f_{\rm DM}\simeq 1.2 \times 10^{-4}$, with a 90% credible interval of $7.3 \times 10^{-5} \lesssim f_{\rm DM} \lesssim 1.8 \times 10^{-4}$.

20. Longitudinal-field particle acceleration in channels generated by strong electromagnetic waves in pair plasma[2611.05948]
Abstract

Relativistically strong electromagnetic waves interacting with pair plasmas appear in astrophysical settings and may become accessible in future laboratory experiments using high-power lasers. Transverse modulations and filamentation can turn extended wavefronts into structures of a finite transverse width. We examine the interaction of such a finite-width wave incident on a cold, uniform pair plasma using a three-dimensional kinetic particle-in-cell simulation. The wave does not penetrate the plasma and instead advances by creating a fully evacuated channel. The wave loses energy at a rate that cannot be explained by plasma clearing alone. We find that the excess losses are caused by strong particle acceleration near the walls of the channel. The acceleration is driven by a longitudinal electric field associated with the transverse wave structure and the geometry of the self-generated channel.

21. Orbital Architecture of Super-Earth-Cold-Jupiter Systems from Transit Duration Ratios[2611.05963]
Abstract

Super-Earths are commonly found with cold Jupiters, yet it is unclear whether these Super-Earth-Cold-Jupiter (SE-CJ) systems differ architecturally from other Kepler-like systems (Non-SE-CJ). One might expect the CJs to excite eccentricities and mutual inclinations among the SEs. We use two metrics: the normalized transit duration $T/T_0$, set by the host-star density, and the pairwise duration ratio $\xi$ between neighboring transiting planets. We find no population-level difference in $T/T_0$ between the samples. Forward modeling of $\xi$, which is more directly sensitive to mutual inclinations because it isolates the impact-parameter contrast, finds no evidence that SE-CJ systems are more mutually inclined than Non-SE-CJ systems, and hints they could be even flatter. We infer a Rayleigh dispersion in mutual inclination of $\delta \approx 1.0^\circ$, or a characteristic eccentricity of $e \approx 0.035$ (assuming equipartition), compared with $\delta \approx 1.5^\circ$ and $e \approx 0.05$ for Non-SE-CJ systems. Many SE-CJ systems therefore retain nearly coplanar, low-eccentricity architectures despite hosting distant giants. We further show that secular coupling among the inner Super-Earths usually dominates over forcing from the cold Jupiter, leaving the inner planets resistant to excitation. Our analysis is limited to SE-CJ systems with multiple transiting SEs: systems in which the CJ has strongly excited the inner architecture are expected to present as single-transiting, and cannot be studied with the $\xi$ metric. Another caveat of our result is that undetected cold Jupiters in the comparison sample could dilute the measured $\xi$ distribution.

22. Spectroscopic diagnostics of coronal fan loops using EIS/Hinode and AIA/SDO[2611.05995]
Abstract

We utilize spectroscopic and imaging data from the EIS/Hinode and the AIA/SDO to determine plasma and wave parameters along coronal fan loops. We determined the temperature along the loop lengths using the EM-loci method with EIS spectral lines and identified multi-thermal plasma components at temperatures ranging from 0.8-1.05, 1.2-1.4, and 1.6-1.8 MK. We further compared these temperature distributions along the loops with those derived from DEM analysis carried out using AIA coronal passbands. The multi-thermal nature of coronal fan loops can be well resolved using EIS spectroscopic data. We determined the density along the loop using the line-ratio method from the EIS Fe XII line pair. We found that density decreases slowly from the expected hydrostatic equilibrium. Our results demonstrate the multi-thermal nature of overdense loops, which can be well resolved with EIS spectroscopic data. We also determined the velocity amplitude of Alfvén waves from the non-thermal broadening of EIS Fe XII spectral line profiles. We further estimated the Alfvén wave energy flux in the corona by combining these parameters with the recently reported magnetic field strength of $\approx$200 G at the coronal footpoint of fan loops. The estimated Alfvén wave energy flux is $\approx$ 2$\times 10^7$ erg cm$^{-2}$ s$^{-1}$, significantly higher than those reported in earlier studies, and can contribute to coronal losses due to radiation along the fan loops in the active region.

Abstract

The paper analyses the orientation of the spin axes of Kuiper Belt (KB) bodies that have satellites. For most of these bodies, the axes orientations were estimated under the assumption that the orbital planes of the satellites lie close to the bodies' equatorial planes. It was found that the spin axes of large scattered disks and plutinos' bodies, including four dwarf planets, are inclined at relatively small angles to the planes of their heliocentric orbits. 24. Optical linear polarization of the Be–X-ray pulsar binary LS V +44 17[2611.06249]
Abstract

We present the optical $BVR$ polarization measurements for the Be–X-ray pulsar binary LS V +44 17 from its giant outburst in early 2023 and subsequently over three years. 25. A search for sterile neutrinos at ultrahigh energies at the Pierre Auger Observatory[2611.06284]
Abstract

We investigate the sensitivity of the Pierre Auger Observatory to sterile neutrinos with masses in the MeV-to-100 MeV range. To this end, we consider a minimal $3+1$ framework in which a single sterile state mixes mainly with the $\nu_\tau$ flavor through an active–sterile mixing angle $\theta_N$ to produce a mass eigenstate $N_1$. 72. Stable Ballistic Prograde Cyclers in the Three-Body Problem[2606.29189]
Abstract

We report the first continuous families of stable, ballistic, prograde cycler orbits in the circular restricted three-body problem: periodic trajectories that alternately undergo temporary capture and orbit each primary. We construct continuous families of symmetric cyclers from intersections of the stable and unstable manifold tubes of the $L_1$ Lyapunov orbit and exhibit stable examples across more than two orders of magnitude in mass ratio, from the Sun-Jupiter regime to the equal-mass limit. Linear stability separates naturally into planar and out-of-plane components. A planar-stable branch of every computed family is created simultaneously with a hyperbolic branch in a saddle-center bifurcation of the return map at the family's maximal Jacobi constant, while out-of-plane instability occurs only through isolated parametric resonances. Every computed family contains a subfamily that is linearly stable to both planar and out-of-plane perturbations. These stable cyclers persist into the full three-body problem for sufficiently small third mass. We conjecture that saddle-center birth is universal among cycler families, suggesting that stable cyclers are a generic feature of three-body dynamics.

73. Gravitational Wave Birefringence in generalized Palatini Chern Simons[2607.20689]
Abstract

The cosmological propagation of gravitational waves (GWs) can exhibit amplitude and phase polarization distortions when parity symmetry is broken, a phenomenon known as cosmological birefringence. In this paper, we investigate the phenomenology of GW birefringence in a gravitational model $f(R)$ coupled to a dynamical Chern-Simons (dCS) term, analyzed in the metric and Palatini formalisms. At the background level, this model can lead to dynamical dark energy, while for GW propagation we find that the Palatini formalism predicts both amplitude and velocity birefringence, whereas the metric formalism predicts amplitude birefringence only. We also find that the birefringence effects can either be suppressed or enhanced by the $f(R)$ interactions, depending on the specific form of $f(R)$. Considering three common $f(R)$ models (Hu-Sawicki, Exponential, and Hyperbolic gravity) fit to recent cosmological data, we find that birefringence is enhanced relative to the $f(R) = R$ case, by $1-10\%$ in the Palatini formalism and up to a factor of 2 in the metric formalism. We also translate current GW birefringence constraints to bounds on the dCS coupling within our model. Finally, we show that in our model the birefringence effect grows polynomially with source redshift, in contrast to the linear-distance scaling commonly assumed in phenomenological models of GW birefringence in the current literature.

74. Neutron star cooling with medium-modified Urca rates[2609.34338]
Abstract

We study the effects of in-medium modification of the elementary cooling processes on observable properties of isolated neutron stars. We deduce the neutron star mass distributions compatible with the cooling analysis and compare with current theoretical models. We conclude that current cooling data require fast direct Urca cooling, moderated by proton superfluidity, to be active in most neutron stars, and that the DU onset threshold must lie below canonical masses. In that case medium modifications of modified Urca rates are practically insignificant, but the nn Bremsstrahlung rate plays a dominant role.

75. IceCube data for astrophysical neutrinos exhibit q-statistical behavior[2610.14129]
Abstract

Relevant IceCube recent data for astrophysical neutrinos are analyzed. Similarly to cosmic rays and proton-proton LHC collisions, the corresponding spectra are satisfactorily described, for the entire interval of measured energies, through $q$-Gaussians. This fact reinforces the already available evidence that nonadditive entropies (either of the $S_q$ class or of the $S_\delta$ one, or even alternative ones) appear to play a crucial role in systems such as black holes and related cosmological phenomena (e.g., dark energy). The IceCube data yield high values for the momenta-distribution nonadditive index ($q \simeq 1.6$, hence $q_{energy} \simeq 1.3$), to be compared with the cosmic rays values ($q_{energy} \simeq 1.2$) and the LHC values ($q_{energy} \simeq 1.1$). This fact neatly indicates very-long-ranged space-time correlations. The detailed associated physical mechanisms remain, at the present stage, as an instigating open issue.
