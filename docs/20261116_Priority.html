1. Effect of galaxy-informed merger timescales on massive black hole binaries in LISA[2611.17409]
Abstract

Over a decade of direct N-body simulations tell us that massive black hole binary (MBHB) merger timescales are strongly dependent on the structural and kinematic properties of the host galaxy merger remnant. The physical stages of stellar scattering and gravitational wave (GW) emission of MBH coalescence occur below the resolution limit of most cosmological simulations, requiring post-processing to achieve accurate merging times. We apply a recently developed galaxy-informed merger timescale for MBHBs to the Romulus25 simulation and compare with current MBH merger rate and event rate estimates for the upcoming Laser Interferometer Space Antenna (LISA). We find a median stellar scattering time of   10^8.2 yr^-1, roughly an order of magnitude faster than those produced by timescales that rely on extrapolations of the stellar density profile. We predict a merger rate of   11.5 yr^-1 corresponding to 46 merger events over a 4-year LISA mission. We also investigate the importance of the relative orbital inclination of the MBHB with respect to the host galaxy and of nuclear star cluster (NSC) modeling, finding that the quickest merger events correspond to MBHBs that counterrotate and galaxies assigned to host NSCs, which may be important for host galaxy identification in the multi-messenger era.

2. Astrometric microlensing by isolated black holes in the Roman Galactic Bulge Time Domain Survey[2611.17410]
Abstract

Most of the hundred million stellar-mass black holes (BHs) expected in the Milky Way are predicted to be isolated. Characterizing this BH population will provide insights into stellar evolution and supernova explosion physics. The Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope Galactic Bulge Time Domain Survey (GBTDS) will enable the detection of isolated BHs through astrometric microlensing, apparent stellar position shifts caused by deflection of distant starlight. We combine a Galactic model, stellar population synthesis model, BH initial–final mass relation, and kick velocity distribution to simulate events caused by isolated BH lenses. We assess the expected yield, sensitivity, characterization potential, and signal characteristics of these events to inform GBTDS search strategies. We predict $487 \pm 37$ isolated-BH microlensing events with detectable astrometric signals, 82% of which have impact parameters beyond 2 Einstein radii and consequently have small or undetectable photometric signals. Standard astrometric fits yield unit weight errors > 1.2 for 18% and > 2 for 2% of these events. Sensitivity peaks for 5-6$M_\odot$ BHs and is limited by the five-year GBTDS baseline. Weak microlensing parallax constraints limit BH mass precision, but events with Einstein timescales > 100 days and angular Einstein radii > 1.2 mas have high BH lens probabilities within our Galactic model. Wide-separation astrometric microlensing offers an independent discovery channel for isolated BHs beyond photometrically selected events. Realizing this opportunity requires dedicated multi-season GBTDS searches, joint astrometric and photometric modeling, and accounting for other astrometric variability. Although precise individual masses are challenging for these events, probabilistic lens classification could lead to Galactic abundance constraints on the population of isolated BHs.

3. Properties of stellar disks and spheroids in simulated galaxies[2611.17415]
Abstract

The optical morphology of a galaxy is determined by the relative importance of their disk and spheroidal components. Although the scaling laws of disk-dominated or spheroid-dominated galaxies have been studied in some detail, less attention has been paid to the properties of each component in galaxies where both coexist. We use a dynamical decomposition of 3452 galaxies from the TNG100 simulations with stellar masses in the range $10 \leq \log_\mathrm{10}(M_*/M_\odot) \leq 12$ to study how, at given mass, the properties of a spheroid are altered by the presence of a disk, and vice versa. We define spheroids as the non-rotating component made up of all stars in retrograde motion, plus an equal number of co-rotating stars mirrored in angular momentum and energy. Disks simply consist of the remainder of (co-rotating) stars. We find that disk properties (rotation speed, size, formation time) depend sensitively on the mass of the spheroidal component and therefore of the total mass of the galaxy in which both reside. At given mass, disks rotate faster, are more extended, and form their stars earlier as the mass of the spheroidal component increases. On the other hand, at fixed mass, the properties of spheroids are less sensitive to the presence of a disk. Roughly speaking, the classical bulges of simulated disk galaxies closely resemble ellipticals of similar stellar mass. The apparent structural invariance of spheroids to the presence of a disk is somewhat surprising, but it likely results because disks form out of high angular momentum material which surrounds the spheroid and thus have less dynamical influence on a spheroid's structure.

4. Euclid Data Release 1 (DR1). Enhancing deep-learning models: the impact of image processing and training data selection for strong-lens detection[2611.17420]
Abstract

Euclid is expected to discover  $10^5$ galaxy-galaxy strong gravitational lenses across  14,000deg$^2$, requiring automated searches with high completeness and low false-positive rates. Despite advances in deep learning, the effects of training-set composition, image representation, and preprocessing on survey-scale detection remain poorly understood. We present an ablation study of the convolutional neural network used in the ECLIPSE strong-lens detection pipeline, evaluating different training configurations on a fixed test set. Using simulated and real Euclid data, we compare VIS-only and pseudo-RGB representations, FITS and JPEG formats, training-set composition, hard-negative selection, and multi-stage training. We also assess ensembling on  67 million DR1 cutouts. Training-set composition and strategy strongly affect high-Recall performance. Omitting simulation based pre-training reduces high-Recall weighted average Precision by 83.7% for VIS-only and 74.5% for pseudo-RGB models. Negative-sample selection also substantially affects performance, while FITS yields modest gains over JPEG at high Recall ( 2-5%). On DR1, the logit-mean ensemble improves average Precision by 28.0% and 39.9% over the individual VIS-only and pseudo-RGB models. Among  70,000 candidates selected for visual inspection, it recovers 11,256 unique grade A (secure) and B (probable) lenses, compared with 9650 and 10,395 for the individual models. The branches show complementary ranking preferences, particularly for lenses benefiting from near-infrared information. Careful training-set design, realistic negatives, and multi-band ensembling improve survey-scale searches. Our final ensemble achieves  80% Recall at the operational selection threshold, outperforming ablation models with higher global classification metrics and highlighting the need to optimise performance for realistic survey conditions.[Abridged]

5. The Gravitational Wave Foreground from Galactic Cataclysmic Variables in LISA[2611.17421]
Abstract

Electromagnetic observations have been the primary medium for studying cataclysmic variable (CV) systems, capitalizing on the invaluable opportunity they present to study binary stellar evolution and accretion. The launch of the Laser Interferometer Space Antenna (LISA) mission, however, will enable a multi-messenger perspective of the population. We extend previous work to explore the gravitational wave (GW) spectra sourced by a local and Galactic population of CVs. Using the Bayesian LISA Inference Package (BLIP), we simulate LISA data containing these CV populations to understand their impact and our ability to jointly model CV and double white dwarf (DWD) GW spectra. While we find that a local CV population has negligible effect on the LISA datastream, a Galactic CV population will have a signal-to-noise ratio of approximately 76, depending on the characteristics of the DWD population present, and will bias existing DWD spectral models if ignored. Current approaches struggle to accurately recover both populations' respective spectra, calling for the creation of multi-component spectral models that include GW contributions from both CVs and DWDs. These results point toward a promising future for the study of CVs with LISA.

6. Effects of Environment on the Star Formation Histories and Shapes of Simulated Galaxies[2611.17427]
Abstract

We investigate how environment is connected to the formation times and three-dimensional stellar shapes of galaxies using the FIREbox cosmological volume simulation. We analyze 1720 resolved galaxies at $z=0$ spanning stellar masses $M_\star\approx5\times10^6$ to $5\times10^{11}\,M_\odot$ and environments ranging from isolated galaxies to groups in $\sim10^{13}\,M_\odot$ halos. We quantify environment using the Perturbation Index (PI), which measures the cumulative tidal influence of neighboring galaxies relative to each galaxy's own binding force. We measure formation times from cumulative star formation histories and shapes from intrinsic stellar axis ratios. Formation times depend most strongly on stellar mass, but below $10^{10}\,M_\odot$, galaxies in more strongly perturbing environments formed their stars earlier, most clearly in the late-time star formation of low-mass galaxies. Galaxies in these environments also have larger minor-to-major axis ratios at all masses: more spherical at low and intermediate mass and thicker along the minor axis at high mass. A partial-correlation analysis shows that, below $10^8\,M_\odot$, the environment-shape correlation is no longer significant at fixed formation time, whereas between $10^8$ and $10^{10}\,M_\odot$ a modest correlation remains. Above $10^{10}\,M_\odot$, the weak environment-shape correlation is not explained by formation time. Below $10^{10}\,M_\odot$, high-PI galaxies also have lower halo masses at fixed stellar mass; at fixed halo mass, environment is only weakly correlated with formation time and shape, whereas formation time and shape remain correlated. We therefore predict that, at fixed stellar mass, dwarf galaxies in stronger tidal environments should appear rounder in projection, with the difference largely tracking the ages of their stellar populations.

7. Soft Excess Variability and a Test of X-ray Reprocessing in AGN Mrk 359 and NGC 4051 using NICER and TESS[2611.17439]
Abstract

Continuum reverberation mapping of AGN accretion disks assumes that X-ray reprocessing drives the UV/optical variations on short timescales. This reprocessing should also produce an X-ray reflection spectrum, which could be the soft excess observed in many Type 1 AGN. Consequently, if the soft excess traces the reflected emission, the soft excess and optical light curves should be correlated because their variability is driven by the same illuminating X-ray continuum. We present results from a cross-correlation study using simultaneous NICER and TESS observations of $\sim$$30\mathrm{~days}$ for two AGN: Mrk 359 and NGC 4051. We performed time-resolved spectroscopic fits to the X-ray data using a model with power-law and soft-excess components. We find no strong correlation between the optical and soft excess light curves, with correlation coefficients $R(\tau)$ $\sim$0.5 for both sources. The total X-ray light curve also shows a similarly poor correlation with the optical variations. However, we find a stronger correlation between the primary X-ray flux and the soft excess flux for both sources. The lack of correlation between the primary X-rays and soft-excess with the optical variations implies that simple linear X-ray reprocessing is not the dominant process driving the optical variability. Either an intervening reprocessor between the inner X-ray region and outer disc is present that dilutes the X-ray-optical correlation, or the optical variability is driven by other mechanisms such as extreme-UV reprocessing or intrinsic disk variability.

8. Insights into low-ionization structures in planetary nebulae: near-IR longslit spectroscopy[2611.17452]
Abstract

Context. Low-ionization structures (LISs) are small, dense clumps within planetary nebulae (PNe) that stand out in low-ionization emission lines, but the physical processes responsible for their excitation and the nature of H2 remain poorly understood. Aims. We aim to characterize the excitation conditions and mass content of LISs in two PNe through near-IR longslit spectroscopy of these structures, combining H2, Brg, and [Fe II] diagnostics. Methods. We obtained H and K band spectra with GNIRS at Gemini North for LISs in NGC 6818 and NGC 7354 –objects with confirmed H2 emission in their LISs. We applied H2 excitation diagrams, vibrational and rotational temperature diagnostics, classical near-IR diagnostic diagrams, and spatial profile analysis to constrain the physical conditions of the molecular and atomic gas phases. Results. H2, Brg, and [Fe II] emission are detected in all structures studied. The H2 level populations are consistent with predominantly thermal, near-LTE excitation, with excitation temperatures in the range  1860-2450 K. The [Fe II]/Brg ratios are well below the values expected for shock or X-ray dominated regions, favoring photoionization as the dominant excitation mechanism of the atomic gas. A clear spatial stratification is observed in both objects, with Brg peaking closer to the central star and H2 1-0S(1) and [Fe II] co-spatial at larger distances. The warm molecular masses derived from H2 emission lines are consistent with those estimated independently from H2 column densities. Conclusions. The diagnostics presented here support a photoevaporation scenario in which LISs are dense clumps being progressively ionized from outside by the radiation of central stars, while their H2 cores remain self-shielded. A slow internal shock may release Fe into the gas phase. These results extend the molecular mass trend established previously to a larger sample.

9. The ISM-CGM Transition of a Star-forming Dwarf Galaxy Resolved in Ionized Gas Emission[2611.17480]
Abstract

The circumgalactic media (CGM) of dwarf galaxies are expected to be tightly coupled to their star formation, as low virial temperatures imply a CGM rich in cold accretion and shallow potential wells allow for more effective feedback. In this work, we use Keck/KCWI and KCRM to spatially resolve the ionized gas at the boundary between the interstellar medium (ISM) and CGM in dwarf galaxy UGC 6578 (M* = 10^7.8 Msun), out to  0.1 Rvir. We discover a clear transition between the ISM and CGM at 0.033 Rvir, where the Halpha surface brightness profile changes from an exponential to a shallow power law, marking the first such detection at M*/Msun < 10^8. BPT diagnostics and ionization budget calculations indicate that the gas in the inner CGM is likely ionized by the galaxy's own star formation activity rather than shocks or the UV background. Spatially resolved emission maps also reveal a kinematically distinct, low-metallicity clump embedded in the inner CGM, and we discuss the possibility that this is a direct detection of ongoing gas accretion. This work serves as a pilot study for the Baryon Recycling in Dwarf Galaxies Emission Survey (BRIDGES), which will combine spatially resolved KCWI observations of the ISM-CGM interface in low-mass galaxies with wide-field observations of diffuse ionized gas out to the virial radius with the MOTHRA array.

10. Multipoles in the IllustrisTNG simulation galaxies and implications for Strong Gravitational Lens modelling in Time-delay Cosmography[2611.17538]
Abstract

Time-delay cosmography is a powerful independent probe of the Hubble constant ($H_0$), yet reaching the sub-2$\%$ precision required to resolve the Hubble tension demands a rigorous treatment of mass model systematics. We use realistic massive elliptical galaxies from the IllustrisTNG hydrodynamic simulation suite to characterise higher-order angular mass complexity in the form of multipoles ($m=1,3,4$) and quantify their impact on $H_0$ inference. We find that these multipole perturbations are common and emergent features of galaxy assembly; in 51$\%$ of our total samples ($54\%$ TNG100-1 galaxies and $85\%$ for TNG50-1 galaxies), the addition of multipole terms to a standard elliptical power-law model is preferred by the Bayesian Information Criterion. Ignoring these structures in lens modelling introduces systematic biases in $H_0$: for perturbations of $\sim2\%$, the biases are of the order of 1–2$\%$ for $m=1$, 4–5$\%$ for $m=3$, and 3–6$\%$ for $m=4$ multipole. While the $m=4$ (boxy/discyness) mode induces a predictable directional bias, odd-order modes ($m=1,3$) introduce significant stochastic scatter. We demonstrate a degeneracy where unmodelled internal multipoles can be effectively absorbed by the external shear component. This combined with low signal-to-noise observations can create “hidden biases” where formally perfect fits to imaging data ($\chi_\nu^2 \approx 1$) could harbour significant cosmological errors. Our results provide an astrophysical basis for multipole priors and highlight the necessity of moving beyond rigid parametric models to achieve high-accuracy cosmography in the era of \textit{Euclid} and LSST.

11. Unsupervised learning reveals a small number of galaxy formation pathways in IllustrisTNG[2611.17559]
Abstract

Understanding the main drivers of galaxy formation and the connection between observable properties and evolutionary pathways is a central goal of extragalactic astronomy. Observations provide single-epoch snapshots of galaxies at different stages of their development, whereas cosmological simulations allow us to trace the full formation histories of individual systems within a theoretical framework. Extracting information from these datasets remains challenging. Our aim is to use machine-learning techniques to unveil physical properties from the simulation datasets. We apply two unsupervised ML techniques, dimensionality reduction and clustering, to identify galaxy evolution key parameters. We use UMAP as our dimensionality reduction tool and k-means as our clustering method. We perform dimensionality reduction on the star formation histories (SFHs) of a sample of aproximately 10000 galaxies drawn from the TNG50 and TNG100 simulations. The resulting representation of the SFHs in a two-dimensional UMAP space reveals a clear ordering of galaxy mass assembly histories, separating quenched and star-forming systems, as well as galaxies with significant ex situ stellar mass contributions. Galaxy properties from simulation catalogs that are not used in the dimensionality reduction show correlations across the UMAP plane. Furthermore, using a k-means clustering analysis, we identify six groups of galaxies with distinct evolutionary histories. These groups show systematic differences in stellar mass, morphology, angular momentum, star formation activity, and merger history, and occupy different regions of the SFR-mass and mass-size planes. Our results indicate that the diversity of galaxy formation histories in IllustrisTNG can be organized into a small number of characteristic evolutionary pathways linking galaxy accretion histories and present-day structural and star-forming properties.

12. Hydrodynamic and photoionization analysis of a Cosmic Dawn dwarf galaxy based on a JWST-inferred star formation history[2611.17582]
Abstract

We investigate the evolution of the emergent spectrum of the Cosmic Dawn dwarf galaxy 2779 using coupled hydrodynamics and photoionization modeling. Evolutionary sequences of spherically symmetric, multi-component models track the star-formation history – derived from JWST data – from $z \approx 29.5$ to $9.5$. BPASS stellar population synthesis is coupled with the evolution of the superwind bubble and the surrounding nebular environment. The emergent spectra include escaping stellar radiation, nebular lines and continuum, as well as X-ray emission from the hot superwind cavity. At early stages, the nebular shell suppresses the escape of Lyman-continuum (Lyc) photons, while allowing X-ray emission to emerge. Significant Lyc escape appears near $z \approx 20$, followed by a non-monotonic evolution of the escape fraction ($f_{\text{esc}}$) and the X-ray luminosity per unit star-formation rate ($L_X/\text{SFR}$). Including diffuse ionizing radiation from the nebula can result in an effective escape fraction $f_{\text{esc}} > 1$. The model sequences terminate at a stage of stagnation or the complete displacement of the outer nebular component into a shell. A comparison with JWST photometry indicates that the final model spectrum generally reproduces the shape of the observed spectral energy distribution, although the overall flux level is too high. An illustrative comparison with a previous model having a lower stellar mass indicates that accounting for the evolving contribution of the nebula can reduce the stellar mass required to match observations. However, a more precise determination of stellar mass requires a self-consistent refinement of the star formation history. These models serve as a basis for constructing representative grids of models, as well as for conducting careful checks on whether dwarf galaxies could have driven the reionization of the Universe on their own.

13. Detectability limits of Ce abundances in Gaia RVS[2611.17591]
Abstract

Gaia DR3 provides medium-resolution Radial Velocity Spectrograph (RVS) spectra for millions of stars, but the extent to which they contain recoverable cerium (Ce) information across stellar parameter space remains unclear. We assess Ce detectability in Gaia RVS spectra and the reliability of DR3 Ce abundances ahead of the larger DR4 catalogue. We compute synthetic spectra across a stellar-parameter grid representative of the Gaia RVS sample. We combine the Cayrel equivalent-width criterion and the Cramér-Rao bound to map Ce detectability as a function of stellar parameters and signal-to-noise (SNR). We also quantify continuum-placement errors and compare the predicted detectability boundary with the Gaia DR3 GSP-Spec catalogue. Ce is reliably detectable primarily in cool giants at [Fe/H] $\gtrsim$ 0.5 dex and with at least [Ce/Fe] $\gtrsim$ 0 at an average SNR of 50, and the detectability region expands toward higher gravities at increasing SNR. Continuum-placement errors of $\gtrsim$1% become a significant source of bias, and at $\gtrsim$2% they completely prevent Ce detection. The criterion we present identifies where RVS Ce abundances, including those from DR4, can be robustly exploited. Outside our detectability region, these abundances should be interpreted cautiously, whether derived by GSP-Spec or by supervised methods. Currently, depending on the assumed continuum-placement uncertainty, between $\sim$40% and $\sim$70% of the published DR3 Ce measurements fall within this region; however, the rest are not reliable. Our findings are particularly important for metal-poor stars, for which the Ce lines in the RVS range are simply not detectable.

14. Observations of stellar feedback: tracers, diagnostics, measurements, and physical interpretation[2611.17592]
Abstract

Stellar feedback is central to galaxy evolution, yet the coupling of radiation, stellar winds, and supernovae to the multi-phase interstellar medium remains uncertain. This review treats this as a problem of inference, framed around four qualitative axes of ignorance: observational diagnostics are indirect; stellar population synthesis models must be ground-truthed before being used to infer feedback quantities; recent high-redshift observations have opened a new regime that may require re-calibration; and stellar feedback and the affected environment are interdependent. We examine the inference chain, from observables to physical quantities and from those quantities to how energy and momentum are transferred to the surrounding medium, across the principal diagnostics of stellar feedback: ionised gas, mechanically-driven bubbles, giant molecular cloud lifetimes, galactic outflows, and Lyman-continuum escape. We ask how much observations reveal about the link between the feedback-driving massive stars and the feedback-driven interstellar medium. We further discuss how stellar population synthesis assumptions, environment (e.g., metallicity, dust content), and galaxy properties (e.g., stellar mass, gas content) affect feedback measurements, and how high-redshift observations are testing calibrations established in the nearby Universe. The emerging picture is that interpreting a feedback measurement requires explicit control of the inference chain and of its physical context; feedback should therefore be contextualised, i.e. interpreted in terms of mechanism, gas phase, environment, and spatial and temporal scale. Progress will depend on measuring feedback-related quantities across the full range of star-forming systems and, more importantly, on obtaining observations (real, and synthetic from post-processed simulations) that match the same tracer and spatial scale across the same systems.

15. Calibrated Period-Luminosity Relations for Mira variables in the Large Magellanic Cloud in the Vera C. Rubin Observatory $zy$-band Photometric Systems[2611.17628]
Abstract

The Vera C. Rubin Observatory's Legacy Survey of Space Time (LSST) is expected to discover numerous Mira variables in nearby galaxies, enabling their application for the cosmic distance scale. Miras are excellent distance indicators at infrared wavelengths, and therefore their period-luminosity (PL) relations in reddest $zy$ photometric systems will be crucial in the LSST era. We present first empirical $zy$-band PL relation for 85 oxygen-rich (O-rich) Miras located in the Large Magellanic Cloud based on time-series observations collected using the Dark Energy Camera (DECam) at the Víctor M. Blanco 4-m Telescope. The DECam photometry was directly calibrated using the Rubin Observatory Data Preview 2 (DP2) data. The observed PL relations were found to exhibit a break around a period of $\sim 300$ days, and therefore we only derived and calibrated the PL relations for Miras with a period shorter than $300$ days. We also explored the Rubin LSST DP2 data for known Mira variables in three Local Group dwarf galaxies. The $zy$-band light curves of Miras in IC 1613 are too sparsely sampled, whereas the DP2 observations of NGC 6822 and the Sagittarius Dwarf Irregular Galaxy cover only a short temporal baseline. For an O-rich Mira in NGC 6822, we obtain a distance modulus $\mu = 23.32\pm0.24$ mag in good agreement with the literature. Significantly improved temporal coverage is expected in the first official Rubin LSST data release that will enable a robust validation of the PL relation in these nearby systems.

16. SN Progenitors of SNRs in OB Associations in the LMC. I. Assessment of Methodology Using N63A[2611.17665]
Abstract

Color–magnitude diagrams (CMDs) can constrain the progenitor masses of supernova remnants (SNRs), but mass estimates for massive stars are affected by limited temperature sensitivity, extinction, crowding, and nebular contamination. We use the Large Magellanic Cloud (LMC) SNR N63A and its OB association LH83 as a benchmark to evaluate CMD-based mass estimates. Among the tested diagnostics, the extinction-corrected MCPS $B$ vs. $U-B$ CMD provides the most useful constraint on the massive-star population. The $V$ vs. $B-V$ CMD is less reliable, while the reddening-free $W$-$Q$ diagram does not improve individual mass estimates because of its larger photometric uncertainties. Gaia EDR3 recovers bright stars missing or saturated in MCPS but provides weaker separation among the hottest stars. For seven spectroscopically classified stars, the $B$ vs. $U-B$ CMD-based masses are lower than the HR-diagram masses inferred using the same MIST tracks by $8.0~M_\odot$ on average, with a scatter of $7.0~M_\odot$. Isochrone comparisons suggest that most of the massive stars in LH83 formed about 7-15 Myr ago, complicating progenitor mass estimates based on the present-day mass function. Nevertheless, the masses of D243-1a and D243-2, stellar lifetimes predicted by the MIST models, and the empirical bubble-size-progenitor-mass relation suggest a progenitor mass of order 25-30 $M_\odot$. Our results highlight the potential and limitations of this approach for studying other LMC SNRs in OB associations.

17. Counter-rotating Ionized and Neutral Gas in a Star-forming Elliptical Galaxy[2611.17740]
Abstract

In early-type galaxies, gas that is kinematically misaligned with the stars generally rotates together across all gas phases, implying a single external origin. Using upgraded Giant Metrewave Radio Telescope (uGMRT) 21 cm imaging and SDSS-MaNGA spatially resolved spectroscopy, we studied MaNGA 10214-1902, a compact ($R_e = 0.78$ kpc), star-forming elliptical galaxy with stellar mass $\log M_*/M_{\odot} = 9.90$, in which the ionized gas counter-rotates with respect to the stars. It hosts $\log(M_{\rm HI}/M_{\odot}) = 9.65$ in a 45 kpc HI disk that co-rotates with the stars and has a central column-density depression. Inside this depression, the ionized gas ($r \lesssim 2$ kpc) rotates in the opposite sense to both the stars ($\Delta{\rm PA} = 152^\circ \pm 20^\circ$) and the HI that surrounds it ($\Delta{\rm PA} = 179^\circ \pm 10^\circ$). HI co-spatial with the ionized gas is not detected, so a fainter inner HI component with a different orientation cannot be ruled out. This central gas is star-forming in the [SII] and [OI] diagnostic diagrams and has EW(HI) $>6~\mathrm{\mathring{A}}$ at the center. The 1.4 GHz radio-continuum star formation rate agrees with the spectral energy distribution based value, showing no evidence of a dominant active galactic nucleus. The stellar population at the center is younger than the outskirts and has the lowest D$_{\rm n}$4000. These results are consistent with the acquisition of gas with opposite angular momentum into the center of a pre-existing HI disk that co-rotates with the stars, which can fuel the most recent central star formation.

18. Beyond the Inner Slope $γ$: A Schwarzschild-based Probe of the Core-Cusp Problem in the Milky Way Dwarf Spheroidal Galaxies[2611.17835]
Abstract

We present a spherical Schwarzschild orbit-superposition framework for constraining the dark matter density profiles and stellar orbital structures of the Milky Way dwarf spheroidal galaxies. We model the line-of-sight velocity distribution with a Gaussian mixture whose parameters vary smoothly with projected radius through a mixture density network trained on unbinned stellar velocities. This approach reduces the effect of statistical fluctuations inherent in velocity moments on their kinematic constraints. Their covariance and smoothing-induced correlations are included, and the halo parameters are sampled with Markov Chain Monte Carlo. Mock tests for this model show that the density profiles and slopes near the half-light radius are generally recovered well, although the inferred velocity anisotropy shows some systematic deviations. Applying the model to Sculptor, Draco, and Ursa Minor, we find that the enclosed mass $M_h$ and logarithmic density slope $S_h$ at the projected half-light radius are less sensitive to the adopted two different priors than the asymptotic inner slope $\gamma$, consistent with earlier studies. We further compare the inferred slopes at the three-dimensional half-light radius $r_h$ and $0.5r_h$ for a given stellar mass with FIRE-2 $\Lambda$ cold dark matter ($\Lambda$CDM) and self-interacting dark matter (SIDM) cosmological simulations including baryonic feedback. This comparison avoids introducing halo masses or virial radii that are not directly observable. The inferred slopes are broadly consistent with FIRE-2 $\Lambda$CDM predictions, although current uncertainties and the limited SIDM sample do not yet allow a robust distinction between dark matter models.

19. Euclid Data Release 1 (DR1). AstroVink-TREX: Automated strong gravitational lens segmentation as input for mass modelling pipelines[2611.17894]
Abstract

Strong gravitational lens modelling requires the lens light, lensed source light, and contaminants to be separated in each image before mass modelling codes can fit the system. Within Euclid this separation has so far been done by hand, which does not scale to the more than 15,000 candidates in the Euclid Lens Identification Pipeline and Scientific Exploration (ECLIPSE) catalogue of Data Release 1 (DR1). We present AstroVink-TREX , a neural network that performs this separation automatically and predicts three flux maps, in a standard format directly usable by mass-modelling pipelines. The network uses a hybrid architecture, pairing a vision transformer encoder reused from the lens detection network with a parallel convolutional branch. A custom loss function penalises both flux and structural error, while allowing independent weighting per component. From the network's predictions we also derive the modelling centre, fitting region, conjugate points, and binary maps, providing all the inputs that a modelling pipeline needs to run without manual setup. We evaluate the efficiency of the network on DR1 systems against existing fits and through expert validation. Across approximately 3000 inspected systems, taking 75% agreement between inspectors, just 39 lens models fail because of a segmentation error. Following this validation, the network provides the component separation and modelling setup for every system modelled in DR1. With automated segmentation, lens modelling is no longer limited by manual preprocessing and can now operate at the same scale as lens discovery.

20. Asteroseismology of blue straggler stars in $ω$ Centauri. Stellar collisions as a possible formation scenario[2611.17995]
Abstract

Context. Blue stragglers are intriguing stars that appear more massive and younger than expected from single-star evolution, yet their evolutionary histories remain elusive because of the multiple possible formation channels. A promising and poorly explored avenue for addressing this problem is asteroseismology, which provides the most stringent constraints on internal structure and evolution. Aims. We investigate five SX Phoenicis stars from the core of the globular cluster \omega Centauri that pulsate in two radial modes and occupy the blue-straggler region of the colour-magnitude diagram. Seismic models are constructed assuming a collisional origin and compared with our previous seismic studies based on single-star evolution. For comparison, we also explore a binary-evolution scenario involving mass transfer for one of the stars. Methods. The evolution of the parent stars is computed with MESA. The collision is then modelled with the MMAMS code, which reconstructs the merger remnant using an entropic variable. The subsequent evolution is followed with MESA up to the current age of the cluster. Finally, we construct seismic models reproducing the observed frequencies of the two radial modes. Results. We present the first seismic modelling of SX Phe stars (blue stragglers) based on the collision-formation scenario. Convective-core overshooting is essential for rejuvenation and for obtaining seismic models in the main-sequence phase of evolution, but it is not always sufficient. Whether a merger remnant undergoes rejuvenation depends in a complex way on the masses of the parent stars, metallicity, and collision time. We identify three evolutionary behaviours: no, delayed, and immediate rejuvenation. The collisional seismic models imply younger ages and higher masses than the seismic models based on single-star evolution.

21. WISE-VAULT: Decade-long Mid-Infrared Variability of WISE AGN[2611.17996]
Abstract

49. Restoring Consistency with Poynting's Theorem in Numerical MHD and RMHD[2611.18508]
Abstract

For perfectly conducting plasmas, Poynting's theorem states that the time rate of change of electromagnetic energy density equals the negative of the divergence of the Poynting flux. To the best of our knowledge, this involutive theorem has never been mimetically incorporated in codes. We show that there is an inconsistency in MHD and RMHD codes that evolve face-centered magnetic fields in globally-divergence-free fashion. This is because the face-centered flux of energy density, which incorporates a Poynting vector, is evaluated using one-dimensional Riemann solvers that are applied at face-centers. However, the face-centered magnetic fields are evolved according to edge-centered electric fields that come from two-dimensional Riemann solvers that are applied to the edges of a zone. While edge-centered electric fields are used to evolve the magnetic field, they have no influence on the face-centered Poynting flux. For situations where the magnetic energy is much larger than the thermal energy, this causes a negative pressure to be evaluated at the zone-centers. This paper restores consistency by using edge-centered variables to evaluate a face-centered Poynting flux. A simple accuracy-preserving correction is made to the face-centered flux of energy density. We show that it helps considerably in simulations that have very strong magnetic fields.

Abstract

Extremely large telescopes (ELTs) rely on segmented mirrors and thick support structures that fragment the pupil. In single-conjugate AO (SCAO), this produces differential pistons, or petal modes. The Pyramid Wavefront Sensor (PWFS), baseline for ANDES, MICADO, HARMONI, and GMT-NGSAO, has poor sensitivity to these modes in the visible under realistic residual turbulence. We propose the Minimum Mean Square Error (MMSE) Soft-Limiter, a deterministic, lightweight controller combining a subspace MMSE estimator derived from von Kármán priors with a continuous temporal regularization.
