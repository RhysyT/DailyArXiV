1. Testing the Universality of RAR Model with Constraints on Fermionic Dark Matter for M31[2611.24991]
Abstract

The Ruffini–Argüelles–Rueda (RAR) model describes dark matter as a self-gravitating system of massive fermions in thermodynamic equilibrium within general relativity. It suggests a dual morphology in galaxies: a highly degenerate quantum core mimicking a central supermassive black hole, and a classical halo that explains galactic rotation curves. Earlier applications of the model to the Milky Way restricted the fermion mass to $mc^{2}=48\text{--}345~\text{keV}$, and the orbits of the S-stars around Sagittarius A$^{\ast}$ (Sgr A$^{\ast}$) favored $mc^{2}=56~\text{keV}$, whereas a recent thermodynamic stability analysis allows only $mc^{2}\approx194\text{--}387~\text{keV}$. In this work, we apply the RAR model to the Andromeda galaxy (M31), which hosts a dark compact object of approximately $1.4\times10^{8}~M_{\odot}$ within $0.19~\text{pc}$, roughly 33 times the mass of Sgr A$^{\ast}$. We solve the relativistic equilibrium equations for $mc^{2}=6\text{--}60~\text{keV}$. For all these masses, the fermionic core lies within $0.19~\text{pc}$ and provides the central mass without a black hole. With a bulge and disk added, the total rotation curve is statistically acceptable ($\chi^{2}/N_{\text{dof}}\approx1.18$) but lies below the disk data at $3\text{--}11~\text{kpc}$, an offset attributed to the baryonic models we adopted. The Navarro–Frenk–White (NFW) and Burkert profiles fitted to the same data give a lower $\chi^{2}$ but require an additional black hole. The stability of the M31 core against gravitational collapse limits the fermion mass to $mc^{2}\lesssim60~\text{keV}$. With the earlier Milky Way range, a single fermion can therefore describe both galaxies only for $mc^{2}=48\text{--}60~\text{keV}$, which includes $56~\text{keV}$. With the thermodynamic range, no fermionic core can supply the central mass of M31, so M31 would have to host a black hole.

2. Eos: a merger-induced starburst at the dawn of Galactic low-$α$ disc formation[2611.25023]
Abstract

The last significant merger of the Milky Way, the Gaia-Sausage-Enceladus (GS/E), is thought to have reshaped the early Galaxy and to mark the transition from the high-$\alpha$ to the low-$\alpha$ disc. The stars formed in situ during this merger offer a direct record of that epoch but are hard to identify. Using APOGEE DR17 abundances, we study Eos, a population of in-situ low-$\alpha$ stars on halo-like, non-rotating, eccentric orbits. The metal-poor part of Eos has a nitrogen dispersion as large as that of the pre-disc Aurora population and up to 1.7 times that of the low-$\alpha$ disc at the same metallicity. Heating alone cannot produce this: the Splash, a disc heated onto halo orbits by the same merger and selected in the same way, shows no excess over its parent disc. This part of Eos was therefore born in poorly mixed gas. Eos is also chemically intermediate between the accreted GS/E debris and the coeval in-situ Milky Way stars, and stars with more GS/E-like chemistry move on more extended orbits. Its stars are older than the bulk of the low-$\alpha$ disc but younger than the high-$\alpha$ disc and the Splash. We compare these observations with a merger-induced starburst seen in an Auriga simulation. There, a GS/E-like merger funnels the stripped progenitor gas into a mixing lane of intermediate metallicity, where stars form with intermediate abundances, a large chemical dispersion and a chemodynamical gradient set by their birth site, reproducing the observed signatures of Eos. We therefore interpret Eos as the surviving relic of the GS/E merger-induced starburst, born largely from the partially mixed gas bridging the two galaxies and marking the onset of the Milky Way's low-$\alpha$ disc. Eos thus provides a direct observational view of the star formation and galaxy evolution driven by the Milky Way's last significant merger.

3. Top-heavy Initial Mass Functions are not enough at cosmic dawn[2611.25027]
Abstract

A top-heavy stellar initial mass function (IMF) has been proposed to explain the unexpected abundance of ultraviolet (UV)-bright galaxies at cosmic dawn. We test this possibility using the metallicity-dependent, top-heavy IMF recently proposed by Chon et al. (2024), which is motivated by simulations of metal-poor star formation. We compare this IMF with a Salpeter IMF, self-consistently including the associated changes in UV emission, supernova feedback, metal enrichment, and dust production. At fixed star formation rate, the Chon IMF enhances the intrinsic UV luminosity by a factor ${B}_{1500}\simeq4.7$-$2.8$ over halo masses $M_h=10^{8-12}\,M_\odot$. However, its larger supernova rate suppresses star formation and increases dust attenuation, reducing the net boost to ${B}_{\rm UV}\simeq2.0$-$1.06$. For halos relevant to the bright end of the UV luminosity function, the enhancement is only ${B}_{\rm UV}\simeq1.2$-$1.4$, or about $0.3$ mag. Calculations with the DELPHI semi-analytic model confirm that the self-consistent Chon and Salpeter UV luminosity functions differ by less than $0.5$ dex at $z\simeq11$-$17$. Thus, a top-heavy IMF alone cannot explain the bright galaxy excess. Conversely, the predicted pair-instability supernova abundance differs by factors of $2$-$6$ between the two IMFs, providing a potentially cleaner observational diagnostic of the high-mass stellar populations at these early epochs.

4. Influence of variable IMF on the dynamical black hole mass estimates in massive ETGs[2611.25035]
Abstract

Several studies have shown that the stellar initial mass function (IMF) in massive early-type galaxies (ETGs) is not universal. Recent results from stellar population synthesis models, based on integral-field unit (IFU) observations, indicate that the IMF varies radially, becoming increasingly bottom-heavy towards galaxy centres. This radial variation alters the stellar mass-to-light ratio ($\rm M_{*}/L$) and can significantly affect dynamical black hole (BH) mass estimates in massive ETGs. In this study, we investigate the impact of a radially varying IMF on BH mass estimates for five massive galaxies: NGC3923, NGC4261, NGC4636, IC4296, and IC4329. Using DYNAMITE, a triaxial Schwarzschild orbit-based modelling code, we perform mass modelling of these galaxies based on MUSE and SINFONI IFU observations. Our models incorporate radially varying stellar $\rm M_{*}/L$ profiles derived from spatially resolved stellar population modelling with a free IMF, and we compare the resulting BH masses with those obtained from dynamical models assuming a constant stellar $\rm M_{*}/L$. All galaxies in our sample show bottom-heavy IMFs in their central regions, with the IMF generally becoming lighter towards larger radii. We obtain constrained BH masses for NGC3923 and IC4329, while only upper limits are obtained for NGC4261, NGC4636, and IC4296. Compared with the constant-$\rm M_{*}/L$ models, the variable-$\rm M_{*}/L$ models yield BH masses lower by approximately 83% and 11% for NGC3923 and IC4329, respectively, while the BH mass upper limits for the other galaxies can increase or decrease. Our results demonstrate that spatial variations in the IMF and stellar $\rm M_{*}/L$ can have a non-negligible but strongly galaxy-dependent impact on dynamical BH mass measurements. These results highlight the importance of accounting for such variations in future dynamical modelling efforts.

5. Twisted resonant orbits: Quantifying the deceleration of the Galactic bar[2611.25039]
Abstract

Recent structural discoveries in Gaia Data Release 3 reveal distinct, chevron-shaped over-densities in the radial phase space of the halo. Although initial interpretations identify these features as the phase-mixed relics of a past galaxy merger, their prominent asymmetry and persistence at high metallicities strongly challenge this interpretation. These features are better explained as a dynamical response to a Galactic bar, where stars are trapped in resonances. We establish the fractional $v_r$ asymmetry of the chevrons as a quantitative diagnostic tool for the bar's rotational state. We utilise test-particle simulations of a Milky Way-like galaxy to model various bar pattern speed evolutions, including decelerating and constant speed scenarios. By mapping the fractional asymmetry of the phase-space chevrons across a parameter space of bar deceleration rates and pattern speeds, we establish a direct correlation between the bar's deceleration and the rotational response of the resonant orbits. Comparing these simulated constraints with observational data from Gaia DR3 provides a framework for measuring the current deceleration of the Galactic bar, and a probe of the amount of dynamical friction occurring between the baryonic and dark matter content of the Milky Way. We find a dimensionless deceleration parameter $\eta \equiv - \dot{\Omega}_b/\Omega_b^2 = 0.0015^{+0.0016}_{-0.0009}$, equivalent to $\dot{\Omega}_b = -2.23^{+1.50}_{-2.46} \, \text{km s}^{-1} \text{kpc}^{-1} \text{Gyr}^{-1}$, in agreement with previous estimates obtained with independent methods. Gaia DR4 should sharpen this measurement considerably.

6. Ram Pressure Is Unable to Strip All Phases of the Interstellar Medium out of Spirals Falling into Galaxy Clusters[2611.25049]
Abstract

Following the expansion of the Universe, spiral galaxies may fall at large speeds into galaxy clusters. Their interaction with the intracluster medium (ICM) may remove part of their gas and reduce their capacity for making new stars, as envisaged by Gunn & Gott. Ram pressure stripping (RPS) is commonly invoked to explain this gas loss and the wakes or tails left behind in-falling galaxies. Here we revise the impact of RPS on individual dense clouds and particularly focus on turbulent viscous stripping (TVS), driven by Kelvin-Helmholtz instabilities at the cloud-ICM interface, that affects the high-density and low-temperature molecular gas. For an extended gaseous body to decelerate, momentum from its exposed surface must be communicated into the gas by a hydrodynamic disturbance able to propagate through it. Three-dimensional hydrodynamic calculations follow this interaction with dense clouds. At cloud number densities $n_{\mathrm{cl}}=10^2$ cm$^{-3}$, ram pressure acting through the transmitted shock strongly compresses and deforms the cloud and produces only a small deceleration; at $n_{\mathrm{cl}}=10^3$ cm$^{-3}$, the slower transmitted shock produces much weaker deformation. At both cloud densities, TVS continuously removes gas from the cloud surface and forms qualitatively indistinguishable trailing wakes, with comparable mass-loss rates, suggesting that TVS acting on individual dense clouds can generate filamentary substructure within jellyfish-galaxy tails.

7. Kinetic Tomography with Diffuse Interstellar Bands in APOGEE DR17[2611.25084]
Abstract

Context: Mapping gas flows in the interstellar medium is key to understanding the formation of structure in our Milky Way. Methods that simultaneously reconstruct clouds' distances and line-of-sight velocities are called kinetic tomography (KT). Diffuse interstellar bands (DIBs) are powerful tracers for such maps because they trace the integrated dust density and the dust-weighted velocity. Previous DIB-KT works had small samples with coarse kpc-scale resolution and could not capture vertical disk structure. Aims: Make 4D (3D position + radial velocity) maps of the Milky Way from new, high-quality 15,273 Å DIB detections in APOGEE DR17. Methods: We combine variational inference and Gaussian processes with a mixture likelihood for low signal-to-noise detections and marginalization over distance uncertainties. We calibrate the model on mock fields and spectral injection tests and validate the map against independent tracers of the dust density (3D dust maps) and velocity substructure (masers and young stellar clusters). Results: We recover a posterior distribution over 3D maps of the density and velocity structure of the 15,273 Å DIB within 6 kpc of the Sun. We measure the large-scale structure of the DIB disk (scale length, height and flare) and detect vertical distortions consistent with the warp. On smaller scales, the map resolves distance ambiguities in gas longitude-velocity diagrams. From the second-order velocity structure function, we measure a slope (0.64 $\pm$ 0.04) consistent with Kolmogorov turbulence, amplitude consistent with expectations for molecular clouds, and a preliminary ratio of compressive-to-solenoidal modes. Conclusions: With larger DIB catalogs expected from SDSS-V, this framework should resolve kinematic substructure at the scales needed to constrain the formation and dynamics of individual clouds and voids. 8. Off-Nuclear AGN Signatures in MaNGA: Spatially resolved selection using MEGACUBES[2611.25085]
Abstract

We present a spatially resolved search for off-nuclear active galactic nucleus (AGN) candidates in the MaNGA survey. Using megacubes, we analyze over 10,000 galaxies, separating AGN activity from diffuse ionized gas with refined equivalent-width and surface-brightness thresholds. Combining a spatial radial algorithm with BPT, WHaN, and WHaD diagnostics, we classify 261 nuclear AGNs, 201 off-nuclear AGNs, and 159 'quiet' (composite-spaxel-dominated) sources. We test whether off-nuclear AGNs reside in distinct host galaxies or whether the offset is a local, gas-driven phenomenon. Nuclear and off-nuclear AGN hosts are statistically indistinguishable in stellar mass, age, star formation rate, and gas-phase metallicity. However, the excitation and kinematics of the AGN-ionized gas differ significantly: nuclear AGN spaxels have [O III]$\lambda5007$ luminosities $\sim 0.7$ dex higher than off-nuclear spaxels, whose lower [O III]/H$\beta$ ratios indicate genuinely lower-excitation gas rather than an obscured view of the central engine. We also find an orientation trend: 72% of nuclear hosts are face-on, versus 50% of off-nuclear hosts, so projection effects contribute to, but do not fully explain, off-nuclear detections. The classical WHaN boundary at $\log$([N II]/H$\alpha$) = -0.4 misclassifies high-metallicity diffuse ionized gas in integral field data; an empirical shift to -0.25 removes over 48,000 spurious AGN spaxels. Finally, the off-nuclear fraction implies an episodic AGN phase lifetime of $\sim 5 \times 10^4$ yr, consistent within a factor of a few with independent fading-AGN estimates and supporting short, recurrent accretion bursts.

9. A novel pathway to stellar-mass black hole growth: Re-accretion of gas from stellar wind bubbles[2611.25110]
Abstract

The formation of high-mass stellar black holes (BHs) observed in mergers by gravitational wave observatories remains difficult to explain using standard models of stellar evolution. We investigate whether newly formed stellar-mass BHs in dense molecular clouds can significantly increase their final mass by accreting gas from pre-existing, wind-blown interstellar bubbles produced by their progenitor stars. We performed 1D and 3D hydrodynamic simulations of interstellar bubbles driven by stellar feedback, incorporating thermal conduction, radiative cooling, gravity, ionizing radiation, stellar winds, and supernova (SN) explosions. We modeled the subsequent accretion onto the central BH for two formation scenarios: core-collapse SN and direct collapse. Our parameter space study encompasses over 500 1D simulations, varying initial cloud densities, gravitational binding energies, progenitor masses ($40-257\,M_\odot$), and metallicities ($0.66\,Z_\odot$ and $0.016\,Z_\odot$), and tracks the bubble evolution for millions of years post-BH formation. Scenarios exhibiting significant re-accretion were further validated using high-resolution 3D simulations. Our simulations show that BHs formed via direct collapse from low-metallicity progenitors can accrete up to $6.9\,M_\odot$ of gas from massive inner shells formed by late-stage slow winds, resulting in final BH masses of up to $93\,M_\odot$. Conversely, we find no significant re-accretion in scenarios involving an SN explosion or high-metallicity progenitors; in these cases, fast winds or SN ejecta efficiently evacuate dense structures from the bubble interior, leaving behind a hot, low-density medium. [abridged] This mechanism may help explain the unexpectedly high masses of BHs observed in gravitational wave events.

10. Multiphase Gas in High-$z$ Cosmic Sheets[2611.25220]
Abstract

We present high-resolution cosmological simulations of individual cosmic sheets ($z=3-6$) using the moving-mesh code AREPO to study cold gas ($T\approx10^4\rm ~K$) in the diffuse cosmic web. We find cosmic sheet gas forms a fragmented morphology, evident as kiloparsec-scale variations in the neutral hydrogen column density ($N_{\rm HI}$). Fragmentation arises from the bending and disruption of the cold-dense sheet core into a multiphase medium. Nonlinear thin shell instability (NTSI) and later shear-induced vorticity drive this bending, rather than previously suggested thermal or Richtmyer-Meshkov instabilities. Higher resolution enhances fragmentation. Across a fourfold range in spatial resolution, $N_{\rm HI}$ covering fractions increase by factors of $\approx 1.5-3.5$ for partial Lyman-limit systems (pLLS) and Lyman-limit systems (LLS). An order-of-magnitude estimate suggests sheets may host a large fraction of the ultra-low metallicity (p)LLS population. Equivalent MP-Gadget simulations show qualitatively similar fragmentation, but numerical smoothing effects are present. Cosmic-sheet gas properties remain unconverged despite our high resolutions.

11. The HI Mass Fractions of Milky Way Analog Samples[2611.25221]
Abstract

We estimate average HI mass fractions, f_HI = M_HI/M_star, for nine Milky Way analog samples (MWAs): galaxies selected on similarity to the MW in terms of various properties. We find most (seven) MWAs have lower <f_HI> than a recent estimate for the MW, although only the two largest have significantly lower estimates. We suggest that either the MW has an unusual f_HI for its properties, or we are overestimating its HI content, one of many challenging global measurements to make from our perspective inside our own Galaxy.

12. Sherlock 'Chrones: The Curious Case of NGC 6791 and Asteroseismic Targets Therein Reveals Mixing Length Dependence on Evolutionary Stage[2611.25306]
Abstract

As single-aged populations containing stars spanning a range of masses but otherwise with similar properties, star clusters provide valuable testbeds for stellar evolution theory. The old, metal-rich, open cluster NGC 6791 is well studied, but the availability of asteroseismic observations of individual cluster members allows for a more in-depth analysis. Driven by mounting evidence that the solar assumption for convection used in almost all 1D stellar models is not adequate, and in light of the increased precision provided by modern data and asteroseismic constraints, we built and compared custom isochrones with varying values of the convective mixing length, $\alpha_\text{MLT}$, to two clusters with well-determined ages: NGC 6791 and the well-studied, metal-poor globular cluster M92. For both clusters, we found that the best-fitting mixing length parameter decreases monotonically as a function of evolutionary phase, finding values between $0.98-1.10\alpha_{\odot}$ for NGC 6791 and $1.16-1.21\alpha_{\odot}$ for M92. In NGC 6791, this corresponds to an 18% decrease in the best fitting mixing length between the main sequence turn-off and upper end of the red giant branch. The degree of variation found within this one object is therefore comparable to variation across the literature in $\alpha_\text{MLT}$ fits for other non-solar targets, calling single-valued $\alpha_\text{MLT}$ fits to clusters into question. It is also consistent with $\log g$-$\alpha_\text{MLT}$ trends predicted by higher-fidelity, 3D convection analyses. Our results therefore illustrate the need for more flexibility in our treatment of the mixing length in isochrones to properly model clusters.

13. The Cosmic Infrared Background from Population III.1 Supermassive Stars and Black Holes[2611.25355]
Abstract

We investigate the cosmic infrared background (CIB) that is expected to arise from the Population (Pop) III.1 supermassive black hole (SMBH) formation scenario, which involves early SMBH seeding via supermassive primordial star formation enabled by dark matter annihilation heating. We model the emission from Pop III.1 supermassive stars, including their growth phase, flash ionization phase, and early black hole accretion phase, as well as accompanying emission from lower-mass Pop III.2 stars. The CIB is composed of stellar and SMBH accretion disk continuum radiation, nebular continuum emission, and Ly$\alpha$ radiation redistributed through scattering in the neutral intergalactic medium. Based on the feedback regulated Pop III.1 model of Petkova et al. (2026), our fiducial model assumes $\sim 10^5\,M_\odot$ Pop III.1 stars forming by $z_{\rm flash}=20$ with a comoving number density of $\sim0.2\,{\rm cMpc}^{-3}$. For this model, the combined stellar contribution is less than $1\%$ of existing direct and diffuse CIB measurements. However, the contribution to the CIB from $z\sim6-20$ SMBH accretion is expected to be larger than the Pop III.1 stellar component for average Eddington ratios $\lambda_{\text{Edd}} \sim 0.01$. Latest CIB observations place constraints of $\lambda_{\text{Edd}} \lesssim 0.3$ for the fiducial Pop III.1 model during these epochs.

14. Dark Matter Model Implications of a Candidate Sub-halo Near the Sun, and the Data Needed to Discriminate Among Them[2611.25359]
Abstract

Pulsar-timing accelerations point to a localized mass concentration near the Sun, consistent with a dark matter sub-halo. We work out what such an object implies for the nature of dark matter, organizing the implications by their observable effect: suppression or enhancement of structure formation, internal structure and survival after formation, and emission or scattering by the constituents. We discuss ultralight wave dark matter, thermal and nonthermal warm dark matter, dark acoustic oscillations and dark matter-baryon scattering, elastic and inelastic self-interactions, invisible decays with recoil, primordial black holes, indirect detection, and a possible connection to the LUX-ZEPLIN high-energy recoil candidate. We then ask what new data would discriminate among these models. The existing accelerations are measured outside the tidal radius, where they are blind to the interior mass distribution, to the object's velocity, and to the sub-halo population. We identify four routes that lift these degeneracies: accelerations measured inside the halo, which separate point-mass, cuspy, and cored profiles; lensing and accretion, which test whether the mass is compact; the morphology of annihilation or decay emission, which probes the inner profile; and a search for a comoving stellar population, which tests whether the object is dark and measures its velocity. We also contrast this program with pulsar-timing-array searches for transiting dark matter substructure, which probe vastly lighter objects through a different observable.

15. The non-equilibrium state of the Galactic disk $-$ new insights from GALAH DR4[2611.25804]
Abstract

The non-equilibrium state of the Galactic disk has drawn extensive attention since ESA's \textit{Gaia} launch. Complex structure appears across a multi-parameter space of stellar abundances, ages, and kinematics. Using GALAH DR4, we investigate ridge and phase-spiral structures in chemical-abundance, phase ($v_i,x_i$), action–angle ($J_i,\theta_i$), and frequency–action ($\Omega_i,J_i$) spaces ($i=R,\phi,z$). Our sample includes 333,412 disk stars with reliable chemodynamical data. In the $R$–$v_\phi$ plane, ridges trace near-constant $J_\phi$ tracks and show coherent variations in $\theta_R$ and $\theta_\phi$. In $J_\phi$–$J_R$, ridges are most prominent when encoded by $z_{\rm max}$ and preferentially associate with metal-rich, low-$\alpha$ populations. In the solar neighbourhood ($R=7.2$–$9.2$ kpc), we recover a clear phase spiral in $z$–$v_z$. Encoded in $v_\phi$, $\Omega_R$, or $\Omega_\phi$, one-armed spirals appear, whereas the $v_R$-encoded map shows a two-armed spiral. Projecting actions onto $z$–$v_z$ reveals residual spiral signatures in $J_R$ and $J_\phi$, indicating that the phase spiral encodes in-plane orbital information. Both structures depend on age: ridges remain at similar locations with varying contrast, while the phase spiral is more tightly wound in younger and more loosely wound in older populations. Several ridges correspond in $J_\phi$ to known moving groups, and excluding stars in all six moving-group regions makes the phase spiral less distinct in mean radial velocity maps. These links support interpreting ridges, phase spirals, and moving groups within a common phase-space dynamical framework. Combined with trends in orbital properties, age, and abundance, these findings suggest a common dynamical origin and distinct chemodynamical responses of a perturbed Galactic disk.

16. The origin of the apparent ULX deficit in NGC 3256[2611.25857]
Abstract

Ultra-luminous X-ray (ULX) sources are bright, off-nuclear X-ray point sources, mostly associated with high-mass X-ray binaries (HMXBs), with a compact object accreting from a donor star at super-Eddington luminosities. Although their HMXB nature implies a connection to star formation processes, previous works have found that luminous infrared galaxies (LIRGs) appear to have a deficit of ULXs compared to their very high star formation rates (SFRs). Here, we analyze three Chandra observations of NGC 3256, a merging LIRG with SFR  50-60 Msun/yr. Based on local scaling relations, it could be expected to host between 100-120 individual ULXs, which could have a non-negligible impact on the energetics and feedback processes of the galaxy. We aim to characterize all its detected X-ray point sources to determine if there is a ULX deficit in this galaxy. We also aim to explore the potential impact of host-galaxy obscuration and metallicity in said deficit. We characterize the luminosity of each X-ray point source in each Chandra observation, and use all observations to determine their potential ULX nature and the fraction of transient ULXs, as well as construct a ULX luminosity function for this galaxy. We find that the galaxy hosts 10-15 ULXs, making it one of the most ULX-rich galaxies known to date. While comparison with the ULX/SFR ratio of nearby galaxies predicted as many as  100 ULXs, we show that, when accounting for its very high metallicity, NGC 3256 does not have a ULX deficit; and may even present an excess. While the high obscuration in LIRGs has previously been invoked to explain the ULX deficit, we conclude that it is not required to explain the ULX to SFR ratio in NGC 3256. If obscuration still plays a role, then LIRGs may actually be significantly more efficient at producing ULXs than normal galaxies (of the same metallicity), in disagreement with previous results.

17. Neutral hydrogen in the Shapley Supercluster core II. Gas content and star formation in the filaments connecting the core[2611.25871]
Abstract

We investigate the neutral atomic hydrogen (HI) content of galaxies across the Shapley Supercluster (SSC) at z = 0.048 by combining new deep MeerKAT L band observations of five filament pointings with archival MeerKAT Galaxy Cluster Legacy Survey observations of the supercluster core. Together with optical data from the Shapley Supercluster Survey, these observations provide a homogeneous view of galaxy evolution from the virialised cluster environment to the surrounding filaments. We compare the HI content, star formation activity, gas depletion timescales, and morphological properties of galaxies relative to the gas fraction and star forming main sequences. The global HI detection fraction increases from 30 per cent in the cluster centre to 45 per cent in the filaments. While HI detected galaxies in the cluster centre are dominated by transition zone systems, the filament population is shared nearly equally between star forming main sequence and transition zone detections, indicating ongoing environmental transformation. Their gas properties also differ: cluster galaxies exhibit progressively depleted HI reservoirs towards the red sequence, whereas filament galaxies remain primarily gas rich. Within the filaments, long HI depletion timescales exceeding the Hubble time are concentrated in specific populations, notably 71 per cent of transition zone and 57 per cent of gas rich detections, indicating the inefficient conversion of atomic gas into stars. The HI detected filament population is dominated by late type galaxies, while the underlying non detected population is primarily composed of early type systems, consistent with environmental pre processing before cluster infall.

18. Magnolia: a highly magnetized galaxy at redshift $z\simeq9$[2611.25937]
Abstract

The theory of galactic magnetic field growth via dynamo action predicts that the interstellar medium (ISM) of early galaxies would remain virtually unmagnetized on large scales. However, recent observations of strong dust polarization in galaxies at redshifts $z\simeq2.6$ and $z\simeq5.6$ challenge this paradigm. If galaxies indeed host strong, large-scale, coherent magnetic fields at high-$z$, then galaxy evolution and current theories of magnetic field amplification in galaxies should be revisited. To address the full physical complexity of early magnetic field growth in galaxies, we have designed MAGNA SERRA, a suite of radiation-magneto-hydrodynamical (RMHD) cosmological zoom-in simulations with non-equilibrium chemistry. In this first paper from the MAGNA SERRA suite, we present Magnolia, a galaxy hosted in a $\simeq 10^{11}~\mathrm{M}_\odot$ dark matter halo simulated with RAMSES-RT with a $\simeq 10^4~\mathrm{M}_\odot$ mass ($\simeq 10 \,\mathrm{pc}$ spatial) resolution. We find that, at $z\simeq9$, Magnolia has a stellar mass of a few times $10^8~\mathrm{M}_\odot$ and is strongly magnetized, reaching a magnetic field of a few $\mu G$ and a plasma beta $\beta\simeq 0.1$ in the densest regions. This strong amplification occurs over a timescale of about 40 Myrs due to the highly dynamical nature of the galaxy, which creates strong compressions and shear. The power spectra of the magnetic and kinetic energies and the spectral energy transfer functions do not suggest dynamo action, but rather an initial magnetic field amplification through a large-scale compression, and a later re-organization of this field from small to large scales. Our findings show that strong galactic magnetization is possible at very high redshifts, which means that magnetic fields can play a dominant role throughout a galaxy's evolutionary history. 19. From a mass invariant in a gravo-turbulent medium to the characteristic mass of the star-forming cores[2611.26162]
Abstract

The origin of the observed universality, or, say, the limited variations, of the stellar initial mass function (IMF) and its precursor, the prestellar core mass function (CMF), and thus of the characteristic mass of stars and cores, across a wide range of different galactic environments remains an open, fundamental problem in astrophysics. In this paper, we demonstrate that we can explain this property from gravity and turbulence only, using a mass time-invariant that remains valid for a broad class of turbulent flows throughout the star formation process. This mass invariant is entirely determined by the large-scale normalisation of the field of density fluctuations in the Galactic Interstellar Medium (ISM), induced by turbulence. Using high-resolution numerical simulations, we first verify that the temporal invariance of this quantity is preserved in a self-gravitating turbulent medium. Then, introducing a self-consistent model of the statistical properties of the density field in the self-gravitating multiphase turbulent ISM, we show that the universality of the characteristic mass of star-forming cores is imposed by this invariant together with the scaling of the Larson relations. Our model successfully predicts the characteristic mass of the cores in Milky Way-like star-forming regions, as well as in more extreme environments such as high-mass star-forming regions and massive early-type galaxies. This scenario suggests that the characteristic mass of the CMF, and then probably the characteristic stellar mass, originates from a universal, fundamental property of turbulence and is imprinted both by the very early stages of the star formation process in the gravo-turbulent interstellar medium, when the dynamics of the medium are still dominated by turbulence, and by the statistical properties of small-scale self-gravitating structures.

20. The Silk Nebula Revisited: A Comment on Wu et al. (2026) and New Constraints from Archival Survey Spectroscopy[2611.26243]
Abstract

Wu et al. (2026) used one LAMOST MRS-N plate to suggest that the Silk Nebula, a $17^\circ$-long \Oiii $\lambda$5007 filament in Gemini, is possibly associated with a supernova remnant at a fiducial distance of $\sim$160 pc. We test this against their published values and independent archival data. The \Ni\ airglow in their blue-arm spectrum lies at air, topocentric rest wavelengths, so their \Oiii\ velocities omit the heliocentric correction and are $10.1\pm1.0$ \kms\ too high. Corrected and combined with DESI DR1 and SDSS spectra, \Oiii\ lies at \vhel\ $=+13.3\pm1.0$ \kms, the velocity of \Nii\ and of the local warm ionized medium (WIM). Their \Ha\ velocities are 4.5–6.7 \kms\ redder than \Nii. Their tabulated distances are in kpc, not units of 100 pc, and kinematic distances carry no information in this direction.
