1. A Positive Turn: Coupled Quintessence and Cosmological Neutrino Masses[2611.14584]
Abstract

In $\Lambda$CDM, the CMB and DESI BAO upper limits on the total neutrino mass are in tension with the lower bounds from oscillation experiments, and analyses allowing negative effective masses favor negative values. We investigate whether a coupled quintessence (CQ) model in which dark matter interacts with a canonical scalar field $\phi$ through an exponential mass coupling $m_{\rm c}(\phi)\propto e^{-\beta\phi/M_{\rm Pl}}$ can alleviate this tension. Combining CMB, DESI, and supernova data, we find that CQ yields neutrino-mass constraints comparable to those obtained in CPL and consistent with oscillation experiments within $2\sigma$ for normal ordering, without requiring the scalar-field equation of state to cross the phantom divide. We further show that it alleviates the tension more effectively than alternative interacting dark energy models that instead favor more negative effective masses. This comparison shows that whether dark sector interactions can relax neutrino mass bounds ultimately depends on the interplay of their effects on primary CMB anisotropies, geometry, growth, and lensing.

2. Early-Excess SNe Ia Show Strong C II Absorption[2611.14618]
Abstract

Some type Ia supernovae (SNe Ia) exhibit an early excess in their rising light curves, but the physical origin of this behavior remains uncertain. The processes driving these excesses affect the ejecta at early times and may also imprint on the outermost layers. We investigate whether early excesses are associated with differences in unburned material by measuring pre-maximum carbon absorption. Using multi-component Gaussian fits to the blended Si II $\lambda6355$ and C II $\lambda6580$ region, we measure pseudo-equivalent widths and velocities for a sample of 35 nonpeculiar SNe Ia with rising light curve categorizations. We find that early-excess objects ($n=13$) tend to exhibit stronger C II absorption than non-excesses ($n=22$) at phases earlier than approximately $-13$ d, with a two-sample K-S test of the earliest measurements yielding $p=0.025$. In contrast, the HV Si II components do not clearly separate the two groups. Early-excess SNe Ia tend to be more luminous and decline more slowly, but objects with similar maximum-light properties span a range of C II pseudo-equivalent widths, suggesting that this effect is distinct from the known luminosity-width relation. We show that, at least in some cases, when objects have similar light-curve shape and photospheric temperature, the effect we see is not driven by ionization but by distinct differences in the explosion, such as carbon abundance and density and the efficiency of burning in the WD. Our results provide a new constraint on SN Ia explosion models for early excesses, as they must produce a tendency for stronger early C II absorption.

3. Robustness of the Baryonic Acoustic Oscillation Peak Position against Monopole Fluctuations in Galaxy Surveys[2611.14619]
Abstract

Precise measurements of the baryonic acoustic oscillation (BAO) peak position are one of the key goals in modern galaxy surveys. We investigate the impact of the monopole fluctuations in galaxy surveys on the BAO peak position in the galaxy two-point redshift-space multipole-correlation functions. The background mean density of a galaxy sample is estimated from the survey itself by averaging the observed galaxy number density, and this averaged number density (monopole) is subtracted to obtain the observed galaxy number density fluctuation, which includes an additional contribution from the monopole fluctuations, propagating to galaxy clustering analysis. We compute the contributions of the monopole fluctuations to the redshift-space multipole-correlation functions. While the monopole contributions in galaxy two-point correlation function are anisotropic and non-negligible in amplitude, the contributions are so smooth around the BAO peak position that they are almost completely absorbed in the BAO broadband model, leaving the BAO peak position unaffected at the sub-percent level. Our results demonstrate the robustness of the BAO analysis against the unknown systematic error.

4. Type Ia-CSM Supernovae. I. Uniform Light-Curve Characterization and Inference on the Underlying Type Ia Explosions[2611.14620]
Abstract

We present the largest uniform study to date of 50 thermonuclear Type Ia supernovae interacting with circumstellar medium (SNe Ia-CSM). As the first paper in a series delineating the population properties of this class, this work focuses specifically on a uniform analysis of their light curves. Our results show that SNe Ia-CSM display a significantly more uniform light-curve morphology than interacting core-collapse SNe IIn, closely resembling the luminous-slow subgroup. The Ia-CSM sample spans approximately an order of magnitude in peak luminosity, total timescale, and radiated energy, with a median optical peak luminosity of $\approx2\times10^{43}$ erg s$^{-1}$ ($\approx-20$ mag). We find a distinct bimodality in the radiated energy distribution: a predominant population of energetic events with $\approx 2\times10^{50}$ erg and characteristic timescales of $\approx100$ days, and a secondary population that is roughly an order of magnitude less energetic with shorter timescales of $\approx50$ days. Decomposition of the luminosity contributions from underlying SN Ia and CSM interaction by the Ia-CSM spectral classification reveals a diverse range of underlying SN Ia subtypes spanning from luminous 1991T-like to faint 1991bg-like. Both SN Ia and CSM properties are broadly consistent with the core-degenerate channel resulting from common-envelope evolution followed by core merger, where the presence of dense CSM is dictated by the delay time between the common-envelope phase and the thermonuclear explosion. Subsequent papers in this series will utilize numerical simulations, semi-analytical modeling, spectroscopy, and host-galaxy environments to further constrain the progenitor and explosion pathways to SNe Ia-CSM.

5. A self-shadowing black hole accretion disk at sub-Eddington luminosities[2611.14626]
Abstract

Accretion onto black holes powers some of the most energetic sources in the Universe, yet its behaviour across the luminosity range remains poorly understood. Specifically, as the luminosity approaches the Eddington limit ($L_{\rm Edd}$), the inner accretion disk is expected to depart from the standard thin-disk solution, becoming vertically extended. The luminosity threshold and observational signatures of this transition remain, however, uncertain. We present multiwavelength spectroscopy obtained during the decay of a black hole X-ray binary outburst. At the peak ($\sim 0.2\,L_{\rm Edd}$), a cool optical and near-infrared spectrum dominated by strong recombination lines of hydrogen and He I is observed. However, as the luminosity decreases, it switches to a hot, highly irradiated, nearly featureless spectrum. This puzzling behavior reveals the emergence of a vertically extended inner disk at the outburst peak, shielding the outer regions from high-energy radiation produced near the black hole; a scenario supported by contemporaneous X-ray spectra. Observations of similar systems suggest that this phenomenon may be common among black hole accretion disks. Self-shadowing structures at luminosities well below $L_{\rm Edd}$ have major implications for black hole accretion flows and their observational properties, affecting inferred black hole spins, X-ray polarization measurements, and the physical conditions of the outer disk and its associated outflows.

6. Impact of dynamical, quadratic, and higher-order tidal effects on gravitational-wave observations of binary neutron stars[2611.14628]
Abstract

Neutron-star binaries are prime gravitational-wave sources for current and next-generation detectors, and provide a unique probe for the behaviour of nuclear matter at supranuclear densities. Extracting information about this requires accurate waveform models that capture tidal effects during the late inspiral. We incorporate state-of-the-art tidal corrections into a post-Newtonian inspiral waveform, including dynamical tides and nonlinear contributions from quadratic Love numbers through eighth post-Newtonian (8PN) order. Using Bayesian model comparison, we find that these corrections can significantly affect the gravitational-wave signal, with the 8PN tidal response becoming comparable to lower-PN tidal contributions in the subsolar-mass regime. For exceptionally loud GW170817-like binaries observed by third-generation detectors such as the Einstein Telescope, higher-order tidal effects can be distinguished with high statistical significance, while neglecting them can introduce substantial biases in the inferred tidal deformabilities and, consequently, in constraints on the neutron-star equation of state. These corrections are particularly relevant for putative subsolar-mass binaries: the lower compactness of their constituents enhances tidal effects and makes them potentially important even for current detectors. Our results highlight the importance of higher-order tidal corrections for precision gravitational-wave measurements of dense nuclear matter.

7. Observational constraints on CO sequestration and disk mass in the planet-hosting PDS 70 disk[2611.14632]
Abstract

Exoplanets accrete gas, dust, and ice from their natal environment, setting the chemical composition of their atmospheres and interiors. The abundance of the major volatiles in disks can be used to possibly link exoplanet atmospheres to their formation location in the disk. In this work, we aim to quantify the peak CO abundance and disk mass of the PDS 70 disk, the first disk with a direct confirmation of two accreting planets in its dust cavity. We present the first detection of N$_2$H$^+$ and C$^{17}$O in this disk. The C$^{17}$O emission shows marginal statistical evidence for an azimuthal asymmetry trailing that previously seen in the continuum emission. We then compare the disk integrated fluxes of N$_2$H$^+$, CO isotopologues, and the continuum to an existing grid of thermochemical models. We find that the derived disk mass drops by a factor of $\sim 3.4$ from $0.039^{+0.067}_{-0.018}$ M$_{\odot}$ to $0.011^{+0.011}_{-0.006}$ M$_{\odot}$ if the C$^{17}$O line flux is included in the fitting procedure; the quoted intervals do not include systematic uncertainties. CO on the other hand is sequestered with an effective abundance of $(4.8^{+4.3}_{-2.7} - 17^{+8}_{-8}) \times 10^{-6}$ relative to H$_2$ in the probed disk layers after accounting for CO freeze-out and isotope selective CO photodissociation for the adopted chemical network and ionization prescription. The range in peak CO abundances reflects the uncertainty in the N$_2$H$^+$ emission, assuming either optically thin emission at 21 K or optically thick emission. Therefore, the PDS 70 planets are possibly accreting the final portion of their atmospheres from the outer disk molecular layer where CO is sequestered. Thus, CO sequestering may need to be considered when interpreting the chemical composition of planetary atmospheres.

8. Signatures of CH$_4$ and NH$_3$ in the atmosphere of $β$ Pictoris b[2611.14646]
Abstract

$\beta$ Pictoris is a nearby (19.7 pc), young ($\sim$ 24 Myr), bright ($V = 3.85$) planetary system hosting three directly imaged planets. Previous high-resolution spectroscopy of $\beta$ Pic b has detected H$_2$O and CO, but not CH$_4$ or NH$_3$. Combining observations across a broad wavelength range has been shown to maximise sensitivity to such weak molecular signals. We searched for previously undetected molecular species in the atmosphere of $\beta$ Pic b using high-resolution near-infrared spectra. We combined 44 CRIRES+ spectral settings covering the H, K, L, and M bands and cross-correlated the extracted planetary spectra with molecular templates. For species with uncertain detectability, we weighted the observations by their molecular information content and data quality, independent of the measured candidate-species cross-correlation functions. We recover H$_2$O and CO at cross-correlation S/N values of approximately 200 and 50, respectively. We additionally identify tentative CH$_4$ and NH$_3$ signals near the expected radial velocity of $\beta$ Pic b, with S/N values of approximately 5.3 and 5.7. Equivalent extractions at the same projected separation on the opposite side of the star show no comparable peaks. We classify the CH$_4$ and NH$_3$ signals as tentative because their cross-correlation S/N values do not fully account for possible residual systematics. If confirmed, these would be the first detections of both species in the atmosphere of $\beta$ Pic b.

9. Natal Kicks of Compact Binaries: I. Population Synthesis Framework and Illustrative Examples[2611.14649]
Abstract

The natal kicks imparted to neutron stars and black holes at birth are critical for understanding massive-star evolution, compact-object formation, and compact binary merger rates. However, constraining these kicks is challenging, since they cannot be directly observed. Here, we outline a framework for inferring natal kick probability distributions for compact binaries. Our method embeds the rapid binary-population code COSMIC in a Monte-Carlo workflow that evolves ensembles of simulated binaries. From those which host compact objects, we can select subsets that match the observational characteristics of any given system (e.g. component masses, orbital period, mass-transfer status, systemic velocity). As a proof-of-concept, we apply this framework to six compact binaries spanning a wide range of companion masses and compact-object types. For each system, we identify the evolutionary channel(s) that could have led to the current observational characteristics, and isolate regions of allowed natal kicks and progenitor parameter space. We reconfirm that at least one black hole system, XTE J1118+480, must have received a natal kick $v_{\rm NK} > 100$ km s$^{-1}$. The neutron-star system, GX 301-2, is also inferred to have received an impulsive kick exceeding 280 km s$^{-1}$. We discuss the strengths and limitations associated with population-synthesis-based inference methods and explore the effects of adopting different prescriptions for critical binary evolution phases. We provide a detailed demonstration of the non-linear relationship between $v_{\rm NK}$ and $v_{\rm sys}$, highlighting the shortcomings of relying on observations alone to measure natal kicks. The inferred kick velocity distributions across our benchmark sample are generally consistent with previous constraints.

10. Hydrolyzed Hazes on Water-rich Exoplanets: Composition and Prebiotic Chemistry[2611.14666]
Abstract

Although photochemical hazes complicate the spectroscopic analysis of sub-Neptunes, the very presence of hazes can teach us about the habitability of these planets. Aside from affecting a planet's atmospheric and surface dynamics, hazes offer a source of organics potentially including prebiotic molecules like amino acids, nucleobases, and sugars. These organics are particularly compelling for temperate, water-rich planets where hazes may react with liquid water in condensate clouds or surface reservoirs. Hydrolysis reactions drive molecular reorganization and oxygenation, increasing the chemical complexity of the prebiotic inventory. This water-based evolution of hazes in their environment is largely unexplored and will have consequences for their chemical and optical behavior. In this work, we examine the chemical evolution of temperate water-rich exoplanet haze analogs in liquid water. High-resolution mass spectrometry finds thousands of CHNO molecules with bonding behaviors and chemical complexities distinct from the unaltered haze analogs. The oxygen weight percent increases significantly in the soluble fraction and moderately in the insoluble fraction after hydrolysis, with different carbon bonding structures demonstrating the redistribution of functional groups. The hydrolyzed samples exhibit a broader distribution in H/C and O/C ratios, and the molecular formulas suggest complex carbohydrate chemistry that challenges current theories of prebiotic organic delivery.

11. On the Excitation of Sloshing Oscillations in Hot Coronal Loops[2611.14676]
Abstract

Observations of hot coronal loops have often shown the presence of compressive plasma oscillations. These oscillations are usually preceded by a flare-associated localised brightening in the vicinity of one of the footpoints of the coronal loop. The flare heating produces an impulsive injection of plasma into the coronal loop, along with a plasma perturbation that bounces back and forth between the two footpoints, termed as sloshing oscillations. While the association of these oscillations with flares is apparent, the pertinent connection between their excitation and the flare characteristics remains poorly understood. In this study, using data from the Atmospheric Imaging Assembly (AIA) onboard the Solar Dynamics Observatory (SDO), we present a unique set of observations, where the response of a single coronal loop due to repeated flares in the same active region is evident. Our analysis reveals that in three out of five flare events, sloshing oscillations are triggered, while in the other two events, only plasma flows are observed. By examining the duration of plasma injection associated with each flare, we find that sloshing oscillations occur when the injection duration is shorter than the travel time of the plasma perturbation across the loop. In contrast, no oscillations are observed when the injection duration is comparable to the travel time. These results reveal the dependence of sloshing oscillation excitation on the temporal characteristics of flares.

12. CosmoSim: Lens-parameter estimation of the Einstein Cross NGC 6505 using machine learning[2611.14678]
Abstract

CosmoSim is an open-source tool for simulating gravitational lenses and reconstructing lens mass distributions, either by ray tracing a parametric lens model or through a Roulette expansion of the lens potential. It is also used to generate the large, labelled synthetic image sets needed to train machine-learning (ML) parameter estimators. We present the first application of CosmoSim to real data. A convolutional neural network trained only on CosmoSim images estimates Singular Isothermal Ellipsoid lens and source parameters directly from observed images. We test the pipeline on 21 images of 19 strong lenses from Euclid and the AGEL survey, after removing the foreground lens light with a custom decontamination tool built on AstroPy. As a case study we reconstruct the Einstein Cross NGC 6505 both from the lens model published by the discovery team and from parameters predicted by our ML model. Both reconstructions recover the observed four-image structure, and the ML-based reconstruction performs at least as well as the literature-based one: it scores better on masked structural similarity, intersection-over-union and Chamfer distance, with an essentially equal Hausdorff distance. Across the ensemble, the predicted Einstein radii are consistent with catalogued values within the quoted uncertainties (mean relative difference 0.08 for Euclid and 0.19 for AGEL lenses), and the enclosed masses agree to within a median of 0.12 dex. The predicted ellipticities differ systematically from the catalogued values, while the comparison of orientations is inconclusive. These results establish CosmoSim as a tool that can be applied directly to observational data.

13. Natal Kicks of Compact Binaries: II. Neutron-star and black-hole systems share similar kick distributions[2611.14680]
Abstract

Natal kicks - kinematic impulses to neutron stars and black holes at birth - play a decisive role in the end-stage evolution of binaries hosting massive stars, in determining the global phase-space properties of compact objects, and in setting expected merger-rates detectable through gravitational-wave observations. Inferring the kick ($v_{\rm NK}$) for individual systems requires well constrained binary system properties and centre-of-mass kinematics, which must then be modelled to constrain likely system characteristics at supernova. As a result, population-wide studies of kicks remain few and far between. Here, we model plausible evolutionary histories for 68 accreting binaries, encompassing almost all black-hole and neutron-star X-ray binaries with 3D kinematics measurements, constraining allowed $v_{\rm NK}$ distributions for each source using a simulation framework presented in our previous paper. The resultant $v_{\rm NK}$ distribution for the sample can be simply represented by a broad Gamma distribution with mean $v_{\rm NK}$ = $128^{+17}_{-15}$ km s$^{-1}$ and scatter $\sigma$ = 117 km s$^{-1}$. Intriguingly, the distributions for black-hole vs. neutron-star systems are found to be formally indistinguishable. Despite still being limited by small-number statistics, we find that black holes can receive large natal kicks, and that kicks for all remnant types span similar ranges (0-700 km s$^{-1}$). There is a hint, but no statistically significant evidence at present, for $v_{\rm NK}$ scaling inversely with black-hole mass. Our work suggests either that similar physics drives kicks and shows evidence for effects beyond simplistic fallback-moderated scaling for black holes, or that selection effects conspire to erase differences between remnant types in current accreting-binary samples. Larger samples are needed to provide robust discriminatory tests between these possibilities.

14. Dwarfs of Fire and Ice: UV-Irradiation-Driven Thermal Inversion, Disequilibrium Chemistry, and a Cloud-Free Atmosphere in a Strongly Irradiated Brown Dwarf Probed by JWST/NIRSpec Phase-Resolved Spectroscopy[2611.14703]
Abstract

We present time-resolved JWST/NIRSpec PRISM spectrophotometry of the white dwarf (WD)-brown dwarf (BD) binary SDSS J141126.20+200911.1 (SDSS1411). Close WD-BD binaries provide powerful laboratories for studying the effects of stellar irradiation on substellar atmospheres because of their high companion-to-host flux ratios, short orbital periods, and intense UV irradiation from the WD. We measure strong wavelength-dependent variability, with day-night relative flux variations reaching  280%. The SDSS1411B phase curves are well described by a second-order Fourier model, and correlations among the Fourier amplitudes suggest larger flux and temperature variations in the upper atmosphere. Non-irradiated forward models under chemical equilibrium do not reproduce the observed phase-resolved SDSS1411B spectra, indicating substantial departures from isolated-BD atmospheric structures. In contrast, atmospheric retrievals successfully reproduce both the dayside and nightside spectra. The retrievals reveal a prominent dayside temperature inversion, favor cloud-free atmospheres, and provide evidence for CO2 disequilibrium chemistry. An energy-budget analysis indicates low day-to-night heat redistribution efficiency and constrains the BD cooling age to  3 Gyr, while the inferred BD radius is significantly more compact than predicted by BD evolutionary models. These results reveal the rich atmospheric dynamics and disequilibrium chemistry of a UV-irradiated BD and demonstrate the power of full-orbit JWST phase-resolved spectroscopy for constraining the atmospheric physics of strongly irradiated substellar objects.

15. The Closest Super-Eccentric Giant Planet: Dynamical History and Orbital Future of Gaia-3b[2611.14714]
Abstract

With new high-resolution spectra and radial velocities (RVs) from the NEID spectrometer, we reaffirm the planetary designation of the previously identified super-eccentric giant planet Gaia 3b. Our additional 23 RVs span 356 days, including an RV observation obtained within hours of the periastron passage. By modeling all of the available RVs we collapse a previously tri-modal orbital period posterior distribution and report a more precisely measured value of $P=331.52\pm0.02$ days. Our measure of the eccentricity ($e=0.9462\pm0.0017$) is in agreement with previous measurements and thus corroborates the status of Gaia 3b as the third most eccentric planet known to date. We generate synthetic realizations of the Gaia astrometric time series and derived RUWE to show that only the planetary-companion and close to equal-mass binary cases are plausible when compared to the reported RUWE. We then show that the close to equal-mass binary case is unlikely based on the Gaia color-magnitude position and the shape and amplitude of the bisectors measured from the NEID cross-correlation functions. The confirmation of Gaia 3b's planetary status makes it the closest known super-eccentric giant planet ($d \approx 35$ pc) and leads us to anticipate that Gaia DR4 will detect more super-eccentric planets orbiting nearby Sun-like stars. Finally, due to the presence of a distant stellar companion, we demonstrate that a plausible pathway exists for excitation of the planet's eccentricity by von Zeipel-Kozai-Lidov oscillations.

16. The Gaia-alerted 2024–2025 brightening of V346 Nor[2611.14723]
Abstract

Young stellar objects (YSOs) build their final mass through a highly unsteady, episodic accretion process, often accompanied by outflow and ejection events. Objects undergoing episodic high-accretion states are known as eruptive YSOs. V346 Nor is an embedded eruptive YSO that underwent a sudden optical brightening in 2024 July that triggered an alert from the Gaia Photometric Science Alert system. With the aim of characterizing the physical processes behind this recent brightening event, we analyze new and archival photometric and spectroscopic monitoring data. We study how the brightness, colors, and spectral features changed with time in the past decade. We also fitted an accretion disk model to the observations in which the stellar mass–accretion rate product $M_{\star}\dot{M}$ and the line-of-sight extinction $A_V$ are free parameters. The accretion rate in V346 Nor has been higher ($M_{\star}\dot{M} \sim 10^{-4}\,M_{\odot}^2\,$yr$^{-1}$) since 2021 than during any previous historical peak, although it was relatively constant during the 2025 February-May brightness maximum, which was driven instead by a drop in extinction from $A_V \approx 20$ mag to $10$ mag. V346 Nor has accreted about 2\% of the stellar mass since its outburst. A P Cygni profile in H$\alpha$ indicates a strong outflowing wind reminiscent of classical FUors. The variability of the CaII infrared triplet lines and transient CO bandhead emission point to a highly unstable inner disk and accretion region. The persistence of forbidden line emission indicates ongoing jet activity. The brightness and color variations of V346 Nor are the result of a superposition of changes in the accretion rate and the line-of-sight extinction. With record-high accretion levels and strong wind and jet activity, V346 Nor is in one of its most active phases since its original outburst in the 1970s.

17. Origins of Satellite-System Building Blocks I: Solid Delivery During Jupiter's Growth[2611.14733]
Abstract

Jupiter's satellites formed within a circumplanetary disk (CPD), but the origin and delivery of the solids from which they accreted remain uncertain. Here, we investigate the delivery of planetesimal material to the CPD while consistently accounting for the growth of Jupiter and the evolution of its surrounding planetesimal reservoir. Using N-body simulations coupled to an evolving CPD model, we quantify the solid mass supplied through planetesimal ablation and direct capture. We find that, although proto-Jupiter rapidly opens a gap in the planetesimal disk, planetesimals continue to enter the CPD for more than 1 Myr. Under efficient ablation, the mass deposited in the CPD can reach approximately 1 Me, about one order of magnitude higher than the mass delivered through direct capture. However, the low efficiency of pebble accretion within the CPD implies that forming the Galilean satellites from ablated material alone may require a very large pebble reservoir. Directly captured planetesimals, despite contributing less total mass, can instead provide satellitesimals that grow through mutual collisions and serve as seeds for subsequent pebble accretion. Our results therefore favor a hybrid formation pathway in which the Galilean satellites grow through a combination of satellitesimal and pebble accretion. We further demonstrate that even a planetesimal reservoir containing less solid material than the minimum-mass solar nebula can supply sufficient building blocks to form the Galilean satellites. The balance between the ablation and direct capture depends sensitively on the efficiency of aerodynamic heating: if ablation is weaker than commonly assumed, direct capture can become the dominant source of satellite building blocks. These results connect the formation of Jupiter and its satellites by showing how Jupiter's growth regulates both the duration and form of solid delivery to its CPD.

18. General Relativistic and Non-Inertial Effects on Magnetic Reconnection Rates[2611.14740]
Abstract

We develop a gauge-independent theory of magnetic reconnection beyond the standard special-relativistic framework of inertial systems in flat spacetime. Our definitions of reconnection and its rate are independent of reference frame and coordinate system, allowing physical effects of gravity and net linear acceleration to be distinguished from coordinate effects. We extend the mesoscale theory of Liu et al. [PRL 118, 085101 (2017)], in which the reconnection rate depends on physics both within the diffusion region and in an upstream ideal, force-free region. The theory identifies three non-inertial and curved-spacetime (NICS) effects, including gravitationally induced magnetic tension: gravitational distortion of the reconnection plane changes the curvature of magnetic field lines. Applying the theory to a simplified accretion plasma near )a compact object, we find that (1) whether gravity increases or decreases the reconnection rate depends on the system's orientation relative to the gravitational source, with the greatest increase occurring when the outflow jet points toward or away from the source; and (2) the magnitude of the gravitational effect scales with the dimensionless parameter $\bar{\kappa} \equiv r_s\Delta y^2/r^3$, where $r$ is the Schwarzschild radial coordinate, $r_s$ is the source's Schwarzschild radius, and $\Delta y$ is the mesoscale length.

19. Turbulence Properties of Corotating Interaction Regions at 1 AU: a Comprehensive Superposed Epoch Analysis[2611.14779]
Abstract

We perform a comprehensive superposed epoch analysis (SEA) of more than 200 corotating interaction regions (CIRs) using WIND spacecraft observations at 1 AU. The stream interfaces are identified by Minimum Variance Analysis (MVA), and turbulence properties are evaluated using wavelet transforms over a wide range of temporal scales. The analysis of normalized cross helicity ($\sigma_c$) and normalized residual energy ($\sigma_r$) reveals distinct turbulence behaviors across frequencies. The spectral indices of both magnetic and velocity fluctuations smoothly transition from steeper in the slow wind to shallower in the fast wind, while a localized steepening of the velocity spectra near the interface indicates enhanced dissipation due to compression. Across broad frequency bands, $\sigma_c$ shows a clear dip at the stream interface, signifying increased inward Alfvén wave energy, whereas $\sigma_r$ displays a peak-valley-peak structure mainly driven by large-scale velocity shear. In lower-frequency ranges, velocity shear artificially enhances velocity fluctuation energy, producing strong peaks in $\sigma_r$, while higher-frequency ranges show a smooth increase of $\sigma_r$ from slow wind to fast wind. Nearly half of the analyzed CIRs are accompanied by a heliospheric current sheet (HCS), with many HCSs closely aligned with the stream interface, suggesting an intrinsic link between the two structures. Our findings offer valuable clues for reconciling discrepancies among earlier observational and simulation studies, and provide new insight into how compression and velocity shear modulate solar wind turbulence near CIRs.

20. Self-interacting Dark Matter signatures from stars extremely magnified by galaxy clusters[2611.14800]
Abstract

The spatial distribution of cluster-lensed star transients with respect to the macroscopic critical curve (CC) is sensitive to underlying substructures predicted by different classes of dark matter (DM) models. \citet{Broadhurst_2025} have shown that wave-like DM composed of ultra-light bosons better explains both the width and negative skewness (towards the interior of the CC) of the lensed star spatial distribution discovered in the “Dragon” lensed galaxy ($z = 0.73$) in the Abell 370 (A370, $z = 0.375$) cluster field, compared with the case of regular cold dark matter (CDM) subhaloes. In this work, we consider the case of self-interacting DM (SIDM) and investigate how the lensed star spatial distribution depends on the velocity-dependent self-interaction cross-section of SIDM particles, and whether constraints can be placed on the cross-section strength at relevant velocities. Establishing a cross-section strength-dependent (sub)halo gravothermal core-collapse probability, we found that the SIDM appears to be the only known class of models thus far in which the sign of skewness is set by its own particle parameters. The observed lensed star distribution in the Dragon galaxy can be well explained, in terms of both skewness and width, when a significant fraction of subhaloes have undergone core collapse and exhibit a steep power-law density slope. Given the cluster redshift, this qualitatively favours an (effective) cross-section strength of $500 -1000\,\textrm{cm}^{2}/\textrm{g}$ for halos with mass $10^{6} - 10^{8}\,M_{\odot}$ (velocity dispersion of $\sim 1-10\,\rm{km}/\rm{s}$), when the role of environmental effects on the core-collapse (de)acceleration is not considered, and limits the three-dimensional inner density slope of the collapsed halo to $\Gamma \gtrsim 2.5$ (Bayes factor of $\sim 10^{2}$).

21. A Taxonomic Characterization of Targets in the Emirates Mission to the Asteroid Belt[2611.14813]
Abstract

The Emirates Mission to the Asteroid Belt (EMA) will investigate seven main-belt objects to improve our understanding of the distribution and evolution of water and metal-rich materials within the asteroid population. 22. Earth accreted increasingly oxidized material as it grew constraining Theia to be small and relatively oxidized[2611.14842]
Abstract

Many different Solar System formation scenarios successfully reproduce the main astrophysical properties of the inner Solar System. Here, we use the chemistry of Earth's mantle to determine whether proposed Solar System formation scenarios can produce Earth and to constrain the characteristics of both the Proto-Earth and the Moon-forming impactor, Theia. To do this, we use a coupled N-body accretion and core-mantle differentiation model (Rubie et al., 2011, 2015b) applied to a range of proposed formation scenarios (Classical, Annulus, and Grand Tack scenarios). This model calculates the mantle chemistry of Earth-like planets in Solar System formation simulations and adjusts the initial redox state of the building blocks in a synthetic protoplanetary disk until the mantle of the simulated Earth closely matches the bulk silicate Earth. We find that a strong oxidizing gradient among the initial solids of the protoplanetary disk is favored to reproduce the chemistry of Earth's mantle, in agreement with Rubie et al. (2015b). Abstract

The cooling of neutrons stars, which are composed of ultra-high-density nuclear matter, strongly depends on the equation of state because the dominant cooling sources are neutrinos produced in their interiors. Here, we investigate the cooling properties of neutron stars, focusing on effective nucleon masses, whose impacts have been recently examined in supernova explosion and proto-neutron star cooling. 193. Active learning at a graduate summer school: feasibility, student response, and the role of instructor training[2611.13583]
Abstract

Active learning is becoming standard practice in undergraduate STEM instruction in North America and is slowly being adopted in graduate courses. Graduate summer schools, one- to two-week programs in which students from many institutions gather for intensive, specialized instruction in their field, are common in physics; they are taught by researchers chosen for their expertise, and their pedagogy remains almost entirely traditional lecturing. We report a study at a one-week graduate school in neutrino and astroparticle physics attended by 38 students from 8 countries. Six lecturers with limited or no prior experience of teaching with active learning introduced Peer Instruction-style active learning into their lectures after approximately four hours of preparation led by a physics-education specialist. Each of the three main lecture streams was taught traditionally on one day and in the active format on the other. Students were surveyed after each of the first two days and at the end of the week; all six instructors were surveyed as well. In every active lecture, spanning three instructors and three topics, 82-100% of respondents reported that the interactive segments helped them stay engaged and check their understanding, and in every stream the difficulty was rated as better matched on the active day. At the end of the week, 33 of 36 wanted such teaching in future graduate courses, and not one respondent disagreed. All six instructors judged the approach productive and intend to use it again; only two found implementation straightforward. With brief expert-led preparation, lecturers new to active learning implemented it successfully for a heterogeneous audience, and both students and instructors want more. The school was also the first time any of the six instructors implemented structured active learning: a summer school can develop its lecturers as well as its students.
