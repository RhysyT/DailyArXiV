1. Identity Crisis: Untangling Mass-Ordering Artifacts from Compact-Object Pairing in Gravitational-Wave Populations[2610.18962]
Abstract

Mass distributions of binary black hole mergers inferred from gravitational-wave observations offer clues to how binaries form. These distributions conventionally label the heavier component as the primary, with mass $m_1$, and the lighter as the secondary, with mass $m_2$, such that $m_1 \geq m_2$. This ordering folds the physically labeled mass distribution along the equal-mass line, potentially reshaping the underlying population structure. In this Letter, we determine which population features can arise from mass ordering and which require genuine astrophysical structure. We show that ordering alone can produce (1) an $m_2$ distribution that declines more rapidly than the $m_1$ distribution, without intrinsic differences between the physically labeled components, and (2) an apparent concentration along equal masses, without correlations that preferentially pair black holes of equal mass. Moreover, different physically labeled populations can produce the same ordered mass distribution. Crucially, however, a preferred companion mass that remains constant as $m_1$ increases requires a characteristic mass scale already present in the physically labeled population. Applied to binary black hole mergers in the fifth Gravitational-Wave Transient Catalog, our model recovers such a scale near $m_2 \simeq 50\,\mathrm{M}_\odot$. Around this scale, the merger-rate density declines much more rapidly toward higher $m_2$ than toward lower $m_2$, ruling out an equal or weaker high-$m_2$ decline at 99.45% credibility. This structure therefore requires a characteristic mass scale in at least one physically labeled component, but masses alone cannot determine which component carries it. Any formation model reproducing the observed high-mass binary black hole merger population must contain such a scale.

2. The Second Catalog of Low-Energy Fermi-LAT Sources[2610.18972]
Abstract

We used 14 years of Fermi-Large Area Telescope (LAT) data to derive a catalog of sources detected between 20 MeV and 200 MeV. For each source we provide localization, spectral, and association information. The Second Catalog of Low-Energy Fermi-LAT sources (2FLE) contains 450 sources detected at $TS>25$, with median energy flux of $F_{20-200 \, \text{MeV}} = 1.9\times10^{-11}$ erg cm$^{-2}$ s$^{-1}$. Using a Bayesian and likelihood-ratio method, we associate 377 2FLE sources with a known counterpart. Of the 325 objects associated with extragalactic sources, the dominant source class represented in the 2FLE are flat-spectrum radio quasars (FSRQ). Additionally, 52 sources are associated with Galactic sources. Three associated sources have not been previously detected in any $\gamma$-ray catalog and represent new $\gamma$-ray sources of known origin. We find that 387 2FLE sources have a positional cross-match with the 4FGL-DR4 sources. For the sources with a 4FGL counterpart, we compare the spectrum of the 2FLE source to the 4FGL spectral energy distributions. Of the 73 2FLE sources without multi-wavelength associations, 13 have a 4FGL counterpart that lacks classification, while 60 are newly discovered $\gamma$-ray sources. Among these 60 sources, 51 are found at high latitudes ($|b|>10^{\circ}$), and 38 are detected at $>10\sigma$. Additionally, we find that out of the 450 2FLE sources 45 are TeV emitters, and 6 are spatially coincident with a 1LHAASO source. The 2FLE helps us pave the way for reducing the “MeV gap", and provides the most sensitive catalog between 20-200 MeV to date.

3. Audible curvaton: modulated phase transition as the origin of large-scale curvature perturbations and gravitational wave anisotropies[2610.18975]
Abstract

We propose a post-inflationary first-order phase transition modulated by a light spectator field as the origin of primordial large-scale curvature perturbations. Spectator fluctuations acquired during inflation modulate the bubble nucleation history, generating curvature perturbations together with correlated intrinsic gravitational-wave (GW) anisotropies. We show that this mechanism can reproduce the observed amplitude of curvature perturbations while producing a loud stochastic GW background in the frequency bands of space-based detectors such as LISA, Taiji, and TianQin. A concrete benchmark yields strongly enhanced intrinsic GW anisotropies and a local primordial non-Gaussianity signal within reach of future large-scale-structure surveys.

4. Thermodynamically consistent equation of state tables for use in giant planet and brown dwarf interior modeling[2610.18982]
Abstract

Equation of state (EOS) tables used in giant planet modeling exhibit regions of strong thermodynamic inconsistency, or disobedience to the first law of thermodynamics. These generally result from smooth interpolation over physical discontinuities or between thermodynamic sub-domains governed by different physical models. The effect of thermodynamic inconsistency on structure models of planets evolved with these EOS has never been studied in detail, although it is understood to introduce spurious sources or sinks of energy or entropy in the models, leading to inaccuracies. Here, we present a method for improving the thermodynamic consistency of an existing EOS table, based on constructing a Helmholtz free energy $F(\rho,T)$ covering the EOS domain and deriving all the other necessary EOS quantities from it. This method produces EOS tables with roughly thousandfold better thermodynamic consistency across the EOS domain of interest, as quantified by three thermodynamic consistency metrics derived from the first law. We also present a controlled experiment comparing giant planet models evolved with MESA using the original EOS and the $F$-derived, thermodynamically consistent versions, and find that the models can differ by up to $5\%$ in radius at an age of 5 Gyr, and by up to $25\%$ in luminosity at earlier stages of evolution; thermodynamic consistency is thus an especially important consideration for characterising young directly imaged planets. We present the $F$-derived, thermodynamically consistent versions of the tables for download at \href{ this https URL }{ this https URL }, along with example MESA inlists to reproduce our evolution models.

5. Scalar-Assisted Growth of Supermassive Black Holes: Boson-Mass Windows and Observational Constraints[2610.18986]
Abstract

We study a phenomenological cosmological model of black-hole growth in which a finite baryonic reservoir and a bosonic scalar cloud feed the black hole simultaneously. The baryonic component accretes through an Eddington-like channel, while the scalar cloud transfers mass through an effective quasibound-state decay rate. Evolving the system directly in redshift within a flat $\Lambda$CDM background, we scan over $\mu$, $M_0$, $M_{c,0}$, and $M_{b,0}$ to assess whether scalar-assisted accretion can accelerate the formation of $10^9M_\odot$ supermassive black holes. We find that the scalar contribution is highly selective in parameter space. For the mass scales explored here, scalar transfer is most efficient for $\mu\sim10^{-19}-10^{-16} \mathrm{eV}$, producing rapid cloud depletion and significantly earlier black-hole growth, while outside this interval the evolution remains mainly baryonic. More generally, this efficient window reflects a preferred range of the dimensionless coupling $\alpha=\mu M_{\rm BH}$ and therefore shifts approximately as $\mu\propto M_{\rm BH}^{-1}$. Comparison with observed quasar masses and luminosities instead favors lower boson masses, broadly around $\mu\sim10^{-21}-10^{-20}\mathrm{eV}$. These results are not necessarily in tension, since the boson mass that maximizes early growth depends on the characteristic black-hole mass and evolutionary stage. This separation between dynamical efficiency and observational compatibility leaves open complementary roles for different bosonic mass scales in supermassive black hole growth and dark-matter phenomenology.

6. Kinetic Theory of the EFTofLSS : A Halo-Based Approach[2610.19001]
Abstract

The Effective Field Theory of Large Scale Structure (EFTofLSS) provides a coarse-grained description of the non-linear gravitational clustering of dark matter, in which the effects of short-scale physics are encoded in an effective stress tensor and a set of counterterms. In this work, we develop a framework to investigate the physical origin of these counterterms by relating the effective stress tensor to the dynamics of individual dark matter halos in cosmological N-body simulations. We find that halo accretion contributes to the trace of the halo-based effective stress tensor, contributing to the background pressure and effective sound speed, while anisotropic accretion generates the traceless part, consequently contributing to the shear viscosity. For the halo population with $M_\mathrm{vir}>10^{12}\,M_\odot/h$, we find that halos account for $\sim\!95\%$, $\sim\!85\%$, and $\sim\!70\%$ of the full box background pressure, effective sound speed, and shear viscosity, respectively, depending on the EFT cutoff scale. The contributions to the counterterms are dominated by cluster-scale halos, $M_\mathrm{vir} \sim 10^{14} - 10^{15}\,M_\odot/h$ and halos with larger inflow rates, supporting an interpretation in terms of accretion-driven, out-of-equilibrium dynamics. Our results provide a short-scale, kinetic-theory interpretation of the EFTofLSS counterterms and establish a framework for connecting the properties and dynamics of short-scale structures to physics-informed priors for large-scale cosmological observables.

7. First L-type substellar object discovered in the vicinity of the embedded cluster NGC 2023[2610.19009]
Abstract

We present the first substellar object in a star-forming region spectroscopically confirmed with Euclid, namely J054158.70-021031.4. This young and faint source is located close to the embedded open cluster NGC 2023. It has a spectral type of L3, a reddening of Av = 3 mag, proper motion consistent with the nearby cluster NGC 2024, and a magnitude of $\sim24.5$ in the Euclid optical band. At the distance of 403 pc, its inferred mass lies close to the planetary-mass boundary. We discovered the object by searching for ultracool dwarf candidates in the Euclid Early Release Observations. This work demonstrates the potential of Euclid to unveil previously hidden populations of very low-mass objects, located in obscured regions where star formation is ongoing.

8. SPICY 30021: an embedded bona fide FUor with a mid-infrared precursor[2610.19018]
Abstract

Unstable mass accretion in young stellar objects (YSOs) often leads to observable outbursts, which play a key role in stellar mass assembly during pre-main-sequence evolution. The highest-amplitude, longest-duration events are classified as FUors. Until recently, only 50 FUor events had been confirmed. Many occur in embedded protostars, which are difficult to monitor in optical surveys. We present photometric and spectroscopic observations of SPICY 30021, an eruptive young star discovered in the mid-infrared (MIR) NEOWISE time series. We aim to construct its spectral energy distribution and characterise its eruptive nature. We compiled multi-wavelength photometric time series from NEOWISE, VVV and VVV-RENEW, and obtained photometric and spectroscopic observations during the outburst with SOAR, Magellan and VLT, including data from the ESO public survey VVVX-GalCen. We identified the outbursting source, separated it from nearby companions, and estimated the line-of-sight extinction from spectroscopic features. Stellar parameters were constrained using radial velocity, proper motions and stellar evolution models. SPICY 30021 is an optically obscured FUor with a total interstellar and circumstellar extinction of Av = 12.5 mag. Its spectrum closely resembles those of classical FUors. Multiple methods yield a distance of 1.7+_0.4 kpc. Its accretion luminosity is Lacc = 32 L_sol, moderate among FUors. Quiescent near-infrared photometry is consistent with a young 0.9 M_sol progenitor, although the mass depends on the adopted model and distance. We confirm SPICY 30021 as a bona fide FUor event. Its MIR precursor suggests an outside-in triggering mechanism.

9. Velocity-field estimation techniques for on-disk solar coronal bright fronts (CBFs) and shock-wave propagation[2610.19032]
Abstract

Coronal mass ejections (CMEs) are large expulsions of plasma and magnetic fields from the solar corona into the heliosphere. Coronal shock waves are driven by CMEs, and accurate Solar Energetic Particles (SEP) forecasting requires knowing when and where shocks form, how fast they propagate, and how they interact with the coronal magnetic field. By measuring the speed, geometry and temporal evolution of coronal bright fronts (CBFs), one can create more realistic predictions of SEP onset and intensity at Earth, thus velocity-field estimation techniques supply these critical parameters. High-resolution velocity field maps can provide even deeper insight. Instead of yielding only an overall speed of the front, techniques like FLCT and optical flow can show the spatial distribution of velocities across the entire wave dome or front. These analyses reveal which parts of the front are moving fastest and how the front might be deforming as it expands. Here we discuss a set of practical parameters for Lucas–Kanade, Horn–Schunck, FLCT, and Farneback algorithms, arrived at by combining analytic reasoning with empirical measurements on a single event—and demonstrate the potential of these techniques for on-disk shock-wave propagation during the X1.5-class event that took place 10 May 2022, processed by the wavelet-based Wavetrack technique. A better understanding of how to fine-tune these parameters could improve the precision of algorithms running in pipelines, and is especially valuable where per-event hand-tuning is impractical.

10. The Atacama Cosmology Telescope: DR6 Constraints on Isotropic Time-Dependent Cosmic Birefringence from Axion-like Particles[2610.19049]
Abstract

Axion-like particles (ALPs) with sufficiently low masses are dark matter candidates because they could account for much or all of the observed dark matter energy density and help explain observations of galactic substructure. Such pseudoscalar fields could couple to photons, causing a time-dependent rotation of the polarization of the light with an oscillation frequency equal to the ALP mass. We use measurements of the polarization of the cosmic microwave background (CMB) by the Atacama Cosmology Telescope (ACT) between 2017 and 2022 to search for this effect using an EB power spectrum estimator. By probing oscillation periods from days to years, the data constrain the coupling between ALPs and photons for ALP masses between $2.5\times 10^{-23}$ eV and $4.8\times 10^{-20}$ eV. We find no evidence for such a signal and set a 95% upper bound on the axion-photon coupling constant, $g_{\phi\gamma} < 1.1\times10^{-11}\left({m_\phi}/{10^{-21}\,\mathrm{eV}}\right)\,\mathrm{GeV}^{-1} .$ This limit assumes that ALPs make up all of the dark matter and that the ALP field amplitude is stochastically distributed, which leads to more conservative bounds than the deterministic-field assumption used in some previous CMB polarization analyses. Among searches for ALP-photon coupling using CMB polarization, we set the strongest bounds to date under the stochastic-field assumption. This work develops a methodology to analyze over 10,000 time-sliced "depth-1" polarization maps from ACT, a data product that will be standard for time-domain astrophysics with the Simons Observatory, enabling searches with a higher sensitivity and observing span.

11. Understanding the Complex Morphology of a Coronal Mass Ejection. III: How to Create a Severe Geomagnetic Storm[2610.19096]
Abstract

On April 21, 2023, a complex filament eruption produced an Earth-directed coronal mass ejection (CME) that triggered the first severe geomagnetic storm of solar cycle 25. The primary objective of this work is to understand the physical origin of the severe impact of this CME on Earth. We explore the CME's complex morphology by simulating the eruption using the thermodynamic magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) model CORHEL-CME. We model the pre-eruptive structure as two intertwined magnetic flux ropes and validate the early evolution of the event by comparing synthetic observables generated from the model with extreme-ultraviolet and white-light observations. We then compare the simulated CME structure with in situ measurements. From our analysis, we conclude that the CME merged with a stream interaction region (SIR) before reaching Earth. This interaction compressed the CME sheath, producing a sustained negative $B_n$ component that, together with the associated enhancement in solar wind density and velocity, triggered the geomagnetic storm. The internal configuration of the magnetic flux rope, which also maintained a sustained southward component during its propagation toward Earth, intensified the storm to severe levels. This model-data comparison underscores the power of using MHD simulations, when validated by multi-viewpoint observations, to reveal hidden dynamical processes and improve the understanding and prediction of space weather impacts.

12. A Distinct Type of Standstill in Ultra-Compact White Dwarf Binaries[2610.19105]
Abstract

AM CVn stars are ultra-compact, semi-detached binaries consisting of a white dwarf primary and a hydrogen-poor secondary. In this work, we analyse the AM CVn-type systems MGAB-V270, ZTF18aaxuusk and ASASSN-19ct, which have similar properties and show unusual post-superoutburst behaviour. We focus on the occurrence of standstill-like phases. In MGAB-V270, superoutbursts are followed by a series of echo outbursts that evolves into a standstill-like phase before the system returns to quiescence. ZTF18aaxuus shows similar post-superoutburst activity, with one echo-outburst series also developing into a standstill-like phase. ASASSN-19ct exhibits comparable echo outbursts but no standstill-like phases. Periodicity analysis using TESS data shows that superhumps can persist during both echo outbursts and standstill-like phases. Although standstills have previously been observed in AM CVn stars, the standstill-like phases we observe appear to represent a distinct phenomenon associated with post-superoutburst activity. We compare our results to other AM CVns that may exhibit similar behaviour.

13. Chandra-JWST Probing the Effects of Moderate X-Ray Superflares on Protoplanetary Disks[2610.19228]
Abstract

Powerful X-ray flares from young stars may rapidly alter the physical and chemical conditions of their planet-forming disks, but direct observational tests remain scarce. We present a Chandra-JWST/NIRSpec study of infrared spectral variability associated with pre-main-sequence (PMS) X-ray flares in the Orion Nebula. Combining six Chandra/ACIS-I observations with three NIRSpec-MOS visits, we analyze two-epoch spectra of 10 X-ray Flare stars and 12 matched non-flaring Controls among 84 observed disk-bearing PMS stars. We investigate three diagnostics: narrow 2.8-4.3 micron features, HI Br-alpha, and the CO fundamental band. Flare stars show tentative evidence for relatively fewer variable narrow features at longer wavelengths than Controls, while feature incidence decreases strongly with projected distance from the dominant ionizing O star Theta1 Ori C. Most classifiable features appear in absorption. These properties suggest that at least some features may arise in externally irradiated disk atmospheres and/or associated photoevaporative flows, with their apparent epoch-to-epoch variability potentially reflecting spatial sampling of such locally structured material rather than intrinsic temporal changes. Br-alpha behaves differently: flare stars show systematically stronger variability and uniquely exhibit absorption-core profiles, suggesting control by magnetospheric accretion and stellar magnetic processes rather than disk irradiation. The CO fundamental band shows no significant flare-associated variability, consistent with thermochemical models predicting weak responses to moderate X-ray superflares. This pilot study demonstrates the potential of coordinated Chandra-JWST observations to probe how young stellar magnetic activity affects planet-forming environments, motivating future monitoring to build larger superflare samples and extend them into the rare X-ray mega-flare regime.

14. Paleo-heat flux estimates across Enceladus derived from flexural response of the ice shell[2610.19242]
Abstract

Saturn's moon Enceladus has active plumes suggestive of locally high heat fluxes at present, and the present-day heating is reasonably well characterized. However, the moon's thermal history is not well understood. In this study, we model flexural deformation of chasmata and dorsae in various regions of Enceladus to find the effective elastic thickness of the ice shell and constrain temporal and spatial variations in heat flux. We find paleo-heat fluxes of 60-$\geq$350 mWm$^{-2}$, with most clustered around 125-200 mWm$^{-2}$. Present day measured heat fluxes at the South Polar Terrain (SPT) are comparable to our estimated paleo-heat fluxes in non-SPT regions. Our paleo-heat fluxes, however, are significantly higher than the corresponding present day heat fluxes for the same locations inferred from shell thickness maps. This suggests that regions outside the SPT experienced a past episode of heating (heat fluxes in excess of 100mWm$^{-2}$) followed by a cooling and thickening ice shell. A relatively constant paleo-heat flux for terrains of different ages (determined from crater densities) supports the "resonance locking" hypothesis of dissipation in Saturn and might imply a long-lived resonance between Enceladus and Dione. Our results suggest that Enceladus's tidal heating appears to have been consistent over time. Consistent peak heating at Enceladus could support a long-lived subsurface ocean, which has important consequences for astrobiology and the habitability of the outer solar system.

15. The Limits of Tidal Circularization and Atmospheric Escape for Mini-Neptunes and Super-Earths: The Case of TOI-2257 b[2610.19280]
Abstract

With a 35-day orbital period and $\sim$0.5 eccentricity, TOI-2257 b occupies a sparsely populated corner of transiting mini-Neptune parameter space and may lie near the orbital distance at which tidal and atmospheric escape process become negligible. To test this hypothesis, we performed simulations of the combined stellar, tidal, and atmospheric evolution and confirmed that TOI-2257 b does lie near this boundary, regardless of whether it formed with or without a primordial hydrogen envelope. In the mini-Neptune scenario, we find that all cases retain an envelope of at least 0.02 M$_\oplus$ for 8 Gyr, but that the envelope mass can be halved if it was initially larger than 0.2 M$_\oplus$. We find that tidal evolution only occurs for cases with initial envelope masses larger than 0.02 M$_\oplus$ because their radii are large enough to generate significant torques. In the super-Earth scenario, the planet could lose $\sim$10 Earth ocean masses of surface water during the $\sim$75 Myr pre-main sequence and therefore could be habitable if it formed with $\gtrsim$2x Earth's surface water mass. The predicted tidal heating surface flux tends to be similar to Io, with no cases approaching the flux required to drive a runaway greenhouse. We find that if the planet is rocky, then eccentricities larger than 0.62 today are strongly disfavored, implying that larger eccentricities could confirm the gaseous nature of the planet. These results suggest that TOI-2257 b could provide valuable constraints on mini-Neptune properties and new insight into the radius valley shape as a function of eccentricity.

16. ExoEcho: Exoplanet Ephemerides Change Observations. III. A JWST Survey for Transit Timing Variations of 37 Exoplanets[2610.19328]
Abstract

Transit timing variations (TTVs) provide a powerful probe of planetary dynamics and orbital evolution. We extend the ExoEcho project by incorporating James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) transit observations and analyze 84 light curves of 37 exoplanets, including 20 hot Jupiters, 11 Neptune-like planets, and 6 super-Earths. We derive updated transit ephemerides and conduct a systematic search for long-term and periodic TTV signals. We identify two strong TTV candidates, including HATS-6 b and HAT-P-18 b. The hot Jupiter HATS-6 b exhibits a long-term trend with $\dot{P} = 230.5 \pm 46.8\ \mathrm{ms\ yr^{-1}}$, potentially driven by tidal interactions with its host star. In addition, the Neptune-like planet HAT-P-18 b shows periodic TTV signal with amplitude of $92.9 \pm 13.7$ s. We also show that the previously claimed positive period derivative in WASP-121 b is inconsistent with our new transit timing data. Although continued monitoring is required to confirm these findings, our results demonstrate the potential of JWST for high-precision transit timing and extend TTV studies beyond the traditional hot-Jupiter population to lower-mass exoplanets.

17. Search for Sub-Solar Mass Binaries in the First and Second Part of LIGO's Fourth Observing Run O4a-O4b[2610.19329]
Abstract

The observation of sub-solar mass neutron stars and black holes would signify a new class of astrophysical objects since these systems are not expected to form via standard stellar evolution. We present the first sub-solar mass search for binaries consisting of neutron star or black holes that uses the data from both the second part (O4b) and first part (O4a) of the fourth observing run of the Advanced LIGO detectors. The search covers primary masses 0.1 to 2 $M_\odot$ and secondary masses 0.1 to 1 $M_\odot$. Tidal effects up to $7\times10^5$ are accounted for in extremely low-mass neutron stars. No statistically significant candidates are identified. We place a $90\%$ confidence upper limit on the merger rate $\mathcal{R}_{90}$ for sub-solar mass black holes and neutron stars to be $< 5.2\times10^{2}\,\textrm{Gpc}^{-3}\,\textrm{yr}^{-1}$ for a chirp mass of 0.5 $M_\odot$, a factor $2.2 \times$ lower compared to previous O1-O4a searches. We constrain the effective local dark matter fraction to be $\tilde{f}_\textrm{PBH}<0.6\%$ for $M_{\textrm{PBH}}=0.8 \,M_\odot$, approximately $1.5$ factor improvement to previous O1-O4a constraints. Given our model assumptions, our local dark matter fraction constraints are $2-7$ times lower than the OGLE microlensing survey for $M_{\textrm{PBH}}\ge{0.6}~M_\odot$.

18. Magnetic Reconnection Governs the Entire Lifecycle of Solar Eruptive Magnetic Flux Rope[2610.19348]
Abstract

While magnetic flux ropes (MFRs) are widely accepted to exist prior to many solar eruptions, the physical mechanism responsible for triggering and subsequently driving their eruptions remains debated. Classical models commonly attribute eruption onset to ideal magnetohydrodynamic instabilities, particularly the torus instability, while magnetic reconnection is often regarded as a secondary process. Using high-resolution three-dimensional MHD simulations, we investigate the eruption of an MFR that forms self-consistently through flux cancellation driven by converging motions and photospheric diffusion following an initial shearing phase. The pre-eruption MFR rises quasi-statically and reaches a height where the decay index exceeds the classical torus instability threshold, yet the system continues to evolve quasi-statically without eruption. Instead, eruption begins only when the current sheet beneath the MFR becomes sufficiently thin for magnetic reconnection to set in. Increasing numerical resolution systematically delays the eruption onset, whereas the decay index at eruption onset remains nearly unchanged, demonstrating that the onset is governed by reconnection rather than torus instability. During the eruption, magnetic reconnection continuously converts surrounding sheared arcades into newly reconnected twisted flux that merges with the pre-eruption MFR and continuously to build the erupting MFR. A component-resolved analysis of the Lorentz force work further shows that the newly reconnection flux contributes predominantly to the main acceleration phase. These results demonstrate that magnetic reconnection plays a central role throughout the entire lifecycle of the eruptive MFR: it builds the MFR, directly triggers its eruption, and provides the dominant driving of the eruption.

20. A possible evolutionary connection between high-mass X-ray binary pulsars and isolated rotation-powered pulsars[2610.19595]
Abstract

We investigate the long-term spin evolution of high-mass X-ray binary (HMXB) pulsars in the Small Magellanic Cloud (SMC) using archival measurements of spin periods and period derivatives from XMM-Newton, Chandra, and RXTE. In the P-|dP/dt| plane, the HMXB pulsars follow an approximately linear relation in log-log space with a slope of 1.02 +/- 0.11, similar to the trend shown by the youngest isolated rotation-powered pulsars (RPPs; e.g., Manchester et al., 2005; Lorimer and Kramer, 2005). For comparison, young RPPs from the ATNF Pulsar Catalogue give slopes that depend strongly on the characteristic-age cut: 1.10 +/- 0.16 for characteristic ages below 10,000 years and 0.72 +/- 0.08 for characteristic ages below 100,000 years. The full non-recycled population shows no significant correlation. The HMXB pulsars lie in the same characteristic-age range (approximately 1,000-10,000 years) as the youngest RPPs, but at spin periods two to three orders of magnitude longer. Reference characteristic-age contours in the HMXB P-|dP/dt| plane are roughly parallel to this relation, consistent with a continuous evolutionary sequence.

21. Cosmology with strong gravitational lenses[2610.19598]
Abstract

Three of the most pressing open questions in cosmology – the nature of dark energy and dark matter, and the growing tension in the value of the Hubble constant H$_0$ – call for cosmological probes that are independent of, and complementary to, traditional methods. Strong gravitational lensing offers exactly that: a purely gravitational probe of the universe's expansion history and matter distribution, sensitive to dark matter's presence regardless of whether it interacts with light. Time-delay lenses and multiplane lenses can measure H$_0$ and the dark energy equation of state directly, while the distortion of lensed images can reveal the abundance and internal structure of dark matter subhalos. After a brief theoretical and historical introduction, I review recent breakthroughs enabled by JWST and by advances in lens modeling and stellar dynamics. Examples include the recent measurement of H$_0$ with 3\% precision in flat $\Lambda$ cold dark matter cosmology, and a tentative detection of dark matter self-interaction. Strong lensing is entering a pivotal era: wide-field surveys with Euclid, Rubin, and Roman, and radio surveys with the Square Kilometer Array, are poised to increase the number of known strong lenses by orders of magnitude, positioning strong lensing to play a central role in answering these open questions in the decade ahead.

22. Correlation of galactic very-high-energy gamma-ray sources detected by LHAASO with high-energy neutrinos[2610.19649]
Abstract

We conducted an investigation into the possible correlation between very-high-energy (VHE, $E>$ 0.1 TeV) gamma-ray sources, as identified by the Large High Altitude Air Shower Observatory (LHAASO), and high-energy neutrino events captured by the IceCube Neutrino Observatory. For this analysis, we utilized the First LHAASO catalog, which comprises 90 VHE gamma-ray sources, and the IceCube Event Catalog of Alert Tracks (IceCat-1), which includes 348 track-like neutrino events. Through Monte Carlo simulations, we assessed the correlations between the neutrino events and LHAASO sources, achieving the best p-values of 0.237, 0.312, and 0.115, respectively, for all LHAASO sources, KM2A sources, and WCDA sources. These findings imply that the spatial coincidence between LHAASO sources and neutrino alerts is relatively weak. Furthermore, under the assumption that gamma-rays are exclusively generated through hadronic interactions, we estimated the most optimistic theoretical neutrino fluxes from LHAASO sources and compared them with the IceCube observed fluxes. The results indicate that the majority of these sources are improbable counterparts to the detected neutrinos. In conclusion, LHAASO sources could be potential galactic neutrino emitters but currently do not exhibit compelling evidence, thereby underscoring the necessity for elevated sensitivity levels in forthcoming detection instruments like IceCube-Gen2.

30. Stationary Resistive MHD Solutions Near an Isolated Three-Dimensional Magnetic Null[2610.20015]
Abstract

Magnetic null points define the magnetic field topology in stellar coronae, planetary magnetospheres, and astrospheres, and are commonly associated with magnetic reconnection. We investigate the local structure of stationary three-dimensional (3D) resistive, incompressible magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) flows in the vicinity of an isolated magnetic null and an isolated velocity stagnation point, assuming that the two nulls are colocated. The governing MHD equations are solved self-consistently using a first-order local expansion of the magnetic and velocity fields about the null. For any given magnetic-null configuration, the equations admit a two-parameter family of stationary solutions, comprising multiple solution branches with distinct velocity topologies. Along these families, the velocity-null polarity can change at bifurcation points where the flow becomes effectively 2.5D. A single magnetic null can therefore support positive-radial, negative-radial, and spiral stagnation flows, with qualitatively different inflow, outflow, and rotational properties. The associated pressure and resistivity distributions are likewise determined self-consistently and vary across the solution family, demonstrating that they are coupled to the flow topology rather than prescribed independently. The generic solutions do not require the velocity and magnetic separatrices to coincide, allowing plasma to cross the magnetic fan separatrix and providing a local reconnective flow. These results demonstrate that, in the presence of nonuniform resistivity, the magnetic topology alone does not uniquely determine the associated stationary flow, pressure, or resistivity structure around a 3D magnetic null, revealing the nonlinear feedback between all macroscopic variables and resistivity. The constant-resistivity field-aligned and spiral field-crossing solutions identified previously are recovered as special cases.

Abstract

The engulfment of a neutron star (NS) by a massive companion initiates a highly dynamic common-envelope (CE) evolution phase. As the NS spirals into the dense stellar core, it is subjected to hypercritical accretion rates that threaten to rapidly collapse the NS into a black hole (BH). However, if the infalling envelope possesses sufficient angular momentum and magnetic fields, the NS might survive longer and launch feedback-driving jets. To investigate this, we perform fully coupled, axisymmetric General Relativistic Magnetohydrodynamic (GRMHD) simulations of hyperaccreting NSs, featuring energy-integrated two-moment neutrino transport and a 13-isotope nuclear reaction network. We systematically vary the envelope rotation profile and the magnetic field strength of the NS surface ($B_{\rm surf} \sim 5 \times 10^{10} - 5 \times 10^{13}$ G). In non-magnetised models, we find that envelope rotation naturally forms a centrifugal barrier and a geometrically thick accretion disk, which suppresses the mass accretion rate and lowers the neutrino luminosity; conversely, the intrinsic spin of the NS has a negligible global impact. In magnetised models, the differential rotation of the accretion flow vigorously amplifies the toroidal magnetic field via the $\Omega$-effect, driving the expansion of magnetic towers. Crucially, for strong initial surface magnetic fields ($B_{\rm surf} \gtrsim 2.3 \times 10^{13}$ G), the intense magnetic pressure could completely halt the accretion flow at the NS surface and evacuates a low-density polar funnel. We conclude that while this highly magnetised NS engine successfully delays prompt BH formation and may launch low-power precursor jets (with powers up to ${\sim} 10^{46}~{\rm erg/s}$) capable of generating observable X-ray flares, it lacks the energy budget to unbind the massive envelope, potentially setting the stage for a subsequent BH-driven explosion.

Abstract

Joint analyses of baryon acoustic oscillations, type Ia supernovae and the cosmic microwave background show a preference for an evolving dark-energy equation of state, and recent studies suggest that cosmic inhomogeneities can produce an apparent evolution of dark energy within general relativity. Abstract

77. Collider Probes of Dark Energy Microphysics[2606.27441]
Abstract

The physical origin of dark energy remains one of the most profound open questions in modern physics. Although cosmological observations tightly constrain the equation of state parameter $w$, this information alone does not reveal the underlying microphysics, as many distinct theoretical models can reproduce the same expansion history. A key discriminator among these models is the sound speed of dark energy perturbations, yet this quantity remains largely unconstrained by current astrophysical observations. In this work, we propose a fundamentally new approach: using collider measurements of beyond-the-Standard-Model (BSM) mediator resonances as a probe of dark energy microphysics. We construct a unified effective-field-theory framework in which a dynamical dark energy scalar is coupled, through symmetry-motivated derivative interactions, to a pseudoscalar mediator in the 2HDM+$a$ model. These interactions naturally induce invisible decays and modify the propagation of the BSM mediator in a dark energy background, leading to measurable distortions of resonance properties at colliders such as the LHC. We show that the decay widths, branching ratios, and kinematic structure of the mediator resonance become sensitive to the propagation properties of dark energy fluctuations, in particular the sound speed. As a result, collider observables provide a direct and complementary handle on dark energy microphysics, with the potential to distinguish between models that are otherwise indistinguishable through cosmology alone. Our results establish a new paradigm in which high-energy collider experiments can probe the physics of cosmic acceleration, revealing a connection between the smallest and largest scales in nature and opening a novel experimental pathway to uncover the fundamental origin of dark energy.

Abstract

The growth of primordial perturbations during the matter-dominated era is primarily driven by dark matter.
