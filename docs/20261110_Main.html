1. Optimal summary statistics to constrain w0waCDM with the DESI BGS[2611.10084]
Abstract

The Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI) is the first stage-4 spectroscopic instrument installed on the 4-m Mayall Telescope at Kitt Peak National Observatory in Arizona. It contains 5000 robotically positioned fibers, allowing the measurement of as many galaxy spectra in one observation ( 20 minutes). The data acquisition (2021–2028) aims at constraining the cosmological parameters, and especially the parameters describing the evolution of the dark energy with time. However, standard clustering analysis based on 2-point statistics only capture the Gaussian information of the matter density field, while the analytical models used to match these statistical probe are limited to quasi-linear scales, as they are based on perturbation theory. Therefore, the non-Gaussian and non-linear information of the density field is not exploited. To go beyond these limitations, several alternative clustering methods have been developed that rely on a simulation-based pipeline. In this presentation, we focus on the DESI Bright Galaxy Survey, a magnitude-limited sample of bright galaxies in the redshift range of 0.1 < z < 0.4. We present how accessing the non-linear and non-Gaussian information of the density field allows around 40% improvement on the constraints of some $\Lambda$CDM parameters. We are also extending the method to constrain w0waCDM in order to improve DESI's sensibility at low redshift where the Universe is dominated by dark energy.

2. Gravitational Production of Non-minimally Coupled Vector Dark Matter in Warm Inflation[2611.10090]
Abstract

We study the purely gravitational production of a massive vector dark matter with a non-minimal coupling to the Einstein tensor, $\xi G_{\mu\nu}X^\mu X^\nu$, in warm inflation. The background, $V=\lambda\phi^4$ with a dissipation coefficient linear in temperature, is constrained by CMB observations and parametrized by the dissipation ratio $10^{-4}\le Q_0\le1$. We consider both unavoidable production channels: freeze-in from the Standard Model bath through graviton exchange, and cosmological gravitational particle production (CGPP). For freeze-in, we compute the scattering amplitudes including the contact interaction induced by $\xi$, which enhances the production of light dark matter through the longitudinal mode and suppresses that of heavy dark matter through destructive interference. For CGPP, the slow-roll parameter is not small during warm inflation, which enhances the production of light dark matter by up to three orders of magnitude relative to cold inflation. Combining both channels, CGPP excludes most of the light freeze-in branch, leaving $m_X\lesssim2.4\times10^{-5}$ GeV, a narrow window around $10^{10}$ GeV for strong dissipation, and $m_X\gtrsim10^{14}$–$10^{15}$ GeV. Only the heavy branch remains viable for all $|\xi|\lesssim1$.

3. The deuterium-rich atmosphere of a mature super-Jupiter[2611.10129]
Abstract

The deuterium-to-hydrogen ratio (D/H) has provided key insights into the origin and evolution of the Solar System. Using the James Webb Space Telescope, we detected deuterated methane at $48\sigma$ in a mature giant exoplanet, $\epsilon$ Ind Ab. We measure the planet's D/H ratio to be $(4.4 \pm 0.7) \times 10^{-5}$, which is similar to those of Uranus and Neptune but about twice those of Jupiter, Saturn, and the protosolar nebula. $\epsilon$ Ind Ab's heavy elements (C, O, N, and S) are uniformly enriched to a level similar to Jupiter's. These measurements require $\epsilon$ Ind Ab to have accreted solids with $\mathrm{D/H} \gtrsim 1\%$, at least 30 times more deuterium-rich than typical Solar System comets. Such D/H values are found in the interstellar comet 3I/ATLAS and in organic molecules in star-forming regions, indicating that $\epsilon$ Ind Ab accreted pristine solids formed in a very cold environment.

4. FAIR Universe Weak Lensing ML Uncertainty Challenge: Dataset and Cosmological Inference Competition[2611.10228]
Abstract

Weak gravitational lensing is a powerful cosmological probe that enables direct mapping of the matter distribution and precise constraints on the fundamental parameters governing structure formation in the universe. In recent years, machine learning has shown strong potential for extracting cosmological information from weak-lensing data beyond traditional summary statistics. However, progress has been limited by the lack of standardized benchmarks that capture the complexity of the dataset, simulator imperfections, and various systematic biases encountered in realistic analyses. To address this gap, we present the FAIR Universe Weak Lensing Machine Learning Uncertainty Challenge, one of the NeurIPS 2025 competitions, as a benchmark dataset and evaluation study. Our dataset reflects realistic weak-lensing observations, incorporating multiple systematic biases, and thereby providing a controlled testbed for robust cosmological inference under uncertainties. We summarize the main methodological advances developed by the winners of our challenge, including data augmentation with conditional optimal transport, an AI-agent-driven approach for parameter inference, ensemble learning, Vision Transformer-based architectures, and physics-informed feature integration. By unifying the benchmark dataset and the winning solutions, this work provides a community resource for developing and evaluating machine learning methods for weak lensing cosmology and, more broadly, for scientific inference under systematic uncertainties.

5. Thermally unfavorable conditions of volatile existence in near-Earth asteroid 1998 KY26, the Hayabusa extended mission's target[2611.10275]
Abstract

The near-Earth asteroid 1998 KY26, the target of the Hayabusa2# extended mission, has been proposed as a dark comet that may host volatiles, particularly water, owing to an anomalous out-of-plane nongravitational acceleration that may result from outgassing. This asteroid, however, may also belong to a largely anhydrous taxonomic group. Whether outgassing-driven acceleration is possible strongly depends on how the asteroid stores volatiles in its interior and releases them upon heating at the surface. Thus far, the contentious topic of the existence of volatiles on 1998 KY26 that can cause outgassing has not been extensively examined from a thermophysical standpoint. Here, we show that all locations on the surface or in the interior of 1998 KY26 have orbit-averaged temperatures significantly above the sublimation point of volatiles in vacuum. Across 13 thermal characterizations, this observation is found to be consistent despite parameter variations in equivalent diameter, thermal conductivity, Bond albedo, specific heat capacity, and spin pole ecliptic latitude and longitude. Our temperature model results indicate that the water outgassing rate on 1998 KY26 would be too high to maintain buried ice, refining the understanding of the asteroid's composition.

6. Current Closure and Gamma-Ray Signatures of Spark-Gap and Current-Sheet Activities in Radiative Black Hole Magnetospheres[2611.10428]
Abstract

We investigate the evolution of an axisymmetric black hole magnetosphere using general relativistic particle-in-cell simulations, focusing on its global current structure, variability, and radiative effects. We include inverse-Compton (IC) scattering of ambient photons and subsequent two-photon pair production, and compare simulations with and without synchrotron-curvature (SC) radiation reaction. Starting from an axisymmetric vertical magnetic field, the system develops a polar spark gap and a variable equatorial current sheet that together sustain the global poloidal current. For the parameters explored in this study, we observe that the spark gap oscillates upon the onset of SC cooling, whereas the current sheet is less sensitive to radiative cooling. Time-dependent electric fields in the return-current layer trigger magnetic-field rearrangement followed by magnetic reconnection. Cross-correlation analysis finds no significant correlation between the two regions' variability, suggesting dynamical independence. We further perform post-processing ray-tracing calculations to evaluate the radiative efficiencies of the IC and SC processes. High-energy particles dominate the bolometric luminosity, which reaches up to 1 percent of the Blandford-Znajek luminosity for SC emission and 0.1 percent for IC emission. The relative IC luminosity to SC is higher at lower viewing angles because particle momenta are more anisotropic near the pole than near the equator, due to efficient acceleration along the local magnetic field lines. Together with energetic estimates, these results suggest that both regions can contribute to high-energy emission in active galactic nuclei, with spark-gap photons reaching the sub-TeV regime.

7. Faint Thermonuclear Transients from the Double Detonations of Common White Dwarfs[2611.10431]
Abstract

The double detonation model is a viable channel to explain many observed Type Ia supernovae (SNe Ia), with thin helium shelled white dwarf (WD) progenitors of 0.9–1.1 M$_\odot$ reproducing normal SNe Ia observables. Recent work has also shown that all C/O WDs $\leq$ 1.0 M$_\odot$ can sustain a helium shell detonation that triggers a detonation of the underlying core. As WDs of lower masses (0.5–0.7 M$_\odot$) far outnumber those at higher mass, it is important to explore their double detonation outcomes. To that end, we conducted multidimensional double detonation simulations of low mass C/O cores with very thin helium shells, finding explosion outcomes that differ non-trivially from those at higher masses. Not surprisingly, their cores are incompletely burned with low yields of radioactive elements. Though still formally unbound in isolation, some of the ejecta could remain bound to a nearby companion. In addition, the offset initiation of the core detonation wave and its failure to burn through the entire core lead to a very asymmetric distribution of $^{56}$Ni that creates dramatically viewing angle-dependent light curves and spectra. We find that the peak bolometric luminosities for the same explosion vary up to nearly 2 magnitudes over the sky, and that the spectra and light curves from these models are within the range of faint SNe Iax. In particular, our 0.50 M$_\odot$ models agree well with the photometry of SNe Iax 2008ha and 2024vjm.

8. Polarization Signatures of Magnetic Reconnection Bubbles near Black Holes[2611.10525]
Abstract

Motivated by the observed horizon-scale polarization variability in multi-epoch Event Horizon Telescope (EHT) data, we develop a time-dependent general relativistic polarized ray-tracing framework to study how localized magnetic reconnection near a black hole imprints itself on synchrotron polarization. We model the reconnection phenomenologically as a transient magnetic bubble. While keeping the underlying spacetime, emission prescription, and polarization transport scheme unchanged, the bubble weakens the local ordered field, reorganizes its geometry, induces a conservative local kinematic response, and relaxes through a finite magnetic-memory prescription. We explore how the key bubble parameters control the recovery of the magnetic-field strength, the spatial spreading of the disturbed region, the persistence of the post-reconnection imprint, and the resulting polarization signatures. Perturbations dominated by different magnetic-field components produce distinct polarized morphologies, EVPA evolution, and Stokes-(Q, U) trajectories: azimuthal perturbations favor outward EVPA shifts, radial perturbations favor inward shifts, and vertical perturbations mainly suppress the polarized intensity. For the illustrative M87^*-like cases, the phase evolution of the \beta_2 polarization coefficient further traces how localized magnetic reconfiguration contributes to the global EVPA twist of the ring. These results show that horizon-scale polarization variability can provide a sensitive diagnostic of localized magnetic restructuring and that the present framework offers a physically transparent way to interpret time-dependent polarimetric signatures in future EHT observations.

9. SMMAN studies of AGN variability. II. Radio and optical quasi-periodic oscillations and changing variability states in the relativistic jet of the blazar PKS 0754+100[2611.10572]
Abstract

We report the detection of $\sim$280-day quasi-periodic oscillations (QPOs) in the SMMAN observations of PKS 0754+100 at 4.8 and 23.6 GHz, which may also be present in VLBI observations made at 15 GHz. The onset of a transition of the amplitude of variability in the radio flux density, observed more clearly in the 15 GHz and 23.6 GHz observations, coincides with the detection of a likely QPO of $\sim$1.4 days in optical observations made by the Transiting Exoplanet Survey Satellite. This transition in radio variability also occurs close in time to the disappearance of the secondary knot in the VLBI radio map during the lower variability state. An X-ray flare was observed 100 days before this transition, suggesting that the mechanisms causing these quasi-periodicities are related. After the transition to a higher variability state, both the 15 and 23.6 GHz light curves exhibit indications of QPOs at $\sim$400 days. For the first time, a QPO detected in the radio band from a BL Lac has been seen to appear to shift to a QPO of lower frequency, with that transition occurring near the appearance of a QPO in optical emission at a much shorter period. This multi-waveband behavior could be related to the internal helical structure of the jet, where an instability or kink develops due to magnetic reconnection and propagates outward.

10. Constraining Cosmic Deceleration using the Solar System[2611.10596]
Abstract

We investigate whether Solar System dynamics can provide independent constraints on the present day cosmological deceleration parameter $q_0$, offering a complementary approach to conventional cosmological probes. By relating cosmic acceleration to a weak Hooke type radial perturbation, we derive bounds using planetary perihelion precession, trans-Neptunian objects, and Oort cloud dynamics. We obtain relatively robust planetary bounds of $|q_0|\lesssim 10^9$, while the outer Solar System yields potentially stronger, albeit more model dependent, constraints approaching $10^5$. Through Monte Carlo uncertainty propagation and sensitivity analyses, we explore the statistical robustness of these estimates and their dependence on orbital measurements. Finally, we demonstrate how future precision astrometry and outer planet missions could improve these limits by several orders of magnitude, establishing Solar System dynamics as a complementary laboratory for probing cosmological acceleration.

11. Self-bound quantum dark fermion stars: saturation, a coupling-capped maximum mass, and tidal signatures of a vector dark sector[2611.10662]
Abstract

I show that a dark sector whose fermions couple to both a scalar and a vector mediator saturates in close analogy with nuclear matter: the energy per particle develops a minimum at finite density, cold dark matter of this kind is self-bound, and its stars, computed here in full general relativity, possess the sharp surfaces of strange quark stars. Saturation makes the maximum stellar mass a property of the Lagrangian rather than an inheritance of the Chandrasekhar scaling: it rises eightfold across the saturation window, which a critical vector coupling closes, so the theory caps its own maximum mass, at $3.9\,M_\odot$ for a fermion of $1\,\mathrm{GeV}$ and the fiducial saturation density, a ceiling shared by the whole family of couplings rather than by any single parameter set. Metastable couplings support two disconnected stable families, one dilute and gravitationally bound, the other dense and self-bound. The quantum-pressure correction, confined by saturation to a surface layer a few Compton wavelengths thick, governs the smallest dark nuggets whilst shifting stellar masses by less than one part in $10^{21}$. A $1.4\,M_\odot$ member of the fiducial family has a radius of $13.3\,\mathrm{km}$, a compactness of $0.155$, and a tidal deformability of $1.6\times 10^{3}$, almost an order of magnitude above the measured neutron-star value, so at a given particle mass and couplings a single well-measured inspiral distinguishes these stars from neutron stars and from their scalar-only counterparts, whilst the deformability surviving at the maximum mass separates them, in principle, from black holes.

12. Probing the origin of the LHAASO Peanut with a hidden central accelerator[2611.10703]
Abstract

A highly asymmetric, giant Peanut-shaped structure has been detected by the Large High Altitude Air Shower Observatory (LHAASO) in ultra-high-energy gamma rays at high Galactic latitude, $b\approx-17.5^{\circ}$, suggesting anisotropic particle transport around a Galactic accelerator. We investigate whether its spectrum and morphology can arise from a hidden central accelerator embedded in a projected anisotropic halo. The baseline model accounts for the diffuse strip and central excess, while a phenomenological prescription for a localized magnetic field reorientation toward the line of sight (LOS), together with a particle-conserved remapping of the electrons downstream of the far excess into an emitting column of prescribed transverse extent, enhances the emission at the far excess position. We explore various source ages, along with different combinations of ambient magnetic field strength and parallel diffusion coefficient, while accounting for radiative cooling when estimating the viewing geometry. The exploration reveals that relatively rapid parallel transport enhances the far component while preserving a dominant diffuse strip. Representative configurations remain compatible with the observed spectral energy distribution, although the fitted far component remains comparatively weaker than the central component above 100 TeV, even in cases where their component fluxes are comparable at 25-100 TeV. These results illustrate the plausibility of a hidden central accelerator as the origin of the LHAASO Peanut, while identifying the remaining difficulty in reproducing the relative brightness of its compact components within the adopted phenomenological framework.

13. Stochastic Acceleration Downstream of Astrophysical Shocks[2611.10745]
Abstract

Generation and evolution of turbulence at shock fronts are crucial aspects of the process of particle acceleration in different classes of sources. Here we aim at describing the fate of non-thermal particles and turbulence in the downstream region of an astrophysical shock, both in the case in which turbulence is solely produced upstream and advected through the shock and in the case in which additional turbulence is injected downstream. In particular we will provide an estimate of the effect of stochastic acceleration on particles accelerated at the shock and then advected behind it. The calculations will be specialized to supernova remnant shocks. The temporal evolution of the particle distribution function downstream of the shock and the power spectrum of the turbulent perturbations are both followed by solving numerically the transport equations for the two quantities, accounting for the damping of waves due to the particles that resonantly interact with them, which makes the problem non-linear. Our calculations show that, if waves are only self-generated upstream via Bell instability, they are not able to significantly modify the particle spectrum in the downstream region. Additionally, turbulent cascading in $k$-space quickly drains their energy, further limiting the stochastic acceleration of particles. If, on the other hand, there are additional sources of turbulence downstream, then these waves can contribute meaningfully to second order Fermi acceleration and alter the spectrum of particles. This may reflect in the spectrum of gamma radiation produced through $pp$ scattering downstream. Although second order Fermi acceleration is typically slower than diffusive shock acceleration, particles are expected to stay downstream of shocks for much longer times during which stochastic acceleration can modify their spectrum and affect the spectra of multi-messenger emissions.

14. Radial Velocity Confirmation of Two Binary Systems Detected with the Pulsation Timing Method[2611.10959]
Abstract

We present follow-up radial velocity (RV) results for the subdwarf B$+$white dwarf binaries TIC 202354658 and TIC 69298924, which were originally identified using the pulsation-timing and Fourier-modulation methods applied to ultra-short-cadence photometric data from the Transiting Exoplanet Survey Satellite ($TESS$). Using spectra obtained with the Gemini-North telescope and the Nordic Optical Telescopes, we derived the radial velocity curves for both targets. The orbital solutions we obtained are consistent with the predictions based on pulsation timing, and they provide more precise orbital information compared with the previously obtained results. We therefore confirm that both TIC 202354658 and TIC 69298924 are binary systems with white dwarf companions. The RV solution of TIC 202354658 indicates a relatively large eccentricity ($e$ = 0.313 $\pm$ 0.013), which would be uncommon for a short orbital period sdB binary.

15. Is Dark Energy Inhomogeneous?[2611.11094]
Abstract

The discovery of dark matter deficient galaxies motivates a fundamental question: Could sufficiently large regions of the Universe be deficient in dark energy as well? We investigate this possibility by developing a phenomenological framework that distinguishes genuine dark energy deficiencies from ordinary matter voids through nonlinear expansion, coherent peculiar velocities and density-momentum correlations. We show that a $10\%$ dark energy deficiency can suppress the expansion of a representative nonlinear void by approximately $2.7\%$, generating peculiar velocity residuals of order $60$-$100\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$ across scales of $30$-$50\, Mpc$. Intriguingly, we find that deficiencies of order $20\%$ extending over several hundred megaparsecs could also influence the locally inferred Hubble constant, potentially contributing to the $H_0$ tension, subject to stringent isotropy and dynamical consistency constraints. We identify observational strategies involving DESI, Rubin, peculiar velocity surveys, and cosmic void catalogues, opening a new avenue for testing whether the dark energy driving cosmic acceleration is truly homogeneous.

16. The Role of Small-Scale Flux Emergence in Pre-eruptive Magnetic Destabilization[2611.11096]
Abstract

Recent high-resolution observations have reported the emergence of small-scale magnetic flux shortly before the onset of solar flares; however, their role in solar eruption processes remains unclear. In this study, we perform three-dimensional magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) simulations to examine the effect of small-scale emerging flux on the eruption process. We construct a bipolar active region by extrapolating a potential magnetic field from a pair of sunspots and drive the system with sustained rotational motions to build up strong shear along the polarity inversion line. A small-scale emerging flux, amounting to approximately 0.6%-3.0% of the total magnetic flux and having a polarity opposite to that of the main sunspot, is then introduced at the center of the bipole. As a result, although no significant differences are found in the pre-existing magnetic field during the early phase with or without the emerging flux, the current sheet formed in the sheared field lines collapses more rapidly, and the reconnection-driven eruption is initiated earlier in the case with small-scale emerging flux. This result suggests that small-scale emerging flux accelerates the destabilization of the pre-eruptive magnetic configuration through enhanced current-sheet thinning and magnetic reconnection, leading to an earlier eruption onset. Furthermore, we find that the involvement of highly twisted field lines during the pre-eruption phase plays a crucial role in determining the eruption onset time.

17. The Fate of Collisional Neutrino Flavor Conversion: Fixed Points, Period Doubling, and Chaos[2611.11351]
Abstract

Core-collapse supernovae and neutron star mergers are among the most energetic astrophysical events and promising sources of multimessenger signals. Their dynamics and nucleosynthesis depend sensitively on neutrino transport, yet the impact of neutrino flavor evolution in these dense environments remains poorly understood. Predicting the outcome of collisional flavor instability (CFI) remains a challenge, despite progress in local-relaxation methods for fast flavor conversion. Here we construct homogeneous collective-precession states with stationary flavor populations in two-flavor quantum kinetics with collisional relaxation. The method applies to arbitrary energy spectra and energy-dependent collision rates. Our numerical examples exhibit persistent oscillations and a period-doubling route to chaos. Even in these regimes, the stationary populations estimate the time-averaged flavor populations to within $10\%$ in our tests. These fixed points provide a physically motivated input for incorporating CFI into astrophysical simulations, with implications for neutrino heating and ejecta nucleosynthesis.

18. Solar cycle variations of meridional flow and differential rotation in a non-kinematic dynamo model[2611.11548]
Abstract

Flux transport dynamo models have emerged as the leading paradigm for explaining the observed solar cycle. However, most of these models are kinematic and axisymmetric. We develop a non-kinematic dynamo model by specifying large-scale flows, such as the differential rotation and meridional flow. Thus, our model does not require the $\Lambda$ effect or other parameterisations of turbulent transport to generate the large-scale flows; it only solves the fluctuating flows and thermodynamics. Our model is inherently 3D by construction, although it is currently operating in axisymmetric mode because the source for poloidal field generation (Babcock-Leighton $\alpha$) is assumed to be axisymmetric. Our model reproduces solar-cycle variations in both the meridional flow and the differential rotation – specifically, the torsional oscillation in some agreement with observations. The model naturally exhibits some variability in the solar cycle, even without incorporating stochastic fluctuations, offering a new methodology for modelling the non-kinematic solar dynamo in 3D.

19. Detecting Baryon Acoustic Oscillations from Galaxy Clusters: Distance Constraints[2611.11587]
Abstract

We present a measurement of the baryon acoustic oscillation (BAO) feature using the largest galaxy cluster sample with spectroscopic redshift information to date, comprising 169,679 clusters selected from the WH24 catalogue based on the DESI Legacy Imaging Surveys. We measure the two-point correlation function in two redshift bins, $0.17\leq z<0.35$ and $0.35\leq z<0.5$, corresponding to effective redshifts of $z_{\rm eff}=0.25$ and $0.43$, with the BAO feature detected at approximately the $4\sigma$ level or above in both bins. By fitting the measured correlation functions, we obtain dilation parameters of $\alpha=0.97\pm0.07$ and $\alpha=1.01\pm0.04$, corresponding to volume-averaged distance measurements of $D_V/r_d=6.70\pm0.48$ and $D_V/r_d=11.25\pm0.47$, respectively. Using the Planck 2018 sound horizon calibration, we obtain $H_0=66.6 \pm 4.9 \,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}$ within the flat $\Lambda$CDM framework. We further combine our cluster BAO measurements with cosmic microwave background and Pantheon+ Type Ia supernova observations to constrain dynamical dark energy within the Chevallier–Polarski–Linder parametrization. The resulting constraints are consistent with $\Lambda$CDM, which lies within the $68\%$ joint credible region in the $w_0$–$w_a$ plane. Our results demonstrate the potential of galaxy clusters as complementary tracers for BAO measurements and cosmological constraints.

20. Evolving stellar wind environment around M dwarfs and its influence on magnetic star-planet interactions[2611.11642]
Abstract

M dwarfs are the most abundant group of stars in the Galaxy and they are expected to host a large number of exoplanets orbiting in the habitable zone. It is believed that stellar winds of M dwarfs can have a substantial effect on these exoplanets and on the observations of these systems. However, a systematic investigation of how the stellar winds of M dwarfs varies on timescales of a few months to years and how this impacts exoplanets around these stars is missing. We aim to investigate how variability of magnetic field properties of M dwarfs affects their stellar winds, which is essential for future studies of star-planet interactions (SPIs) and planetary habitability. Such variability might shed light on whether SPI signatures are going to repeat. We have compiled a total of 18 magnetic field maps for 6 M dwarfs, with masses between 0.1 and 0.6 $M_\odot$, and rotation periods between 2.7 and 175.6 days. We use these maps as input of 3D magnetohydrodynamic stellar wind simulations. We derive the mass-loss rate, the angular momentum-loss rate, and the Alfvén radius. For the stars with confirmed exoplanets (DS Leo, YZ Cet, and GJ 1151) we investigate the variability of stellar wind properties at the orbit. We find mass-loss rates between $1.0\times10^{-14}~M_\odot~{\rm yr^{-1}}$ and $2.3 \times 10^{-13}~M_\odot~{\rm yr^{-1}}$ and angular momentum-loss rates between $4.6 \times 10^{27}~{\rm erg}$ and $4.9 \times 10^{31}~{\rm erg}$. For individual stars the Alfvén radius, mass-loss rate, and angular momentum-loss rate varied with time by up to a factor of 1.5, 3.8, and 7.4 respectively. Our results follow the observational relation between mass-loss rate and X-ray flux. Due to the time-varying Alfvén surface, exoplanets orbiting DS Leo and YZ Cet move in and out of the sub-Alfvénic regime, affecting the nature of star-planet interactions and prospects for their detection.

21. Vacuum energy interacting with dark matter and dark radiation: alleviating the $H_0$-$S_8$ seesaw[2611.11835]
Abstract

Extensions of $\Lambda$CDM that raise $H_0$ with additional energy before recombination, such as dark radiation and early dark energy (EDE), also raise $S_8$. We study whether a late-time energy exchange between the vacuum and the dark sector can offset this increase. The coupling rate scales as $\bar m H^{\alpha}$ and is negligible before recombination for $\alpha \le -2$; the energy is transferred along the four-velocity of geodesic cold dark matter, so the dark matter produced by the vacuum carries no density perturbation and slows the growth of structure. We add the exchange to dark radiation ($\bar m\tilde\Lambda$CDM) and to a one-parameter EDE model ($\bar m$EDE), and constrain the models with Planck 2018, DESI DR2 BAO and Pantheon+ data (CBS), and with the SH0ES calibration added (CBSH) to test the exchange when $H_0$ is held at a high value. For CBS the posteriors favour an energy flow from the vacuum into dark matter at 1.8-2.7$\sigma$, and the exchange lowers $S_8$ by 0.02-0.08 but does not raise $H_0$: $\bar m\tilde\Lambda$CDM gives $H_0 = 68.83 \pm 0.91$ km s$^{-1}$ Mpc$^{-1}$, 3.1$\sigma$ below SH0ES. For CBSH the exchange removes the increase of $S_8$ caused by dark radiation, giving $S_8 = 0.800 \pm 0.016$ at $H_0 = 71.34 \pm 0.70$ km s$^{-1}$ Mpc$^{-1}$, and about a third of that caused by EDE for $\alpha = -2$, with the coupling at 1.1-2.0$\sigma$. The reduction of $S_8$ is concentrated at $z \lesssim 0.3$: at the redshifts probed by KiDS-Legacy the lensing amplitude decreases by only 1-2%, the shear $\chi^2$ of the dark-radiation models changes by less than 3, and the exchange reduces but does not remove the excess shear $\chi^2$ of EDE. A late-time exchange can therefore offset part of the $S_8$ increase of early-time solutions, but it does not ease the Hubble tension, and current data do not require it.

22. The Dusty Circumstellar Structure of the Yellow Hypergiant IRC +10420: Constraints from the SED and Near-Infrared Imaging Polarimetry[2611.11853]
Abstract

The yellow hypergiant IRC +10420 is a post-red supergiant evolving toward higher temperatures. We model its circumstellar dust with the radiative transfer code RADMC-3D, including polarized scattering, constrained by the spectral energy distribution (SED) from 0.4 $\mu$m to 1.3 mm and by the K' (2.2 $\mu$m) imaging polarimetry of Shenoy et al. (2015), assuming a pole-on view. The model consists of an equatorial structure (a flared inner disk of 0.3 $\mu$m grains at 300-3000 au, a ring at 3000-16,000 au whose surface density peaks at $\approx 10^4$ au, and a thin mid-plane disk of 300 $\mu$m grains) embedded in a spherical envelope of 0.15 $\mu$m grains with $\rho \propto r^{-2.3}$. It reproduces the SED within 0.07 dex and the total- and polarized-intensity profiles; the fractional polarization peaks at 15% at 1.7 arcsec (observed: 14%). The rising surface density of the ring produces the polarized-intensity plateau, and 0.5 $\mu$m grains at its outer edge the decline beyond 2 arcsec; grains of $\gtrsim 0.7$ $\mu$m are excluded. A thin disk of large grains suggests a settled mid-plane. Stellar temperatures of 7000-8500 K fit equally well. Of the total extinction ($A_V \approx 6.9$ mag), 5 mag is interstellar. Most of the $7.4 \times 10^{-2}$ $M_\odot$ of dust is equatorial; the envelope implies a mass-loss rate of $\approx 10^{-5}$-$10^{-4}$ ($\psi$/100) $M_\odot$ yr$^{-1}$. Because equatorial dust redirects starlight poleward, the pole-on SED overestimates the luminosity by $\approx 1.5$; at the Gaia distance, $L_\star \approx 3.3 \times 10^5$ $L_\odot$. Subtracting a scaled stellar PSF removes most of the nebular light within $\approx 1$ arcsec.

23. Ejecta fate and deposition in the Didymos-Dimorphos system after the DART impact[2611.11883]
Abstract

Kinetic impact is a promising technique for asteroid deflection in planetary defense. The NASA Double Asteroid Redirection Test (DART) spacecraft collided with Dimorphos, the satellite of the binary asteroid (65803) Didymos, at 6.1449 km s^{-1} in 2022. Observations revealed changes in both the orbit of Dimorphos around Didymos and the heliocentric orbit of the Didymos-Dimorphos system. The ESA Hera spacecraft will arrive at the system at the end of 2026 to investigate the post-impact surfaces. Here, we present an analytical model, based on Maxwell's Z model, to predict ejecta behavior following the DART impact. The model provides the relationships among ejection velocity, ejection angle, and ejecta mass, allowing the momentum enhancement factor beta to be estimated for both Dimorphos and the Didymos-Dimorphos system. We show that the observed beta can be reproduced when 0.3-1% of the impact kinetic energy is converted into ejecta kinetic energy and the effective strength of the target is less than 100 Pa. The escaping mass is estimated to be 0.3-3.0% of the mass of Dimorphos. The globally averaged and minimum ejecta thicknesses on Dimorphos are estimated to be 2.0 m and 0.5 m, respectively, indicating that the entire surface was resurfaced by ejecta deposition, although boulders larger than 1 m were only partially buried. The model also predicts the momentum partition parameter kappa_p to be as low as 0.6, suggesting that the mass and the bulk density of Dimorphos may be revised upward. These predictions provide quantitative constraints for interpreting forthcoming Hera observations.

24. The shape and velocity of black hole coronas[2611.11884]
Abstract
