1. General-purpose equations of state for hadron-quark matter: first application to accretion-induced phase transitions in neutron stars[2610.30233]
Abstract

Strong first-order phase transitions in the cores of neutron stars (NSs) can have profound implications for astrophysical observables, however, their existence remains an open question. Since most NSs form in binary systems, they are expected to experience prolonged episodes of mass accretion that gradually increase their central density and can potentially trigger such a transition. This might, in turn, generate observable multi-messenger signals, as suggested by previous studies. Motivated by this prospect, we generate a new set of equations of state for hadron–quark matter with first-order phase transitions of various strengths, including neutrino absorption opacities in the quark phase as well. We then perform general-relativistic radiation hydrodynamics simulations of NSs undergoing accretion-induced phase transitions. In contrast to previous findings, our results indicate that these systems do not produce unbound ejecta for the models considered, making accretion-induced phase transitions unlikely sources of observable electromagnetic transients. However, we find that transitions are accompanied by intense neutrino emission, with peak luminosities reaching $\sim10^{52}~\mathrm{erg/s}$ for the strongest transition considered. To facilitate future numerical studies, we make the family of equations of state and neutrino opacities used in this work publicly available, enabling the systematic exploration of the role of the phase transition strength.

2. The coupled impacts of atmospheric dynamics and cloud microphysics on hot Jupiter atmospheres. I. An application to WASP-43b[2610.30243]
Abstract

Understanding the influence of cloud-circulation feedback on planetary climates is crucial to interpreting solar system and extrasolar planet observations. Hot Jupiter phase curve observations have reported westward optical phase curve offsets and weak absorption features, which may be explained by the presence of clouds. JWST observations have also revealed tentative silicate cloud absorption features in emission and transmission spectroscopy. While a number of 3D atmospheric dynamics models of hot Jupiters and microphysical models describing cloud formation exist, few combine these two approaches. To help elucidate the impact of clouds on hot Jupiters, we introduce a post-processing scheme with the 3D GCM ADAM with SPARC (formerly SPARC/MITgcm), the 1D microphysical cloud model CARMA, and the radiative transfer code PICASO. We first simulate the cloud-free atmosphere of WASP-43b with ADAM, then incorporate resulting thermal profiles into CARMA to simulate WASP-43b's cloud structure, and finally generate phase-dependent emission spectra from our results with PICASO. By comparing to JWST observations, we infer that WASP-43b has a cloudy nightside, a mostly clear morning and evening limb with patchy clouds, and a clear dayside at the level of the photosphere. Our microphysical model predicts the condensation of five cloud species, including silicates, but overestimates the cloud opacity at all phases compared to observations, requiring additional modeling to explain. The results from this post-processing setup are promising for more meaningful characterization of WASP-43b's atmosphere, and will serve as a comparison baseline for our upcoming indirect coupling of both models, as well as for other future studies.

3. 2Flow2SN: Dusty Supernova Cosmology with Photometric Redshifts and Contamination using Neural Simulation-Based Inference[2610.30244]
Abstract

Photometric Type Ia supernova (SN Ia) cosmology in the era of the Vera C. Rubin Observatory's Legacy Survey of Space and Time (LSST) demands statistical frameworks that simultaneously account for selection effects, photometric redshift uncertainties and non-Ia contamination at scale. We present FlowSN2, a modular statistical framework combining hierarchical Bayesian modelling and simulation-based inference (SBI), that reframes photometric SN cosmology from a regression task to a joint inference problem over light curve summaries and photometric redshifts. By employing neural likelihood estimation via normalising flows, FlowSN2 approximates the data distributions of selected objects in a form that can be reused across different cosmological models, achieving amortisation over the Hubble diagram. This amortisation enables inference across alternative cosmological parameterisations, such as $w_0w_a\mathrm{CDM}$, away from the fiducial training cosmology. The framework integrates a standardisation model that disentangles intrinsic SN variations from extrinsic host-galaxy dust effects and adopts a principled Bayesian resampling approach to propagate SN classification uncertainty, without requiring an explicit contaminant likelihood. Across simulated tests spanning five cosmological parameter sets, our framework recovers true cosmological and population parameters, with posteriors in close agreement with both tractable analytical solutions and pure-Ia baselines. Conveniently, the framework enables Bayesian model comparison at negligible additional computational cost, providing a valuable tool for addressing debates surrounding SN standardisation and evolving dark energy. Capable of scaling to over a hundred thousand SNe without retraining, FlowSN2 offers an interpretable and statistically principled solution for cosmological inference with upcoming wide-field photometric surveys.

4. The physical and chemical structure of Titan's upper atmosphere: energy balance, photochemistry and Jeans escape[2610.30258]
Abstract

Titan's upper atmospheric layers provide a unique opportunity to study how photochemistry, photoionisation, haze microphysics and escape processes shape its atmospheric chemical structure, providing a unique benchmark for understanding these processes in exoplanets. Using a 1D first-principles approach, we extend the Kompot model to simulate Titan's upper atmosphere and investigate its thermal and chemical structure, and Jeans escape. We performed self-consistent simulations of Titan's upper atmosphere, above the dense haze layer, over a 1D radial grid by solving the coupled hydrodynamics, thermal and photochemical kinetic, and energy balance equations. We considered heating by stellar X-ray and ultraviolet (XUV) and infrared (IR) radiation, and cooling due to radiative emission of methane (CH4) and hydrogen cyanide (HCN). We also included additional nineteen photochemical and 117 thermal reactions to Kompot's standard chemical network. Our modelled atmospheric temperature structures lie within the observed variability of Titan's atmospheric temperature profile. Our simulations reproduce observed nitrogen (N2) and CH4 densities in the upper atmosphere, and abundance of minor chemical species also broadly agree with observationally derived abundances. Near the exobase, our CH4 mixing ratio overlap with Cassini-Huygens results, and our model H2 mixing ratios also exhibit broad agreement with observations. Our simulations suggest no significant thermal loss of CH4, even under the conditions of an early Sun's XUV environment.

5. Density-split analyses of the pairwise kinetic Sunyaev-Zeldovich effect[2610.30267]
Abstract

We consider the dependence of the kinetic Sunyaev-Zeldovich (kSZ) effect on the environments of foreground cosmological structures. Standard pairwise kSZ estimators exploit the fact that galaxies below separations of $\sim 200\,h^{-1}{\rm M}_\odot$ fall towards each other on average, yielding a negative mean pairwise kSZ signal. However, this signal is the residual from a near-cancellation between outflows from underdense regions and infall towards overdensities giving a large variance in the pairwise velocity distribution and hence a low signal-to-noise ratio for the mean kSZ signal. This effect is especially marked at the largest scales, where the pairwise velocity distribution becomes more symmetric about zero. We show that splitting galaxies according to their local overdensity allows the convergence and divergence flows to be separated, yielding pairwise kSZ signals of opposing sign, with a reduction in variance for each subset. We use simulations to perform a detailed analysis of this phenomenon, comparing the cosmological information content in the global pairwise kSZ signal with our new density-split pairwise kSZ estimator. We consider galaxy catalogues comparable to datasets from the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument, together with cosmic microwave background instrumental noise characteristic of recent and planned ground-based experiments. We predict that the density-split pairwise kSZ will increase the significance of detection by only a modest factor; nevertheless, we expect improvements by factors of respectively 11 and 4 in constraining $\sigma_8$ and $\Omega_{\rm m}$ when using the combination of Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument and Simons Observatory Large Aperture Telescope surveys.

6. Optimal cosmological cross-correlation analysis with U-Net-based processing[2610.30294]
Abstract

Cosmological cross-correlation analyses are commonly based on two-point statistics, which are optimal for Gaussian random fields but do not capture all relevant information in the presence of non-Gaussianity, which is ubiquitous in late-time cosmic fields. This motivates investigations of higher order statistics, and one method is to use machine learning to construct a correlation-optimized tracer field that can utilize cross-correlation information beyond two-point statistics. Using a tracer field as input and a paired target field as part of the cost function, we test whether a U-Net can learn a transformed tracer field that maximally correlates with the target field. We train a 1D U-Net on paired simulated fields and study controlled cases based on Gaussian and log-normal random fields. We train and quantify performance using the Pearson correlation coefficient with the target field and compare the network output with the analytic linear solution known in the Gaussian limit. For Gaussian fields, the U-Net reproduces the expected linear solution closely and behaves as an effective matched filter in frequency space. For log-normal fields, the network departs from the linear estimator and achieves an improved correlation with the target field, increasing the mean Pearson correlation from 0.857 for the linear estimator to 0.953 for the U-Net output. This indicates that the network is sensitive to information beyond the power spectrum. These controlled 1D experiments show that U-Net-based tracer transformation can effectively extract correlation-relevant information beyond the power spectrum, providing a promising framework for enhancing cosmological cross-correlation analyses.

7. Solar Photospheric Flows and the Origin of Active Regions[2610.30333]
Abstract

The early evolutionary stages of some active regions (ARs) are analysed with the aim of resolving one of the paramount-importance problems of heliophysics: what plays a primary, active role in the formation of active regions and sunspots—magnetic fields or motion of solar plasma? In particular, the first view underlies the popular model of rising $\Omega$-shaped, twisted flux-tube loop of strong magnetic field. In this study, optical images obtained by the Helioseismic and Magnetic Imager (HMI) of the Solar Dynamics Observatory are used along with full-vector maps of magnetic field based on HMI data. Maps of fluid-particle tracks are constructed using local correlation tracking of photospheric brightness elements. Comparisons of optical and magnetic maps for three developing active regions confirm the conclusion made by Bumba and Howard in the mid-1960s that the supergranulation pattern is not destroyed by the emerging magnetic field of the developing AR, which would be inevitable if a loop of the strong-magnetic-field flux tube rose through the subphotospheric layers. There are no signs of spreading flows on scales comparable to the size of the whole active region. Various forms of dissymmetry between the leading and trailing parts of ARs are revealed. Next, an example of a well-developed sunspot group is considered, which demonstrates that the twist of the magnetic configuration may develop much later than the group starts growing. It is concluded that plasma flows appear to be determinant with respect to magnetic fields. The considered scenarios of the origin of ARs are in sharp contradiction with the rising-tube model.

9. Ellipsoidal Modeling Framework for TNOs: the triaxial shape of (50000) Quaoar and its implications for Weywot and the dynamical environment[2610.30494]
Abstract

The three-dimensional shapes of Trans-Neptunian Objects (TNOs) are notoriously difficult to determine due to their slow orbital motion and minimal variations in aspect angle over human timescales. To overcome this geometric degeneracy, we developed a versatile modeling framework capable of deriving ellipsoidal shapes by simultaneously combining stellar occultations and rotational light curves, including parameterizations of localized surface albedo. We applied this methodology to the large TNO (50000) Quaoar, jointly modeling 37 stellar occultation events and two photometric datasets. Our MCMC analysis demonstrates that Quaoar is best represented by a triaxial shape with equatorial semi-axes $a = 620.7^{+3.3}_{-2.8}$ km and $b = 546.5^{+6.0}_{-5.6}$ km, and a polar semi-axis $c = 511.5^{+5.3}_{-5.5}$ km, yielding a bulk density of $1.663 \pm 0.127$ g cm$^{-3}$. By isolating Quaoar's barycentric motion from the astrometric offsets, we determined the mass of its satellite, Weywot, to be $(1.485 \pm 0.360) \times 10^{18}$ kg, corresponding to a system mass ratio of $q = 0.0012 \pm 0.0003$ and a satellite bulk density of $0.95 \pm 0.60$ g cm$^{-3}$. Furthermore, dynamical simulations using the derived triaxial gravitational field reveal that potential additional satellites can only maintain stable orbits beyond 2.4 Quaoar radii, as inner bodies would be strongly perturbed. Regarding the ring system, particles at the location of the secondary ring (Q2R) location exhibit libration associated with the 5:7 spin-orbit resonance (SOR). Similarly, particles at the location of the main ring (Q1R) location show libration of the 2:6 SOR angle for orbital eccentricities larger than 0.2, providing a plausible mechanism for the azimuthal width variations observed in Q1R.

10. Simulations of a Conducting Sphere Moving through Magnetized Plasma II: Effect of Internal Magnetic Fields and Enhancement of Alfvénic Drag[2610.30506]
Abstract

Magnetic interaction between astrophysical objects can play an important role and produce electromagnetic radiation in various systems, ranging from planet-moon and star-planet systems to binary compact objects. We extend our previous numerical study of an ideal magnetized plasma flowing past a conducting sphere to the case where the sphere is magnetized. Such a flow generates magnetic disturbances and produces a drag force on the sphere. We examine the drag coefficient as a function of the strength and orientation of the conductor's internal magnetic field relative to the background magnetic field. We find that a strong internal magnetic field that is aligned with the background field significantly enhances the drag coefficient compared to the unmagnetized case; this enhancement is related to the increased magnetic flux that connects the conductor and the open, external field region. We also explore how the drag coefficient changes as the flow transitions from the sub-Alfvénic to the super-Alfvénic regime. Our results suggest that magnetic interaction may play a larger role in orbital evolution of some binary systems than previously expected.

11. ULTRAMAN: A Proposed ultrasound detector for macroscopic dark matter[2610.30507]
Abstract

We propose ULTRAMAN (ULTRAsound Macro ANalyzer), a passive ultrasound array experiment designed to search for macroscopic dark matter (macros) through coherent acoustic disturbances generated as compact objects traverse a controlled target medium. Unlike existing dark matter detectors that primarily rely on scintillation, ionization, or other signatures of energy deposition, ULTRAMAN exploits the acoustic response of matter to reconstruct individual highly supersonic trajectories using synchronized multielement ultrasound arrays. The framework connects macro stopping power and interaction cross section to the resulting acoustic pressure, while the expected event rate probes the macro mass and abundance. A genuine candidate would appear not as an isolated radio frequency spike, but as a correlated multichannel track consistent with a single highly supersonic passage through the target. Representative macro, nuclearite, axion quark nugget, and primordial black hole benchmarks are shown to occupy distinct regions of the resulting acoustic observable space. Existing ultrasound technology offers a realistic route to an event-level terrestrial search for macroscopic dark matter, with null observations translating into complementary constraints on its parameter space.

Abstract

OGLE microlensing of the Magellanic Clouds sets the leading bounds on primordial black holes (PBHs) as dark matter from $10^{-8}$ to $10^{3}\,M_\odot$. Holding the detected events and survey efficiencies fixed, we show that changes to the Milky Way halo, the stellar lens populations and the Large Magellanic Cloud halo, each motivated by recent data, weaken the published strict 20-year bound by up to $\simeq6$, and by over an order of magnitude in an extreme stress test. 13. Quasi-Localization of Short-Wavelength Perturbations in Softened Laplace-Lagrange Chains[2610.30515]
Abstract

We examine the long-term evolution of localized eccentricity perturbations in a softened Laplace-Lagrange chain. The secular coupling between mass elements is long-ranged, suggesting that any disturbance imposed at a single radius should disperse rapidly across the chain. We show that this expectation fails once the secular interaction is regularized by a finite softening length $\beta$: while broad eccentricity profiles propagate smoothly, as textbook secular theory anticipates, narrow profiles remain confined to their launching site for millions of secular periods. The origin of this quasi-localization lies in the dispersion relation of the softened chain. The group velocity of eccentricity waves collapses by orders of magnitude on spatial scales below the softening length, so that narrow perturbations project almost entirely onto a nearly flat sector of the band, where little differential precession is available to disperse them. Propagating the initial wavenumber spectrum through the dispersion relation reproduces the retained fraction of angular momentum deficit across the full range of perturbation widths with no adjustable parameters. The confined perturbation is flanked by an apsidally polarized (oppositely-aligned) response, which we show to be the real-space imprint of this spectral projection. The softening length therefore sets a dynamical scale below which eccentricity perturbations spread only on timescales far longer than the secular period.

15. Early panchromatic spectra reveal helium in the thermonuclear supernova 2024kce[2610.30674]
Abstract

Faint Type I supernovae blur the boundaries between spectroscopic classes, obscuring their progenitors and explosion mechanisms. One proposed origin is a double detonation, in which ignition of an accreted helium shell triggers the explosion of a carbon oxygen white dwarf. Surviving helium is a direct prediction of this mechanism, but its strongest near-infrared feature overlaps with carbon and is ambiguous in isolation. Here we present SN 2024kce, observed with simultaneous optical and near-infrared spectroscopy eleven days before maximum light. We identify He I absorption at three wavelengths, all consistent with a velocity of 16,400 km/s, well above the photosphere. This is the first detection of helium before maximum light in a thermonuclear supernova. Helium at these velocities requires nearby radioactive material for non-thermal excitation, pointing to ash from a detonated surface helium shell. A double detonation model reproduces the optical spectral evolution, suppressed blue flux, and He I 1.083 micron absorption feature. SN 2024kce shares properties with both subluminous Type Ia supernovae and Ia-like calcium-rich transients, providing evidence that the same explosion mechanism operates across these classes. From early optical and near-infrared spectra to Rubin's First Look images, SN 2024kce showcases the full reach of modern observatories in exposing the physics of rare explosions.

Abstract

Recent DESI baryon acoustic oscillation (BAO) analyses suggest a preference for an evolving dark energy equation of state (EoS) that crosses the phantom divide $w=-1$. 18. Distinct stellar-parameter dependencies of granulation and mesogranulation in red giants[2610.30805]
Abstract

Granulation and mesogranulation represent different spatial and temporal scales of convection, but are commonly assumed to follow the same stellar atmospheric scaling relations in red giants. We test this assumption using the largest sample assembled for this purpose to date – 9,328 Kepler red giants spanning the red giant branch and red clump phase, cross-matched with APOGEE stellar parameters. We model each star's power spectrum background to obtain the radius-corrected amplitude and characteristic frequency of both components, and derive empirical scaling relations with effective temperature, surface gravity, and metallicity through Bayesian model comparison. Gaussian Process and symbolic regression provide independent validation. We find that granulation and mesogranulation are not self-similar: their surface-gravity dependencies are statistically indistinguishable, consistent with a shared envelope geometry, but their temperature dependencies diverge: mesogranulation's amplitude rises steeply with Teff, whereas granulation's shows no statistically justified dependence, and both frequencies respond weakly and similarly. We propose that this pattern is consistent with granulation forming in a thin near-surface layer, where radiation takes over from convection and the Mach number depends only weakly on Teff. Mesogranulation originates deeper, where convection carries the full flux and its Mach number and cell size increase with Teff. Metallicity raises both amplitudes but has little effect on the frequencies. Our results indicate that the self-similarity assumption underlying two-component background models does not hold, with direct implications for background modeling and its downstream application in Galactic archaeology.

19. Relativistic Dynamical Evolution of Kilonova Spectral Emission[2610.30879]
Abstract

We develop a relativistic dynamical model for the thermal evolution of expanding kilonova ejecta, in which the effective temperature is determined by the ejecta mass, opacity, velocity, and radioactive heating rate. The resulting thermal evolution follows a broken power law as the ejecta transition from the optically thick to the optically thin regime. We incorporate this thermal history into a relativistic spectral calculation that accounts for the equal-arrival-time surface geometry and Doppler boosting, enabling us to model the observed kilonova spectrum and constrain the underlying ejecta properties. We investigate the effects of the model parameters and relativistic corrections on the observed temperature and spectral shape, and find that light-travel-time delays and Doppler shifts can produce significant spectral distortions at high ejecta velocities. We apply the model to the early-time X-shooter spectrum of the kilonova AT2017gfo and infer the ejecta mass, opacity, and velocity through Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) fitting with a Student's $t$ likelihood. We find that relativistic effects become increasingly important for fast ejecta ($v \gtrsim 0.5c$), particularly at large viewing angles, where the observed temperature can differ substantially from the rest-frame effective temperature. These results highlight the importance of incorporating relativistic and equal-arrival-time effects when interpreting early-time kilonova spectra, especially for rapidly expanding ejecta.

Abstract

Shock breakout (SBO) occurs when a radiation-mediated shock wave, formed during a core-collapse supernova (SN) explosion, breaks out from the stellar surface of the progenitor or from the circumstellar material (CSM), if the CSM is optically thick. The X-ray outburst XRO 080109, associated with a Type Ib/c SN 2008D, has been suggested to be an SBO event from a regular supernova. The recent detection of EP260321a, associated with a broad-lined Type Ic SN 2026gzf, by the Einstein Probe adds a new SBO event. We show that the non-detections in radio follow-up observations of EP260321a imply a shock velocity $\lesssim0.1c$, whereas the radio data of XRO 080109 suggest a shock velocity of $\simeq0.2c$. Finally, the lower shock velocity and larger CSM mass can be unified in a picture in which a more massive CSM decelerates the radiation-mediated shock more significantly before it breaks out.

71. An E-value Reanalysis of DESI's Dynamical Dark Energy Signal[2607.28918]
Abstract

Cosmological surveys such as DESI release data in stages, and each release reprocesses earlier observations and supersedes its predecessor. Testing the same hypothesis at every release inflates the false-positive rate, and only the collaboration can estimate the cross-release covariance that an exact combination needs. We show how to combine evidence across such releases using public products alone. From each release we compute an e-value, a non-negative statistic with expectation at most one under the null, and we average these e-values with weights fixed in advance. Provided each release's own likelihood is correct, this average controls the false-positive rate at every release under arbitrary dependence between releases. For DESI BAO at a fixed Planck background, DR2 alone gives an e-value of $33.97$ against $\Lambda$CDM ($p \le 0.0295$) under our reference test specification, a flat prior on $(w_0, w_a)$ over a fixed box. The equal-weight average of DR1 and DR2 is $17.51$ ($p \le 0.0571$), below the rejection threshold of $20$. We did not fix these weights before DR2 was published, and the weights we now fix ahead of DR3 give $p \le 0.0874$ at DR2. Without a model of the cross-release covariance, an exact combination that counts each observation once can take any value. Under explicit models it ranges from below the threshold to nearly double the DR2 value, depending on the structure assumed. The evidence is concentrated in the LRG2 bin, which also changed most between releases. Without it the DR2 e-value falls to $0.49$, and a look-elsewhere correction over the seven redshift bins leaves no rejection. DR2 does not reject under wider priors either. The signal is therefore fragile and specification-dependent. We recommend that surveys report per-release e-values with a stated test specification and a combination rule fixed in advance, and publish the cross-release covariance.
