1. A Burst of Reionization in the Dark Ages[2610.12552]
Abstract

Primordial overdensities at small scales can be significantly larger than those observed at large scales. As a result, they can collapse much earlier, at redshifts $z \sim 100$. In these collapsed halos, stars and black holes can form, where they can then contribute to an early reionization of the universe. If this epoch is temporary, the free electrons will recombine yielding a low level of ionization leading into cosmic dawn. This contributes to the optical depth of the universe, and adds more weakly to low-$\ell$ polarization signals of ionization compared to later sources. We study the effects of a redshift-localized source of reionization $\Delta \tau_{burst}$ in the context of a broad suite of cosmological data to constrain this scenario, finding a preference of approximately $\sim 2-2.5\sigma$ for a new contribution. Regardless of Planck reprocessing used (PR3 - SRoll2+plik or PR4 - LoLLiPoP+CamSpec/HiLLiPoP), the results are consistent, favoring a new contribution $\Delta \tau_{burst} \sim 0.012$ ($0.001 < \Delta \tau_{burst} < 0.022$ at 95\%) and a similar total optical depth $\tau \sim 0.073$. The precise era of reionization is somewhat discrepant depending on the higher-$\ell$ reprocessing used, with HiLLiPoP favoring $z\sim 45$, CamSpec $z\sim 100$ and plik $z\sim 165$. If such a source is present at $40\lesssim z\lesssim150$, LiteBIRD should be able to detect it, while Simons Observatory should also constrain it.

2. The accretionally heated atmospheres of protoplanets and brown dwarfs[2610.12562]
Abstract

The ranks of accreting planets and brown dwarfs are growing. To help interpret their spectra, we study how accretion affects a planet's atmosphere. The atmosphere is irradiated from above by whatever nonthermal radiation from the accretion shock does not directly escape into space. This accretional heating drives a temperature inversion. Even relatively slow accretion, corresponding to mass doubling times longer than system ages, can raise temperatures by many hundreds of Kelvin at ambient pressures of order millibars. The heated atmosphere radiates an optical/infrared excess. For environments that are not too dusty, the excess concentrates in molecular lines that trace the heated layer. Thus an accretionally heated atmosphere may be distinguished by its muted absorption bands. Irradiation from the accretion shock also changes the chemical composition of the atmosphere, further altering spectra. Atmospheric retrievals promise to map out the deposition of accretional energy, helping to decide between ”hot” and ”cold” start modes of planet formation.

3. The road to $H_0$, $Ω_m$, and $w$ with a global neutrino-telescope network[2610.12577]
Abstract

The newly measured correlation between the neutrino and X-ray luminosities of Seyfert galaxies has yielded the first neutrino-based measurement of the Hubble constant. Building on this result, we forecast the reach of a global neutrino-telescope network by 2030, utilizing full-sky IceCube exposures combined with KM3NeT-ARCA and Baikal-GVD. We find that this 2030 configuration sharpens the neutrino-measured Hubble constant from today's $H_0 = 66^{+44}_{-37}$ to $H_0 =66^{+15}_{-13}$ km s$^{-1}$ Mpc$^{-1}$. Additionally, we project that the disk-corona slope of the neutrino X-ray luminosity relation ($L_\nu\propto L_X^\beta$) will reach $\beta=0.72\pm0.04$, enabling a $\geq \!5\sigma$ exclusion of the calorimetric value $\beta=1$. Finally, we show that, if a high-redshift population of neutrino sources, such as JWST's "little red dots," is identified and obeys the same luminosity relation, we can obtain a new independent measurement of the matter density $\Omega_m$ and the dark energy equation-of-state parameter $w$.

4. Nailing down the Hubble constant value through time delay cosmography: A 3% measurement from SN Refsdal[2610.12595]
Abstract

We present the first inference of the Hubble constant and matter density parameter from a strongly lensed supernova (SN) by exploiting the mass model of a galaxy cluster enhanced by an extended surface brightness reconstruction. This has resulted in the most precise H0 measurement from a single strong lens so far, outperforming the 3.9% result from the lensed quasar DES J048-5354. Building upon the mass model of MACS J1149.5+2223 relying on image positions only, we incorporate the surface brightness distribution of the SN Refsdal host, to infer some cosmological parameter values within a flat LCDM framework. We consider two cosmological parameterizations, with either H0 and $\Omega_m$ or H0 alone treated as free parameters. We explore four different mass-model realizations to assess some of the systematic uncertainties associated with the constraint weighting and source reconstruction. Although known as one of the main systematic effects, we deliberately keep the cluster total mass parametrization fixed to that of the previously published point-like mass model to enable a fair comparison. This reveals that incorporating 77,000 HST pixels reduces the uncertainty on the inferred value of H0 by a factor of 2.0. Our final measurements are $H_0=65.0^{+2.0}_{-1.8}$ km/s/Mpc and $\Omega_m=0.30^{+0.08}_{-0.09}$, in agreement with previous results from lensed SNe. This corresponds to a substantially improved precision, reaching a remarkable 3.0% uncertainty on the value of H0. The contributions of the uncertainties on the lens total mass and on the measured time delays to the final H0 error are now comparable, indicating that future improvements will depend on more precise time-delay measurements and continued control of systematic effects. With sub-percent time-delay values reachable at cluster-scale, this mass model approach has the power to lead to unprecedentedly precise H0 measurements.

5. Dust-driven vertical shear instability in an isothermal shearing box: I. Turbulence and gas dynamics without dust feedback[2610.12648]
Abstract

The classical vertical shear instability (VSI) is a baroclinic instability that arises from height-dependent azimuthal velocities and is a leading candidate for driving anisotropic turbulence in discs with extended vertical modes. The classical VSI requires fast cooling times and radial temperature gradients, which are absent in an isothermal stratified (barotropic) setup. We investigate the dust-driven vertical shear instability in an isothermal, stratified shearing box, where the VSI is generated by drag forces acting on the gas due to dust rather than radial thermal stratification. We construct two-fluid (gas+dust) vertically stratified equilibria including dust diffusion and use these profiles to initialise 2D (radial-vertical) hydrodynamical simulations with FARGO3D. To separate dust-driven vertical shear instability (DVSI) from drag instabilities, we employ a dust-analogue model in which dust-induced drag on the gas is prescribed as a time-invariant and height-dependent acceleration, such that the underlying spatial distribution of the dust is assumed to be constant. The DVSI grows from regions of strongest vertical shear away from the midplane, exciting predominantly radially short, vertically extended disturbances (large k_x/k_z) and producing banded structures in the perturbed azimuthal and vertical velocities during the linear phase. In the nonlinear regime, the instability saturates via a Kelvin-Helmholtz-like parasitic instability that breaks the coherent vertical-shear modes into smaller-scale eddies, which balance the growth of the vertically extended modes, driving sustained vertical stirring. Dust-driven vertical shear can drive anisotropic turbulence and vertical mixing in an isothermal model without invoking global thermal gradients and could play an important role in the stratified streaming instability.

6. The GAPS programme at TNG LXXXII. The first atmospheric investigation of the gas giant WASP-13b at high resolution with GIARPS[2610.12677]
Abstract

Low-density transiting gas giant exoplanets are key benchmarks for atmospheric studies. Despite their large-scale heights, many of them remain poorly characterised. We present the first atmospheric investigation at high-resolution of the inflated (R$_\mathrm{p}$$\sim$1.4 R$_{\mathrm{J}}$), hot Jupiter (T$\sim$1500$ \mathrm{K}$) WASP-13 b aiming at constraining its atmospheric composition and refine its orbital geometry. We analysed four transits observed with GIARPS at the TNG. Updated stellar and planetary parameters were re-derived from the most precise literature values, and a new Rossiter-McLaughlin analysis was performed to measure the projected spin-orbit angle ($\lambda$) and stellar rotational velocity ($v\sin{i_\star}$). Atmospheric signals were searched using high-resolution spectroscopy, both with cross-correlation and single-line analyses, targeting key atomic and molecular species (e.g. \ion{He}{I}, H$\alpha$, \ion{Na}{I}, H$_2$O, CO). We find a well-aligned orbit ($\lambda$ = 2.7$\pm$1.1$^\circ$) and $v\sin{i_\star}$ = 4.5$\pm$0.1 km s$^{-1}$, improving upon previous constraints. No significant atmospheric absorption is detected in either VIS or NIR data. Injection-and-recovery tests exclude an isothermal atmosphere with metallicity [M/H]$\lesssim$10$\times$[M/H]$_\odot$ and simultaneously a grey cloud top pressure P$_{\rm cloud}\gtrsim10$ mbar, under the assumption of chemical equilibrium. WASP-13 b appears to belong to the class of low-density hot Jupiters with weak or featureless transmission spectra, most likely shaped by the presence of high-altitude clouds. Although a highly metal-enriched atmosphere cannot be fully excluded, current evidence disfavours this scenario. Further observations with next-generation facilities will be essential to characterise the atmospheric properties of this challenging target.

7. Global analysis of tensor mode helicity[2610.12947]
Abstract

A detection of helicity in a primordial gravitational-wave background (GWB) would provide compelling evidence for parity violation in the early universe and point toward physics beyond the standard picture of inflation. Since big bang cosmology is not time translation invariant, we identify that the so-called “regular” and often-neglected “irregular” solutions are independent degrees of freedom that can in principle carry different, even opposite, helicities. We note that this possibility complicates the interpretation of a measured net helicity. To determine the sensitivity of various GW detector configurations to helicity, we introduce a universal helicity efficiency coefficient that provides a unified geometric description of helicity sensitivity across gravitational-wave detectors which generalizes and improves previous estimators. We apply this helicity efficiency coefficient to estimate the sensitivity of a range of future GW experiments to helicity across twenty orders of magnitude in frequency assuming a fully helical background with an amplitude just below the current constraints.

8. The Neptunian Final Parsec Problem: How Inertial Waves Enable Neptunes and Gas Giants to Survive Tidal Migration[2610.12968]
Abstract

Observations indicate that some hot Neptunes and Jupiters underwent high eccentricity migration (HEM). A longstanding issue facing HEM theory remains, however: in order to shrink their orbits by ${\sim}1{-}2$ orders of magnitude, planets must dissipate ${\gtrsim}10{\times}$ more orbital energy than is contained in their structural binding energy. Previous work suggests that excitation of planets' fundamental modes (”$f$-modes”) at very high eccentricities, $e{\sim}$0.99, can shrink semi-major axes by ${\sim}$an order of magnitude (${\sim}5{\rightarrow}0.5$ au) while safely bleeding tidal energy to space. We examine how planets traverse the remaining order of magnitude in orbital shrinkage, $a{\sim}$0.5${\rightarrow}$0.05 au, which still poses a danger for Neptunian planets due to their lower binding energies (their ”final parsec” problem). To assess whether tidally-migrating Neptunes balance their energy budgets, we perform coupled orbit/structure calculations tracking their frequency-dependent tidal response under inertial waves (IWs). We show that tidal radius inflation at moderate eccentricities sweeps Neptunes' fundamental modes back into resonance with tidal forcing; only a small amount of tidal heat relative to a Neptune's binding energy is needed to drive this $f$-mode excitation. This excitation of Neptunes' $f$-modes may offer a route to safe circularization, provided the $f$-mode dissipates without harming the planet. In contrast, eccentric gas giants ($e{\sim}0.9$) circularize without drama over ${\sim}$Gyr. Our theory predicts that warm Neptunes on a HEM track ($a{\gtrsim}0.1 \ \mathrm{au}, \ e{\gtrsim}0.8$) should be rare, in opposition to warm gas giants and lower eccentricity Neptunes. Observational confirmation of our predictions would constrain the interior structures of Neptunian planets via tidal theory.

9. Star Cluster Ecology VIII. – Dynamically Forming S-Type and $α$Centauri-like Systems[2610.13127]
Abstract

Aims: To analyse the impact of dynamically formed binaries emerging naturally from various star cluster environments on already established planetary systems. Methods: Numerical simulations replicating the star-forming region NGC 1333 are conducted. We consider three models, each using identical cluster realisations and simulation parameters. The cluster itself is initially sub-virial and fractally distributed. Model A assumes planetary systems are initially in mean motion resonance. In Model B, the same planetary systems are moved out of resonance, but remain intrinsically stable. Finally, Model C removes all planets from the simulation. Ten independent realisations are integrated per model until 30 Myr. Results: Dynamically formed binaries emerge at similar rates across models, indicating planets play little role in their formation. We also find a higher production rate of wide binaries compared to previous numerical studies which we attribute to the sub-virial and sub-clustered initial conditions. S-type and binary S-type planetary systems naturally emerge in our simulations, while P-type systems are rare. For Model A, the fraction of S-type or binary S-type systems is f_{\rm S}=0.02 and $f_{\rm Bin, S}=0.05, respectively. Rogue planets originating from a binary system are ejected with a substantially larger velocity than those around singletons. Planet-star collisions and planet capture events occur disproportionately within binary systems. Finally, several \alphaCentauri-like systems emerge in our simulations, suggesting that the nearest stellar system to the Sun may have a dynamical origin.

10. Formation Dynamics of a Rotating Solar Spicule-like Event and Associated Energy-Mass Transport[2610.13173]
Abstract

We performed three-dimensional radiative magnetohydrodynamic(3D RMHD) simulations in an upward-directed (positive-polarity) open magnetic field with an initial background strength of 10 G. We identified vortex-associated spicule-like events and carried out a detailed analysis of one representative event. Prior to the formation of this spicule-like event, a photospheric vortex appears and twists the magnetic field lines, then develops upward into higher atmospheric layers, forming a magnetic tornado system. This event is generated during this process. At the periphery of this magnetic tornado, twisted magnetic field lines with negative vertical components reconnect with both external positive-polarity background fields and central positive-polarity fields. The magnetic reconnection sites rise and rotate together with the developed magnetic tornado, and many jet-like substructures in this event are closely associated with plasma upward ejections from these reconnection regions. A vertical force analysis shows that, for this event, plasma uplift in the photosphere and lower chromosphere is dominated by the pressure-gradient force, whereas in the upper chromosphere, the Lorentz force gradually becomes dominant. At a height of about 4 Mm above the solar surface, this event produces a mean upward mass flux density on the order of $10^{-7}~{\rm kg~m^{-2}~s^{-1}}$ and a mean upward Poynting flux density of approximately $525~{\rm W~m^{-2}}$. The wave-mode decomposition results show that the energy transport associated with the rotational spicule-like event is dominated by the Alfvén mode. Alfvén waves, fast-mode waves, and slow-mode waves carry energy into the lower corona in an approximate ratio of $10:7:3$.

11. Flux Evolution of PSR B0540–69 and the Spectral Energy Distribution of its Pulsar Wind Nebula[2610.13182]
Abstract

The young Crab-like pulsar PSR B0540–69, located in the Large Magellanic Cloud, underwent a spin-down rate transition (SRT) in 2011 December, after which its pulsar wind nebula (PWN) was reported to brighten up to 2018. In this work, we extend the study of its X-ray and $\gamma$-ray flux evolution through 2026 by combining observations from multiple X-ray instruments with data from the Fermi Large Area Telescope, thereby constructing a flux history of the PSR$+$PWN system spanning more than two decades. We find that the enhanced X-ray luminosity of the PSR$+$PWN system has persisted for more than 14 yr after the SRT, up to early 2026, whereas the pulsed X-ray flux remains unchanged across all epochs. This confirms that the brightening originates from the PWN. In the $\gamma$-ray band, neither the pulsed nor the non-pulsed flux shows any detectable long-term variation between 2008 and 2025. Together, these results imply that the additional spin-down energy released at the SRT has been continuously injected into the PWN over decades, placing strong constraints on the energy budget and physical mechanism of the transition. Combining the non-pulsed X-ray and $\gamma$-ray fluxes measured here with previously published radio-to-UV data, we further construct a broadband spectral energy distribution of the PWN, which shows properties markedly different from those of the Crab Nebula.

12. Detailed study of HD 219134: a benchmark K-dwarf with a compact planetary system[2610.13200]
Abstract

Space missions such as CoRoT, Kepler, and TESS have transformed asteroseismology and its synergies with exoplanet science. We revisit the nearby K-dwarf HD 219134 to investigate the tension between asteroseismic and interferometric radii, update its fundamental parameters, and reconstruct the rotational, magnetic, and atmospheric evolution of the system. We combine forward asteroseismic modeling with seismic inversions to assess systematics from non-seismic constraints, radius measurements, and surface modeling. The revised stellar properties are then used for rotational evolution, stellar-wind, and high-energy irradiation calculations. Seismic inversions rule out the most recent interferometric radius, while the asteroseismic and spectroscopic results are compatible with the 2019 interferometric measurement. We obtain $M=0.779\pm0.015_\mathrm{(stat)}\pm0.006_\mathrm{(sys)}\ M_\odot$, $R=0.753\pm0.005_\mathrm{(stat)}\pm0.002_\mathrm{(sys)}\ R_\odot$, and an age of $8.92\pm0.89_\mathrm{(stat)}\pm0.19_\mathrm{(sys)}$ Gyr. These systematics include only non-seismic constraints; literature combined uncertainties from physical inputs and surface effects are $0.007M_\odot$, $0.002R_\odot$, and $0.81$ Gyr. Rotational and wind modelling shows that standard magnetic braking is broadly compatible with the present-day rotation and angular-momentum loss. Weakened braking gives a closer match but challenges standard wind-loss scalings. The inferred X-ray history suggests early stripping of the innermost planets, while the outer planets likely retained part of their primordial envelopes; HD 219134d experienced only moderate erosion. The apparent radius discrepancy likely reflects underestimated uncertainties in previous interferometric analyses rather than an astrophysical inconsistency. We advocate adopting the asteroseismic radius, which is robust to modeling assumptions and optimisation strategies.

13. Towards a coupled activity-fallback model for comets I) 67P/Churyumov-Gerasimenko's water activity from a microphysics model with dynamic dust-depth[2610.13230]
Abstract

We apply a two-layer thermophysical model, with water sublimating from below a dust-crust with dynamically varying depth, to simulate the water outgassing rate, dust-ejections, and non-gravitational forces and torques of comet 67P/Churyumov-Gerasimenko. The dust-depth is determined at each time-step by the balance of dehydration and subsurface pressure, leading to alternating ejections and crust build-up. This model is the first stage of implementing a fully coupled activity-fallback model that self-consistently incorporates dust-fallback into the ejection depth calculation, but already a number of conclusions can be drawn from these results. The two-layer model with only dehydration and ejections is able to reproduce much of the Rosetta outgassing, dust-ejection data and non-gravitational dynamics, particularly in the comet's more primitive southern terrain. Best agreement is found for nucleus material made up of small aggregates ($d_{agg}\sim1$ mm) with not-too-high dust-to-ice ratios ($\delta\sim2-8$) and low average strengths ($\lesssim1$ Pa) that decreases with depth. Disagreements between the observations and model are greatest for the northern-facing and dust-covered terrains, suggesting the importance of surface modification by fallback and material processing here, which will be the subject of investigation in the next paper.

14. Resistive alignment of a black-hole magnetosphere[2610.13288]
Abstract

Recent numerical simulations have shown that black-hole magnetospheres evolve rapidly toward alignment between the magnetic axis and spin axis but the physical origin of this process has remained unclear. We develop an analytic theory of this alignment within resistive relativistic magnetohydrodynamics. Taking the slow-spin Blandford-Znajek monopole solution as the exterior magnetic field, we reduce the induction equation to a relaxation equation for the current-sheet inclination angle. Our analysis shows that alignment results from second-order spin effects in the Blandford-Znajek solution combined with explicit resistive dissipation. We test this prediction using resistive-GRMHD simulations at several spins and resistivities and find a regime in which the measured late-time secular decay agrees quantitatively with the analytic prediction.

15. Extreme mass ratio inspirals from the Hills mechanism, The eccentricity distribution of Hills EMRIs in LISA[2610.13294]
Abstract

Extreme mass ratio inspirals (EMRIs) are promising sources for future space-based gravitational-wave detectors. We investigate EMRIs produced through the Hills mechanism, in which a stellar-mass black-hole binary (BBH) is tidally separated by a massive black hole (MBH), leaving one component bound to it. We present the first self-consistent modelling of this channel, combining an orbit-averaged Fokker-Planck treatment of the nuclear star cluster (NSC), three-body modelling of the binary-MBH encounter, Monte Carlo evolution of captured black holes (BHs), and fully relativistic EMRI trajectories to follow the evolution from binary supply down to final plunge. We find that Hills EMRIs are substantially more circular than classical EMRIs formed through two-body relaxation. For an MBH of $4 \times 10^6 M_\odot$ and circular, equal-mass BBHs of $20 M_\odot$ with initial separation of $0.1$ AU, EMRI mean eccentricities are approximately $0.097$ at our adopted LISA-band entry threshold and $0.034$ at plunge, compared with $0.478$ and $0.228$, respectively, for classical EMRIs. Of the initial BBHs, approximately $0.4$ per cent become EMRIs, whereas approximately $60$ per cent of captured BHs do. For comparable NSC initial compact-object mass, we find approximately $1.6 \times 10^{-8}$ Hills EMRIs per year per galaxy, approximately $12$ per cent of the classical EMRI yield. The Hills channel contribution to the overall eccentricity distribution depends strongly on the binary mass fraction in NSCs. We discuss implications for identifying EMRI formation channels with LISA and for quasi-periodic eruptions.

16. Flip-Flop Shocks and Tori in Bondi-Hoyle-Lyttleton Accretion onto Rotating Kerr-Weyl Black Holes and Their Quasi-Periodic Variability[2610.13384]
Abstract

We present GRHD simulations of Bondi-Hoyle-Lyttleton accretion onto a rotating black hole in Weyl conformal gravity, described by the Kerr-Weyl metric. The equatorial flow is evolved for spins a=0.9M and a=0.5M and several Weyl coefficients, which enter through an effective curvature parameter k, and is compared with Kerr runs sharing the same setup. For the k considered, the horizon, ISCO and test-particle frequencies differ from Kerr by at most  2%. However, the curvature term grows as kr^2 and acts at the accretion radius R_acc. The flow is controlled by one quantity, eta=|k|R_acc^3/M, the ratio of curvature-induced to Newtonian acceleration at R_acc. For eta< 0.03 a stable shock cone forms, indistinguishable from Kerr. For eta 0.2-0.3 the anti-de Sitter-like term traps shocked gas behind the black hole and drives persistent flip-flop oscillations. For eta> 1.6 the cone is destroyed and an oscillating torus forms. Spin modifies only the inner few gravitational radii and does not change the regime. In strongly deviating models the mean accretion rate is 1.7-3 times the Kerr value and varies by 20-60% rms, versus 0.1-1% in Kerr-like flows. The power spectra show global QPOs with fundamentals at 2.9-4.8 Hz for a 10 Msun black hole, with harmonic ladders whose 3:2 and 2:1 ratios come from one non-sinusoidal oscillation, not resonances. These lie in the low-frequency QPO band and are unrelated to test-particle frequencies ( 70-200 Hz). They scale as f_0=xi f_K(R_acc), with xi 0.7-1.1 for stable cones and xi 0.23-0.38 for flip-flop and torus regimes. Strong oscillations would be detectable within seconds for a bright Galactic source. The required |k| exceeds galactic-rotation-curve values by 18-36 orders of magnitude, so a detected shock-driven QPO would measure a local deviation from Kerr, and its absence would constrain |k|.

17. Detecting redshift space distortions with forthcoming 21cm intensity maps from MeerKLASS[2610.13393]
Abstract

Redshift space distortions (RSD) provide a powerful measurement of structure formation in modern cosmology. In this work, we explore the potential of 21cm intensity mapping with the MeerKAT Large Area Synoptic Survey (MeerKLASS) to detect RSD and probe gravity within the redshift range of MeerKAT's UHF band ($0.4<z<1.45$). Using simulated sky observations, we examine the impact from observational effects caused by blind foreground removal and MeerKAT's primary beam, plus $k$-cuts to the measurements. With a multipole expansion formalism, we demonstrate how the quadrupole of the HI power spectrum can be modelled and also show how a foreground transfer function can reconstruct the distorted signal. With 1,115 deg$^2$ of simulated MeerKLASS observations at $0.4<z<1.0$, our proposed analysis pipeline reveals a $6.7\sigma$ detection of an RSD-induced quadrupole relative to one caused by anisotropic observational effects alone. We also explore parameter inference with the full MeerKLASS 10,000 deg$^2$ survey. Within forecasts, we demonstrate how a tomographic analysis across the full UHF redshift range can be leveraged to address certain parameter degeneracies with e.g. the HI bias ($b_{\rm HI}$) and cosmic density of HI ($\Omega_{\rm HI}$). By adopting a redshift-dependent model for the bias and HI density combined with simulation-informed priors, we demonstrate how degeneracies can be alleviated, and indicate the potential for MeerKLASS to reach competitive precision on simple probes of modified gravity.

18. Fermentation heterotrophy can reproduce Enceladus's H2 and CH4 under limited serpentiniaztion[2610.13434]
Abstract

Saturn's icy moon Enceladus is a prime target in the search for life beyond Earth. Abundant H2 and CH4 in Enceladus's south-polar plume have been shown to be consistent with hydrogenotrophic methanogens feeding on products of long-lived serpentinization. However, thermo-permeability models make sustained core-wide H2 production by serpentinization uncertain. We therefore explore whether an alternative metabolism based on fermentation-driven heterotrophy could reproduce Cassini H2 and CH4 escape fluxes under limited serpentinization. We develop a coupled ecosystem model of fermentation and methanogenesis in a hydrothermal-ocean-plume framework, using glycine as a proxy for amino-acid-like fermentable organics. With effectively no abiotic H2 and CH4 input, purely heterotrophic communities with fermenters and methanogens can reproduce both Cassini-derived flux intervals given sufficient hydrothermal glycine-equivalent supply, while the estimated organic stock could sustain the required H2 production on gigayear timescales if fully available. We then test a scenario with input ranges for fermentable organic matter, H2, and CH4 informed by asteroid-sample measurements and geochemical estimates of limited serpentinization. Simulations within both Cassini-derived flux intervals are concentrated in communities with acetoclastic methanogens; Bayesian model comparison favors the same communities. This holds even allowing for greater abiotic H2 and CH4 inputs that reduce the fermentable-organic supply required to reproduce the plume. We therefore identify fermentation heterotrophy as a testable alternative to purely hydrogenotrophic, serpentinization-dominated biotic interpretations and motivate future plume searches for co-exported fermentation products, including acetate and ammonium, together with measurements of methane carbon isotopes.

19. The Hydrological Cycle and Circulation of Tidally-Locked Planets with a Finite Moisture Inventory[2610.13517]
Abstract

Tidally-locked planets express a range of climate regimes distinct from those found in our solar system. Their atmospheres are often dominated by planetary-wide overturning circulations and super- rotating jets. In this paper we explore the hydrological cycle of such planets and how it might depend on the total inventory of condensate. To this end we use a set of idealised GCM simulations to probe the response of the hydrological cycle to the surface moisture budget in their atmospheres. We find that for low moisture inventories the climate quickly develops a barren day side and a moist night side where the moisture is trapped, as might be expected. For higher moisture inventories the drying of the day side is significantly delayed by the development of a substellar lake just east of the substellar point, which is sustained by the presence of a local thunderstorm-like convective system that persists even after the lake disappears. The timescales for the lake to disappear are millennia, suggesting that even a weak transfer of moisture through surface processes could maintain a moist day side. We also find that adjusting the threshold at which the evaporative rate becomes constrained by the remaining surface liquid depth modifies the longevity of the lake and surrounding day side conditions. These findings have implications for understanding a diverse range of planetary climates and habitability, given the distribution of moisture and presence of a substellar lake.

21. How long could volcanic plumes persist in the Venus atmosphere?[2610.13641]
Abstract

Resolving the ambiguity surrounding current Venusian volcanism is a primary objective for upcoming missions like EnVision and VERITAS. This study uses the Venus Planetary Climate Model to simulate the dispersal and detectability of volcanic plumes containing water vapour (H2O), hydrogen chloride (HCl), carbon monoxide (CO), and carbonyl sulphide (OCS) within a three-dimensional atmospheric environment. We model localised gas enhancements at altitudes probed by nightside spectral windows (8.62, 20.91, and 35.45 km above surface). Results indicate that plumes persist longest in the deep atmosphere (8.62 km) at equatorial latitudes, where H2O enhancements remain distinguishable from low background variability for up to 100 hours, forming distinct downstream streaks. In contrast, at 35 km, high intrinsic variability in chemically active species (CO and OCS) driven by atmospheric dynamics obscures plume signatures and chemically inert species disperse within 0.5-2 Earth days. Plumes at high latitudes disperse more rapidly than at low latitudes at all altitudes. We perform sensitivity tests with trace gas increases of 50%, 40%, 30%, 20%, 10%, and 3% above baseline and plume injection times of 6, 12, 28, 38, and 126 hours at all three altitudes and two latitudes. We conclude that observing inert gases, in particular H2O, at low altitudes and latitudes offers the best opportunity for successful plume detection.

22. Relaxation Dark Energy: Compositional Dynamics and the Future of Cosmic Acceleration[2610.13739]
Abstract

If cosmic acceleration is driven by a positive cosmological constant, the universe approaches an eternally accelerating de Sitter state, whose interpretation in quantum gravity remains elusive. We develop a framework in which acceleration is instead a transient stage of dark-sector relaxation towards compositional equilibrium: matter and dark energy approach a stationary density ratio, eventually diluting like pressureless matter. Guided by linear response theory and Onsager's near-equilibrium regression hypothesis, we describe this evolution through a composition variable measuring departure from equilibrium. Extending constant fractional relaxation to the full background evolution yields the dark-energy equation of state $w=w_0a^{-n}$, where $n$ is the relaxation rate per e-fold of expansion. This history connects early dark-energy suppression, an upward phantom crossing and a density maximum to a pressureless scaling future. Using DESI DR2 BAO and Planck distance priors, with and without three alternative supernova compilations, we find a nominal $2.6$–$3.6\sigma$ preference for nonzero relaxation over constant $w$. The composition framework also motivates a constitutive prescription for linear perturbations that remains regular at phantom crossing. Near equilibrium, regular long-wavelength composition disturbances relax at the background rate to leading order, while small-scale fluctuations propagate as damped sound waves. We prove, however, that for separately conserved matter and dark energy, the pointwise constitutive relation cannot sustain a regular, gradient-stable negative-pressure branch throughout its full past evolution. We explore modifications of the pressure response and their consistency with neighbouring homogeneous histories, identifying requirements for extending the near-equilibrium description to the full cosmological evolution.

23. The origin of the large heavy element contents of warm Jupiters[2610.13762]
Abstract

Planet interior models have revealed that warm Jupiters should harbor large heavy element masses (up to 30-40% of the planetary mass). Within the pebble drift and evaporation scenario, inward drifting pebbles can evaporate and enrich the inner disc with vapor to super-stellar values, accreteable by giant planets. As H$_2$O ice evaporates in the inner disc, while CO$_2$, CH$_4$ and CO evaporate in the outer disc, the enrichment of the disc with vapor decreases with increasing orbital distance. We use the chemcomp code to model the migration and growth of planets via pebble and gas accretion in discs around stars with different masses and discs with different sizes. We show (i) that pebble drift and evaporation can explain the wide range of derived heavy element contents of giant planets. (ii) Giant planets forming around stars with larger disc sizes can accrete larger quantities of heavy elements, because these discs can maintain a high pebble flux for a longer time. (iii) Planets forming or migrating interior to the water ice line can accrete larger amounts of heavy elements via the accretion of vapor enriched gas compared to planets forming in the outer disc regions. (iv) Metal rich stars can allow the formation of planets that are more enriched in heavy elements, if they form in the inner disc regions. Planets forming in the outer disc regions will be - independently of the disc metallicity - much less enriched compared. (v) Planets forming in discs with the same size and total mass, but around stars with higher mass, are only marginally more enriched compared to their counterparts forming around lower mass star. Our study suggests that the trend of increasing total heavy element mass with increasing planetary mass is caused by the accretion of vapor enriched gas in discs with different sizes in the combination with different origins of warm giant planets from the inner and outer disc.

25. Perpendicular transport in isotropic magnetic turbulence. The role of stochastic drifts[2610.13865]
Abstract

In cosmic ray propagation through turbulent magnetic fields, transport perpendicular to the mean field plays an important role in various astrophysical environments. Numerical simulations have shown that parallel and perpendicular diffusion coefficients exhibit different energy scalings, causing their ratio to increase with energy, but a comprehensive theoretical explanation remains elusive. Here, we investigate perpendicular diffusion using test particle simulations in synthetic isotropic turbulence spanning 6-7 orders of magnitude in wavenumber, together with a theoretical extension of nonlinear guiding center theory that incorporates stochastic drifts. Our approach combines field line random walk and random guiding center drifts to describe perpendicular transport. We find that parallel and perpendicular diffusion coefficients exhibit the same energy scaling only for $r_L \lesssim 10^{-2} L_c$, where $r_L$ is the particle Larmor radius and $L_c$ is the turbulence coherence length. At higher energies, perpendicular diffusion develops a stronger energy dependence, consistently across different turbulence levels. Our theoretical model suggests that this behavior arises from stochastic drifts induced by turbulent magnetic field components parallel to the mean field.

26. The Redshift-Space Galaxy Power Spectrum at Two-Loop Order[2610.13867]
Abstract

Contemporary large-scale structure analyses center around the galaxy power spectrum, modeled with one-loop perturbation theory. This description naturally breaks down at large wavenumbers, prohibiting the extraction of cosmological information from small-scale clustering. In this work, we present a complete model for the redshift-space galaxy power spectrum up to two-loop order, derived in the framework of the Effective Field Theory of Large Scale Structure. This accounts for a wide variety of effects, including fifth-order galaxy bias, redshift-space distortions, small-scale non-linearities, spatial non-locality, long-wavelength bulk flows, coordinate distortions, and stochasticity, involving a total of 49 free coefficients. Our model additionally encodes all deterministic contributions to the one-loop bispectrum and trispectrum (as well as the tree-level quadspectrum and pentaspectrum), which depend on many of the same parameters. We implement the model numerically, using the \textsc{vegas} algorithm to precompute the loop integrals; this allows the full likelihood to be evaluated in milliseconds. The approach is validated using power spectra measured from high-resolution simulations, considering both dark matter and realistic galaxy samples. The extension to two-loop order greatly increases the range of scales over which the power spectrum can be modeled, with $k_{\rm max}$ rising from $0.2h\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-1}$ to $0.34h\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-1}$ for a luminous red galaxy sample at $z=0.5$. Much of the additional information is absorbed by the numerous fifth-order biases and counterterms; for this reason, constraints on $\sigma_8$ are not significantly enhanced by the inclusion of two-loop terms. Future work will assess the full implications of the two-loop model for cosmological parameter inference, both alone and in combination with higher-order statistics.

35. Unifying spherical convolutions through lifting[2610.13892]
Abstract

We develop a unified description of convolution on the sphere by lifting to the rotation group. A number of definitions of spherical convolution have been proposed, differing in whether the kernel is axisymmetric or directional, whether the output is on the sphere or the rotation group, whether the signals carry spin, and in their computational cost. No unified treatment exists. Regarding the sphere as the homogeneous space $S^2 \cong \text{SO(3)}/\text{SO(2)}$, we lift a spin $s$ field to the rotation group, convolve on $\text{SO(3)}$, and project back to the sphere through the adjoint of the lift for a potentially different spin $s'$. We recover every rotation-equivariant convolution considered previously through constraints on the kernel, spin choices and inclusion or omission of the lift and projection. For maps between fields of fixed input and output spin, we prove that directional kernels, output on the sphere and equivariance cannot be achieved simultaneously. We make explicit that spin fields are sections of associated line bundles, linking the physics and bundle literatures, and express the spin rotation phase as the character of the twist of the section. We present harmonic-space algorithms for the exact and efficient computation of lifted spherical convolutions of band-limited fields by appealing to sampling theorems on the sphere and rotation group. All operators are implemented in the open-source s2conv code, and numerical experiments confirm the reductions and the equivariance of the operators to machine precision. Finally, we illustrate the operators on fields measured on the Earth and on the celestial sky.
