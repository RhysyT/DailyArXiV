1. Ultralight scalars and invisible gravitons[2610.14119]
Abstract

Unless the tensor to scalar ratio is small enough, the non-adiabatic mode associated with the ultralight bosons may be dangerously comparable with the adiabatic spectrum of curvature inhomogeneities. We point out here that this statement depends on the timeline of the post-inflationary expansion rate. Provided the decelerated evolution is slower than radiation for a sufficiently long period, the relative weight of the non-adiabatic and adiabatic components does not conflict with the direct constraints coming from the large-scale observations. Conversely the presence of ultralight bosons excludes the presence of long stages expanding faster than radiation. The synthesis of the light nuclei and the spectrum of the relic gravitons at high frequencies set the relevant constraints on the duration of the decelerated stages, on the delayed dominance of radiation and on the mass range of the ultralight scalars.

2. Mapping Wolf-Rayet winds with spectro-interferometry I. Detection of multiple co-rotating interaction regions in WR 6[2610.14348]
Abstract

WR 6 is the prototype for periodic variability due to large-scale wind structures in Wolf-Rayet (WR) stars, showing a consistent periodicity of 3.76 d in photometry, spectroscopy, and polarimetry. However, the origin of this variability is debated, attributed either to corotating interaction regions (CIRs) or a low-mass companion. We use time-resolved interferometry to investigate the origin of periodic variability in WR 6. We aim to detect and characterize structures in the stellar winds of WR 6 and discriminate between the CIR and low-mass companion scenarios. Using the extended configuration of the Auxiliary Telescopes at VLTI, we obtained six GRAVITY epochs over six months to search for the presence and variability of wind-structures in WR 6. We also obtained complementary spectroscopic observations during one of the epochs to monitor contemporaneous spectral line variability. For all six epochs of GRAVITY observations: (i) WR 6 is consistent with a single star and has no spatially resolved companion detected above $M\gtrsim6\,M_\odot$; (ii) The He I line-emitting region is spatially resolved, while the He II lines remain unresolved, consistent with atmosphere models; (iii) Around 2.19 $\mu$m, discrete line-emitting features offset from the center of the star are detected. The number, positions, and wavelengths of these features vary across epochs. Spectroscopic monitoring reveals spectral line residuals that broadly correspond to the two features seen in the GRAVITY data of the fifth epoch. Finally, the first three epochs, observed on three consecutive nights, show significant variability in these features but cannot be directly linked across epochs. We thus report the detection of multiple large-scale structures in the wind of WR 6 with VLTI/GRAVITY. We favor CIRs as their likely origin owing to temporal and spatial variability that cannot be reconciled with a low-mass companion.

3. The effect of triples on the Chemically Homogeneous Evolution Channel for producing Gravitational Wave Sources[2610.14353]
Abstract

Despite the growing number of gravitational-wave (GW) detections, the formation of merging binary black holes (BBHs) is still debated. One of the proposed formation channels is Chemically Homogeneous Evolution (CHE), which makes distinct predictions for the properties of the resulting black holes (BHs): high masses ($\gtrsim35\, M_\odot$) and large, orbitally-aligned spins. These predictions are based on studies that consider this channel only in isolated binaries. However, most massive stars reside in triples. We investigate how a tertiary affects the CHE channel. We use detailed binary models computed with MESA for the inner CHE systems coupled with analytical prescriptions for the hierarchical triple dynamics. Specifically, we study the von Zeipel-Lidov-Kozai mechanism that can accelerate the merger of the inner BBHs through high-eccentricity excitations. We show that a tertiary companion enables mergers at higher metallicities, where the classical binary CHE channel does not operate. At $Z_\odot/3$, triples produce BHs with lower masses (around $15\,M_\odot$) and lower spins (around 0.25) compared to the low-metallicity classical scenario. We further expect triple dynamics to lead to spin-orbit misalignment and potential residual eccentricities at merger. In triples at $Z_\odot/3$, half of the mergers occur within 1 Gyr. Moreover, 10% of the population leads to prompt mergers within 1 Myr (1% within 1 kyr) that are of potential interest for electromagnetic counterparts. We conclude that the triple CHE channel is promising as (i) it may contribute more substantially to the local merger rate than had been expected for the classical CHE channel due to the shorter delay times and effectiveness at higher metallicities and (ii) produces mergers with properties that more closely resemble the lower masses and lower spins inferred for most of the observed GW merger population.

4. TESS Pulsation Timing Reveals the JWST Calibration Star HD 158485 as a Double-pulsator Binary[2610.14371]
Abstract

HD 158485 is an A-type $\delta$ Scuti star in the CALSPEC network and the JWST absolute-flux calibration program. Gaia DR3 assigns it a binary classification probability close to unity but provides no orbital solution. Using TESS observations from Sectors 14–26, 40–41, 47–60, and 73–86, we measure the pulsation phases of six $\delta$ Scuti modes. The frequencies form two groups with opposite light-travel-time delays, revealing two pulsating components. A direct light-curve forward model yields an orbital period of $P_{\rm orb}=382.80^{+0.80}_{-0.76}$ d, eccentricity $e=0.242^{+0.045}_{-0.046}$, projected light-time amplitudes of $195.8^{+9.2}_{-8.9}$ and $239.7^{+6.8}_{-6.8}$ s, and a dynamical mass ratio of $q=M_B/M_A=0.818^{+0.046}_{-0.045}$. A blind prediction based only on the first two long TESS campaigns reproduces the third campaign without updating the orbital parameters, with a Pearson correlation coefficient of $r=0.983$. Its held-out rms is 83.8 s, compared with 289.9 s when the period is fixed at 365.25 d. A 5.15-$\sigma$ Hipparcos–Gaia EDR3 proper-motion anomaly independently indicates unresolved astrometric motion. The unresolved companion can also affect spectrophotometric predictions: a conditional PB2-constrained two-atmosphere model differs from the corresponding single-star atmosphere prediction by 1.6\% over 1–5 $\mu$m and 2.5\% over 5–28 $\mu$m. HD 158485 therefore shows that a calibration-relevant binary can lack a catalogued orbital solution while two independent pulsation clocks reveal and measure its orbit.

5. Reawakening of GS 1843+00: A Multiwavelength Study of its 2026 Giant X-ray Outburst[2610.14494]
Abstract

GS 1843+00 (Ginga 1843+009) is a poorly studied Be/X-ray binary system that underwent a major X-ray outburst in 2026 February-May after nearly sixteen years of quiescence. We present an X-ray, optical, and radio study of GS 1843+00 during this outburst, focusing on the evolution of the neutron star emission geometry, Be-disk connection, and possible outflow activity. We analyse three NuSTAR observations obtained at different stages of the outburst, complemented by archival RXTE and Chandra data, optical spectroscopy and imaging with the Nordic Optical Telescope, and a MeerKAT radio observation. NuSTAR observations reveal coherent X-ray pulsations around 29.7 s with a spin-up rate of $(-5.1\pm0.1)\times10^{-8}$ s s$^{-1}$ during the outburst. The pulse profiles show strong evolution with energy and outburst phase, with distinct morphologies even at comparable luminosities. The pulsed fraction varies between 1 and 16% and exhibits a complex reversal in energy dependence above 7 keV, increasing with energy during the first two observations and decreasing during the third. The 3-79 keV broadband spectra are described by thermal Comptonization with a blackbody and an Fe K$\alpha$ emission line, with no evidence for a cyclotron absorption feature. Phase-resolved spectroscopy reveals variations in the Comptonization and blackbody parameters, indicating changes in the accretion-column structure. Optical spectroscopy confirms the presence of a Be-circumstellar disk through H$\alpha$ and Paschen-series emission lines. No radio counterpart is detected, with a $3\sigma$ upper limit of 36 $\mu$Jy beam$^{-1}$ at 1.28 GHz. Weaker H$\alpha$ emission compared with previous epochs indicates that the Be-circumstellar disk was in a different state during the current episode, while the radio non-detection suggests that any associated outflow activity was faint or below the detection limit.

6. Impact of Decay-Chain Feeding by Long-Lived Heavy Nuclei on Nuclear Cosmochronology[2610.14526]
Abstract

Nuclear cosmochronology provides an independent method for determining the ages of astrophysical objects without relying on Galactic chemical evolution models. Long-lived radioactive actinides synthesized via the rapid neutron-capture process ($r$-process) particularly thorium and uranium serve as ideal chronometric nuclei. However, predicting the initial abundances of these nuclei and their subsequent long-term evolution remains a major source of uncertainty in nuclear cosmochronology. In this work, we investigate the impact of decay-chain feeding from long-lived progenitor nuclei on nuclear cosmochronology and estimate the corresponding age corrections. We develop a self-consistent radioactive decay network that includes all relevant decay pathways and couple it to $r$-process nucleosynthesis calculations. By tracking the long-term evolution of $r$-process nucleosynthesis based on neutron star merger trajectories, the dominant long-lived nuclei shaping the evolution of the chronometric elements Th and U are identified as $^{232}$Th, $^{235}$U, $^{236}$U, $^{238}$U, and $^{244}$Pu. It is found that delayed feeding substantially modifies the long-term evolution of actinides particularly for $^{232}$Th, which receives significant late-time contributions from heavier progenitor nuclei such as $^{244}$Pu and $^{236}$U. As a consequence, the U/Th and Th/X chronometers exhibit significant sensitivity to delayed feeding from the progenitor nuclei $^{244}$Pu and $^{236}$U, with age corrections reaching $\sim5$ Gyr. These results suggest that decay-chain feeding from long-lived progenitor nuclei plays a crucial role in nuclear cosmochronology and should be properly accounted for in age determinations based on actinide chronometers.

7. Magnetar X-ray flares as elementary reconnection events in a self-organized critical magnetosphere[2610.14581]
Abstract

Magnetar bursts are commonly attributed to magnetic energy release, yet the physically relevant dissipation unit has remained uncertain. Here we argue that this unit is not the burst as conventionally defined, but the short substructures within bursts, namely magnetar X-ray flares (MXFs). Using 789 MXFs from SGR J1935+2154, we show that their waiting times separate into two components, interpreted as reflecting clustered intra-episode events and longer inter-episode intervals, that their proxy emission measure and temperature exhibit distinct rising and declining trends, and that their energy release scale (ERS) proxy distribution has a high-end slope in the same statistical regime as solar and stellar flares. Combined with the previously measured MXF duration slope, these results identify MXFs as a three-dimensional fractal-diffusive self-organized critical (FD-SOC) system with $\beta_{\rm diff}\simeq2$, implying reconnection avalanches that expand linearly through an active magnetosphere. We infer that magnetar active episodes are global unloading states assembled from many elementary flares. Magnetar X-ray flares are therefore the physically relevant units for magnetar outburst statistics and for connecting magnetar high-energy activity to other impulsive phenomena.

8. TDCOSMO 2026: A 3% measurement of the Hubble constant from strong lensing and stellar kinematics[2610.14781]
Abstract

We present cosmological constraints from 11 strongly lensed quasars with measured time delays (hereafter, the TDCOSMO-2026 sample). In addition to expanding the sample, we improve many aspects of our blind analysis with respect to our 2025 analysis. Among other improvements, spatially resolved kinematics are available for nine systems, including six with JWST-NIRSpec, compared to one in 2025; for three lens models, JWST-NIRCam images now replace HST; axisymmetric dynamical modeling is applied directly to the spatially resolved data, instead of an axisymmetric correction factor applied to spherical models; and the line-of-sight convergence is now based on the Euclid Flagship simulations instead of the Millennium Simulation. Our baseline and primary result is based on the TDCOSMO-2026 sample, in combination with a prior on $\Omega_{\rm m}$ from the DES-Dovekie supernova sample. In the flat $\Lambda$ cold dark matter ($\Lambda$CDM) cosmology, we find $H_0=71.1^{+2.4}_{-2.3}$ km.s$^{-1}$.Mpc$^{-1}$, a 3.3% uncertainty that accounts for the mass-sheet degeneracy. The SLACS and SL2S samples are in excellent agreement with the TDCOSMO-2026 sample, improving the precision on $H_0$ in flat $\Lambda$CDM to 3.0%. Using the DESI-DR2 baryon acoustic oscillations (BAO) likelihood instead of DES-Dovekie yields fully consistent results. We also present constraints in the open $\Lambda$CDM, $w$CDM, $w_0w_a$CDM, and $w_{\phi}$CDM cosmologies; the TDCOSMO $H_0$ inference is robust and consistent across all of them. The combination of the TDCOSMO+SLACS+SL2S likelihood with those from DESI-DR2 and DES-Dovekie disfavors $w=-1$ at the $2.3$-$2.5\sigma$ level in all three dark energy extensions that we tested. We find, e.g., $w_0=-0.88^{+0.04}_{-0.05}$ in $w_{\phi}$CDM and $w_0 = -0.85^{+0.07}_{-0.06}$ in $w_0w_a$CDM, while $w_a = -0.48^{+0.46}_{-0.44}$ remains consistent with zero.

9. Gravitational waves from strong first-order phase transitions in an expanding Universe[2610.14806]
Abstract

We investigate the gravitational waves generated by first-order phase transitions (PTs) in an expanding Universe. Using a scalar field-fluid coupled model, we perform three-dimensional lattice numerical simulations of slow first-order PTs with strong strength. We observe that the contribution from sound waves to the gravitational wave (GW) signal significantly dominates over that from bubble collisions. The sound wave spectra exhibit two peaks located at the mean bubble separation and the sound-shell width. Rotational modes are generated in the fluid during the bubble collisions, and their magnitude grows with the PT strength. The lifetime of the sound wave source increases with decreasing $\alpha$: for weaker transitions the source can persist for longer than a Hubble time, while for stronger transitions it decays within a Hubble time. When the walls propagate as deflagrations, the GW spectral shape changes drastically, with steeper ultraviolet tails and a shorter-lived sound wave source. We also observe that the magnitude of the normalized GWs is roughly an order of magnitude smaller than obtained in the Minkowski spacetime simulations.

10. TDCOSMO 2026: new insights on the Hubble tension from strong lensing time delays[2610.14881]
Abstract

We discuss the measurement of the Hubble constant ($H_0$) in flat $\Lambda$ cold dark matter cosmology based on the blind hierarchical analysis of 11 multiply-imaged quasars (hereafter the TDCOSMO-2026 sample) and described in greater detail in a companion paper. In addition to the time delays, we use lens models based on high-resolution imaging from the Hubble Space Telescope (HST) and from the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST), analyzed jointly with axisymmetric dynamical models based on JWST-NIRSpec, VLT-MUSE, and Keck-KCWI spectroscopy. Our primary result $H_0=71.1^{+2.4}_{-2.3}$ km.s$^{-1}$.Mpc$^{-1}$ is based on the TDCOSMO-2026 sample, in combination with $\Omega_{\rm m}$ constraints from the DES-Dovekie supernova (SN) dataset. Our 3% precision accounts for the mass-sheet degeneracy. Importantly, our measurement is independent of the local distance ladder (LDL). Among individual late-universe probes, only the traditional LDL is more precise. We discuss our result in the context of the Hubble tension, the difference between $H_0$ measured in the late universe and the prediction based on early-universe probes such as the cosmic microwave background (CMB). We construct combinations of independent late-universe probes and show that the tension with the Planck CMB prediction is robust to excluding any one of the methods based on Type-Ia Supernovae, Cepheids, or the Tip of the Red Giant Branch. For example, we construct a 'no-SN' $H_0$ measurement, by replacing the DES-Dovekie constraint on $\Omega_{\rm m}$ with that from DESI baryon acoustic oscillations, and then combining TDCOSMO-2026 with independent non-SN late-universe probes. This 'no-SN' late-universe probe is in 3.7$\sigma$ tension with Planck. Even after completely excluding the LDL, the tension with Planck persists at the 2.7$\sigma$ level.

11. A GeV Gamma-Ray Spectral Excess in the Fermi Blazar 4FGL J0136.5+3906[2610.14924]
Abstract

We report the detection of a $\sim${19} GeV gamma-ray spectral excess (peak energy in observer frame) at $\sim$5.7$\sigma$ significance (3 degrees of freedom, without correction for trials and the look-elsewhere effect) in the Fermi-LAT data of the blazar 4FGL J0136.5$+$3906, based on $\sim${17} years of observations. The excess manifests as a distinct bump-like feature in the 100 MeV$-$1 TeV spectrum, with a flux of $\sim$3.6$\rm \times10^{-10}~ph~cm^{-2}~s^{-1}$. This Letter presents the observational finding without providing a definitive physical interpretation.

12. Joint Constraints on the Hubble Constant and Cosmic Baryon Density from Localized Fast Radio Bursts and DESI DR2 Baryon Acoustic Oscillations[2610.15536]
Abstract

Fast radio bursts (FRBs) probe free-electron columns along cosmological sightlines through their dispersion measures (DMs), whereas baryon acoustic oscillations (BAO) constrain cosmic expansion geometry through acoustic distance ratios. In this work, we leverage their distinct physical sensitivities to simultaneously determine the Hubble constant ($H_0$) and cosmic baryon density ($\Omega_{\rm b}$) without external cosmic microwave background (CMB) or Big Bang nucleosynthesis (BBN) priors. Combining 120 localized FRBs and DESI DR2 BAO, we obtain $H_0=71.4^{+2.2}_{-2.5}\ {\rm km\ s^{-1}\ Mpc^{-1}}$ and $\Omega_{\rm b}=0.0503^{+0.0025}_{-0.0021}$ in $\Lambda$CDM, corresponding to precisions of $3.3\%$ and $4.6\%$, respectively. The inferred $H_0$ is statistically consistent with both SH0ES and CMB, and $\Omega_{\rm b}$ agrees with the joint constraint from BBN and CMB. Adopting IllustrisTNG-based DM distributions and fixing the diffuse-gas baryon fraction tightens the constraint to $H_0=68.8\pm1.0\ {\rm km\ s^{-1}\ Mpc^{-1}}$, but lowers the central value toward the CMB-inferred value, identifying extragalactic DM modeling as the dominant systematic uncertainty. These results establish FRB-BAO complementarity as a new avenue for simultaneously constraining $H_0$ and $\Omega_{\rm b}$.

13. Mapping the Contracting Corona in GS 1354-64 with Insight-HXMT and XRISM: Evolving X-ray Lags and Quenched Reflection across a Complete State Transition[2610.15569]
Abstract

We present a comprehensive broadband spectro-temporal analysis of the dynamically confirmed Galactic black hole transient GS 1354-64 during its 2025-26 outburst, using near-simultaneous observations from Insight-HXMT and XRISM. The high spectral resolution of the XRISM-Resolve microcalorimeter, coupled with the high-cadence broadband coverage of Insight-HXMT, reveals a dramatic evolution in the inner accretion geometry. During the early hard-intermediate state, the spectrum was dominated by a hot Comptonizing corona accompanied by relativistically broadened reflection features. As the outburst progressed, the corona cooled efficiently, the photon index steepened (Gamma >  2.5), and the reflection signatures, including the broad Fe K-alpha emission, were quenched, yielding a featureless, disk-dominated continuum. Concurrently, Type-C quasi-periodic oscillations (QPOs) evolved from 0.25 Hz to 6.52 Hz. Cross-spectral timing analysis revealed that the broadband X-ray time lags changed from pronounced positive (hard) delays ( 160 ms) at low frequencies to near zero as the source softened. However, a statistical analysis incorporating the measurement uncertainties shows that the broadband lags lack a significant monotonic correlation with the QPO frequency, photon index, or fractional rms. Instead, the scattered lag behavior suggests a competition between positive Comptonization delays and negative thermal reverberation. While the spectral softening and QPO acceleration provide strong evidence for a geometric contraction of the corona (whether interpreted via Lense-Thirring precession or the Two-Component Advective Flow (TCAF) paradigm), the broadband timing properties underscore the turbulent and inhomogeneous nature of the inner flow as the advancing Keplerian disk drives the source into the soft state.

14. Revisiting the Multiwavelength Data of GRB 240825A: Implications for a Two-Stage Central Engine and a Dusty Environment[2610.15703]
Abstract

Reanalyzing the multi-wavelength data of GRB 240825A, we identify two distinct prompt-emission episodes, i.e. an early, spectrally soft plateau and a subsequent bright, spectrally hard gamma-ray pulse. We show that this signature is consistent with a two-stage central engine model, in which the early soft plateau is powered by the dipolar spin-down radiation of a transient proto-magnetar, while the later hard pulse arises from a relativistic jet launched by fallback accretion onto the nascent black hole via the Blandford-Znajek mechanism. Adopting simulation-motivated initial black hole (BH) masses ($M_{\rm BH,0}$) and spins ($a_0$), our fits to the second episode yield total masses supplied to the post-collapse disk of $M_{\rm fb}=0.45$–$0.96 M_\odot$, fallback radii of $r_{\rm fb}=16.9$–$17.6 r_g$, and characteristic fallback timescales of $t_{\rm fb}=0.13$–$0.25$ s. The corresponding maximum inner accretion rates range from $0.18$ to $0.70 M_\odot \mathrm{s}^{-1}$, which is sensitive to $a_0$. Joint spectral fits with optical-X-ray afterglow data reveal a coupled temporal evolution of dust extinction and equivalent hydrogen column density. Incorporating this extinction evolution, an external forward-reverse shock model in a wind-like medium can account for the radio-to-GeV band afterglow data. Although ambient dust and the bright host galaxy may obscure any associated supernova, late-time optical reddening remains compatible with a supernova contribution. Applying the empirical relation between the prompt spectral peak energy and the SN peak bolometric magnitude gives an illustrative estimate of $M_{\rm bol,peak}=-19.46\pm0.35$ mag for a putative associated SN. These results support a two-stage evolution of the central engine and variable dusty environment of GRB 240825A, which provides insights into the nature of its progenitor.

15. Bayesian exploration of weakly interacting models of dark matter admixed neutron stars based on multi-density constraints[2610.15713]
Abstract

We present a unified Bayesian analysis of Higgs-portal, neutron-decay, and axion-like-particle (ALP)-mediated dark matter in neutron stars within the single-fluid approximation. Using a common relativistic mean-field description of hadronic matter, we combine finite-nucleus and heavy-ion collision data, chiral effective field theory, NICER measurements of four pulsars, and GW170817. The individual dark-sector parameters remain weakly determined, while model-dependent shifts in nuclear skewness reveal the sensitivity of the inferred hadronic equation of state to the assumed dark-matter mechanism. The three scenarios yield substantially overlapping mass-radius and tidal-deformability predictions, with median maximum masses near $2.1\,M_\odot$. For $1.4\,M_\odot$ stars, the Higgs-portal model predicts higher $f$-mode frequencies and shorter damping times than the neutron-decay and ALP models, although their credible regions overlap. Comparison with three consistently reanalysed two-fluid models gives the largest Bayesian evidence to neutron decay: under the adopted priors, its Bayes factors relative to the Higgs-portal and two-fluid alternatives are approximately $24$-$37$, but only $2.2$ relative to ALP. This ranking concerns complete dark-matter models and does not establish a general preference for either fluid description or evidence for a dark component itself. Improved nuclear constraints and future gravitational-wave asteroseismology could provide complementary information to disentangle the hadronic and dark-sector contributions.

17. Radial increase of the compensated interplanetary magnetic field magnitude as a result of the growth of spherically polarized Alfvén waves[2610.15741]
Abstract

Measurements from the NASA Parker Solar Probe (PSP) mission demonstrate that inside $\sim20$ solar radii ($\Rs$) the magnitude of the interplanetary magnetic field does not fall off simply as $1/R^2$, with $\avg{|B|}\,R^2$ 15–20\% below its value at $\sim60\,\Rs$. Here we use PSP magnetic field measurements over $10.6$–$56.8\,\Rs$ to show that the outward rise of $\avg{|B|}\,R^2$ is not due to the global structure of the heliospheric magnetic field or proximity to the heliospheric current sheet but is the direct result of the growth of large-amplitude, spherically polarized Alfvén waves (so-called `switchbacks'). Over the same distance range, $\avg{B_R}\,R^2$ stays constant, so that the sector-signed radial flux is conserved. The wave amplitude $\epsilon = B_{\perp,{\rm rms}}/\avg{B_\parallel}$ grows from $0.26$ to $0.57$ as $R^{1/2}\cos\alpha$, and $\avg{|B|}/\avg{B_R}$ rises by $20\%$. A simple model parametrized by the broadening of the distribution of deflection angles reproduces the effect to high accuracy. These results demonstrate that as the relative wave amplitudes grow with increasing solar distance, a dynamo-like process operates to enhance the field magnitude by up to $20\%$. By enhancing magnetic pressure this has implications for the large scale solar wind structure in the low-beta inner heliosphere, as well as partially resolving the long-standing discrepancy between remote measurements of the solar magnetic field and those made in situ by earlier missions farther from the Sun.

18. Rotating Vector Model predictions in millisecond pulsar magnetospheres with dipole and quadrupole field components[2610.15779]
Abstract

The rotating vector model (RVM) is widely used to interpret pulsar radio polarization under the assumption of a dipolar magnetic field. Motivated by tentative evidence for non-dipolar magnetic structure in millisecond pulsars provided by recent modeling of surface thermal X-ray pulsations, we examine how a global quadrupole component modifies position-angle (PA) behavior within the classical RVM framework. We numerically integrate magnetic field lines in static vacuum magnetospheres composed of a centered dipole component that is inclined with respect to the spin axis, and a centered quadrupole component aligned with it. We then compute the open field line regions (OFLRs), emission skymaps, and PA curves for a range of dipole inclinations, relative quadrupole strengths, and light cylinder radii corresponding to two trial spin periods of 1 ms and 5 ms. We find that quadrupole-modified magnetospheres produce a wide variety of PA behaviors and substantially alter the morphology and total area of OFLRs, with the OFLR area varying non-monotonically with quadrupole strength and, in some cases, exceeding that of the corresponding pure dipole. Depending sensitively on the field configuration and viewing geometry, three broad PA regimes can be distinguished: a) nearly dipolar PA swings that are largely insensitive to emission altitude; b) moderately distorted, altitude-dependent PA tracks that can bias dipolar RVM fits; and c) strongly dispersed PA behavior associated with emission from different OFLRs, which can remain incompatible with a single dipolar PA track even within narrow emission altitude ranges. These results demonstrate that dipole-like PA curves do not uniquely imply a purely dipolar magnetic field, while global quadrupole components alone are insufficient to reproduce the full complexity of observed millisecond pulsar PA profiles.

19. Scaled three-dimensional simulations of giant-planet accretion and disk clearing[2610.15876]
Abstract

We develop accelerated three-dimensional simulations of growing planet pairs that draw on a finite gas reservoir. An annulus compression (Scaling A) shortens the predicted clearing time and shrinks the computational domain at equal grid resolution while preserving key physical parameters. Planetary seeds start with Hill radii equal to the disk scale height, retaining three-dimensional accretion. We demonstrate the method in scaled PDS 70 and Solar-System configurations, assuming perfect Hill capture by growing planets on circular orbits. We compare Scaling A at radius-compression factor \(\Gamma=1.75\) with the unscaled PDS system at matched resolution, through a 1\% reduction of the interplanetary gas inventory. The scaled run is nearly an order of magnitude faster, with similar accreted mass and a comparable partition of gas between the planets. For the Solar-System configuration at \(\Gamma=3\), the scaling forecasts a speedup of nearly two orders of magnitude at equal local resolution. The scaling is complementary to orbital advection. On eight CPU cores of a desktop computer, the scaled PDS 70 and Solar-System runs reduce the gas inventory between the orbiting planets by roughly half in less than a day. Accretion-driven orbital convergence narrows the shared feeding zone and, together with gas clearing, limits the planetary growth budget. In our scaled PDS 70 and Solar-System runs, the angular momentum carried by meridional inflow directly constrains delivery theory. These results establish a practical route to testing accretion theory against simulations at matched scaled parameters, before applying it to unscaled planetary systems.

20. How much can shape degeneracies affect the Hubble constant measurement?[2610.15999]
Abstract

Cluster lens models are constrained almost entirely by observed lensed image positions, leaving a large space of mass perturbations with arbitrary amplitudes such that they reproduce every image exactly. Such degeneracies are known as shape degeneracies (ShaDes). We show that these ShaDes shift the observed time delays, by the difference of the perturbing potential between the two images, without depending on the number of source/lens planes. Bounding their amplitude by preserving the positivity of the convergence, image multiplicity, and parity, in MACS J0138.0$-$2155, they can contribute at least $\simeq7$ days to the delay between the detected images of SN-Encore (i.e., $17.5\%$ of the measured value of $39.8$ days) and $\simeq112$ days (i.e., $2.8$–$3.7\%$ of predicted value of $3,000-4000$ days) to the reappearance intervals predicted for SN-Encore and SN-Requiem. These error bars transfer one-to-one to $H_0$, and remain invisible in comparisons of independent models.

21. Integration of a CME Flux Rope Model with Magnetic Field Draping in Heliospheric Simulations[2610.16004]
Abstract

Abstract

We present a novel computational method for hybrid (kinetic ions, quasi-neutral fluid electrons) simulations of non-relativistic magnetized collisionless plasma shocks, observed near the Sun, in the solar system, and beyond. Abstract

We present a density functional-based equation of state for warm, dense nuclear matter with a transition to deconfined quark matter for applications to simulations of supernova explosions and neutron star mergers, but also for the cosmological evolution of Q-balls. The double copy relates gravitational data to gauge-theory data, most sharply through color-kinematics duality for flat-space scattering amplitudes.
