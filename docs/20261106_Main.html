1. Latest $ν$s from the Earliest Galaxies: Probing neutrino self-interactions with JWST[2611.07013]
Abstract

Our Universe serves as a complementary laboratory for particle physics interactions. One intriguing possibility is that neutrinos may have beyond-the-Standard-Model (BSM) self-interactions, which can delay their free streaming and have an observable effect across different redshifts. Such interactions can induce scale-dependent changes in the matter power spectrum. The James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) has opened up a new window onto the early Universe by discovering a large population of high-redshift galaxies up to $z \sim 15$ and various probable galaxy candidates at even higher redshifts, between $z \sim 15-30$. Such high-redshift observations can probe the matter power spectrum on small scales, since structure formation is hierarchical and the structures hosting these high-redshift galaxies are therefore expected to be small. We, $\textit{for the first time}$, use the UV luminosity function data from JWST and combine them with the $\textit{Planck}$ CMB data to probe neutrino self-interactions ($\nu$SI). If only one neutrino flavor experiences such BSM interactions, then $\textit{Planck}$ + JWST data give a 95% C.L. upper limit on $\mathrm{Log}_{10}(G_{\mathrm{eff}}/\mathrm{MeV}^{-2})$ $\approx$ $-2.75$ and when all three flavors are interacting, the corresponding constraint becomes $\lesssim -3.41$; both stronger than those obtained using only $\textit{Planck}$ data. Our results highlight the power of this new observable, the high-redshift UV luminosity function from JWST as a complementary probe to terrestrial experiments, showing that small-scale observables can open a new window onto smaller neutrino self-interaction strengths.

2. Phase Offsets Between the Minimum Light and Radial Velocity in Long Secondary Period Stars[2611.07018]
Abstract

Long secondary periods (LSPs), observed in a substantial fraction of luminous red giants, remain one of the longstanding problems in red giant variability. Previous studies have found a systematic phase relation between their photometric and radial velocity (RV) variations. We investigate this relation using ASAS-SN photometry and Gaia RV curves for 983 LSP stars, and compare it with other types of Long Period Variables. We confirm that LSP stars have a relatively narrow distribution of phase offsets between the phase of minimum RV and minimum light, with a mean value of $\Delta\phi=-0.15\pm0.10$ in phase ($-0.96\pm0.64$ rad). Interestingly, this phase offset is similar for Miras, showing that this quantity is not sufficient to distinguish LSPs from pulsational variability. However, pulsating red giants show a relation between period and RV amplitude, with the RV amplitude generally increasing toward longer periods. LSP stars deviate from this relation, showing RV amplitudes of only a few km/s despite their long periods. The stacked light and RV curves of the LSP sample also show remarkable homogeneity and a coherent phase relation. In the binary interpretation of LSPs, the observed phase offset requires the obscuring material to be shifted in orbital phase with respect to the companion, consistent with models in which a low-mass companion produces a dusty wake in the wind of the red giant.

3. General-relativistic neutrino-radiation hydrodynamics of crust ejection in magnetar giant flares[2611.07022]
Abstract

Magnetar giant flares are thought to eject $\gtrsim 10^{-9}\,M_\odot$ of baryons, as inferred from the radio afterglow of the 2004 flare of SGR 1806-20. One proposed mechanism is a shock driven into the neutron-star crust by the hot, high-pressure layer that the flare creates above the surface. The unbound crust could undergo $r$-process nucleosynthesis and power a short-lived "nova brevis". We study this crust ejection with general-relativistic neutrino-radiation hydrodynamics simulations performed with AthenaK, using a tabulated nuclear equation of state and two-moment neutrino transport, for flare pressures corresponding to magnetic fields $B = 10^{15}$-$10^{16.5}$ G, and compute the nucleosynthesis of the ejecta with a nuclear reaction network. We find that such flares unbind $10^{-10}$ to $5\times10^{-8}\,M_\odot$ of crust. The ejecta keep the electron fraction of the crust to within a few hundredths, and weak interactions affect it mainly in the most energetic flares. The fast, high-entropy ejecta freeze out mostly as $\alpha$ particles and free neutrons. In the stronger flares nuclei up to the third $r$-process peak do form, but those with $A \geq 90$ make up at most $\approx 8\times10^{-4}$ of the ejected mass, or $\approx 3.5\times10^{-11}\,M_\odot$ per flare, and less than $10^{-5}$ for $B \leq 10^{15.5}$ G. A nova brevis from these ejecta would be powered mainly by the decay of free neutrons. Like earlier work, our models assume spherical symmetry, a flare that covers the whole star, and no magnetic field. A field tangential to the surface would hold the crust down and weaken the shock, and a flare confined to part of the surface should eject proportionally less mass. We therefore regard our ejecta masses as upper limits for a given flare pressure, and expect realistic magnetar flares to produce negligible amounts of $r$-process material.

4. Is the hard X-ray polarization of Cyg X-1 stable?[2611.07032]
Abstract

We present results from hard X-ray observations of Cyg X-1 in the hard state, conducted during, and proximate to, the July 2024 balloon flight of XL-Calibur. Temporal and spectral observations from NICER, NuSTAR, and Swift-BAT have been included, complementing XL-Calibur polarimetric observations in the $\sim$19-64 keV range. We perform joint NICER+NuSTAR+Swift-BAT spectral analyses in the 1.0-195.0 keV range and reference these to the day-by-day polarization analysis. XL-Calibur previously reported (5.0$^{+2.7}_{-3.0}$)\% polarization degree (PD) and polarization angle (PA) aligned with the jet axis, when integrating over the four observing days. In this paper, we present day-by-day polarimetry results, which indicate variation in the polarization parameters. For July 12-13, the PD is ($16.8\pm4.9$)\%, at PA $\sim$25$^{\circ}$ shifted from the jet, while for the remaining days, the PD and PA are consistent with the integrated values. At the same time, we observed that the reflection fraction parameter ($\mathrm{R_{f}}$) of spectroscopic model increases by factor of two. These changes place specific requirements on the PD and flux of additional component responsible for them. Such requirements are more likely to be satisfied by geometric changes in corona near the BH than by scattering in the vicinity of the stellar companion.

5. Breaking dark degeneracies in $k$-essence models with redshift-space distortions[2611.07033]
Abstract

Dynamical dark energy models are often difficult to constrain with background observations alone due to parameter degeneracies. This is especially true when the dark energy sector evolves through an effective pressureless matter phase, leading to degeneracies between the model parameters and the matter density parameter. In this work, we show that redshift-space distortion (RSD) measurements help break this degeneracy when combined with expansion history probes including Pantheon$^{+}$ supernovae and DESI DR2 BAO measurements. We demonstrate this in the ghost and dilatonic ghost condensate $k$-essence models, restricting the observational analysis to physically admissible solutions by imposing the absence of ghosts and gradient instabilities. Given the small scalar sound speeds in these models, we also explicitly check the validity of the quasi-static approximation used in the growth analysis. Our results show that combining growth-rate data with stability requirements and standard background observations provides a fast and efficient way to constrain potentially degenerate dark sector models. Lastly, we discuss the matter clustering constraints on these models in light of the recent $S_8$ discrepancies.

6. The JWST Early Release Science Program for Direct Observations of Exoplanetary Systems IX: A Multi-Wavelength Framework for Source Characterization in JWST Coronagraphic Imaging of the HIP 65426 System[2611.07034]
Abstract

We present an updated analysis of the JWST coronagraphic imaging data of the HIP 65426 system. We aim to search for and characterize potential planetary companions as well as provide updated astrometry and photometry for the known exoplanet HIP 65426 b following updates to the JWST and spaceKLIP pipelines. Our measured photometry and astrometry show significant differences to those in Carter et al. (2023) but are consistent to within $2.2{\sigma}$ in separation, $1.5{\sigma}$ in position angle, and $3.7{\sigma}$ in flux. Our results indicate that pipeline updates have changed the photometry and astrometry significantly in some filters, especially in terms of observed flux. We see the greatest changes in separation in F444W and F1550C, position angle in F250M, and flux in F250M, F300M, F410M, F1140C, and F1550C. The SED of HIP 65426 b remains consistent with a young, giant planet best fit by models with a cloudy atmosphere and an effective temperature estimate of  1436 K. Using both NIRCam and MIRI data, we identified 21 candidate companion sources in addition to the known planet. Of the 21 sources, 13 were attributed to astrophysical sources with eight background stars, four galaxies, one unknown, and zero planets. While one of these sources was detected in Chauvin et al. (2017), the remaining point sources were undetected in previous ground-based observations, which makes this work an initial epoch of astrometry for these objects. One galaxy in particular appears as a point source in MIRI but is highly extended in NIRCam and exposes a potential drawback to single-epoch MIRI imaging in a blind search for companions.

7. Viable Phantom Crossing in Softly Broken Shift Symmetric Gravity[2611.07035]
Abstract

We demonstrate a viable fit to current cosmological data from a simple Horndeski modified gravity theory that softly breaks shift symmetry. This SBSS model crosses the phantom divide $w=-1$ at $z\approx0.5$ and fits observational data (baryon acoustic oscillations and supernova distances, and cosmic microwave background power spectra) better than $w_0w_a$CDM, by $\Delta\chi^2\approx-3$ for the same number of effective parameters, while providing a physical foundation for cosmic acceleration. The theory is healthy, has no cosmological constant or potential, and has substantial protection against high energy quantum loop corrections.

8. The Composition of M-dwarf Exoplanet Hosts: Atmospheric Parameters and O, Mg, Si, and Fe Abundances from IR Spectroscopy[2611.07037]
Abstract

Determining M-dwarf abundances is an important step to discerning the chemical evolution of the Milky Way and characterizing exoplanetary systems, especially since these objects commonly host small exoplanets. However, a dearth of M-dwarf abundance measurements exist compared to Sun-like stars, partially due to their complicated optical spectra dominated by molecular absorption lines and low luminosities. To overcome these issues, we have obtained high resolution H-band spectra of 40 planet-hosting M-dwarf stars and 2 M-dwarfs in wide binaries with FGK primaries with IGRINS on Gemini South and on the McDonald Observatory 2.7m telescope, and NIRSPEC on Keck. Abundances were derived by comparing each observed spectrum to synthetic spectral grids generated with MOOG and MARCS atmospheric models. Effective temperatures were derived using OH and H$_{2}$O lines and [Fe/H] from Fe I and FeH lines. Both Mg and Si abundances were determined using atomic features. Overall, we find that our abundances are broadly consistent with previous literature M-dwarf abundances. We complemented our abundances analysis with UVW velocities and Milky Way stellar population membership probabilities and identified 2 potential thick disk stars from our kinematic and chemical analysis: GJ 1252 and L181-1 which was a previously known thick disk star. Kinematic stellar population probabilities and a low [Fe/H] abundance makes Kepler 42 a likely thick disk star, although uncertain Mg and Si abundances make classification difficult. Lastly, we have determined Si/Mg and Fe/Mg molar ratios for M-dwarf exoplanet hosts and find the abundance distributions leads to exoplanet core-mass fractions between 20 - 40$\%$.

9. The retirement party of GRS 1915+105: the radio fading to quiescence of the archetypal microquasar[2611.07039]
Abstract

After more than 25 years of near-Eddington activity and over six years in a Compton-thick obscured phase, the black-hole X-ray binary GRS 1915+105 has begun to fade in the radio to a level lower than ever previously recorded, possibly marking the onset of its final descent towards quiescence. With the accretion flow hidden in the X-rays, the radio emission is currently the best tracer of the accretion activity. We aim to establish whether the fading marks the end of the outburst that began in 1992 or a temporary switch-off. We present the long-term MeerKAT monitoring of the source from the ThunderKAT and X-KAT programmes up to 2026 July, deep dual-band (L- and S-band) MeerKAT Director's Discretionary Time observations, and three Einstein Probe/FXT X-ray pointings bracketing them. Following a last Jy-level flare in early 2025, the 1.28 GHz flux density declined monotonically to $\sim$0.2-0.4 mJy by 2026 March, and after 2026 May 9 it was no longer formally detected in our 10-min monitoring observations. Deep MeerKAT observations in 2026 July detect the source at $\sim$150 $\mu$Jy in L band and $\sim$90 $\mu$Jy in S band, the lowest radio flux densities ever recorded from the system, corresponding to $L_{\rm R}\simeq2\times10^{28}$ erg s$^{-1}$ at 1.28 GHz, with a spectral index $\alpha=-0.67\pm0.41$ across 1.28-2.62 GHz. The radio decline is the first direct evidence that the central engine itself is fading rather than merely receding further behind the absorber. The steep spectrum is consistent with either a combination of optically thick and thin emission, or an unusual, intrinsically steep core-jet spectrum. Should this mark the onset of quiescence, disc-instability models predict it to last of order $10^4$ yr. The switch-off of the central engine may shed light on the nature of the donor star and on the origin of the very-high-energy gamma-ray emission in this system.

10. An analytical framework for atmospheric tides on rocky planets. II. Semidiurnal thermotidal torque[2611.07060]
Abstract

The thermodynamic acceleration induced by atmospheric thermal tides has a major impact on the rotation of temperate rocky planets, where gravitational and radiative tidal forcings are of comparable strength. This acceleration drives planets away from synchronous rotation, thereby affecting their climate, large-scale circulation, and surface conditions. These implications underline the need to better constrain the dependence of the thermotidal torque on the tidal frequency and on the physical parameters characterising the star-planet system, as this relationship remains insufficiently understood. In particular, the role of resonantly excited Lamb waves requires clarification due to its potential impact on Earth's length of day (LOD) during the Precambrian. Building on a previous work, which provided a fully analytical formulation of the atmospheric tidal response of rocky planets, we apply this framework to isentropic and isothermal atmospheres. We perform a comprehensive functional analysis of the resulting torque and compare the analytical predictions with numerical solutions from earlier general circulation model (GCM) studies. The analytical model agrees well with GCM results, accurately capturing the frequency dependence of tidal torque both for paleo-Earth and present-day Earth. More generally, the solutions capture the torque peak near synchronous rotation and the amplification associated with Lamb-wave resonance. Near resonance, the GCM results exhibit a stronger braking torque than accelerating torque. This asymmetry is reproduced by allowing tidal heating to lag behind insolation. The agreement with GCM spectra suggests that thermal tides in the benchmark simulations are driven predominantly by an indirect thermal forcing characterised by a substantial time delay, in contrast to the direct absorption of sunlight that dominates semidiurnal tides on present-day Earth.

11. Systematic Study of Cosmic Rays in Star-Forming and Starburst Galaxies using Fermi-LAT and Multiwavelength Data[2611.07189]
Abstract

The majority of $\gamma$ rays from star-forming galaxies (SFGs) and starburst galaxies (SBGs) are expected to originate from interactions between high-energy cosmic rays (CRs) and the interstellar medium, making $\gamma$-ray observations a powerful tool for probing CR populations and interstellar environments. Using 15 years of Fermi-LAT data, we conducted a systematic study of seven sources. The $\gamma$-ray spectra of the bright SBGs M82 and NGC 253 exhibit a decrease in energy flux below a few hundred MeV, suggesting a substantially smaller contribution from CR electrons than previously inferred. Detailed fits of the spectral energy distribution, which combine our GeV $\gamma$-ray measurements with reported radio and TeV $\gamma$-ray results, indicate that the electron-to-proton ratios in M82 and NGC 253 are comparable to that of the Milky Way. In contrast, energy densities of CRs in these two SBGs are 2.5–8 times lower than that of the magnetic field, unlike the Milky Way. For the nearby Local Group galaxy M31, we decomposed the $\gamma$-ray emission into two components, revealing a central source with a soft spectrum and a compact size of less than 0.33 degree, which indicates a considerable contribution from unresolved sources. Our new analysis also reduces the $\gamma$-ray fluxes of several SBGs, bringing all SBG sample members to levels comparable to or below the calorimetric limit, with calorimetric efficiencies ranging from 0.33 to 1.20. In contrast, all SFG sample members exhibit low efficiencies at the 10\% level. We developed a theoretical model of interstellar $\gamma$-ray emission that successfully reproduces the observed calorimetric efficiencies, providing a robust framework for interpreting and predicting $\gamma$-ray emission from SFGs and SBGs.

12. Coordinated Space and Ground Monitoring of Accretion Bursts in a Protoplanetary Disk: MIRI and iSHELL observe the long-term modulation of gas within a small inner disk cavity[2611.07253]
Abstract

DQ Tau is a TTauri eccentric binary with pulsed accretion bursts regulated by its $\sim 16$-day orbital period. We have monitored this system with seven instruments from ground and space over 40 days. We present results on molecular gas variability from nine JWST-MIRI and nine IRTF-iSHELL epochs over three sequential accretion bursts with accretion luminosity pulses increasing by factors of $\sim 9$-80. A fundamental new measurement from this analysis is the line broadening of CO (from iSHELL) and H$_2$O (from MIRI) lines, which we use to estimate Keplerian radii and their variability in parallel to the line luminosity. This unprecedented dataset clearly shows that the molecular emission does not react like HI and the 5-$\mu$m continuum with each accretion burst: the line luminosity and width of all hot molecular gas tracers decrease over $\sim30$-35 days, consistent with an emitting region decreasing in temperature and mass as it recedes to larger radii within the $\sim 0.2$-0.4 au inner cavity perturbed by the binary. The infrared molecular spectrum of DQ Tau is affected more by disk dynamics then by chemical changes triggered by variable accretion, supporting model expectations for chemical relaxation timescales that are much longer than the duration of these accretion bursts ($\gg 10$ days). The variability observed in all tracers in this study is consistent with a scenario where gas mass builds up near the disk edge to modulate the burst peak intensity and gas density in the inner cavity every $\sim4$-5 orbital periods. The origin of this modulation is still unclear.

13. Using Structure Functions to Characterize Protoplanetary Disk Velocity Residuals[2611.07305]
Abstract

Protoplanetary disks, the birthplace of planetary systems, are highly dynamic environments. High angular and spectral resolution observations have revealed non-Keplerian motions in nearly every source studied, indicative of (magneto-)hydrodynamical instabilities or planet-disk interactions. However, these velocity perturbations rarely exhibit ordered structure, limiting our ability to attribute them to a specific mechanism. We propose the second-order structure function as a tool for recovering structural properties of velocity perturbations in the presence of significant noise. Using morphological models, we demonstrate how easy-to-calculate heuristics and parametric model fits together identify structure in noisy data and recover its properties, including correlation lengths, their radial scaling where genuinely present, and the anisotropy. We further outline a workflow to de-noise the data and to build a matched null model against which inferences are compared. Applying the framework to one numerical simulation each of the gravitational, magneto-rotational and vertical-shear instabilities, we recover their structure at high significance and the structural heuristics separate all three pairs, with the gravitational and vertical-shear instability simulations remaining the hardest to distinguish once observationally degraded. As a demonstration on real data, 12CO observations of TW Hya reveal azimuthally elongated velocity structure that persists once the large-scale m=1 component is removed. We also delineate where a Gaussian description breaks down for coherent features such as spiral arms, and how inclination affects the recovered heuristics. The methodology is implemented in the eddy package and provides a foundation for statistically characterizing turbulent velocity structure in high-resolution disk observations.

14. Double Neutron Star Mergers and Fast Blue Optical Transients: A Circumbinary Disk Connection[2611.07326]
Abstract

Post-common-envelope evolution (CEE) circumbinary disks (CBDs) form when part of the ejected envelope remains bound and circularizes around the stripped core and its companion. Leveraging results of scale-free hydrodynamical simulations, we develop a framework to model the effect of such disks on subsequent binary evolution, and implement it in the population synthesis code COMPAS. Applying our framework to double neutron star (DNS) merger progenitors, we find that CBDs generally induce substantial orbital shrinkage, changing the merging DNS population and lowering the overall merger rate. We explore an extensive grid of binary-evolution and disk models, and find that CBD interactions reduce the DNS merger rate by up to $28\%$, primarily through 'premature' mergers between the neutron star (NS) and the giant's core. These mergers may power luminous fast blue optical transients (LFBOTs), occurring at a fiducial rate of $\simeq11 \:\rm Gpc^{-3} \rm yr^{-1}$. Our results indicate that natal kicks contribute little to LFBOT offsets, and that they should be associated with young stellar populations, though not necessarily active star-forming regions. Since LFBOT progenitors would otherwise produce short-delay DNS mergers, CBDs further challenge DNS mergers as the source of early r-process enrichment. For surviving DNS mergers, accretion from the disk leads to higher chirp masses, potentially explaining the puzzling mass of GW190425. Our framework provides a general tool for studying CBD-binary interactions in post-CEE systems.

15. Algebraic classification of single-field inflationary dynamics[2611.07331]
Abstract

We develop an algebraic classification of canonical single-field inflationary dynamics using an exact depressed cubic equation for the inflaton velocity. Its root structure identifies a branch-swap boundary distinct from the limiting boundary of downhill motion, where ultra-slow-roll inflation naturally emerges. Independently, the conservation of momentum conjugate to the comoving curvature perturbation in the long-wavelength limit provides a criterion for the growth or decay of the gauge-invariant difference between comoving and uniform-density curvature perturbations. Combining these criteria, we construct a unified dynamical chart in terms of the Hubble and potential slow-roll parameters, illustrating its applicability to constant-roll inflation and simple toy models. Our classification shifts the emphasis from assigning models to fixed categories toward understanding the dynamical regimes traversed by inflationary trajectories and their implications for primordial perturbations.

16. High precision timing of seven binary millisecond pulsars with the European Pulsar Timing Array[2611.07363]
Abstract

Binary millisecond pulsars (MSPs) serve as exceptional astrophysical laboratories for a wide range of astrophysical studies, all of which rely on precise property measurements allowed by pulsar timing. In this paper, we present high-precision timing solutions for seven binary MSPs monitored by the European Pulsar Timing Array. By combining data collected from the five largest radio telescopes in Europe, as well as their coherently combined form, the Large European Array for Pulsars, we achieve phase-coherent timing solutions for all seven pulsars. We conduct a Bayesian timing and noise analysis on the combined data, yielding measurements of astrometric and orbital parameters with broadly improved precision, some reported for the first time. For PSRs J0125-2327, J1741+1351 and J2043+1711, we determine their distances and transverse velocities from astrometric measurements, and infer their masses via Shapiro delay measurements. Additionally, we examine the various contributions to the secular orbital-period variations for PSRs J0125-2327, J1125+7819 and J1741+1351, and estimate their kinematic distances. For PSRs J0125-2327 and J1741+1351, we detect the signature of annual-orbital parallax in the timing data. This allows us to derive a strongly favored, unique solution for the orbital ascending node and inclination angle for PSR J0125-2327, and a tightly constrained ascending node for PSR J1741+1351. Moreover, we use the orbital periods and their derivatives for several binary MSPs to place upper limits on gravitational-wave strain in both the sub-nanohertz and microhertz frequency regimes. Our results show that the orbits of binary MSPs could provide stringent constraints in microhertz range, thereby possibly complementing standard pulsar-timing-array analyses in the future.

17. No Significant Rotation of the Spiral Structure in the IM Lup Protoplanetary Disk[2611.07405]
Abstract

Spiral structures are often observed in protoplanetary disks, but their origins remain uncertain. Yoshida et al. (2025) reported a pattern speed consistent with local Keplerian rotation for the $m=2$ spiral in the dusty disk around IM Lup using multi-epoch ALMA observations, supporting a gravitational instability (GI)-driven origin. However, recent studies revise the disk geometry of IM Lup, motivating a reassessment of its spiral pattern speed. In this study, we reanalyze the disk geometry of the same multi-epoch data using visibility-based continuum emission modeling. The inferred disk geometries differ from those adopted in previous studies by a few degrees but are consistent across epochs and with recent independent estimates based on gas emission analyses. The revised geometries yield weaker residuals and more physically plausible radial spiral phase profiles. Unlike previous studies, our remeasured spiral pattern speeds are consistent with zero rotation within the uncertainties. We conclude that the previously reported Keplerian-like motion can be explained by a mismatch in the adopted disk geometry, which affects the spiral phases measured in Band 6 (2017, 2019) and Band 7 (2024) differently. In addition, we identify a tentative large-scale one-armed spiral-like structure extending beyond the known $m=2$ spiral, which may provide clues to additional perturbations, such as an embedded planet or late-stage infall. Future high-resolution Band 6 observations with a longer time baseline will be essential for more accurately determining the spiral pattern speed.

18. Systematic Search for Cataclysmic Variables in Radio Surveys: First Radio Detection of IX Velorum[2611.07448]
Abstract

We present a systematic search for radio emission from cataclysmic variables (CVs) across four wide-field radio surveys in the 0.7-4 GHz frequency range: VLASS; RACS; VAST; and EMU. We generated a reliable catalog of 2225 optically-identified CV candidates using a multi-stage cross-match, combined with systematic and manual verification; 1374 are matched to Gaia sources within the radio surveys' footprints. Among these, we detected six CVs in at least one epoch (at 1e16.5-1e17.5 erg/s/Hz, consistent with the typical population of radio-detected CVs); five had been detected in radio before. Finally, we created population-wide image stacks of the CVs within 100 pc, using 14 epochs from 5 CVs in EMU to constrain their median persistent specific luminosity to <3e14 erg/s/Hz.

19. Probing the Seed Effect of Supermassive Primordial Black Holes with Weak Gravitational Lensing[2611.07574]
Abstract

We investigate the cosmological implications of supermassive primordial black holes (PBHs) as a component of dark matter (DM), focusing on their seed effect on large-scale structure formation. In contrast to the Poisson effect associated with the discrete distribution of PBHs, the seed effect arises because PBHs act as early gravitational seeds, accreting surrounding DM and developing dense DM spikes to form dressed PBHs. These structures enhance the matter power spectrum, particularly on small scales. We model their contribution within the halo-model framework under two prescriptions for DM accretion: an optimistic scenario (Case 1) and a conservative scenario (Case 2), which predict different modifications to the 1-halo term. We constrain the PBH abundance using weak-lensing measurements from the Dark Energy Survey Year 3 (DES Y3), performing a Markov Chain Monte Carlo analysis to derive upper limits on the PBH fraction $f_{\rm PBH}$, as a function of the PBH mass $M_{\rm PBH}$. We find that weak lensing places competitive constraints on supermassive PBHs, with the upper limits on $f_{\rm PBH}$ becoming progressively stronger toward higher PBH masses. Case 1, which predicts more efficient DM accretion and hence a larger enhancement of the matter power spectrum, yields stronger constraints than Case 2; in particular, it excludes $f_{\rm PBH}\gtrsim10^{-4}$ for $M_{\rm PBH}\gtrsim10^{10} M_\odot$. We further forecast the sensitivity of the Vera C. Rubin Observatory's Legacy Survey of Space and Time (LSST) using mock weak-lensing data and find that the constraints could improve by approximately two orders of magnitude. Our results establish weak gravitational lensing as a sensitive probe of the seed effect of supermassive PBHs and demonstrate its potential to complement other cosmological and astrophysical searches.

20. The Influence of the Inclination of the Accretion Disk in Black Hole X-Ray Binaries on the Energy Spectrum Characteristics[2611.07590]
Abstract

During the ascending and descending phases of some black hole X-ray binaries(BH XRBs), it was found that the X-ray Eddington ratio ($ L_{X-ray(3-25keV)}/L_{Edd} $) and the photon spectral index $\Gamma$ had an anticorrelation when the X-ray Eddington ratio was less than a specific value, and a positive correlation when it was greater than that value. The reason why the cross points are different for different BH XRBs were still uncertain. However, for different BH XRBs, there is a positive linear correlation between the X-ray Eddington ratio at the cross point and the cosine of the accretion disk inclination. Therefore, We think that the geometric effect of the inclination has a significant impact on the evolution of the energy spectrum of the BH XRBs.

21. Discovery of the redback pulsar PSR J2053+6905 and multi-epoch optical observations of the system[2611.07668]
Abstract

Using the Green Bank Telescope, we discovered the 1.68-ms binary pulsar PSR J2053+6905. The discovery resulted from a targeted search for radio pulsations from a redback candidate, previously identified through optical and X-ray observations and spatially coincident with the $\gamma$-ray source 4FGL J2054.2+6904. We report the first results from the pulsar timing, including the spin period, dispersion measure, precisely measured Keplerian binary parameters, and the pulsar mass function. We also found potential evidence of scintillation and long radio eclipses. To constrain the system parameters, we performed multi-epoch time-series photometric observations using the 2.1-m telescope at the Observatorio Astronómico Nacional on the Sierra San Pedro Mártir (Mexico) and the 1.5-m telescope of the Maidanak Astronomical Observatory (Uzbekistan), complemented by time-series optical spectroscopy with the 10.4-m Gran Telescopio Canarias. The optical light curves revealed a pronounced asymmetry, as well as significant long-term variations. From the spectra, we derived a radial velocity curve semi-amplitude $K = 337(4)$ km s$^{-1}$. Combining it with the pulsar mass function, we constrained the ratio of pulsar and companion masses to $M_{\rm p}/M_{\rm c} = 7.05(8)$. By fitting the stable segments of the light curves with a symmetric direct heating model, we derived constraints on the component masses ($M_{\rm p} \approx 2.1\ {\rm M}_\odot$ and $M_{\rm c} \approx 0.3\ {\rm M}_\odot$), the Roche lobe filling factor, and the companion's day- and night-side temperatures. The long-term evolution of the light curve profiles is likely driven by the dissipation or migration of a cold stellar spot.

22. Thorium and Europium production in the early Galaxy: Implications for nucleocosmochronology[2611.07697]
Abstract
