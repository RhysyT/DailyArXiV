1. Resolving the heart of the Wolf-Rayet binary Apep with ALMA[2610.22406]
Abstract

The Wolf-Rayet (WR) phase is a critical stage in the evolution of massive stars. During this phase, the stars experience a large mass loss in the form of fast winds that are enriched with heavy elements. There are only a few WR binaries in the galaxy, with Apep being a truly exceptional system exhibiting a bright colliding-wind region at radio wavelengths and an impressive dust plume in the infrared. Although the angular separation between the two (WN and WC) components has been indirectly inferred to be about 47 mas, it has yet to be confirmed through direct measurements. We aim to image the Apep system at millimetre wavelengths to determine the position of the WR stars and their mass-loss rates. We observed Apep with ALMA in Bands 6 and 7 in a large baseline configuration, achieving an angular resolution of approximately 20 mas. We used a canonical spherical wind model to convert the measured flux densities into wind mass-loss rates. Towards Apep, we detect point-like emission coming from the WN star and elongated emission most likely from the colliding-wind region; the WC star is not detected. We also detect the O star at 0.7” that has been associated with the system. Our analysis favours mass-loss rates for the WN and WC components of $\dot{M}_\mathrm{WN}\sim 2\times10^{-5}\,M_\odot \,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}$ and $\dot{M}_\mathrm{WC}\sim10^{-5}\,M_\odot\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}$, respectively. The mass-loss rates of the WR stars are significantly lower than previously reported, and we examine several factors that may account for this discrepancy. The diffuse emission from the colliding-wind region cannot be accounted for by the synchrotron flux, and thus additional free-free or dust emission is present.

2. Discovery of eclipses in the cataclysmic variable MASTER OT J232100.42+494614.0[2610.22443]
Abstract

I performed photometric observations of the poorly studied cataclysmic variable MASTER OT J232100.42+494614.0 and discovered eclipses. I observed nine eclipses over two groups of closely spaced nights, separated by a 12-month interval. I precisely calculated the orbital period of the system to be Porb=0.212371533+0.000000069 days. For the eclipses, I created an ephemeris that is accurate for a long time span and suitable for analysing changes in the orbital period. The star's brightness outside of the eclipses varied between 15.61+0.02 and 18.04+0.05 mag. As the brightness decreased, the eclipses became more profound whereas its width remained unchanged. The average depth of eclipses was 1.36+0.03 mag, and the average width at half-depth was 0.0512(15) phases. I calculated the possible inclination of the orbit to be between 72.8 and 76.3 degrees, and the range of the average absolute V-band magnitude of the disc to be between 6.37+0.35 and 6.68+0.35 mag. Although the light curve of MASTER OT J232100.42+494614.0 from the ZTF survey showed insufficiently large outbursts with amplitudes less than 2.5 mag, it should be classified as a dwarf nova, as the average disc brightness and mass transfer rate are below the threshold for thermal instability.

3. Four Which I Knew Not: The Hexadecapole Nearly Cancels the Octupole in Librating Kozai-Lidov Cycles[2610.22487]
Abstract

Phenomena requiring close proximity between two bodies, from black-hole mergers to hot Jupiters, may originate in hierarchical triples, in which secular torques from a distant companion drive the inner binary's eccentricity nearly to unity. Population syntheses of these channels commonly integrate the double-averaged (DA) secular equations truncated at octupole order. We show that, for a fixed outer orbit, this truncation fails for the slow evolution on librating Kozai-Lidov cycles, in which the argument of pericenter librates. On these cycles the leading octupole kicks to $j_z$, the component of the inner angular momentum along the outer one, cancel pairwise, and the hexadecapole enters at the order of the surviving octupole torque, with the opposite sign. Strikingly, as the inner mass ratio vanishes the two nearly cancel, and their sum changes sign within the librating window, so octupole-truncated integrations omit part of the leading slow torque on this class. For $|j_z|\ll1$, the slow dynamics driven by the hexadecapole is approximately solved analytically as a simple pendulum in the doubled azimuth of the eccentricity vector, with closed-form coefficients on rotating and librating cycles. For a circular outer orbit the hexadecapole-modified cycles are solved exactly. For unequal inner masses the octupole adds to the pendulum's torque on librating cycles, where its alternating kick can also flip orbits, and generally dominates rotating ones. DA integrations follow the predicted excursions, periods and flip criteria. Population consequences depend on the validity of the high-eccentricity reduction and on other processes affecting the cycles.

4. An ALMA view on molecular complexity: water, methanol, and methyl formate in the HD 100546 and IRS 48 disks[2610.22503]
Abstract

Water and methanol are the fundamental constituents of interstellar and cometary ices: understanding their abundances in disks is therefore of high importance to reveal how this ice reservoir evolves across the timeline of star and planet formation. We present ALMA band 5 line emission observations of water and methanol in the warm Herbig disks of HD 100546 and IRS 48. While the IRS 48 disk is methanol-rich, water vapor is not detected, implying a high methanol-to-water column density ratio >0.2, possibly reflecting a distinct ice composition. Methanol and water are both detected in the HD 100546 disk, leading to a CH3OH/H2O ratio of  0.02, consistent with the median of the cometary distribution and with the lower edge of the distribution of Young Stellar Objects. We suggest that the gas-phase methanol-to-water ratio in the HD 100546 disk directly reveals the ice reservoir subsequently sublimated in the warm disk stage. Moreover, we report a methyl formate-to-methanol ratio in the IRS 48 disk of  0.2, consistent with the upper edge of the distribution of Young Stellar Objects. The methyl formate-to-methanol ratio in the HD 100546 disk is  2, much higher than in younger and older sources, possibly reflecting a chemical enhancement of gas-phase methyl formate. We additionally constrain non-LTE conditions, optical depths, and emitting regions of the methanol lines in both disks. These results offer a new opportunity to probe the chemical trail of molecular complexity in the intermediate disk stage, bridging early and late phases of the star and planet formation history.

5. The Occurrence of Circumbinary Planets Around TESS M-dwarf Eclipsing Binaries[2610.22625]
Abstract

We present the first determination of the occurrence of transiting circumbinary planets (CBPs) in TESS data. Since tight binaries represent a constrained environment relative to single stars, CBPs contribute to our understanding of planet formation and evolution. We applied our custom single-transit detection algorithm to the recently-published sample of TESS eclipsing binaries containing two M-dwarfs (M&Ms), finding only one signal attributable to a new candidate planet-sized object. We further performed sensitivity analysis and applied corrections to account for the TESS window function to arrive at our survey completeness. We find a 95\% confidence level (CL) upper limit on the occurrence rate of coplanar CBPs with sizes in the range $2-16 R_\oplus$ and with orbital periods up to 600 days of 33.0\%. If the mutual inclinations of CBPs are isotropically distributed with respect to the binaries, the occurrence rate upper limit increases to 40.2\%. Our occurrence rate estimates are limited in part by the misalignment of our short-period binaries relative to the line of sight.

6. Primordial Power Spectrum from the Lensed CMB Temperature Spectrum using Iterative Delensing and Fourier Denoising[2610.22648]
Abstract

The CMB power spectra are a strong probe of the primordial power spectrum (PPS) of the initial inflationary scalar perturbations. There are many proposed models, from the simpler power law form to various well motivated physical models that predict features in the PPS beyond the power law. Given the large parameter space of possible models, it is necessary to have model independent searches for such features. Weak gravitational lensing alters the CMB temperature power spectrum and can bias free form reconstructions of the primordial power spectrum (PPS) if not accounted for precisely, such as when using estimators like the Richardson-Lucy (RL) deconvolution algorithm. We build on our design of the Non linear Iterative Richardson Lucy (NIRL) estimator that updates the lensing contribution using the freeform PPS recovered at the previous iteration, making it truly model independent in the PPS domain while accounting for lensing. Inspired by signal processing and Fourier filtering techniques from radio interferometry, we also design a Fourier space iterative low pass filter (FLIP) to suppress noise overfitting in NIRL reconstructions. We demonstrate the performance of NIRL-FLIP on a range of simulated PPS models with bootstrap estimates of reconstruction uncertainty obtained from simulated realizations, and show that both our estimator and regularization are robust, accurate and precise, and are able to both reduce lensing induced reconstruction bias and suppress noise overfitting by a significant factor in Fourier space. We provide a detailed description of these results and recommend its utility for future model independent searches for hints of novel physics in the form of deviations from a power law PPS. This can further assist in precise and accurate testing of inflation models, in conjunction with joint cosmological parameter estimation.

7. Sparking Dynamic, Relativistic Magnetic Reconnection in a Twisted Flux Tube[2610.22653]
Abstract

Magnetic reconnection around neutron stars (NSs) forms small-scale currents while transferring energy to electron-positron pairs. These dissipating currents cover a wide range of scales, potentially leaving an imprint on time-variable X-ray bursts and pulsed radio emission. We present a reduced, planar model of internal reconnection in a magnetic flux tube with relativistic magnetization, finite resistivity, and strong guide field. Comparing with realistic NS parameters, this model represents the dynamics of a slender magnetic flux tube that is much thinner than the stellar radius. The seed current profile is smooth and does not spontaneously reconnect; instead, a discrete and localized current perturbation is introduced, representing e.g. localized energy release from a NS crust or magnetosphere. Three high-resolution relativistic magnetohydrodynamics simulations are described: one has uniform resistivity and Lundquist number such that reconnection proceeds in the plasmoid-mediated regime; the other two account for the feedback of charge starvation on the resistivity in current layers, where density is depleted; we provide a more general explanation for this depletion. All simulations produce a few dominant magnetic X-points and a secondary Kelvin-Helmholtz instability that excites high-wavenumber currents. We quantify the reconnection rate, the intermittency of dissipation, and the time profile of current spikes. We further present three-dimensional simulations confirming a limited growth of the ideal kink mode in the strong guide field regime. Applications are discussed, including to radio pulse microstructure and current damping below the magnetar surface during an X-ray flare, leading to mass loss and X-ray afterglow.

8. A Multiwavelength View of SPLUS J102139.31-282247.5: an X-ray Luminous and Eclipsing Dwarf Nova[2610.22790]
Abstract

We report the independent discovery of the cataclysmic variable (CV) SPLUS J102139.31-282247.5 using Southern Photometric Local Universe Survey 12 band photometry, following its initial identification via ASAS-SN variability. Here, we investigate the system using optical photometry alongside optical and X-ray spectroscopy. High-cadence optical light curves from publicly available TESS data and a dedicated campaign at the Pico dos Dias Observatory in Brazil revealed a highly inclined system exhibiting pronounced eclipses with an orbital period of 1.81 hours. We found no evidence of a WD spin period. Despite seasonal gaps, the long-term ASAS-SN light curve revealed several outbursts (15 with $\Delta$mag $\sim$ 1.5 brighter than the mean magnitude) and one superoutburst over the past decade. During Swift X-ray telescope observations, the system exhibited X-ray luminosity of $8.5 \times 10^{31}$ erg s$^{-1}$ in the 0.3-10 keV band, originating from thermal plasma emission at a temperature consistent with accretion shocks. Optical spectroscopy from the Gemini Multi-Object Spectrographs on the Gemini South telescope confirms the CV nature of the system, showing strong, double-peaked H and HeI emission lines alongside a weak HeII4686 line. Altogether, these features strongly support the classification of SPLUS J102139.31-282247.5 as a quiescent, eclipsing dwarf nova with an unusually high X-ray luminosity.

9. A Unified Solution to the Low-Mass X-ray Binary Diversity Puzzle: Saturated Magnetic Braking Coupled with Irradiation-Driven Winds[2610.22816]
Abstract

The standard magnetic braking (MB) model has long failed to explain key properties of low-mass X-ray binaries (LMXBs)—including their accretion rates, the formation of ultracompact binaries, and orbital expansion—casting doubt on binary evolution and gravitational-wave progenitor predictions. Phenomenological modifications exist, but a unified, physically grounded mechanism has been missing. Here we show that the overlooked coupling between magnetic saturation and irradiation-driven winds (IDW) provides this missing framework. Accretion-powered irradiation enhances the donor wind, which in turn amplifies MB exactly where needed. Our Saturated MB + IDW (SMB+IDW) model simultaneously resolves three outstanding discrepancies with no fine-tuning: (1) it reproduces the full observed distribution of accretion rates across persistent and transient LMXBs; (2) it naturally explains ultracompact X-ray binary formation; and (3) it quantitatively matches the orbital period distribution of descendant millisecond pulsar (MSP) binaries—a critical independent test failed by all previous models. Critically, the SMB+IDW model transforms MB from an externally imposed condition into a self-consistently regulated process tied to the accretion history. By unifying LMXB evolution to MSPs in a single physical picture, our work resolves the long-standing diversity puzzle and establishes a physics-based foundation for next-generation population synthesis, gravitational-wave progenitor predictions, and compact binary interpretation across cosmic time.

10. What can a uniform reflection pipeline establish about supermassive black hole spin? An audit and a corrected analysis of 57 AGN with XMM and NuSTAR[2610.23081]
Abstract

We audit an automated relativistic-reflection pipeline that fitted one XMM-Newton EPIC-pn and NuSTAR epoch for each of 57 AGN with relxillCp+xillverCp, and repeat the analysis with its failures corrected. The pipeline's apparent constraints were not controlled: Small Window pn backgrounds were never subtracted, the nearest-in-time pairing selected mismatched or nearly empty spectra, single-start fits often stopped short of the minimum, and most fits were inadequate or at a parameter limit. The corrected analysis uses in-window backgrounds, 41 sources with eligible pn–NuSTAR pairs, multi-start fits, bidirectional profiles, criteria fixed in advance and eight model variations. 24 of the 41 reference fits have $p\ge0.01$ and 18 pass all quality checks. Their 90% spin sets comprise 1 two-sided interval (NGC 4051, $0.46\le a_*\le0.93$), 6 lower bounds, 4 upper bounds, 4 disconnected sets and 3 unconstrained profiles. For 10 of the 18 the best fit lies at $i\ge75^\circ$, implausible for type-1 AGN, and most of those constraints disappear when $i\le60^\circ$ is imposed. Only 2 of the 15 constrained sets keep their type both under that limit and with pn restricted to 3–10 keV (an upper bound for Mrk 1040 and a weak lower bound for 1H 0419-577). Simulations show that, with a correct model, the 90% sets contain the true spin in 82–92% of cases without drifting edge-on, but at high inclination one low-spin simulation in six yields a false lower bound. We make no statement about the spin distribution or a spin–jet relation.

11. Maximal efficiency of geometric beaming and polarization of highly super-Eddington X-ray sources[2610.23089]
Abstract

Geometric beaming is an often invoked explanation for the apparent super-Eddington luminosities of ultraluminous X-ray sources. It is a natural consequence of the geometrically thick envelope that is expected to surround the inner accretion disk in the super-Eddington accretion regime; as the radiation can only freely escape through a narrow funnel-shaped cavity, the flux along the funnel axis is enhanced. A fraction of the photons will nevertheless escape the funnel cavity at larger angles, reducing the effectiveness of the beaming. To explore the limits of geometric beaming, we performed Monte Carlo simulations of conservative radiative transfer in an infinitely optically thick funnel while accounting for polarization. We discovered that the beaming becomes inefficient when the funnel opening angle is small $(\lesssim15^\circ$) because the photons tend to remain trapped within the cavity until they reach the upper edge of the funnel where they can escape at larger angles. For most of the photons to escape a narrow funnel through a small solid angle, the funnel has to be unrealistically long. Consequently, geometric beaming could at most boost the apparent luminosity by a factor of a few dozen in our idealized simulations. While larger boost factors on the order of $\sim$100 are possible for funnels with a length of $\gtrsim10^5$ Schwarzschild radii, this requires very narrow opening angles of $\lesssim3^\circ$. Additionally, we found that aberration due to mildly relativistic velocities does not significantly change this result. The maximal polarization degree of light reflected by the funnel remained on the order of a few percent for face-on sources and $\lesssim10\%$ for edge-on sources.

12. Ranking Prompt–Plateau Correlations in Gamma-Ray Bursts with Symbolic Regression[2610.23155]
Abstract

Gamma-ray burst (GRB) correlations probe central engines, relativistic outflows, and radiation processes. The Dainotti relation is an anticorrelation between the X-ray plateau luminosity $L_a$ and rest-frame plateau duration $T_a^{\rm rest}$. We use symbolic regression to compare it with other low-dimensional correlations in 251 GRBs with Swift-XRT plateau properties and Swift-BAT prompt-emission parameters. We perform a Dainotti-guided search, in which $\log T_a^{\rm rest}$ is always present, and an unrestricted search over subsets of at most three variables. The guided search recovers the Dainotti dependence; a D'Agostini fit gives $\log L_a=(51.92\pm0.30)-(1.31\pm0.09)\log T_a^{\rm rest}$, with intrinsic scatter $\sigma_{\rm int}=0.82\pm0.05$ dex. In the unrestricted ranking of 377 feature subsets, the classical Dainotti relation remains competitive, within the top $\sim25\%$ of the tested search spaces. The highest-ranked screened expression is $\log L_a\approx0.855\log L_{\rm peak}-0.782\log T_a^{\rm rest}$, with RMSE $\approx0.46$ and $R^2\approx0.88$. The highest-ranked runs repeatedly reduce to this prompt–plateau structure, without retaining additional spectral, absorption, or afterglow-shape variables. A direct regression gives $\log L_a=4.718+0.776\log L_{\rm peak}-0.805\log T_a^{\rm rest}$, with $R_{\rm P}=0.91$ for 246 bursts. An errors-in-variables refit gives consistent coefficients. After removing shared luminosity-distance dependence, the relation retains non-negligible explanatory power ($R^2\approx0.62$), consistent with a possible intrinsic component. Restricted Monte Carlo null tests give $p\approx0.01$. Plateau luminosity therefore shows a coupled dependence on prompt luminosity and plateau duration.

13. Numerical study of multiple scattering intensities obtained from dust grains of different sizes and compositions in an impact-induced ejecta curtain[2610.23176]
Abstract

Impact cratering of celestial bodies produces an ejecta plume containing underground material, which helps us understand the properties of subsurface grains and the surface evolution. In most cases, the ejecta plume forms an inverted conical structure so-called ejecta curtain. In-situ measurements of light scattering of ejecta curtain, such as the images taken at the Hayabusa2's impact experiment help to constrain the size and composition of these less-processed grains using Monte-Carlo radiative transfer models. Here, we analyse the variation in the predicted scattered intensities of an ejecta curtain set up after that of Hayabusa2's case and test its sensitivity to different compositions, sizes, and phase function of grains. Initially, the optical depth of the ejecta curtain is assumed to be constant. The intensities are seen to deviate from single scattering expectations, which we attribute to the effect of varying scattering angles and optical depths of the photons. Further, we show the importance of using the phase function reproducing actual grain materials in place of HG phase function in impact plume modelling. The results are compared with different values of optical depth and the study is also extended to incorporate a grain-size distribution. It is observed that there is an increase in the magnitude of the model median scattered intensity with the decrease of the power index of the grain-size distribution. Nevertheless, the overall variation in the profile between the different compositions remained the same as that of the monodisperse case with uniform density across the ejecta cone. Thus, we envisage this study to serve as a useful reference for investigating the subsurface grain properties of planetary bodies via impact plume observations, and to aid in improving available ejecta scattering models and also in the selection of prospective targets for future impact missions.

14. Foreground Groups and Host-Galaxy Geometry in the Dispersion-Measure Scatter of Fast Radio Bursts[2610.23333]
Abstract

Localized fast radio bursts (FRBs) show substantial scatter in dispersion measure (DM) about the mean cosmic DM–redshift relation, also known as the Macquart relation. Both foreground structure and host galaxies contribute to this scatter. In this work, we examine foreground tracers and host galaxy properties using a parent catalog of 136 localized FRBs with measured DMs and spectroscopic host redshifts. Among 77 FRBs within the Gfinder footprint, DM excess relative to a fixed Macquart mean increases with foreground group count excess, with a Spearman coefficient of 0.459. Adding the foreground term to a mean host baseline reduces the leave-one-out root mean square error (RMSE) and mean absolute error (MAE) in extragalactic DM by \(13.0\%\) and \(7.5\%\), respectively. For 20 of these FRBs with optical host axis ratio measurements, adding a foreground term calibrated on the other 57 FRBs to the host shape model reduces the observer-frame leave-one-out RMSE from \(76.3\) to \(64.0~\mathrm{pc\,cm^{-3}}\) and MAE from \(54.2\) to \(50.2~\mathrm{pc\,cm^{-3}}\). And the in-sample residual standard deviation in the host rest frame decreases by \(20.2\%\). Idealized galaxy models based on the Milky Way electron density distribution produce DM changes with inclination comparable to the fitted amplitude under the adopted assumptions.

15. The Dancing Bonfire: An Unusual Persistent X-ray Source in M81 Exhibiting Recurrent Luminous Flares[2610.23339]
Abstract

Short-timescale X-ray variability provides a direct probe of unstable accretion onto compact objects, yet extragalactic X-ray binaries (XRBs) with recurrent flaring remain largely unexplored. Here we present a detailed X-ray study of M81F4 (2CXO J095524.2+690957), a persistently luminous X-ray source towards M81 that exhibits recurrent short X-ray flares. Based on multi-mission X-ray observations spanning two decades, it is found that M81F4 remains persistently bright at $L_{0.5-8.0\rm~keV}\sim10^{38}-10^{39}\rm~erg~s^{-1}$, while producing flares lasting a few hundred seconds and reaching peak luminosities of a few $10^{39}\rm~erg~s^{-1}$, with an observed mean (minimum) recurrence time of $\sim0.5$ day ($\sim$6 ks). The stacked flare profile shows a rapid rise followed by a slower decay, but no significant hardness evolution. Spectral analysis reveals that both the flare and non-flare states are dominated by a hard power-law continuum, with evidence of highly ionized Fe lines in the non-flare state spectrum. These properties disfavor classical Type I and Type II bursts and have no close analog among known XRBs. We favor a magnetized neutron star accreting from an intermediate mass donor at a persistently high rate with the flares arising from instabilities at the disk-magnetosphere boundary. If confirmed, M81F4 might represent a hidden population of XRBs in which sustained, magnetically-regulated accretion can lead to brief episodes of super-Eddington emission.

16. Three-dimensional spatial modelling of nuclear de-excitation lines[2610.23380]
Abstract

Galactic cosmic rays (GCRs) are thought to originate from shock acceleration in supernova remnants, but interstellar ionization rates suggest an additional low-energy cosmic-ray (LECR) component. LECRs excite nuclei in the ISM, producing MeV gamma-ray line emission through subsequent de-excitation. Observations of this emission can constrain the LECR flux, morphology, and acceleration mechanisms. We construct all-sky maps of Galactic de-excitation emission using three-dimensional (3D) CR distribution models. By comparing them with the Galactic diffuse gamma-ray emission, we assess the detectability of the de-excitation line components against the underlying continuum. We calculate the emission by combining 3D CR distributions from GALPROP and gamma-ray production cross sections from TALYS. We also model an additional LECR component motivated by the high ionization rates measured in the ISM. The predicted de-excitation emission is relatively weak when integrated over the broad regions commonly adopted in MeV gamma-ray analyses ($|l|\leq30^{\circ}$ and $|b|\leq15^{\circ}$), contributing only $\lesssim 1\%$ above the leptonic continuum even considering the additional LECR component. The all-sky maps reveal that, in the standard scenario, the de-excitation-to-leptonic flux ratio remains low over the entire sky, reaching at most ${\sim}20\%$ toward the Galactic anticenter. In contrast, models including an additional LECR component exhibit a pronounced enhancement toward the GC, where the ratio exceeds $100\%$, indicating favourable prospects for detection through spatially resolved analyses. The 6.1-to-4.4 MeV intensity ratio is particularly sensitive to the maximum energy of the injected LECR spectrum, varying by up to ${\sim} 30\%$. Future MeV gamma-ray observatories could thus identify a distinct LECR population and constrain its acceleration and transport.

17. Prospects for Simultaneously Detecting Long and Short Duration Gravitational Waves from Pulsar Glitches with Current and Future Detectors[2610.23385]
Abstract

The internal mechanisms that drive the sudden change in rotation frequency in pulsar glitches could emit gravitational waves (GWs) at multiple time scales. The potential for GW analyses to detect both long and short forms of emission ("tCW-like" and "burst-like") improves as the detectors increase in sensitivity. We aim to examine how future detectors fare in making this joint measurement. By examining past glitches from the ATNF catalog, we quantify the double-detectability prospects of current- and next-generation GW detector networks. We further explore what astrophysical constraints could be placed following a double non-detection using extrapolations from the 2024 Vela pulsar glitch as a stand-in example. We find that a network with at least one next-generation detector greatly increases the probability of double-detection for many known glitching pulsars and improves the detection threshold in glitch energy by nearly two orders of magnitude over existing LIGO detectors. Additionally, double non-detections with networks with at least one next-generation detector could place dramatically improved limits on properties of the pulsar.

18. Impact of neutron captures on the r-process: I. Propagation of statistical model uncertainties[2610.23395]
Abstract

Background: The r-process is responsible for producing about half of the elements heavier than iron in the Universe, but the underlying nuclear physics is still largely inaccessible to experiments and relies on theoretical calculations. Purpose: We aim to quantify the impact of statistical model uncertainties on the r-process with a large set of neutron-capture rates within the Hauser-Feshbach framework with varied statistical model parameters. Propagating neutron-capture rates to r-process calculations of various astrophysical sites allows us to link abundance features to nuclear uncertainties. Methods: We calculate neutron-capture reactivities using different nuclear level density (NLD) and gamma-ray strength function (GSF) prescriptions. We study their impact on r-process calculations for individual trajectories, mass-integrated ejecta from a neutron-star-merger, and a broad set of 10,000 parametric trajectories with a wide range of electron fractions and entropies. Results: The final abundances are more sensitive to NLDs than to GSFs. Selected NLDs produce pronounced abundance changes between the lanthanides and the third peak. Although the variation is reduced in the mass-integrated total ejecta, a subpeak at A=172-178 and systematic abundance changes at A=180-194 persist. The parametric trajectories show that the NLD sensitivity is greater in the cold r-process regime, where neutron captures continue after photodissociations have become ineffective. Additional conditions with large NLD sensitivities are found at intermediate electron fractions, where small changes in the matter flow can lead to significant differences in the final abundances. Conclusions: Systematic differences in neutron-capture rates between NLD prescriptions affect the final r-process abundances. Therefore, improved experimental constraints and theoretical descriptions of NLDs in these regions are needed.

19. Topological Defects in Cosmic Shear[2610.23401]
Abstract

In this work, we present a novel approach for extracting cosmological information from cosmic shear maps based on topological defects in the shear orientation field. These defects are defined purely by the phase structure of the shear field and are therefore complementary to conventional weak lensing statistics that primarily probe amplitude information. We characterise their statistical properties, including number densities, spatial clustering, and the convergence distribution at defect locations, and demonstrate with Fisher analyses under idealised and realistic survey conditions that topological defect statistics provide a valuable and complementary source of cosmological information. With Euclid-like simulated data, the inclusion of defect statistics leads to constraints on $\Omega_{\rm m}$ and $\sigma_8$ that are up to 3.9 times tighter than those obtained from the convergence two-point correlation function alone.

20. SN 2025wiu: A Type Ib Supernova with Post-Peak Optical Rebrightening[2610.23414]
Abstract

We present optical photometric and optical/near-infrared spectroscopic observations of SN 2025wiu, a Type Ib supernova (SN) at z = 0.046927. The r-band light curve exhibits an initial maximum of approximately -18.2 mag, followed by a weaker optical rebrightening reaching approximately -17.8 mag at +38 days in the rest frame relative to the first maximum. However, the reconstructed bolometric light curve does not show a distinct second peak, but instead exhibits a reduced decline rate during the corresponding phase. The spectra consistently show Type Ib features without narrow emission components associated with circumstellar material. The late-time bolometric decline rate of 0.054 +/- 0.008 mag/day is faster than expected for complete trapping of 56Co decay. A radioactive-decay model for the first peak requires nickel mass = 0.47 solar masses, above the range typical of SNe Ib. A combined radioactive-decay and circumstellar-interaction model reproduces the full bolometric light curve with nickel mass   0.38 solar masses, ejecta mass   4.7 solar masses, and an effective circumstellar mass of   0.10 solar masses, with the interaction contributing from the first peak onward. The lack of interaction signatures in the spectra favours an aspherical, disk-like circumstellar structure engulfed by the ejecta, in which the interaction energy is thermalized below the ejecta photosphere; the fitted circumstellar parameters are therefore effective values rather than direct measurements of the circumstellar geometry. SN 2025wiu demonstrates that optical rebrightening does not necessarily imply a genuine bolometric secondary maximum and highlights the importance of multi-band observations for identifying the powering mechanisms of double-peaked stripped-envelope supernovae.

21. Can coupled thawing quintessence reproduce DESI DR2? A test with two potentials[2610.23497]
Abstract

DESI DR2 favours dark energy whose equation of state evolves crossing $w=-1$ in the past. Coupled quintessence can produce such an apparent phantom crossing without a phantom field, since a coupled dark sector analysed under the assumption of separate conservation yields an effective equation of state below $-1$. We test whether this mechanism can reproduce the DESI result, using two thawing potentials, a cosine and a double-exponential hilltop, confronted with DESI DR2 BAO, Planck PR4 and three supernova compilations. Both models improve on $\Lambda$CDM by $\Delta\chi^2 = 5$–$15$, recovering $64$–$92\%$ of the improvement achieved by $w_0w_a$CDM. They track the $w_0w_a$CDM equation of state at $z\lesssim0.4$ but cross the phantom divide later, near $z\simeq1$ rather than $z\simeq0.4$–$0.5$. The coupling is bounded at $|\beta|<0.073$ ($95\%$), and the two potentials agree on it to within $0.15\sigma$. They differ instead in the equation of state they can reach: the cosine comes closer to the evolution preferred by DESI and is weakly preferred by the fit. The coupling posterior is bimodal, with modes at $\beta\simeq\pm0.05$; only the negative mode produces the crossing, and it is the higher of the two in every analysis. In conclusion, the data favour the coupled quintessence phantom crossing but do not yet require it.

22. Possible detection of Baryon Acoustic Oscillations imprinted in the HI sources from the FASHI catalog[2610.23564]
