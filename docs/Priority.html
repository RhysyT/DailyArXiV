1. A Spectroscopic Census of Cluster White Dwarfs from DESI, LAMOST, and SDSS[2610.14166]
Abstract

Gaia has substantially expanded the sample of candidate white dwarfs (WDs) in open clusters, but many remain without spectroscopic characterization. We leverage the increasingly extensive archives of large public spectroscopic surveys to identify 62 candidate cluster WDs with available spectra from DESI, LAMOST, and SDSS. After discarding sources with very low S/N and unreliable host clusters, 44 candidates remain, including 37 non-magnetic DA WDs, of which 15 had not previously been characterized in the literature for cluster studies. We find that nine are consistent with single-star origins in their proposed host clusters, increasing the combined cluster DA initial–final mass relation (IFMR) sample to 132 WDs. All lie within $2\sigma$ of the best-fit IFMR from the most comprehensive recent cluster WD sample, suggesting these additions do not require recalibrating the empirical IFMR. One is a likely Hyades tidal-tail WD, providing further evidence that dynamical dispersal helps explain the long-standing WD deficit in the nearest cluster. We further compare WDs with spectra from multiple surveys. Among seven Praesepe WDs observed by both DESI and SDSS, DESI gives higher WD masses in six cases, with a mean difference of $0.067\,\mathrm{M}_\odot$, while the inferred progenitor masses differ by as much as $0.459\,\mathrm{M}_\odot$. More broadly, nine of ten single-star cluster DAs with DESI spectra lie above the empirical IFMR, with a mean final-mass residual of $0.06\pm0.04\,\mathrm{M}_\odot$. These results show that survey-dependent differences can strongly affect cluster WD parameters, and should be considered when combining heterogeneous spectroscopy in empirical IFMR studies.

2. Where Cluster Galaxies Reside and How Strongly They Lens[2610.14167]
Abstract

The efficiency of galaxy-galaxy strong lensing (GGSL) in clusters depends jointly on the inner mass distributions of member galaxies and their locations within the cluster potential. Observed GGSL probabilities exceed $\Lambda$CDM predictions by nearly an order of magnitude, but the relative roles of internal structure and environment remain unclear. We disentangle these effects by comparing HST strong-lensing clusters with TNG-Cluster analogs at similar lens redshifts, matched by their primary Einstein radius. For each member, we measure its lensing strength in isolation and when embedded in the cluster field. Members in cluster-critical regions are classified as Major or Minor Perturbers depending on whether they strongly lens in isolation; those in subcritical regions are classified as Intrinsic Lenses, Boosted Lenses, or Inert Members according to whether they lens independently, only with environmental assistance, or not at all. Observed clusters contain 33.7% of selected members in cluster-critical regions, compared with 21.6% in TNG-Cluster, while their Major-to-Minor ratios are similar. Outside these regions, observations contain substantially more Intrinsic and Boosted Lenses and fewer Inert Members, with Boosted Lens fractions of 25.8% and 13.5%, respectively. The discrepancy persists at fixed subhalo mass and after including line-of-sight structure. A mass-conserving contraction mimicking core-collapsed self-interacting dark matter largely recovers the conditional lensing fractions but not cluster-critical occupancy. The GGSL discrepancy therefore reflects both insufficient central concentration in simulated members and differences in their locations within the cluster lensing field.

3. Cluster Ages to Reconstruct the Milky Way Assembly (CARMA). VI. The age-metallicity relation of in-situ clusters and the age of the Universe[2610.14169]
Abstract

Within the homogeneous framework for the derivation of GC ages defined by the CARMA collaboration, we focus on the clusters born in-situ in the MW. As they are the oldest among the old GCs, their absolute age provides constraints about the age of the Universe. Moreover, the shape of their AMR is expected to show the imprints of past merger events, in case these were able to trigger GC formation in the Galaxy. We derive new homogeneous ages for 18 GCs dynamically and/or chemically classified as in-situ, and build the MW AMR from a total sample of 34 GCs. We find that all the selected GCs follow a well defined AMR, with the exception of NGC6642 and Pal1. While the former is likely a member of the LKH merger event, thus putting chronological evidence for the existence of this progenitor on even more robust ground, for the latter we propose an alternative scenario according to which it formed in-situ, but in a metal-depleted region of the Galaxy, possibly due to the interaction with an unspecified merger event. To find evidence of merger-driven GC formation, we use a GMM for the observed age distribution. This is statistically best-fit by 4 components, which we associate with significant events of the MW merger history. Cluster ages were finally calibrated to a more accurate absolute age scale based on asteroseismic measurements of the mass of red giant stars in NGC6791, and the resulting AMR was fit to compute the look-back time for the beginning of cluster formation in the MW. The fit yields t_GC,i=13.59+0.33-0.32 Gyr for initial cluster formation, and tU=13.79+/-0.34 Gyr for the age of the Universe. This value translates to H0=68.72+3.95-3.49(stat.)+2.65-2.32(sys.) km s-1 Mpc-1, where the systematic error comes from a realistic estimate of the zero point uncertainty on the GC absolute ages derived from stellar models.

4. A first view of interstellar Fe-bearing dust grains with XRISM[2610.14172]
Abstract

Understanding the reservoirs of iron (Fe) in our Galaxy is crucial for probing the mechanisms that drive dust grain growth in the interstellar medium (ISM). High-resolution X-ray absorption spectroscopy offers a powerful tool to investigate the chemistry of dust grains, particularly those containing Fe, through spectroscopic features near photoabsorption edges known as X-ray Absorption Fine Structures (XAFS). With the Resolve spectrometer aboard XRISM, we can now examine the Fe K-edge (7.1 keV) owing to its improved spectral resolution in this energy range. This enabled us to probe the dust grain chemistry in high Galactic column densities. In this work, we present a pilot study on dust grain chemistry through the Fe K-edge of the XRISM Performance Verification target GX 340+0. This source is a persistently bright low-mass X-ray binary with a hydrogen column density of 6.6x10^22 cm^-2 along the line of sight, providing sufficient optical depth at the Fe K-edge. By fitting combined dust and gas models, we find that Fe is depleted by 95% into dust along the line of sight towards the source. Moreover, we find evidence that 38% of the solid-phase Fe is in metallic iron, 47% in iron sulfides, and the remainder in silicates. The large contribution from metallic iron and iron sulfides differs from what is typically inferred from X-ray studies of diffuse ISM and may suggest that that these compounds become more significant reservoirs of Fe in higher column density Galactic sightlines.

5. Searching for Nearby Supermassive Black Hole Binaries with GRAVITY[2610.14174]
Abstract

We present a systematic interferometric search for close supermassive black hole (SMBH) binaries in 20 nearby type 1 active galactic nuclei (AGN) observed with VLTI/\grav. We investigate parsec-scale asymmetric emission by modeling the squared visibilities and closure phases with a single circular Gaussian component plus a background point source for the central AGN, and searching for additional compact companions through grid-based binary models. The robustness of the search is validated using injection-recovery simulations and point-spread-function diagnostics. Several features are identified in the initial significance maps, but only one candidate out of 20 shows a persistent compact asymmetry, corresponding to an observed incidence of $5\%$. If NGC 3783 is interpreted as a binary AGN, we derive a Poissonian one-sided $1\sigma$ upper limit of $f_{\rm bin}<16.5\%$ for GRAVITY-detectable binaries among bright nearby AGN (K<12.5) at separations of $\sim1$-$100$ pc. Conversely, if the observed asymmetry in NGC 3783 is associated with asymmetric circumnuclear dust emission, the corresponding upper limit on the binary fraction is $f_{\rm bin}<9.2\%$. We compare these results with predictions from the Horizon-AGN simulation, finding comparable binary fractions. We also noted that the expected fraction strongly depends on the assumed distribution of individual SMBH accretion rates within binaries, which is currently unresolved in cosmological models. Our results demonstrate the potential of infrared interferometry to identify parsec-scale binary AGN candidates and motivate future GRAVITY+ observations to expand the sample and follow up known candidates, helping to confirm their nature and distinguish genuine binaries from asymmetric nuclear structures.

6. Stellar discs and intermediate-mass black holes in galactic nuclei II. Fragmenting the disc and forming the S-stars in a live nuclear star cluster[2610.14179]
Abstract

We present direct $N$-body simulations of a stellar disc around the supermassive black hole Sgr A*, perturbed by an intermediate-mass black hole (IMBH) and embedded in a live nuclear star cluster. A retrograde IMBH with $m_\bullet=2000\,M_\odot$, relative inclination $\theta_\bullet=150^\circ$, and initial semi-major axis $a_\bullet=0.15$ pc breaks the disc into three disc-like components and an angular momentum filament. The stochastic torques of the cluster, together with the IMBH's inward migration, increase the angular separation between these substructures. By ${\sim}5$ Myr, their separations become comparable to those among the observed Galactic-centre young star structures. The innermost ($a\le0.04$ pc) disc stars evolve into an S-star-like population. Relaxation driven by the cluster disperses their orbital planes, excites their eccentricities and depletes the `zone of avoidance' within ${\sim}10$ Myr even without an IMBH. The IMBH further accelerates their orbital-plane reorientation. However, the most eccentric S-stars may require an additional formation channel, such as the Hills mechanism. The distinctive signatures of the IMBH are the well-separated disc components and the eccentricity excitation confined to the radial range swept by its orbit. Dynamical friction from the cluster reduces the orbital size of a $2000\,M_\odot$ IMBH initially at $0.15$ pc by more than a factor of two within $10$ Myr. Prograde IMBHs tend to align with the stellar disc, whereas a $2000\,M_\odot$ retrograde IMBH can anti-align with the radially overlapping disc stars. We also quantify how the IMBH affects the supply of stars to the loss cone and the rates of stellar tidal disruptions and binary separations.

7. Globular cluster Observations for Luminosity-dispersion Distances (GOLD): Project description and velocity catalogue[2610.14181]
Abstract

We introduce and describe the Globular cluster Observations for Luminosity-Dispersion distances (GOLD) project, which has the principal aim of determining independent distances based on the globular cluster (GC) absolute magnitude-velocity dispersion ($M_V - \sigma$) relation calibrated with Milky Way GCs with Gaia distances. The project comprises observations of GCs in 19 early- and late-type galaxies taken with FLAMES/GIRAFFE on ESO's Very Large Telescope. So far, 17 galaxies have been observed. The spectra cover the wavelength range 8483-9000 AA, including the calcium triplet region at a spectral resolution of $R\sim18,000$. The spectra provide line-of-sight velocities with typical uncertainties of $\Delta v = 1-2$ km s$^{-1}$. Using primarily velocities to separate extragalactic sources from foreground stars, we present here our initial sample of 1062 GCs and 101 ultra-compact dwarf galaxies (UCDs). For more than 650 of these objects we present the first velocity measurement. We discuss the available photometry and size measurements, and show that our velocities are in good agreement with previous studies. Our velocity catalogues are made publicly available. Beyond the principal aim of obtaining precision distances, additional science to be explored with these data includes host galaxy halo dynamics and clustering analysis, mass-to light ratios of GCs and UCDs in the sample, the stellar initial mass function and black hole occupation fraction of the GCs, and chemical abundances from high-dispersion spectra.

8. Beyond the Damped Random Walk: Multiband AGN Variability from SDSS to Rubin LSST[2610.14618]
Abstract

The flux variability of Active Galactic Nuclei (AGNs) is commonly modeled using the damped random walk (DRW), but increasingly rich multiband time-domain surveys motivate more flexible stochastic models that characterize variability beyond a single timescale and exploit dependence across bands. Multivariate continuous-time autoregressive moving average (MCARMA) processes extend the multivariate DRW to higher-order stochastic dynamics and provide a matrix-valued power spectral density for characterizing marginal variability and cross-band dependence. In a carefully selected Sloan Digital Sky Survey (SDSS) Stripe 82 sample, higher-order models are selected for 109 of 134 AGNs (81.3\%), with substantial rather than marginal support in most objects; convergence diagnostics indicate that this fraction is conservative. The fitted models also reveal strong cross-band dependence that generally decreases with wavelength separation. Motivated by this richer structure, we investigate its recoverability under Vera C. Rubin Observatory Legacy Survey of Space and Time (LSST) observing conditions. Model-order recovery depends strongly on the underlying stochastic behavior as well as source brightness and observing cadence. Recovery is high across many of the simulated settings, but deteriorates when the characteristic features of higher-order variability become difficult to distinguish from the multivariate DRW. Additional simulations show that this loss of distinguishability can persist over a range of underlying variability behaviors. These results show that stochastic structure beyond the multivariate DRW is prevalent in our selected SDSS sample, while its recoverability with LSST depends on both observational conditions and the form of the underlying stochastic variability. The methodology is implemented in the Python package mcarma.

9. Multiphase Analysis of (U)LIRG Nuclear Activity (MAUNA). I. Spatially Resolved Optical Emission-Line Diagnostics[2610.14653]
Abstract

We present resolved optical integral-field spectroscopy of the 32 luminous and ultraluminous infrared galaxy systems of the Multiphase Analysis of (U)LIRG Nuclear Activity (MAUNA) sample, drawn from the GOALS survey and selected to have JWST/MIRI medium-resolution spectrometer (MRS) observations. The data set combines new Keck/KCWI observations of 10 systems with archival VLT/MUSE cubes for 22 systems, covering H$\beta$ to [S II] at  1 arcsecond spatial resolution ( 0.9 kpc at the sample median distance). Using the optical [N II]/H$\alpha$ BPT diagram, we find that composite and LINER-like excitation together account for most of the classified area (median fractions 0.44 and 0.26), while Seyfert-like gas covers a median fraction of 0.02 but carries a far larger proportion of the line luminosity where it is present. Both the projected size of the Seyfert-classified region, $R_{\rm Sy}=0.08$-$7.5$ kpc, and its [O III] luminosity increase with the bolometric AGN luminosity, with $R_{\rm Sy}\propto L_{\rm AGN,bol}^{0.54\pm0.18}$, as expected for gas photoionized at a constant ionization parameter. The scatter about the luminosity relation depends on dust opacity: the [O III] luminosity per unit AGN luminosity falls by $1.09\pm0.25$ dex per unit depth of the 9.7 $\mu$m silicate absorption feature (Spearman $\rho=-0.60$, $p=0.001$). Optical excitation diagnostics therefore locate AGN-excited gas reliably but systematically underestimate the luminosity the AGN supplies to the host in the most obscured (U)LIRGs.

10. The $R$-Process Alliance: On J0010$-$1735, an Accreted, Chemically Unusual, Metal-poor Star with $R$-process Enhancement[2610.14987]
Abstract

We present a detailed chemo-dynamical analysis of the chemically peculiar very metal-poor ([Fe/H]$=-2.22\pm 0.11$) Milky Way (MW) halo star, 2MASS J00101758$-$1735387 (hereafter J0010-1735), discovered by the $R$-Process Alliance. We derive abundances for 45 elements, including 28 neutron-capture elements, and confirm that \thestar\ is highly $r$-process-enhanced, with [Eu/Fe]=$+1.30$. The light, $\alpha$, and Fe-peak element abundances ratios relative to Fe are all sub-solar, aside from C, Si, and most extremely, Ca. We investigate three possible formation scenarios based on the star's chemical abundances and orbital kinematics: whether it formed in the \textit{in-situ} MW halo, in a MW dwarf galaxy satellite, or in a satellite of the Magellanic Cloud system. Using the position of \thestar\ in the [Mg/Mn]–[Al/Fe] plane, the abundance pattern of both the light and heavy elements, and the kinematics, we conclude that J0010-1735 most plausibly originated in a now-disrupted ultra-faint dwarf galaxy that was accreted into the MW halo. We examine potential chemical enrichment scenarios of J0010-1735, including one similar to the $r$-process-enhanced UFD Reticulum II, or enrichment via a sub-Chandrasekhar Type Ia SNe, potentially supplemented by a Ca-rich gap transient and by a separate neutron-rich site that facilitated $r$-process enrichment. A nuclear cosmochronometric age of $12.5\pm4.2$ Gyr is derived from the [Th/Eu] ratio, consistent with an ancient origin in one of the earliest star-forming systems in the universe.

11. The Galactic disk structure revealed by Gaia XP spectra[2610.15007]
Abstract

The spatial structures of chemical populations constrain Galactic disk evolution and its underlying physical processes. Gaia XP spectra provide one of the largest spectroscopic samples available. We use Gaia XP spectra to study Galactic disk mono-abundance populations (MAPs), measuring their scale heights as a function of radius and radial surface-mass-density profiles. We construct a red giant branch (RGB) sample and divide it into MAPs according to $[\alpha/{\rm M}]$ and $[{\rm M/H}]$. The sample covers a $6\times6$ kpc region centered on the Sun in the Galactic plane and extends to $|Z|<3.7$ kpc. We estimate XP completeness using a Gaia photometric parent sample and combine PARSEC isochrones with a Kroupa initial mass function (IMF) to calculate the selection function and convert observed stellar counts into intrinsic stellar mass densities. We fit vertical density profiles with an exponential model to derive scale heights and integrate the fitted profiles to obtain surface mass densities. The resulting profiles are generally consistent with those from APOGEE. All MAPs show flaring, with the high-$\alpha$ population exhibiting the strongest flaring and the metal-poor and sub-solar low-$\alpha$ populations showing the weakest. Surface-mass-density profiles differ significantly among MAPs: the high-$\alpha$ and metal-rich low-$\alpha$ populations show approximately exponential declines, whereas the other low-$\alpha$ populations exhibit plateau-like features, indicating that the Galactic disk cannot be described by a simple single-exponential profile. These differential structures suggest a complex Galactic disk assembly history likely regulated by birth conditions, secular dynamical heating, radial migration, and external perturbations.

12. Photometric Identification and Dynamical Purification of Blue Horizontal Branch Stars in DESI MWS DR1[2610.15031]
Abstract

Blue horizontal-branch (BHB) stars are ideal tracers for mapping the structure of the Galactic stellar halo. However, traditional spectroscopic identification is limited by data quality and sample size, while broad-band photometric methods often achieve only moderate completeness and purity. Building upon the color-space BHB identification method of \citet{2025ApJS..278...62H}, we synthesize SkyMapper $(u,v,g,z)$ photometry from DESI MWS DR1 spectroscopy and identify 9,237 BHB candidates in the $(g-z)_0$–$(u-v)_0$ color space using the HDBSCAN clustering algorithm. We then construct a Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) Bayesian probability model with capture-recapture (C-R) prior to assign posterior membership probabilities, achieving a probability-weighted purity of 88.9\% and completeness of 94.2\%. Applying an escape-velocity cut based on the Galactic gravitational potential, we remove 1,461 contaminants with unphysical kinematic parameters, yielding a clean photometric BHB sample $\mathcal{S}_c$ of 7,776 stars (retention rate $R_{\rm keep} = 84.2\%$). Systematic analysis of the contaminants reveals that they are predominantly faint sources ($g\sim20$ mag) with very low signal-to-noise ratios (median SNR $<$ 5). When the BHB color–luminosity relation is incorrectly applied to these objects, their space velocities are amplified beyond the Galactic escape limit, confirming that the escape-velocity filter essentially acts as a physical detector of low-SNR–driven distance inflation.

13. The NIVARIA-Local Group simulations: Unveiling the origin of rotation curve diversity in dwarf galaxies and its connection to galaxy morphology[2610.15071]
Abstract

Dwarf galaxies exhibit a large diversity in rotation curve shapes, challenging galaxy formation models within the $\Lambda$CDM paradigm. We present NIVARIA-LG, a new suite of cosmological simulations studying dwarf galaxy evolution in realistic Local Group environments. This intermediate-resolution pilot lays the groundwork for the full-scale NIVARIA project. The simulations combine constrained Local Group initial conditions from HESTIA with the NIHAO galaxy formation model. The simulated galaxies reproduce observed stellar-to-halo mass relations, match the Baryonic Tully-Fisher relation, and capture the distribution of stellar effective radii, including compact and diffuse galaxies. We analyse the structural and kinematic properties of isolated dwarf galaxies with $10^7 \lesssim M_\star/M_\odot \lesssim 10^{10}$, focusing on rotation curve diversity. NIVARIA-LG naturally produces a broad range of rotation curve shapes, including slowly rising systems underrepresented in previous simulations and steep rotation curves. We find that rotation curve diversity is primarily associated with gas extent and inner dark matter structure: more extended gas and shallower inner density profiles correspond to more slowly rising curves, while stellar effective radius shows little independent association. Individual galaxies show that rotation curve shapes change rapidly as gas is redistributed during assembly. Recent accretion events are also associated with more slowly rising curves in the most massive dwarfs. Together, these results suggest that rotation curve diversity is linked to the baryonic cycle, which shapes the inner dark matter distribution. The Local Group environment and its filaments influence this evolution by regulating gas accretion and redistribution, producing the observed diversity in rotation curve shapes.

14. Anisotropic Radiation in Super-Eddington Active Galactic Nuclei: The Responsivity of Broad-Line Regions to Continuum Variability[2610.15255]
Abstract

Anisotropic ionizing radiation is a natural theoretical expectation for super-Eddington accretion in active galactic nuclei (AGNs), implying that the ionizing flux incident on the broad-line region (BLR) clouds differs from that measured along the observer's line of sight. Reverberation mapping studies have revealed systematically shortened time lags of the broad H$\beta$ emission line relative to continuum variations in super-Eddington AGNs, a phenomenon that can be naturally interpreted as a consequence of anisotropic ionizing continuum radiation. To further investigate this anisotropy, we perform a systematic analysis of the H$\beta$ line responsivity, defined as the ratio of the variability amplitude of H$\beta$ to that of the driving continuum, for both sub- and super-Eddington AGNs. By combining the effective responsivities measured relative to the driving continuum, after correcting for the continuum–emission-line delay, with photoionization calculations, we infer the ionizing radiation field received by their BLRs. We find that, in super-Eddington AGNs, the ionizing flux inferred from the H$\beta$ responsivity differs significantly from that observed along the line of sight, whereas the two are in closer agreement in sub-Eddington AGNs. These results provide compelling evidence for strong anisotropy of the incident ionizing radiation in super-Eddington AGNs. We conclude by discussing the broader implications of this anisotropy, including its potential relevance to the "little red dots" recently discovered by the James Webb Space Telescope.

15. Modelling the broken age-[Fe/H] relation and metallicity distribution function of mono-age populations[2610.15295]
Abstract

This study investigates the chemical evolution history and radial migration in the Milky Way utilizing high-precision age measurements based on large-scale stellar spectroscopic surveys. Recent observational data have revealed a distinct break in the age-metallicity relation of stars in the solar neighborhood, suggesting that stellar populations of the Milky Way can be divided into two evolutionary sequences. Based on galactic chemical evolution models, we confirm that this break in the age-metallicity relation can be explained by a recent major gas accretion event, which diluted the metallicity in the disk and triggered a starburst, leading to the formation of an independent age-metallicity sequence. To assess the impact of stellar radial migration, we incorporate a simplified treatment of this process into our multi-zone chemical evolution model. Radial migration is modeled as a probabilistic diffusion process, characterized by a Gaussian distribution. By fitting the model-predicted metallicity distribution functions (MDFs) of mono-age stellar populations to observations, we infer a migration strength of $\sigma = 2.15^{+0.100}_{-0.103}\,{\rm kpc}$ for stars with ages between 2 and 6 Gyr located at 9.5-11.5 kpc, corresponding to an average radial displacement of 1.92 kpc over 10 Gyr. This result is lower than previous estimates of stellar radial migration, suggesting that the role of radial migration in shaping the chemical structure of the Milky Way is less important than previously estimated.

16. The Tilt of the Velocity Ellipsoid in the Milky Way Disk with GALAH-Gaia Data[2610.15349]
Abstract

We investigate the metallicity dependence of the tilt of the velocity ellipsoid using more than 572,000 stars selected from GALAH and Gaia. We find that the tilt angles of disk stars can be well described by the relation $\alpha = \alpha_{0}\,\mathrm{arctan}(Z/R)$, and the $\alpha_{0}$ differ significantly among different disk populations, with $\alpha_{0}$ are 0.78 $\pm$ 0.05, 0.86 $\pm$ 0.07, and 0.77 $\pm$ 0.07 for the whole sample, the Low-[$\alpha$/Fe] and the High-[$\alpha$/Fe] populations, respectively. These results indicate that Galactic disk populations are globally tilted toward cylindrical alignment, implying that the gravitational potential of the Galactic baryonic disk plays a dominant role in shaping the kinematics of dynamically relaxed stars. Our detailed analysis further reveals a metallicity dependence of $\alpha_{0}$ for various populations, with the Low-[$\alpha$/Fe] and the High-[$\alpha$/Fe] populations are respectively $\alpha_{0}$ = 0.12 $\mathrm{sin}$(3.72 [Fe/H] + 1.09) + 0.75 and $\alpha_{0}$ = 0.20 $\mathrm{sin}$(2.99 [Fe/H] + 1.90) + 0.71. These results indicate that the metallicity dependence observed in the Low-[$\alpha$/Fe] population may be primarily associated with resonances induced by the Galactic bar, while the behavior of the High-[$\alpha$/Fe] population may also have been affected by additional dynamical and evolutionary processes, such as violent heating associated with mergers, accretion, and/or the formation of these stars in an environment shaped by mergers of gas-rich systems and/or a turbulent ISM.

17. The DAWN JWST Archive: A spectroscopically-informed population study of the physical properties of galaxies at z>2[2610.15901]
Abstract

Charting the evolution of star-formation, stellar mass and dust build-up over cosmic time relies on the accurate characterisation of the stellar population properties of high redshift galaxies. We present a catalogue of spectroscopically derived physical properties for  12,000 galaxies at z>2, with archival JWST/NIRSpec PRISM spectroscopy and JWST/NIRCam imaging from the DAWN JWST Archive. We present a custom version of the SED fitting code BAGPIPES, used to perform joint spectrophotometric modelling of PRISM spectra and broadband NIRCam photometry. In doing so, we publicly release a science-ready catalogue of the physical properties of galaxies beyond cosmic noon. We report key physical properties, such as stellar masses, star-formation rates (SFR) and timescales, and V-band dust attenuation (Av). We further probe the recent star-formation histories (SFH) of galaxies via their SFRs on 3 Myr, 10 Myr, and 100 Myr timescales, capturing both rising and falling recent SFHs. Through dust-corrected ugi colour-colour diagrams of massive galaxies, we show how quiescent and dusty galaxies populate this colour space at z>2, with galaxies experiencing a recent downturn in star-formation occupying distinct parameter space. We also study the dustiest galaxies in our sample, showing that they are preferentially more massive and strongly star-forming relative to the parent sample, residing on the high-mass end of the star-forming main-sequence. The slope of the attenuation curve has a strong dependence on Av, varying from steep to shallow as the dust attenuation increases and the specific star-formation rate decreases. Our findings show that the unparalleled number statistics of this catalogue of spectroscopically derived stellar population properties uniquely enables the study star-formation, quenching and dust properties of galaxies from cosmic noon to cosmic dawn.

18. An asteroseismic age estimate for the Pleiades using a neural-network emulator of rotating $δ$ Scuti pulsation models[2610.15939]
Abstract

Asteroseismology of $\delta$ Sct stars has strong potential for determining the ages of young stars through individual mode fitting. We present a neural-network emulator trained on approximately 46 million rotating pulsation models. From stellar mass, metallicity, age, and equatorial rotation velocity, it predicts 12 classical observables, two asteroseismic scalars, and the p mode, f mode and low-order g mode frequencies for degrees $\ell=0$-3, including the individual $m$ components of rotationally split multiplets. We couple the emulator to nested sampling through a multivariate likelihood that propagates correlated emulator residuals, making full Bayesian posterior exploration practical. Applied to five $\delta$ Sct stars in the Pleiades observed by TESS, the framework gives mutually consistent individual asteroseismic ages of 107-117 Myr. Combining their age posteriors gives a cluster age of $113.3\pm4.5$ Myr (statistical), consistent with the $112 \pm 5$ Myr lithium-depletion-boundary age and the 110-160 Myr range from rotating-isochrone fitting. Together with spectroscopic $v\sin i$, the inferred equatorial velocities also provide post-fit inclination estimates. Separate perturbed-grid tests using 100 matched synthetic pairs per input-physics variation give median absolute age shifts of $2.1$, $2.3$, and $5.2$ Myr for $|\Delta f_{\rm ov}|=0.005$, $|\Delta\alpha_{\rm MLT}|=0.1$, and $|\Delta Y|=0.02$, respectively; the corresponding 90th percentile shifts are $6.5$, $7.6$, and $16.2$ Myr. These tests describe conditional population sensitivities, not a systematic uncertainty on the Pleiades age. Together, the cluster-age agreement and the scale of these input-physics sensitivities support $\delta$ Sct asteroseismology as a complementary age estimation method for young stars.

19. Constraints on the r-process Delay Time from Dwarf Spheroidal Galaxies[2610.15946]
Abstract

The ancient stellar populations and simple structure of the local dwarf spheroidal galaxies (dSphs) make them excellent laboratories for studying the chemical evolution of the early universe. In this paper, we fit one-zone galactic chemical evolution models to C, Mg, Si, Ca, Fe, Sr, Y, Ba, and Eu abundances in the dSphs Sculptor and Draco. By including the s- and r-processes in our model, we are able to place constraints on the timescale of r-process enrichment in these dSphs. We find that neutron star mergers cannot be the only source of the r-process in Sculptor and Draco, and that a prompt r-process source is required to explain the observed stellar abundances. When neutron star mergers are the only source of the r-process in our models, their minimum delay time must be just 14-16 Myr. The neutron-capture abundances in Sculptor and Draco can also be fit by models with a longer neutron star merger minimum delay time combined with a prompt r-process source that operates on the same timescale as massive star evolution, such as magneto-rotational supernovae. Additionally, the Sr and Y abundances in the dSphs set limits on the Sr and Y yields per r-process event. We find that theoretical r-process yields overestimate Sr and Y production by a factor of up to 100.

20. TractorJAX: GPU-Accelerated SPHEREx Forced Photometry and the Trade-offs of Regularization and External Priors[2610.16007]
Abstract

We present TractorJAX, an open-source GPU reimplementation of the catalog-forced mode of The Tractor. We use it to test whether regularization or external flux priors enable reliable fitting of reference catalogs that extend below the depth directly constrained by undersampled SPHEREx images. For full-depth regularized fits, TractorJAX fits $100\times100$-pixel cutouts at 11.9 ms each on a single NVIDIA H100 GPU. In simulated images of the Abell 2537 galaxy-cluster field, extending the fitted catalog from $m_z<21$ to the full DESI Legacy Imaging Surveys DR10 catalog increases the scatter in fractional flux residuals by 34%, even with regularization. We also test the methods using SPHEREx observations in nine fields, assessing the number of reliably measured sources and photometric-redshift accuracy. Requiring S/N $>5$ in more than half of the 102 channels, we find that the regularized full-depth fit yields 40% fewer sources than the truncated fit. For sources meeting this requirement in both fits, the normalized median absolute deviation of the photometric redshifts, $\sigma_{\rm NMAD}$, is 17% larger in the full-depth fit. Abstract

CMB observations from ground-based observatories are limited in sensitivity by the fluctuating emission from the Earth's atmosphere, mostly due to water vapor inhomogeneities. Even in atmospheric windows, this spurious signal remains the dominant source of contamination in the data. Traditional mitigation techniques include low-frequency filtering, or for polarization measurements specifically, pair-differencing or modulation with a rotating half-wave plate. The first method filters out a significant fraction of the target cosmological signal while the second leaves residuals due to imperfections, temperature to polarization leakage, or polarized atmospheric emission. In this work, we present a new data analysis framework, based on estimation of atmosphere emission templates using a multi-frequency focal plane. The core novelty of this setup is to have detectors dedicated to atmosphere monitoring, allowing the removal of atmospheric contamination with time-domain component separation techniques. We introduce a multipole-dependent atmospheric decontamination factor $A^\mathrm{atm}_\ell$ to quantify the relative reduction of the atmospheric contamination angular power spectrum achievable with this approach. Using this criterion, we demonstrate that our component separation pipeline can outperform a classical filter-bin map-making pipeline by a factor of 4000 for $30\le \ell \le 300$.

154. Parameter estimation in differential equations: Mathematical foundations for satellite gravimetry, review and perspectives[2608.15148]
Abstract

Satellite gravimetry has become essential in many areas of earth science. However, the resolution of satellite gravitational models remains low at scales of a few hundreds km and no gravity recovery methods can take full advantages of unprecedented high accuracy of satellite tracking measurements. We first provide a unified theoretical framework of parameter estimation in differential equations for satellite gravimetry and then briefly review the mathematical methods to compute the gravity field of the Earth from satellite tracking. We focus on the collocation method, Kaula linear perturbations, two-point boundary value problems and orbit-energy-based methods. The numerical integration method is also included in this review, though it has been proved to be mathematically incorrect and physically not permitted. The reason is that it has become the standard method to routinely produce global gravitational models from satellite tracking data, which have been widely applied in many different areas of earth science. Because it is not clear how the incorrect foundation would affect gravity products from satellite tracking, we do not review any applications of these products. We then present a measurement-based perturbation theory to estimate the gravity field of the Earth, which can fully utilize both precise satellite orbits of arbitrary length and unprecedented high accuracy of satellite and inter-satellite tracking. The method is theoretically free of modeling errors, is capable of extracting any small forces from satellite and inter-satellite tracking data and provides a guarantee for high-precision and high-resolution global gravity models. Finally, we assume a reference gravity model and derive local solutions to the Newton's nonlinear governing differential equations of satellite motion for scattered tracking data that can still be important in some applications.

155. Blast.jl: Differentiable Non-Limber Power Spectra for Joint Clustering, Shear, and CMB lensing Analyses[2609.01855]
Abstract

Upcoming large-scale structure surveys require accurate theoretical predictions for angular power spectra on the largest angular scales, where the commonly used Limber approximation breaks down and multiple relativistic and observational effects become relevant. At the same time, modern cosmological analyses increasingly rely on gradient-based inference techniques, motivating the development of fast, fully differentiable algorithms. In this work, we present a major extension of this http URL , a numerical toolkit for the efficient computation of non-Limber angular power spectra. Building on the Chebyshev-polynomial decomposition of the original framework, the updated algorithm incorporates redshift-space distortions, magnification bias, primordial non-Gaussianity, intrinsic alignments, CMB lensing, and the integrated Sachs-Wolfe effect, while remaining fully differentiable through custom automatic differentiation rules. Despite the increased physical complexity, the algorithm retains favorable scaling by precomputing all cosmology-independent quantities. We validate the expanded framework against brute-force integration and established cosmological codes, and provide a detailed analysis of the trade-off between computational speed and precision as a function of the algorithm hyperparameters. We demonstrate the readiness of this http URL for gradient-based inference through a simulated likelihood analysis with LSST Y10-like mock data, recovering unbiased cosmological and nuisance parameter constraints from a 35-parameter model using gradient-based samplers.

156. CMB Data Analysis in the example of the POLARBEAR experiment and the LiteBIRD experiment[2609.32037]
Abstract

In this dissertation, I briefly go through my subject areas — from Astrophysics, Cosmology, to Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB) radiation; and Data Science & High-Performance Computing. I go through the beginning of the Universe briefly in part I and introduce the Physics of the CMB in part II. Then I move on to how it can be observed experimentally, mathematically, and computationally in part III. In parts IV to VI, I write about 2 specific CMB experiments — POLARBEAR & LiteBIRD, together with my research involved in them. In part V, original research on a measurement of the CMB $B$-mode at sub-degree scales is presented using the 3rd-5th seasons of POLARBEAR observations. This demonstrates the capability of a single medium aperture telescope with a Continuously Rotating Half-Wave Plate (CRHWP) to target both the low-$\ell$ and high-$\ell$ $B$-mode science at $50 \leq \ell \leq 3000$. In part VI, the effect of detector crosstalk systematics on the CMB power-spectra is studied in detail in the example of the LiteBIRD experiment. An analytical, map-domain solution is developed, a computational simulation method is implemented, and the two predictions are compared using the LiteBIRD experiment design specification. The general agreement of the two shows that the analytical model well describes the LiteBIRD experiment, and can be used to calculate the systematic effects without performing time-consuming time-domain simulations with a high degree of accuracy. This can be used to forecast the amount of crosstalk systematics and to develop mitigation strategies. A mitigation strategy is presented, which is a hardware-based mitigation method that minimizes the crosstalk systematic effects in the 2pt power-spectra.

157. An empirical calibration of the spectral resolution of JWST NIRSpec PRISM[2610.13492]
Abstract

We present an empirical calibration of the effective spectral resolution of the JWST NIRSpec PRISM disperser based on a large sample of galaxies drawn from archival observations. Our sample spans a wide redshift range, $0.5 \lesssim z \lesssim 10$, enabling a wavelength-dependent characterization of the PRISM resolving power across the full bandpass using the [O III]$\lambda\lambda4959,5007$ doublets. To ensure robust measurements, we impose strict signal-to-noise requirements on both continuum and emission-line detections. Emission-line profiles are fitted with multi-Gaussian models, and the full width at half maximum (FWHM) is measured for each source. The derived empirical resolution curve shows a systematic offset from the nominal pre-launch estimate, lying between the tabulated instrumental resolution and the point-source shutter limit. We also discuss how source size affects the final spectral resolution, and provide an analytical formula that combines wavelength and morphology dependence in a root-sum-square framework. This work delivers a population-averaged, statistically robust calibration of the effective PRISM spectral resolution for typical galaxy observations. The resulting calibration is directly applicable to statistical analyses of galaxy spectra obtained with NIRSpec PRISM. Our empirical calibration exceeds the pre-launch JDox values by a factor of 1.03 to 1.63, depending on source morphology, and provides the first wavelength and morphology dependent calibration of the NIRSpec PRISM spectral resolution.

158. A simple procedure for generating a Kappa distribution in PIC simulation[2512.04272]
Abstract

For kinetic modeling of plasma processes in space, a rejection-sampling procedure for generating a Kappa distribution in particle-in-cell (PIC) simulation is proposed. A Pareto distribution is employed as an envelope distribution. The procedure only requires uniform variates, and its acceptance efficiency is $\approx 0.73$–$0.8$.

159. Cosmic Rays as an Interdisciplinary Earth Observation Tool: From Particle Physics and Atmospheric Processes to Geosciences and Urban Science[2601.19265]
Abstract

The exploration of cosmic rays, which are high-energy particles originating from space and the atmosphere, has historically been associated with particle physics and astrophysics. In the last 20 years, these particles have evolved into valuable tools for observing Earth's systems. This review compiles the use of cosmic rays in three primary areas: (1) particle physics and atmospheric processes, which include cosmic- ray-induced cascades, ionization, and their impact on atmospheric chemistry and radiation; (2) geosciences, where cosmogenic radionuclides assist in the dating of geological materials and cosmic-ray neutrons are used for large-scale monitoring of soil moisture and snow water equivalents; and (3) urban science, where cosmic- ray muons are employed for non-invasive subsurface imaging and, when paired with distributed sensors, serve as the basis for smart city monitoring. This review places particular emphasis on integrating these methods with remote sensing and geographic information systems (GIS), which helps close the persistent scale gap between point measurements and satellite observations, thereby enabling three-dimensional digital representations of subsurfaces. This review concludes by discussing data standards, their integration into operational Earth observation workflows, and future research directions.
