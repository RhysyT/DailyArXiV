1. Spacetime contactability in Galactic civilization networks: Contact counts and percolation[2611.12923]
Abstract

The number of Galactic civilizations does not determine whether they are in contact. Allowing civilizations to appear at different times separates listening, audibility, mutual-listening, and relay networks, with a distinct reply-closing condition requiring a return signal within the sender's lifetime. For each network we derive the contact counts and estimate the connectivity threshold in a uniform population, then apply the results to a structured Galactic disc, separating the detection range $d_{\rm max}$ from the light horizon $cL$ through $\chi=d_{\rm max}/cL$. The contactability is $\eta=\pi n d_{\rm max}^{2}$, where $n$ is the active civilization surface density. Averaged over civilization appearances, the lifetime listening count is $2\eta$ for all $\chi$ and, at fixed detection range, for any lifetime distribution. Audibility and mutual-listening counts sum to $4\eta$, but connectivity depends on threshold behavior rather than counts alone. As $\chi\rightarrow0$, the listening threshold decreases from the coeval value $4.51$ to $1.32\pm0.01$. Assuming an ideal complete survey, our own non-detection gives the reference bound $\eta(R_0)\le2.75$ at the Sun's Galactocentric radius. This benchmark lies above the listening, audibility and relay thresholds throughout the range studied. Unlike the coeval model, the spacetime model can satisfy both this benchmark and a local connectivity criterion, moving the local-contact frontier outward by $3.2$ kpc. Under the same benchmark, civilizations hear at most $5.5$ others on average, while the mean reply-closing count falls below unity for $\chi\gtrsim1$. Contact may therefore be common even when completed replies remain rare.

2. Aster: A Flexible and Robust Collection of Numerical Tools for N-Body Problems[2611.13060]
Abstract

The Aster library is an open-source collection of numerical tools designed for large-scale gravitational N-body simulations. Aster provides a flexible environment to its users, enabling it to be used in the analysis of a broad range of astrophysical problems. The library implements efficient force calculation algorithms, including a spatial tree solver optimized with coordinate-dependent identifiers, which has been fine-tuned for both CPU and GPU architectures. Despite its high degree of modularity, many parts of the library have been optimized with SIMD instructions, heavy parallelization, and support on dedicated hardware. Aster also offers a wide variety of numerical symplectic integrators, such as SABA (from first to tenth order), the classic leapfrog algorithm, and the more advanced Wisdom-Holman (WH) integrator, while also providing a vast collection of mathematical tools, e.g., post-newtonian expansions (1PN, 2PN, 2.5PN), data collection and graphing systems, a built-in renderer, and presets for faster instantiation of large amounts of bodies. In this work we will discuss in depth the details of each module as well as the results of several performance- and accuracy-related tests, probing the library's capabilities in different physical applications.

4. High-precision abundance analysis of a unique CEMP-no star in Sculptor[2611.13115]
Abstract

The Carbon-Enhanced Metal-Poor stars (CEMP-no, low barium) are commonly accepted as the direct descendants of Population III (Pop III) stars which either ended their lives as faint supernovae (SNe; E_SN < 10^51 erg) or were fast-rotating stars (spinstars). While CEMP-no stars are common in the Milky Way (MW) and ultra-faint dwarf galaxies (UFDs), they are surprisingly rare in dwarf spheroidal galaxies (dSphs). This could hide an environmental dependence of the properties of Pop III stars, highlighting the importance of high-resolution studies of CEMP-no stars in dSphs. Here we present the first detailed study of a CEMP-no star with [Fe/H] < -3 in the Sculptor dSph. The high-resolution VLT/UVES spectra allowed us to derive one of the most precise abundance patterns obtained for a star belonging to a dwarf galaxy, providing chemical abundances for 17 elements and 4 upper limits. This star, DR20080, is extremely metal-poor, [Fe/H] = -3.26, and clearly enhanced in carbon, [C/Fe] = +1.60 (after correcting for internal mixing). Furthermore, it has low [Ba/Fe] = -1.28. The oxygen abundance of DR20080 ([O/Fe] = +1.53) is significantly higher than the average found in CEMP-no stars in the MW, while the iron-peak elements are under-abundant ([Mn/Fe] = -1.02; [Ni/Fe] = -0.45). The abundance pattern of DR20080 was compared to theoretical yield predictions of Pop III and Pop II SNe to infer the properties of its progenitor. The best result points toward a double Pop III progenitor scenario, with the major contributor to the abundance pattern being a Pop III faint SN with mass m_PopIII = 43 M_0 and explosion energy E_SN = 0.6 x 10^51 erg, while the minor contributor is a Pop III hypernova of m_PopIII = 19.4 M_0 and E_SN = 10 x 10^51 erg. The unique properties of DR20080 have never before been observed in Sculptor or in any other environment within the MW and its satellite system.

5. Revealing Hidden Excitation in the Core of NGC 4696: New Infrared Diagnostic Diagrams with JWST[2611.13116]
Abstract

The warm molecular and ionized gas in filamentary nebulae of cool-core BCGs provide a sensitive probe of the poorly understood exchange of energy between the ICM, cold molecular gas, and AGN feedback. Optical line ratios commonly yield similar LINER-like classifications across these systems, but do not resolve how the excitation of warm molecular gas varies between compact nuclear structures and surrounding filaments. We present JWST/NIRSpec integral-field spectroscopy of the central $3''\times3''$ ($620\times620~{\rm pc^2}$) of the Centaurus cluster BCG, NGC 4696. The NIR data resolve H$I$ and ro-vibrational H$_2$ emission across compact nuclear apertures, a circumnuclear swirl, and filamentary structures. H$_2$ excitation diagrams yield characteristic low-excitation temperatures of $T_{\rm ex}\simeq1.8-2.2\times10^3$ K, while curvature at higher energies requires hotter or non-thermal components. The H$_2$/Pa$\alpha$ balance varies strongly: filamentary regions have H$_2$ 1-0 S(1)/Pa$\alpha$ ratios 4-8 times higher than the compact nuclear apertures. In contrast, matched-aperture optical diagnostic diagrams show substantially less regional separation, with all regions occupying a low-ionization locus. We thus introduce new spatially resolved NIR diagnostic diagrams that reveal distinct molecular excitation conditions across the nucleus, swirl, and filaments. Ratios are broadly consistent with contributions from AGN-related photoionization, ICM-related cooling radiation, and non-radiative heating, although the present data do not uniquely determine their relative importance. These results demonstrate how spatially resolved NIR spectroscopy reveals excitation variations hidden in conventional optical classifications, providing a new route to constrain the multiphase cooling-feedback cycle in cluster cores.

7. Signatures of mass transfer in open-cluster blue straggler stars. I. Collinder 261: Evidence from rotation, binarity, and chemical abundances[2611.13123]
Abstract

We present the first dedicated $v\sin i$ survey of blue straggler stars (BSS) in the old open cluster Collinder 261 ($t=6.7$ Gyr), based on VLT/FLAMES-GIRAFFE spectroscopy of 40 BSS and 42 turn-off/subgiant (TO/SGB) reference stars. Seventeen of the 39 BSS entering the statistics (44%) rotate faster than 40 km/s, whereas all TO/SGB reference stars are slow rotators; fast rotation is strongly linked to spectroscopic binarity. TESS light curves identify seven eclipsing or ellipsoidal binaries with $P_{orb}=0.5$-2.1 d, all rotating at the velocity set by tidal synchronisation, so in these systems the fast rotation is maintained by a close post-mass-transfer orbit and does not date the formation event. Differential C, O, Ba, and Fe abundances for the 22 slow rotators reveal that 62% carry the surface-pollution signature of mass transfer, in two distinct patterns separated by an empty  1 dex gap in [Ba/Fe]: a Ba-rich group tracing AGB donors and a C/O-enhanced, Ba-normal group tracing donors below the $s$-process activation mass. The BSS iron abundance is consistent with the cluster value; the few apparent Fe excesses are partly explained by differential reddening and do not affect the abundance pattern. The correlated C-O enrichment is opposite to the depletion seen in globular-cluster BSS, indicating binaries wide enough for donors to reach the AGB. With $A^+_{r_h}=0.171\pm0.034$ and $f_{FR-BSS}=44\pm8$%, Collinder 261 extends the globular-cluster rotation-density trends into the open-cluster regime: its BSS were assembled by a continuously operating binary channel, and their chemistry records the dynamical environment of the host cluster.

8. Dust Reverberation Mapping of Low-Mass Active Galactic Nuclei[2611.13181]
Abstract

We perform a systematic dust reverberation mapping study of low-mass ($M_{\mathrm{BH}}\sim10^6$-$10^7 M_\odot$) active galactic nuclei (AGNs) selected from a literature-compiled catalog, using data from the Zwicky Transient Facility and the Wide-field Infrared Survey Explorer. Through quality assessments, we obtain a core sample of 65 sources with W1- and W2-band lag measurements relative to optical light curves. The W2-band lags are generally longer than the W1-band lags, with a median ratio of $\tau_{\mathrm{W2}}/\tau_{\mathrm{W1}}=1.24^{+0.33}_{-0.21}$. We further investigate the relations between dust radius and AGN luminosity ($R_{\mathrm{dust}}-L_{\mathrm{AGN}}$), using different lag measurements and luminosity proxies. Combining our sample with a higher-luminosity AGN sample, we establish unified $R_{\mathrm{dust}}-L_{\mathrm{AGN}}$ relations spanning nearly seven orders of magnitude in bolometric luminosity. The slopes are $0.47\pm0.01$ and $0.44\pm0.01$ for the W1 and W2 bands, slightly below the expectation of $0.5$ from dust radiative equilibrium. In the combined sample, the lag deviations show a negative correlation with Eddington ratio, potentially related to the self-shadowing effect of slim disks. The luminosity-dependent SED variations revealed by the SPHEREx composite spectra suggest that individual luminosity proxies may not fully trace the intrinsic dust-heating luminosity. Both effects may contribute to the shallow $R_{\mathrm{dust}}-L_{\mathrm{AGN}}$ slopes. Future joint optical and near-infrared monitoring with the Vera C. Rubin Observatory's Legacy Survey of Space and Time and the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope will further extend dust RM studies to lower-luminosity AGNs hosting lower-mass black holes.

9. Giant Radio Galaxies from the Third Data Release of the LOFAR Two-metre Sky Survey (LoTSS-DR3): Physical Characteristics and Environments[2611.13185]
Abstract

Giant radio galaxies (GRGs) are radio-loud active galactic nuclei (AGN) with projected linear sizes exceeding 0.7 Mpc, offering insights into the evolution of large-scale radio jets and their environments. We present 2333 GRGs from the LOFAR Two-metre Sky Survey Data Release 3 (LoTSS-DR3), with projected sizes of 0.7-2.9 Mpc, redshifts of $0.126 \leq z \leq 3.517$, and 150-MHz radio luminosities of $1.72 \times 10^{24}$-$3.04 \times 10^{28}$ W Hz$^{-1}$. Of these, 726 ($\sim 31\%$) lie at $z \geq 1$. We investigate their radio properties, lobe energetics, equipartition pressures, black hole masses, mid-infrared colours, and environments. Equipartition pressure decreases with increasing projected size, consistent with declining lobe energy density as radio structures expand, while its positive correlation with redshift persists after controlling for radio power and projected size. WISE colours highlight the diversity of the host-galaxy population, with approximately 75% of sources with available photometry falling within the empirical AGN/HERG region. For BCG-hosted GRGs, physical projected size shows no significant dependence on cluster mass. Although the normalized projected half-size, $D/(2r_{500})$, decreases with increasing cluster mass, this trend is consistent with the expected self-similar scaling of cluster radius rather than an independent suppression of radio-source growth in massive clusters. The substantial high-redshift population provides an opportunity to investigate GRGs at early cosmic epochs, although surface-brightness dimming and inverse-Compton losses against the cosmic microwave background can limit their detectability.

10. TITAN DR1: The Type Ia Supernova Trove from ATLAS in the Nearby Universe - Host Galaxies I: Associations, Redshifts and Derived Properties[2611.13195]
Abstract

We present the host-galaxy catalogue for TITAN DR1, a sample of 8378 spectroscopically confirmed Type Ia supernovae (SNe Ia) at $z \lesssim 0.15$ – the largest homogeneous low-redshift SN Ia sample assembled to date. We describe the association of each SN with its host, the compilation of redshifts, measurements of uniform far-ultraviolet to mid-infrared (FUV–MIR) photometry, and the derivation of host properties through spectral energy distribution (SED) fitting. Combining four host-association methods with manual vetting, we securely identify hosts for 7856 SNe Ia (93.8\%). We compile host-galaxy spectroscopic redshifts for 5522 objects and provide CMB-frame and peculiar-velocity-corrected redshifts. \rev{PSF-matched} photometry is measured for 7568 host galaxies, including both global fluxes and local optical-only fluxes within a fixed 1.5 kpc aperture centred on the SN; \texttt{Bagpipes} returns successful global fits for \rev{all} hosts and local fits for 7329. Stellar mass and rest-frame $g-z$ colour are robustly measured from optical data alone, whereas reliable estimates of star-formation rate, dust attenuation, and mass-weighted age require broader wavelength coverage to break age–dust degeneracies\rev{, and are reported for 5827 hosts}; in our redshift range, MIR data suffice and far-IR coverage is not required. The hosts span a representative low-redshift galaxy population, with a median stellar mass of $\log_{10}(M_*/M_\odot)=10.22$ (\rev{60\%} high-mass; $\log_{10}(M_*/M_\odot)>10$), \rev{71\%} star-forming ($\log_{10}(\mathrm{sSFR}/\mathrm{yr}^{-1}) > -10.5$), and an approximately exponential $A_V$ distribution. This catalogue provides the foundation for forthcoming analyses of the luminosity step and the broader role of galaxy environment in SN Ia standardization and cosmology.

11. Intermediate-redshift slow rotators as the progenitors of the most massive galaxies at z $\sim$ 0[2611.13225]
Abstract

We study the resolved stellar kinematics and star formation histories, and their dependence on the environment, in a sample of 218 intermediate redshift (0.1 < z < 1) galaxies located in the CANDELS and COSMOS fields, observed with VLT/MUSE. The sample covers galaxies with stellar masses ranging M_star  10^8 - 10^12 M_sun, including both star-forming and quiescent systems. For 113 galaxies, found in dense environments of galaxy groups, we derive the spin parameter ($\lambda_{R}$), while for the rest of the sample, we use data from previous papers in this series. Given that the surveys used in this work are flux-limited, the $\lambda_{R}$ distribution of our full sample lacks the bimodality observed in the local Universe, but the subsample of galaxies at z < 0.6 resembles the galaxy population in the nearby Universe, in terms of mass, sizes, and stellar population properties, more than galaxies at z > 0.6. The fraction of slow rotators does not change significantly down to z = 0, but we find that in the z   0 sample, the spin parameter is, on average, higher than at higher redshifts. In our subsample at z > 0.6, we observed evidence for the existence of the kinematic morphology-density relation. The slow rotators in our sample mostly assemble their stellar mass in one or two main episodes of formation, and they finished assembling their mass via star formation by redshift of z $\sim$ 1. It is however, not clear if the loss of angular momentum can be associated with the last star formation episode. Based on their location in the mass - size diagram, the intermediate-redshift galaxies are consistent with being the progenitors of the most massive slow rotators observed at z 0, where the subsequent growth is facilitated through gas-poor (dry) mergers.

12. Identification of the C IV $λλ$5801, 5812 Emission in the Off-Nuclear TDE 2025abcr[2611.13364]
Abstract

TDE 2025abcr is a tidal disruption event (TDE) located approximately 9 kpc in projection from its host galaxy nucleus. Our spectroscopic follow-up with the Very Large Telescope (VLT) reveals C IV $\lambda\lambda5801, 5812$ emission, representing the first identification of this C IV doublet in a TDE. These transitions are uncommon in active galactic nuclei (AGN) spectra but have been observed in supernova flash spectra and Wolf-Rayet (WR) stars. We propose that the doublet arises from the combined effects of continuum fluorescence and photon trapping in the 312 Å transition, analogous to the mechanism operating in nitrogen-sequence Wolf-Rayet (WN) winds. This mechanism requires intense extreme-ultraviolet (EUV) illumination and a high optical depth, providing a distinctive diagnostic of the physical conditions in optically thick TDE outflows. These requirements, together with the relatively narrow widths of the broad emission lines, the presence of Bowen fluorescence features, and the small inferred blackbody radius, favor a compact and dense envelope in TDE 2025abcr. The optical C IV doublet may have been overlooked in previous observational and theoretical studies of TDEs, motivating more detailed radiative-transfer calculations for optically thick outflows. With future TDE samples from Rubin/LSST and WFST, a larger C IV TDE sample will test if this population systematically favors a compact photosphere.

13. The UV luminosity function at 1.6 < z < 2.2 from UVCANDELS[2611.13518]
Abstract

We present an analysis of the rest-frame UV luminosity function (LF) of galaxies at $1.6<z<2.2$ using the UVCANDELS F435W imaging data. Restricting our analysis to sources above $30\%$ completeness to minimize uncertainties at the faint end, we construct a catalog of 5,212 galaxies at $1.6<z<2.2$ down to an absolute magnitude of $M_{\rm UV} = -16.8$, using well-tested photometric redshifts. We perform a maximum likelihood estimate to derive the best-fit Schechter parameters of the UV LF at a median redshift of $z\sim1.9$. We report a best-fit faint-end slope of $\alpha = -1.586 \pm 0.042$. Splitting the sample into two redshift bins, we obtain $\alpha=-1.488 \pm 0.055$ at $z\sim1.75$ and $\alpha=-1.604 \pm 0.066$ at $z\sim2.05$ ($\sim1.4\sigma$ difference), alongside a $\sim2.3\sigma$ brightening in $M^{*}$. Integrating our best-fit LF down to $M_{\rm UV} = -15$, we derive $\rho_{\rm UV} = (1.821 \pm 0.037) \times 10^{26}\ \rm erg\ s^{-1}\ Hz^{-1}\ Mpc^{-3}$ at $z\sim1.9$. Combining our F435W measurements with previous UVCANDELS F275W results and other literature data, we model the redshift evolution of $\rho_{\rm UV}$ from $z\sim0$ to $z\sim10$.

14. Molecular signatures of shocked gas and enhanced cosmic-ray ionization in supernova remnant W49B revealed by ALMA[2611.13519]
Abstract

Galactic supernova remnants (SNRs) interacting with molecular clouds (MCs) provide important laboratories for investigating how supernova feedback is transferred to the interstellar medium. We performed observations of the HCO+ J=1-0, 3-2, and 4-3 lines, as well as the CO J=3-2 line, toward the eastern and western regions of SNR W49B using the Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array (ALMA) and the Atacama Pathfinder Experiment (APEX) telescope. In W49B west, we identify twelve regions with broad-line profiles in both HCO+ and CO emission, with FWHM of 25-60 km/s, providing clear evidence of SNR-MC interaction. By contrast, the ratio remains nearly constant with velocity in other regions. For these regions, we further explore the propagation of low-energy CRs through the post-shock layer. 15. Angular momentum transport in a newborn protostar–disc system: magnetic braking, spiral arms and the magneto-rotational instability[2611.13729]
Abstract

Angular momentum transport in young protostar–disc systems involves magnetic braking, magneto-rotational turbulence and gravitational instability, mechanisms that are usually studied separately and on different scales. We present a three-dimensional non-ideal magnetohydrodynamic simulation, including Ohmic and ambipolar diffusion, that follows the collapse of a $1~M_\odot$ dense core continuously from the cloud scale to the formation of the protostar, and then the resulting protostar–disc system for $\sim 64~\mathrm{yr}$ at a resolution of $\sim 10^{-4}~\mathrm{AU}$ at the protostellar surface. We decompose the azimuthally averaged angular momentum budget into Reynolds, Maxwell and gravitational torques, each split into a radial and a meridional component, over a quasi-steady phase of the evolution. The collapse produces a thick disc an order of magnitude more massive than the protostar, whose specific angular momentum decreases as it accretes. Accretion through the disc is vertically layered, with accreting and decreting layers at the same radius, a pattern set layer by layer by the torques. The transport separates into three regimes delimited by the coupling of the magnetic field to the gas. Magnetic braking operates in the envelope. Maxwell stresses dominate inside $\sim 0.1~\mathrm{AU}$, where the field recouples and the magneto-rotational instability develops. In the ambipolar dead zone that makes up the bulk of the disc, the Reynolds stress dominates, carried by $m=2$ spiral arms in a marginally unstable disc, with the gravitational stress as a secondary contributor. Meridional transport is as important as radial transport, so that a one-dimensional viscous model fitted to the net mass flux would require a negative $\alpha$ over part of the disc.

16. ExoPlanetLab: Bayesian orbit fitting of Gaia-DR4 epoch astrometry, radial velocities and relative astrometry[2611.13770]
Abstract

Gaia Data Release 4 (DR4) provides epoch astrometry from which thousands of giant planets and many substellar and stellar companions can be characterised. We present ExoPlanetLab, an open-source package for Bayesian orbit inference from Gaia epoch astrometry, radial velocities (RVs) and relative astrometry, validated on simulated and real data. The model orbit is compared with the epoch residuals after removing the part absorbed by the single-star fit. A Student-t likelihood describes heavy-tailed data. A robust Keplerian periodogram with bootstrap false-alarm probabilities gives starting values for a joint fit. Five samplers are available: tempered ensemble MCMC, two nested samplers and two reversible-jump samplers. Evidences for a fixed number of companions select that number. Acceleration and scan-angle models test whether an orbit is warranted. The samplers agree within 0.1 sigma on a simulated two-planet system. In 180 injections all fits converge, and all signals with signal-to-noise ratio S >= 10 are detected. The posteriors show no significant bias, but the period coverage is below nominal: 90% of the true periods lie within their 95% credible intervals. None of 30 companion-free data sets gives a false detection. On DR4 pre-release data the orbit of Gaia BH3 is recovered within 1.4 sigma of published solutions. The Student-t model brings the astrometric period and eccentricity of HD 114762 onto its RV orbit. Eight simulated relative positions narrow the period's credible interval to 0.27 of its width, and Octofitter agrees within 0.2 sigma on the same data. The code is validated for single sources with DR4-like epoch astrometry, with or without RVs. Relative astrometry is tested on simulated data only. The main limits are an uncorrelated noise model and the computing cost, which grows linearly with the number of measurements and steeply with the number of companions.

17. On the Existence of an Equilibrium Density Profile for Massive Collisional Star Clusters[2611.13825]
Abstract

The dynamical evolution of massive, dense star clusters is governed by a complex interplay of multiple physical processes, including two-body relaxation, strong encounters, stellar evolution, and galactic tides, superimposed on the orbital motion of individual stars and binaries within the cluster potential. These processes can alter the internal evolution of individual stars and binaries, produce exotic stellar objects, and collectively determine the global structural evolution of the cluster. In this paper, we use the Monte Carlo star cluster evolution code CMC-COSMIC to study how the density profiles of dense star clusters evolve under the combined effects of these processes. We find that for a given initial mass and binding energy, cluster models converge toward a characteristic density profile largely independent of their initial density distributions. This convergence occurs during the late-time, quasi-static phase of evolution, when gravitational contraction is balanced by energy generated through black hole binaries in the cluster core. The shape of this profile is well represented by a King profile with W_0=6.8 but with the virial radius of the final cluster larger than the initial by a factor of about 7. We further show that this equilibrium profile is dynamically stable, i.e., perturbations away from it decay through the cluster's natural dynamical evolution, driving the system back toward the equilibrium profile.

18. Tracing early galaxies back in time: implications for the dearth of galaxies beyond z 15[2611.13835]
Abstract

Although the James Webb Space Telescope has pushed the observational frontier to $z\sim14$, the onset of star formation in the earliest galaxies remains beyond direct reach, making the star formation histories (SFH) of observed galaxies a key tool for probing earlier epochs. We develop and validate a new SFH model based on an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process, capable of reproducing the bursty star formation expected in high-redshift galaxies. Tested on the SPHINX$^{20}$ mock catalogs, it accurately recovers stellar masses and SFRs and reconstructs cumulative SFHs at population level; the formation redshift is well constrained at $z\gtrsim9$ but not at $z\sim8$. Applied to 400 galaxies at $9<z<15$ from the UNICORN/GOODS-South sample, we find that sources observed at $z<10$ typically reached 20% of their stellar mass no earlier than $z\sim12$, disfavoring a clear evolutionary link to spectroscopically confirmed galaxies at $z\gtrsim13$. Among 218 galaxies at $10<z<15$, only 28 had formed 20% of their stellar mass by $z=15$, with a median formation redshift $z_{\rm form,20\%}=16.9^{+1.1}_{-1.1}$, and 24% of our sample had already reached $10^7$M$_{\odot}$ by $z=15$. At $z=17$, only 11 of these 28 galaxies had reached 20% of their stellar mass. The derived survey areas required to detect them are consistent with or slightly more optimistic than previous constraints at the bright end, and larger than some literature estimates at the faint end. At $z=20$, a single galaxy had formed 20% of its stellar mass, yielding a tentative constraint of one object at $M_{\rm UV}=-18.1\pm0.6$ per $\sim200$ arcmin$^2$. Our results suggest a relatively slow decline in star formation activity between $z\sim15$ and $z\sim17$, followed by a more rapid decline up to $z\sim20$.

19. Deriving physical parameters of unresolved star clusters. X. Size evolution of young star clusters in the M31 disc[2611.13836]
Abstract

Context. Star cluster structural properties and their correlations with other cluster parameters (age, mass, galactocentric distance) provide key constraints on cluster formation and dynamical evolution within star-forming galactic discs. In the tenth paper of this series, we analysed structural parameters of 1918 young star clusters (up to 800 Myr) in the disc of M31. Aims. We aim to examine the structural evolution of M31 disc clusters and determine how their sizes correlate with physical and spatial properties. Our goal is to improve the understanding of cluster formation and evolution in spiral galaxy discs. Methods. We use the M31 disc cluster parameter catalogue based on photometric measurements of the Panchromatic Hubble Andromeda Treasury survey data. We perform statistical analysis to determine how cluster structural parameters relate to their physical characteristics and location within the disc. Results. Young clusters in the disc of M31 are compact ($R_{50}\sim1.8$ pc) and expand rapidly during the first $\sim$20 Myr, more than doubling in size. At older ages, M31 clusters follow a weak age-radius relation $(R_{50}\propto t^{0.12\pm0.01}\,\,)$. We find no significant correlation between cluster size and galactocentric distance, indicating that internal processes dominate their early evolution, with minimal impact from the galactic tidal field. 64. Science Prospects for the Southern Wide-field Gamma-ray Observatory: SWGO[2506.01786]
Abstract

Ground-based gamma-ray astronomy is now well established as a key observational approach to address critical topics at the frontiers of astroparticle physics and high-energy astrophysics. Whilst the field of TeV astronomy was once dominated by arrays of atmospheric Cherenkov Telescopes, ground-level particle detection has now been demonstrated to be an equally viable and strongly complementary approach. Ground-level particle detection provides continuous monitoring of the overhead sky, critical for the mapping of extended structures and capturing transient phenomena. As demonstrated by HAWC and LHAASO, the technique provides the best available sensitivity above a few tens of TeV, and for the first time access to the PeV energy range. Despite the success of this approach, there is so far no major ground-level particle-based observatory with access to the Southern sky. HESS, located in Namibia, is the only major gamma-ray instrument in the Southern Hemisphere, and has shown the extraordinary richness of the inner galaxy in the TeV band, but is limited in terms of field of view and energy reach. SWGO is an international effort to construct the first wide-field instrument in the south with deep sensitivity from 100s of GeV into the PeV domain. The project is now close to the end of its development phase and planning for construction of the array in Chile has begun. Here we describe the baseline design, expected sensitivity and resolution, and describe in detail the main scientific topics that will be addressed by this new facility and its initial phase SWGO-A. We show that SWGO will have a transformational impact on a wide range of topics from cosmic-ray acceleration and transport to the nature of dark matter. SWGO represents a key piece of infrastructure for multi-messenger astronomy in the next decade, with strong scientific synergies with the nearby CTA Observatory.

65. The General Antiparticle Spectrometer (GAPS) Antarctic Balloon Payload[2604.19830]
Abstract

The General Antiparticle Spectrometer (GAPS) is an Antarctic stratospheric balloon mission designed to provide unmatched sensitivity to low-energy (< 0.25 GeV/n) cosmic-ray antiprotons, antideuterons, and antihelium nuclei as signatures of dark matter. The distinctive GAPS particle identification technique relies on measuring the energy loss along the track of an incoming antinucleus as it slows down and is captured into an exotic atom, and then detecting the de-excitation X-rays and the nuclear annihilation products. This measurement is realized using a Tracker composed of more than 1000 custom silicon strip detectors and a plastic scintillator time-of-flight (TOF) system instrumenting more than 40 m$^2$. Together, these subsystems provide the velocity and energy resolution, stopping power, particle tracking, and X-ray identification necessary to distinguish rare antinucleus signals from the abundant positive-nucleus backgrounds, all within the constraints of a high-altitude mission. A multi-loop capillary heat pipe system has been developed to maintain the tracker operating temperature with significant mass and power savings over a conventional pump-based system. The first GAPS science payload flew for 25 days during the 2025/26 NASA Antarctic balloon campaign. We detail the design, integration, and commissioning of the payload prior to flight.

66. Calibration of CMB Polarisation Using Cross-Experiment Correlations[2606.11300]
Abstract

Parity-violating physics in the Universe can generate correlations between the Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB) $E$- and $B$-modes, but detecting such signals requires extremely accurate calibration of instruments. We describe a data-driven method to calibrate the relative polarisation angle between CMB experiments using cross-correlations of observations over a common sky region. Unlike standard self-calibration approaches, this method does not assume vanishing isotropic cosmic birefringence or primordial $EB$ correlations when estimating the relative misalignment angle, and therefore preserves sensitivity to parity-violating physics. As a proof of concept, we forecast the performance of this method using the Simons Observatory (SO) Small Aperture Telescopes (SATs) as a calibrated reference. If they can be calibrated to an uncertainty of $0.08^\circ$, as anticipated from the SO wire grid calibration system, we show that the SO Large Aperture Telescope and Planck could be calibrated to uncertainties of $0.10^\circ$ and $0.17^\circ$, respectively, at $\sim 145$ GHz. This approach relies on the availability of at least one well-calibrated instrument, and provides a complementary path to improving polarisation calibration across experiments, enabling more robust searches for parity-violating physics in the CMB, such as cosmic birefringence.

67. Carruthers Data Processing Pipeline: Instrument Effect Correction[2606.21606]

The Carruthers Geocorona Observatory – launched in September 2025 – is NASA's first mission devoted to investigating the fundamental nature of Earth's exosphere from its distant vantage in halo orbit around the Earth-Sun L1 Lagrange point. Its primary payload, the GeoCoronal Imager, consists of two coaligned photometric imagers that measure ultraviolet Lyman-alpha emission radiance from exospheric hydrogen simultaneously at wide- and narrow- fields of view. 68. Identifying narrow-band mutual coupling resonances in SKA-low stations using frequency-independent current basis functions[2610.40008]
Abstract

Mutual coupling between closely spaced elements in aperture arrays introduces narrow-band spectral features in the Embedded Element Patterns (EEPs), threatening measurements of the 21-cm power spectrum with instruments such as SKA-low. We detail a method to identify such features through the construction of frequency-independent current basis functions, utilising a singular value decomposition of frequency-dependent basis functions. We demonstrate the method on a three-element linear array and full pseudo-random station of SKA log-periodic antennas and examine the current power induced by mutual coupling. For the pseudo-random layout station we note many narrow-band resonance features, including strong resonances at 50.8 MHz and 83.6 MHz, where the coupled power on the passive antennas can approach or exceed that of the excited antenna. Simulated delay power spectra for the SKA-low core show that observing in frequency bands including resonance features causes large foreground leakage within the delay power spectra, obscuring the EoR window. For the pseudo-random layout station with SKALA4.0 we identify two significant frequency bands from 59.2–76.2 MHz and 84.2–103.3 MHz which are free of contamination from current resonances, preserving the EoR window in these frequency ranges.
