1. Supermassive Black Hole Winds in X-rays – SUBWAYS. VI. Interplay between the soft X-ray excess and ultra-fast outflows[2610.26936]
Abstract

We present a broadband, multiwavelength study of the interplay between the soft X-ray excess (SE), ultra-fast outflows (UFOs), and warm absorbers (WAs) in 21 active galactic nuclei (AGN; 0.1 <= z <= 0.4) from the SUBWAYS project. We combine simultaneous or quasi-simultaneous UV, optical, and X-ray data from XMM-Newton (EPIC pn and OM), NuSTAR, and the Hubble Space Telescope. We model the SE using a two-coronae framework, in which a warm, optically thick corona (WC) produces the optical/UV and soft X-ray emission, while a hot, optically thin corona produces the hard X-ray continuum. We also test a hybrid model that adds relativistic reflection. The optical/UV coverage constrains the Comptonization seed photon temperature to 0.1-5.5 eV. The two-coronae model consistently describes the broadband emission, with WC electron temperatures kT_e   0.1-0.9 keV and photon indices $\Gamma > 2$, consistent with previous studies. However, 9/21 AGN require the hybrid model, suggesting that the SE has multiple origins whose relative contributions vary. This is especially evident in UFO hosts, where 4/7 observations favor the hybrid model. Compared with sources without UFOs, UFO hosts show lower WC temperatures, harder hot-corona spectra, and positive hardness ratios. These trends should be treated cautiously because of the small sample and their dependence on spectral parameters and instrumental response. We find no significant differences between AGN with and without WAs. Our correlation analysis finds links between the amplification factor and WC temperature, WC optical depth and hardness ratio, and WC slope and optical depth with UV luminosity. We also find a tentative anticorrelation between WC optical depth and UFO outflow velocity.

2. Inferring the shapes of low-mass galaxies at $z>6$ from FIREbox$^{HR}$. Where are the disks?[2610.26969]
Abstract

Context. The three-dimensional shapes of galaxies provide clues to their formation and evolution. Inferring intrinsic shapes from projected sizes and axis ratios requires assumptions about the population distribution of shape parameters. Aims. We test commonly adopted assumptions in Bayesian shape inference for high-redshift galaxies. Methods. We analyze FIREboxHR galaxies with $\log(M_\star/M_\odot)=6.5-9.5$ at $z\sim6-15$. We measure intrinsic shapes from stellar particles using the minimum covariance determinant (MCD) estimator. We also create mock JWST/NIRCam images, measure half-light radii and axis ratios, and infer intrinsic distributions from the joint distribution of projected axis ratio $q$ and size $\log a$. Results. Low-mass galaxies are predominantly prolate, with a substantial spheroidal component. Intrinsic ellipticity $E$ and triaxiality $T$ are well described by Johnson SB distributions, while intrinsic size $A$ is approximately log-normal. We therefore model $(\mathrm{logit}(E),\mathrm{logit}(T),\log A)$ with a multivariate normal distribution and allow general correlations. This model recovers the particle-measured shape distributions more faithfully than a multivariate normal model in $(E,T,\log A)$. Triaxiality-related parameters remain difficult to constrain from projected data without large samples or informative priors. Conclusions. Bayesian forward modeling can recover intrinsic shape distributions when its population model adequately describes the underlying galaxies. An unsuitable distribution can bias inferences about morphological evolution at high redshift.

3. PSR B0355+54: An Unexpected Compendium of Nearly All Scintillation Arc Morphologies[2610.27005]
Abstract

Scintillation arcs are sensitive probes of multipath propagation, offering a window into the distribution of scattering material in the interstellar medium. Contrary to the common observational finding that individual pulsars exhibit only a limited range of scintillation arc morphologies, we report that PSR B0355+54, observed simultaneously at 2.25/8.60 GHz with the Shanghai Tianma Radio Telescope, displays nearly the full range of known arc patterns, many of which evolve rapidly. Across 54 observations from 2020 to 2026, we detect scintillation arcs in 53/53 usable observations at 2.25 GHz and 33/49 at 8.60 GHz. At 2.25 GHz, the secondary spectra exhibit five broad morphological types: forward arcs, inverted arclets, diffuse arcs, diffuse power, and irregular structures, with prominent displaced arclets appearing in several observations. In contrast, the 8.60-GHz spectra are generally dominated by faint forward arcs. Despite these marked morphological differences, the scaled curvatures and their annual variations agree between the two frequencies, showing that both trace the same dominant scattering screen at a fractional distance of $s=0.069^{+0.015}_{-0.014}$ near the pulsar, further supporting a possible association with the \ion{H}{ii} region Sh 2-205. Simulations show that this diversity can arise from variations in the scattered-image distribution within this screen, while brightness reconstructions at 2.25 GHz resolve localized structure down to $\sim0.02~\mathrm{mas}$. Tracking four arclets over 10 h at 8.60 GHz, including two displaced arclets, reveals a common evolutionary trend consistent with the line of sight sweeping across small-scale scattering structure, helping explain the rapid arc evolution.

4. Graph Neural Networks for Astrophysics and Cosmology[2610.27051]
Abstract

Physics seldom makes breakthroughs when we consider objects only in isolation. Instead we model and measure how objects sit among their neighbours, spatial, temporal, or otherwise: a galaxy in its environment, a halo in its history, a photon hit in a detector. Graphs are ideally suited for encoding exactly these relations, and Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) learn the geometric functions that respect them. This review presents the use of GNNs for astrophysics and cosmology, and argues that GNNs often suit physics and physical data better than any alternative: they fit the native geometry of the domain and the signal, enable the incorporation of the symmetries and prior knowledge we already trust and have toiled to build, and stay flexible enough to capture what we do not yet understand. GNNs' physically intuitive workings also render them more interpretable than other deep-learning architectures. We show how we can begin from general principles of the geometry of deep learning and build the graph and the graph neural network from the ground up, and how other architectures, like the convolutional network and the transformer, can be seen as special cases of one unified geometric blueprint. We survey applications across cosmology, galaxy formation, dynamics, and instrumentation; and we chart the frontier. From this survey, we find that only one in six articles outside instrumentation applies GNNs to data rather than just simulations. We also distil a set of best practices, including when not to reach for a graph. Throughout, we will prioritise physical intuition and foundational principles over mathematical machinery whenever possible.

5. The ALMA REBELS Survey: The [C II] Luminosity Function of UV-Selected Galaxies at $z\sim7$[2610.27052]
Abstract

Luminosity functions (LF) are some of the most basic quantities providing insights into galaxy evolution across cosmic time and enabling a direct comparison between observations and simulations. Here, we present the first [CII]158$\mu$m LF at $z\sim7$ from a sizeable UV-selected ALMA sample. Our analysis makes use of 27 galaxies with significant [CII] emission line detections in the epoch of reionization from the REBELS (Reionization Era Bright Emission Line Survey) ALMA Large Program and its pilot programs. We used these line detections and similar from ALPINE to calibrate UV luminosity-$L_{\mathrm{[CII]}}$ and stellar mass-$L_{\mathrm{[CII]}}$ relations at $z\sim7$. Given the challenge in precisely defining a selection volume for the REBELS sample itself, we rely on published UV LF and stellar mass function (SMF) determinations at $z\sim7$ and three complementary approaches to derive the [CII] LF. The UV LF-based method provides a lower limit on the [CII] LF, due to its missing the most dust-obscured systems. Our SMF-based estimate yields a Schechter function with a characteristic luminosity of $L^*_{\mathrm{[CII]}}=10^{9.11^{+0.11}_{-0.27}} L_\odot$ and a steep faint-end slope. From $z\sim7$ to $z\sim5$, our inferred [CII] LF shows an evolution of +0.3 dex in normalisation and +0.1 dex in $L^*_{\mathrm{[CII]}}$. Our results indicate a general underprediction from models of the abundance of [CII] emitters at high-redshift and thus potentially the number densities of massive yet UV-faint systems implied by our SMF-based method. Additionally, they suggest that 63% of the star formation rate density occurs in massive [CII]-bright systems and that $\sim$60-90% of [CII] emitters at $z\sim7$ may be missed by bright UV selections. This highlights the importance of surveys focusing on especially massive, UV-faint sources at $z>6$.

6. How does a galaxy's quenching probability depend on its black hole's mass?[2610.27061]
Abstract

We develop a parametric toy model to explore how black hole mass scatter $\sigma_{\rm BH}$ and the dependence of the quenching probability per unit time on black hole mass, parameterized as a power law with index $q$, jointly control the faint-end slope $\alpha$ of the stellar mass function of quenched central galaxies. By varying $\sigma_{\rm BH}$ and $q$, we identify two regimes. At low scatter, $\sigma_{\rm BH} \lesssim 0.4$ dex, $\alpha$ is strongly sensitive to $q$. Gradual quenching, represented by low $q$, produces shallow slopes, while sharp thresholds, with high $q$, produce steep mass functions, making $\alpha$ a powerful diagnostic of AGN feedback prescriptions. At large scatter, $\sigma_{\rm BH} \gtrsim 0.6$ dex, models with vastly different $q$ converge to similar quenched mass functions, because the intrinsic scatter erases the imprint of the feedback implementation. Comparing to cosmological hydrodynamical simulations and semianalytic models, only models with $\sigma_{\rm BH} \gtrsim 0.4$ dex match the observed faint-end slope of $\alpha \approx 0.7$. {\sc SAGE}, {\sc Dark Sage}, and {\sc COLIBRE}, with $\sigma_{\rm BH}$ of 2.0, 0.6, and 0.4 dex, respectively, reproduce this slope, whereas {\sc IllustrisTNG} and {\sc EAGLE}, with $\sigma_{\rm BH} \lesssim 0.2$ dex, produce steeper slopes. Current observational estimates of $\sigma_{\rm BH}$ allow a wide range of $q \gtrsim 0.8$, so the observed slope alone cannot identify the quenching prescription. If $\sigma_{\rm BH}$ is small, simultaneous measurements of $\alpha$ and $\sigma_{\rm BH}$ can distinguish between competing AGN feedback models.

7. Metal-poor molecular clouds in dwarf galaxies at parsec-scale resolution[2610.27064]
Abstract

Understanding how stars form at low metallicity is challenging because carbon monoxide (CO) emission, which usually probes the molecular gas reservoir, is faint, and tends to be concentrated in parsec-scale clumps. To overcome this difficulty, in order to study the molecular clouds in metal-poor environments, we have acquired CO(2-1) ALMA maps at 3-4 pc resolution of four nearby dwarf galaxies with nebular (O/H) metallicities ranging from roughly 1/9 Z_sun to 1/3 Z_sun. Significant CO signal in the cubes has been identified through two masks, a “strict”, high-confidence, mask and a “broad” one for better completeness. The former traces the cores of the clouds, while the latter includes fainter emission in the vicinity of the cores. Through a cloud-finding algorithm applied to the two sets of masked cubes, we have identified more than 200 molecular clouds across the sample, with roughly 50 in NGC3109, at 1/9 Z_sun, the lowest metallicity parsec-scale cloud population yet observed. The best-fit size-linewidth relations for the resolved clouds are similar among all galaxies, with a slope like the Milky Way (MW), but with lower velocity dispersion at a given cloud size. For a fixed CO-to-H_2 MW conversion factor, the cloud virial ratios tend to be large, and show a weak increasing trend with increasing cloud size, and decreasing apparent surface densities and metallicity. We find evidence for faint but significant CO emission outside CO-bright cores, spanning similar velocities to those observed in the cores. We attribute this to effective shielding by gas and dust, despite the low metallicities of our sample, since the CO tends to be cospatial with dust as traced by far-infrared emission.

8. Optical and Ultraviolet Emission from the Gas Photoionized by Gaia BH3: Archival Constraints and Predictions for the 2029 Periastron[2610.27075]
Abstract

Gaia BH3, a $32.7\,M_\odot$ black hole (BH) on an 11.6-yr, $e=0.73$ orbit around a metal-poor giant at $\approx590$ pc, accretes from its wind. Neither its mass-loss rate $\dot M_{\rm w}$ nor the fraction of captured gas reaching the BH is known. We ask whether optical and ultraviolet lines from gas photoionized by the hot flow are observable and what they reveal. Unlike X-ray and radio data, they count the ionizing photons the gas absorbs and trace its density and velocity. With Cloudy we compute the H$\alpha$ and He II 4686 emission of the wind and inflow along the orbit for $\dot M_{\rm w}=10^{-11}$-$10^{-8}\,M_\odot\,{\rm yr^{-1}}$, Eddington ratios $f_{\rm Edd}=10^{-8}$-$10^{-5}$ and wind speeds of 30 (slow) and 100 km s$^{-1}$ (fast), and test each model against archival UVES and ESPRESSO spectra. $f_{\rm Edd}$ mainly sets the lines; in dense models they come from gas falling freely onto the BH, spread over thousands of km s$^{-1}$. The archival spectra exclude $\dot M_{\rm w}>2\times10^{-9}\,M_\odot\,{\rm yr^{-1}}$ at $f_{\rm Edd}=2\times10^{-6}$ and $>2.6\times10^{-10}\,M_\odot\,{\rm yr^{-1}}$ at $10^{-5}$ (95%, slow wind), Eddington ratios already excluded by the apastron Chandra limit, $f_{\rm Edd}<7.6\times10^{-7}$. Of two simulated campaigns, only 25 epochs around the 2029 periastron go beyond existing limits. For a slow wind, whose capture rate is constant, a $\ge5\sigma$ H$\alpha$ detection would imply an accretion state 2-3 times above the 2024 limit. For a fast wind, whose captured fraction rises 14-fold toward periastron, the campaign detects $\dot M_{\rm w}\simeq10^{-8}\,M_\odot\,{\rm yr^{-1}}$ for $f_{\rm Edd}\gtrsim1.2\times10^{-6}$, which the apastron limit allows. Below the Chandra limit, a slow wind produces no detectable line.

9. Balmer breaks in accreting super-massive black hole binaries[2610.27076]
Abstract

We show that truncated circumbinary accretion disks around super-massive black hole binaries can produce Balmer breaks. For binaries interacting with surrounding disks in a truncated, non-accreting phase, gas accumulates at the cavity edge, and can create conditions conducive to generating spectral discontinuities from $n=2$ hydrogen bound-free absorption. We develop a simple model for predicting the presence of a Balmer edge and estimating the break strength, and we find that the strongest discontinuities are expected for binaries with periods of months to years and total masses $\gtrsim10^6\,\Msun$. These systems share considerable overlap with those binaries anticipated to appear in upcoming time-domain surveys—like LSST and the Roman HLTDS—as periodically variable quasars. We estimate that for typical feeding rates of a few tenths of their Eddington rate, a few-to-10\% of periodic binaries at $z\lesssim1$ should show prominent Balmer breaks, and that this fraction increases for the more massive black holes that can be discovered at higher redshift. We suggest that black hole binaries that truncate their accretion flows will appear as periodically variable sources, and will additionally be intrinsically X-ray weak, deficient in ionizing emission, and in favorable cases, produce notable Balmer edges in their spectra. This combination provides a complementary observational test for massive black hole binary candidates.

10. Dual and lensed AGN at sub-arcsecond separations uncovered by ERIS[2610.27086]
Abstract

We present the first results of the ongoing INAF GTO program focused on the search of sub-arcsecond dual AGN, carried out with the near-infrared integral-field unit SPIFFIER of the Enhanced Resolution Imager and Spectrograph (ERIS) at the ESO Very Large Telescope, operating in adaptive-optics mode with a laser guide star. We report integral field spectroscopy with the low-resolution gratings (R   5000 - 5600) of 34 multiple AGN candidates selected from the Gaia Data Release 3 source catalog via the Gaia Multi-Peak (GMP) method, which preferentially identifies luminous unobscured AGN at sub-arcsecond separations. The targets span the redshift range 0.7 < z < 3.4 and exhibit projected angular separations between  0.2” and  0.9”, corresponding to physical separations of  1.7-7.8 kpc. Of the 34 observed systems,13 ( 40 %) are resolved into multiple AGN-like components; 18 ( 53 %) are classified as AGN plus foreground star superpositions, one as a single AGN, and two as systems with an AGN primary and an unclassified secondary component. Among the multiple AGN systems, we confirm six dual AGN at projected separations of 0.26” - 0.80” (2.2 - 6.7 kpc), in the redshift range z = 0.68 - 1.57, and with velocity offsets in the range {\Delta}v   290 - 950 km/s. We also identify seven gravitationally lensed quasars, only one of which was previously known, including four doubly imaged and three quadruply imaged systems. These results substantially enlarge the census of spectroscopically confirmed compact multiple AGN systems at z > 0.5 and demonstrate the efficiency of ERIS-SPIFFIER for the systematic characterization of sub-arcsecond dual and lensed AGN candidates.

11. The SIMPLIFI Survey: Polarization of Dense Filaments with SOFIA III. Relative orientations of antenna temperature gradients with the magnetic field in the DR21 molecular cloud[2610.27090]
Abstract

We investigate magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) turbulence in the DR21 molecular cloud using far infrared polarimetry from SOFIA HAWC+ and molecular spectroscopy from the Nobeyama 45 m telescope. The Velocity Channel Gradient (VChG) method is applied to thin velocity slices of the 12CO and 13CO J=1-0 emission to determine the antenna temperature gradients and their orientations in the plane of the sky. The relative orientations of the gradients with respect to the magnetic field position angles enable an evaluation of theoretically predicted alignment of elongated, turbulent eddies for Alfvenic, fast and slow modes and self-gravity. The projected Rayleigh statistic is calculated for both the full cloud and 6 regions of interest to provide a quantitative assessment of these alignments. Our analysis finds mostly parallel alignment of the gradient direction and the local magnetic field orientation in the Main Ridge of DR21 that comprises most of the cloud mass. While parallel alignment can result from fast MHD modes or cloud collapse, previous studies show spectroscopic evidence for the large scale collapse of the DR21 cloud, which supports self-gravity as the primary cause of the observed parallel alignment. One of the six sub-filaments that are connected to the Main Ridge exhibits mostly perpendicular alignment of the position angles of the gradient and magnetic field. These perpendicular relative orientations may be a product of Alfven and slow modes in a magnetized, sub-Alfvenic medium even if the sub-filament is accreting onto the Main Ridge.

12. Leviathan: a very massive, extended sub-millimetre galaxy spectroscopically confirmed at $\rm z = 4.05$[2610.27101]
Abstract

We present Leviathan, a sub-millimetre galaxy (SMG) spectroscopically confirmed at $z=4.051$ with JWST/NIRSpec. Modelling 31 bands of photometry from the rest-frame ultraviolet to the millimetre, we find a stellar mass of $\log(M_\star/M_\odot)=11.34^{+0.15}_{-0.22}$, making Leviathan one of the most massive spectroscopically confirmed galaxies at $z>4$, yet consistent with $\Lambda$CDM for a star-formation efficiency of approximately 20%. Although SMGs are often thought to be building the dense cores of compact quiescent galaxies, Leviathan is extended, with a rest-frame $0.9\,\mu\mathrm{m}$ half-light radius of 4.3 kpc. Spatially resolved SED fitting gives a half-mass radius of $3.1\pm0.4$ kpc, consistent with its size in rest-frame $1.5\,\mu\mathrm{m}$ imaging. This is three times larger than quiescent galaxies of the same mass, but consistent with the size-mass relation of star-forming galaxies at $4<z<5$ extrapolated to $M_\star=10^{11.3}M_\odot$. Leviathan forms stars on the main sequence (approximately $400\,M_\odot\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}$) with dust spread across its disc. Both its stellar mass and dust are more extended than those of $z\approx3$ SMGs. Alongside GN20.2b, which has a similar mass and star-formation rate but is three times smaller, these show that massive dusty galaxies at $z\approx4$ can have very different stellar structures, and that not all have their stellar mass concentrated in a compact core.

13. 20,000 Rotation Periods in Nearby Open Clusters and Moving Groups from ZTF and ASAS-SN[2610.27113]
Abstract

Open clusters provide uniquely powerful laboratories for studying stellar evolution because their members share a common age and chemical composition. Stellar rotation of low-mass GKM dwarfs in open clusters provides direct empirical constraints on magnetic braking, with implications for stellar interiors, magnetic activity, and star–planet interactions. However, existing cluster rotation samples are dominated by space-based observations, whose limited spatial coverage and short observing baselines can make it difficult to construct an unbiased sample. Long-baseline ground-based time-domain surveys therefore provide an important complement to space-based photometry. I present a catalog of $\sim$20,000 periodic photometric signals, including stellar rotations, eclipsing-binaries, and classical pulsators, measured from the Zwicky Transient Facility (ZTF) and the All-Sky Automated Survey for Supernovae (ASAS-SN) for members of nearby open clusters and moving groups within 1.5 kpc. This catalog provides a new empirical foundation for studying the mass, chemical composition, and age dependence of stellar angular-momentum evolution and for testing models of magnetic braking across a broad range of stellar masses and evolutionary states.

14. A MUSE Study of Stellar and Ionized-Gas Kinematics in Central Group Galaxies[2610.27169]
Abstract

Understanding gas accretion and feedback in low-mass galaxy groups bridges the gap between isolated ellipticals and cluster-dominant galaxies. We present new Multi Unit Spectroscopic Explorer (MUSE) observations of 17 nearby brightest group galaxies (BGGs), revealing diverse origins for their gas reservoirs. Warm ionized gas, traced by Halpha and [N II], is detected in 77% (13/17) of the sample, with morphologies including rotating disks with or without tails and diffuse emission, rings, and filaments. Unlike brightest cluster galaxies, most BGGs host regularly rotating stars, whereas their gas displays diverse kinematics. Nearly half of the rotating gas disks are aligned with the stars, consistent with internal recycling. However, tails, diffuse emission, or rings in three aligned systems may retain signatures of past accretion or interactions. The remaining disks, ring-like structures, and most filamentary systems are misaligned, supporting gas acquisition through mergers, interactions, or halo cooling. Compact disks are consistent with centrally concentrated, low-angular-momentum gas, possibly linked to residual cooling. Most filamentary systems lie near the precipitation threshold (tcool/teddy   1-2) and preferentially occupy the turbulence-dominated region of the k-plot, supporting condensation consistent with chaotic cold accretion. All galaxies with detected X-ray halos, including those without warm gas, exhibit low central entropies (<30 keV cm^2) and short central cooling times (<3 Gyr). These conditions alone cannot predict multiphase gas, suggesting that AGN- or interaction-driven perturbations and turbulence-induced thermal instabilities help regulate condensation. No single mechanism dominates: BGGs are transitional systems in which internal recycling, environmental accretion, and cooling-driven condensation coexist, linking isolated ellipticals to cluster-dominant galaxies.

15. The GLEAM 4-Jy (G4Jy) Sample: V. Broadband spectral analysis[2610.27171]
Abstract

The G4Jy Sample consists of 1,863 of the brightest sources in the southern sky, as selected at 151 MHz using the GaLactic and Extragalactic All-sky MWA (GLEAM) survey. We supplement existing, low-frequency radio data by completing visual inspection of radio images from the Rapid ASKAP Continuum Survey (RACS) at 887.5, 1367.5 and 1655.5 MHz. Doing so on a source-by-source basis allows us to assess the quality of the association of the radio emission, which we indicate via a flag system. Having multiple radio flux-densities allows us to construct broadband radio spectral energy distributions (SEDs), and these extend up to 20 GHz for sources that also have data from the Australia Telescope Compact Array (ATCA). We fit polynomial functions to these SEDs, and determine the distribution of sources that are best-fit by absorption models, these accounting for 11 per cent of the sample. The SEDs are further modelled using the Radio AGN in Semi-analytic Environments (RAiSE) framework, for three assumed density-profiles (set by typical halo masses of 10^12.5, 10^13.5, and 10^14.5 Msol). The derived source ages show the expected distribution in the radio-power–size (P–D) diagram, whilst derived jet powers show non-linear behaviour. The latter is believed to be the result of higher densities, associated with higher magnetic fields, restricting the expansion of the radio jets/lobes.

16. Ionized Gas Kinematics and Star-forming Clumps in Extremely Metal-poor Galaxies[2610.27201]
Abstract

Extremely Metal Poor (XMP) galaxies are the least chemically evolved objects in the local Universe and provide important constraints on galaxy formation in low-metallicity environments. This work investigates the ionised gas kinematics and star formation properties of XMP galaxies to identify disturbed or multi-component gas motions and explore their connection with star formation. We analyse long-slit spectroscopic observations of nine XMP galaxies obtained with the Intermediate Dispersion Spectrograph on the 2.5m Isaac Newton Telescope. The study uses the H$\alpha$ emission line to trace ionised gas and emission-line ratios to derive gas-phase metallicities. The kinematic analysis reveals that some galaxies exhibit ordered rotation, with kinematic centres not always coincident with the luminous star-forming heads. Most systems show disturbed, asymmetric, or declining velocity profiles, while localized distortions near bright star-forming heads suggest that massive clumps influence the surrounding gas dynamics. We derive dynamical masses for the dominant clumps and investigate the mass–metallicity and size–metallicity relations. The clumps exhibit low metallicities consistent with the XMP regime and comparable to those observed in low-mass dwarf galaxies. Three galaxies show counter-rotating or kinematically decoupled ionised-gas components, indicating complex gas kinematics and possible external gas acquisition. Our sample is predominantly composed of isolated systems, with metal-poor star formation concentrated in compact head regions. These properties may reflect gas accretion through mergers, interactions, or the acquisition of metal-poor gas from the intergalactic medium and cosmic filaments. Internal processes such as outflows, gas mixing, recycling, and stochastic star formation may also contribute to the observed properties.

17. Searching for Dense Perturbers in Cold Dark Matter Simulations[2610.27236]
Abstract

Recently, several low-mass, dense perturbers have been inferred from astrophysical observations of gravitational lensing, stellar streams, and satellite galaxies. If interpreted as dark matter subhalos, their inner densities exceed the predictions of the standard cold dark matter (CDM) model. We systematically investigate this tension using a CDM-only zoom-in simulation of a Milky Way analog. We analyze more than 5000 subhalos with masses down to $10^6\,M_\odot$ and reconstruct their inner density profiles using a tidal truncation model. The simulated subhalos are then compared with the observed compact perturbers in terms of their density profiles, enclosed masses, concentrations, and projected cylindrical masses. None of the simulated CDM subhalos is sufficiently dense to reproduce the inferred mass distributions of the observed perturbers. We further examine the redshift evolution of subhalo concentrations and derive a fitting function to characterize this evolution. Our findings motivate further investigations into exceptionally rare formation histories that could produce such dense perturbers, as well as the possibility of physics beyond the standard CDM model.

18. J-PLUS: Identifying Outlier Spectral Energy Distributions in the Fourth Data Release Using Unsupervised Machine Learning[2610.27252]
Abstract

The selection of candidates belonging to rare or underrepresented stellar populations, as well as novel objects, is essential for spectroscopic follow-up. In this work, we demonstrate how combining broad- and narrow-band photometric information with machine-learning techniques enables this selection. Using photometric data in twelve filters from the Javalambre Photometric Local Universe Survey (J-PLUS) as input, we applied an anomaly detection technique based on autoencoders to identify spectral energy distributions (SEDs) that deviate from the dominant pattern in the J-PLUS objects sample. Subsequently, we applied the t-distributed stochastic neighbor embedding (t-SNE) algorithm to the J-PLUS colors of the outlier SEDs population. Combining information from the SIMBAD database and other astronomical surveys to classify the outliers identified by the autoencoder, we are able to characterize over-densities in the t-SNE map. Our study specifically focuses on well-defined islands in the t-SNE space, corresponding to specific populations. We identified a large sample of white dwarfs and low-mass stars, including a small subset of polluted white dwarfs, and report potential candidates for spectroscopic follow-up, including 19 carbon-rich stars, 11 cataclysmic variables and 170 white-dwarfs-main-sequence binaries, all previously unreported in the literature.

19. Core-Collapse Supernova Rates in the Dark Energy Survey[2610.27328]
Abstract

Over its five-year campaign (2013-2017), the Dark Energy Survey (DES) supernova (SN) survey recorded 31,636 light curves. Using a custom-built, two-stage random forest classifier, we identified 2,445 likely core-collapse SNe (CC SNe) within the survey rate analysis footprint. With this sample, we measure the volumetric CC SN rate from redshift z = 0 to 1.3 in 11 redshift bins, representing the most detailed single-survey coverage to date across nine billion years of lookback time. Over this interval, the CC SN rates are well fit by a power law R_CC(z) = R_0 (1 + z)^\alpha, with R_0 = 1.58 \pm 0.17 \times 10^{-4} \text{ yr}^{-1} \text{ Mpc}^{-3} and \alpha = 2.85 \pm 0.25. The rate turnover, expected at cosmic noon, is not yet apparent. Because CC SNe trace recent star formation, we compare our measurements with recent cosmic star-formation history (CSFH) models. Our measurements are  70 per cent higher than CSFH models using a Salpeter initial mass function (IMF) to predict CC SN rates. Replacing the Salpeter IMF with a Baldry and Glazebrook IMF and a CC SN progenitor mass range of 8-40 M_\odot bridges the gap. Alternatively, CSFHs may miss a hitherto unidentified population of low-luminosity galaxies. We further find that the local ratio of stripped-envelope (SE) SNe to all CC SNe is 31 \pm 5 per cent, remaining constant from z = 0 to 1.3. Combined with an IMF analysis accounting for the "missing progenitor problem" of hydrogen-rich SNe, our results favour a mixture of single massive stars and binaries as SE SN progenitors, with the redshift-invariant ratio favouring binary channels.

20. A $\wedge$-like metallicity profile in the disk of the Sagittarius dwarf galaxy[2610.27526]
Abstract

Unveiling the properties of the progenitors that formed the tidal structures of the Milky Way (MW) is essential for exploring the MW's assembly history. Such studies further afford a unique opportunity to dissect the formation and evolution of dwarf galaxies by contrasting surviving and disrupted systems. However, reconstructing the intrinsic structure of progenitors solely from the positions and velocities of their tidal debris remains a formidable challenge. Here, we decode the chemo-kinematic signatures embedded in the stellar streams of the Sagittarius (Sgr) dwarf galaxy through N-body simulations. Our simulation reproduces a vertical metallicity gradient in the Sgr streams. We show that this feature is naturally explained if the stream originated from a disk component with a $\wedge$-like metallicity profile. Remarkably, similar metallicity profiles are identified in MaNGA survey data for a few (3/217) field dwarf galaxies, suggesting this feature is rare but not unique to Sgr. The vertical metallicity gradient of stellar streams preserves extra detailed galactic fossil records, enabling quantitative reconstruction of the peculiar structure of the satellite galaxy. The similar metallicity profiles of Sgr and MW raise the question of a possible special coevolution.

21. Cosmic-ray melt windows in interstellar ice make a four-carbon ketose but not the sugar pattern of G+0.693-0.027[2610.27778]
Abstract

Erythrulose (ERU), a four-carbon (C4) ketose, is reported towards the Galactic-centre cloud G+0.693-0.027 at a column density comparable to that of glycolaldehyde (GA), while the three-carbon (C3) sugars are not detected. 64. A Solar Optical Clock[2610.28485]
Abstract

Precision spectroscopy underpins quantum sensing, atomic timekeeping, and the search for terrestrial-mass exoplanets. Finding an Earth-Sun analog requires fractional splitting of spectral features that rivals the precision of the best laboratory spectroscopy. This corresponds to resolution of a few parts in $10^5$ of a typical stellar spectral line, or a Doppler velocity precision of less than 10 cm s$^{-1}$. Only a few astrophysical spectrometers reach this regime, and only by dispersing starlight with large gratings and averaging thousands of lines. 66. Deglobalising the fit: how to search for physics beyond vacuum General Relativity in LISA catalogues[2610.27069]
Abstract

The Laser Interferometer Space Antenna will observe up to $\sim 10^{7}$ overlapping signals, which have to be characterised simultaneously with a global fit. This is a challenge for model comparison since rerunning the global fit for every physical model of interest, including deviations from vacuum General Relativity, is computationally demanding. An alternative is a two-step post-processing strategy: a first global fit with vacuum models only, after which a selected group of target sources is reanalysed under a different model with all other sources subtracted. We show that in this strategy a beyond-vacuum effect in a target source can be partly absorbed by the overlapping nuisance sources in the first fit, subtracted with them, and never reach the reanalysis. In the linear-signal approximation, the loss is captured by a single number, the masking ratio $\mathcal{R}\in[0,1]$. A deviation the global fit would detect at $n_\sigma$ is reported by post-processing at $\mathcal{R}\,n_\sigma$, and bounds in null tests are also reported too tight by the same factor. We show how to compute $\mathcal{R}$ from the posterior samples of the vacuum global fit, and give an algorithm that ranks the nuisance sources by how much of the effect each can absorb, so that only those most correlated with it need to be refitted with the target in a down-scaled reanalysis with a few sources. We validate our method with full Markov-chain Monte Carlo inference on a toy model of 21 overlapping chirping sinusoids, where one carries a small non-linear chirp that represents the missed physics, while the other 20 act as nuisance sources. This method can serve as a basis for tests of General Relativity in realistic LISA mock datasets and can be adapted to study the impact of systematics between different gravitational-wave sources.

72. Infrasound Newtonian Noise Estimation at the Einstein Telescope Candidate Site Sos Enattos[2607.21154]
Abstract

We investigate the seasonal variability of atmospheric infrasound and its contribution to Newtonian noise (NN) at the Sos Enattos site, a leading candidate location for the Einstein Telescope (ET). Infrasound data recorded at three stations, SOE0 (surface), SOE1 ($-84$ m) and SOE3 ($-160$ m), are analysed over multiple seasons to characterise both the temporal variability and the depth dependence of the acoustic field. At the surface, the median amplitude spectral density (ASD) at 1 Hz shows no consistent seasonal modulation, and its variability is driven primarily by wind; the apparent dependence on sea state largely reflects its correlation with wind speed. Underground, where local wind noise is suppressed, a winter maximum of 4–7 dB above the quietest season is observed in both years of data. Using the measured sound-pressure spectra, we estimate the corresponding NN contribution for an underground cavern with the dimensions foreseen for ET, assuming an isotropic field of acoustic plane waves. The surface pressure field, acting through the atmosphere above a test mass at 200 m depth, exceeds the ET-D design sensitivity in median conditions between 1.6 and 7.1 Hz, by up to a factor of 4.7. The attenuation is not monotonic with depth: the deeper station SOE3 shows higher levels than SOE1 above a few hertz, pointing to local, anthropogenic sources in the underground infrastructure. These results show that underground placement is essential but not sufficient on its own, and that the acoustic emission of the underground infrastructure and future laboratory equipment will require careful evaluation and mitigation to keep atmospheric NN below the ET design sensitivity.

Abstract
