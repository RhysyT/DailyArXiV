1. Alignment-driven binary evolution in warped circumbinary discs: 3D moving-mesh hydrodynamics of eccentric and inclined binaries[2610.31688]
Abstract

We present three-dimensional moving-mesh hydrodynamical simulations (\arepo{}) of equal-mass binaries in circumbinary discs, spanning binary eccentricity ($e = 0.0,\,0.3,\,0.6$) and inclination ($i = 0^\circ$–$60^\circ$), using a locally isothermal $\alpha$-disc ($H/r = \alpha_{\rm visc} = 0.1$) with fixed Keplerian orbits. Discs above the critical inclination $i_{\rm crit}(e)$, decreasing with $e$, drive their interiors near-polar, so eccentricity alone alters the outcome at fixed $i$. Discs reach polar alignment ten times faster than they align toward the binary plane, settling long before the binary's orbit responds. Alignment dictates how the disc acts on the binary. First, a disc tilting back toward the binary plane spends nearly all its torque reorienting the binary's orbit, whereas a disc driven polar redirects it into the orbit itself, which shrinks 10–20 times faster. Second, at $e = 0.6$ the turn to polar is a single transient reshaping the accretion flow, cavity and orbit, with accretion enhanced ($i = 45^\circ$) or suppressed ($i = 60^\circ$). Third, misalignment suppresses coplanar eccentricity pumping: the attractor at $e \approx 0.4$ survives only below $i \approx 15^\circ$, so misaligned binaries circularise and may enter the gravitational-wave regime less eccentric than coplanar models predict. Around a circular binary, the familiar $\approx 4.5$-orbit accretion modulation vanishes while the disc is strongly warped, returning as it realigns, a time-domain signature of misaligned massive black-hole binaries. Finally, observed polar discs around stellar binaries, such as HD 98800 BaBb's, are likely in their final state; intermediate misalignments reflect slower alignment toward the binary plane.

2. Non-Axisymmetric Pillar Structures due to Embedded Stars in AGN Accretion Discs[2610.31751]
Abstract

Gravitationally unstable outer regions of AGN discs may host in situ formation of massive stars whose heating produces localized vertical "pillars" on the disc surface, in the same region that emits the broad lines. Here we investigate the reverberation-mapping signatures that such pillars would produce. We compute velocity-delay maps $\Psi(\lambda, \tau)$, time-evolving emission-line spectra, and continuum reverberation lag spectra for these structures. We model each pillar as a 2D Gaussian perturbation to the disc height under lamp-post irradiation and weight the line response with Cloudy photoionization grids covering the major BLR lines. We show H$\alpha$, Mg II, and C IV as representative examples. A single pillar produces a localized hotspot in $\Psi$ and a clear "S-wave" pattern in the time-evolving spectrum that persists, though diluted, for $N \sim 100$ pillars. Emission-line fluxes are suppressed in shadows behind the pillars, but when normalized by H$\beta$ (or by the local continuum) the line-to-H$\beta$ ratios move in opposite directions: Mg II/H$\beta$ rises while C IV/H$\beta$ drops. This contrast distinguishes pillars from purely kinematic features that would modulate all lines by similar factors. Across scans of the pillar shape and lamp-post height, the pillar models produce an optical continuum reverberation lag spectrum of the observed magnitude. The once-per-orbit modulation grows with $\sin i$ from foreshortening, and at sufficiently high inclination the pillar's body geometrically self-occults its lamp-facing inner slope, adding a sharper near/far contrast. These signatures provide observational diagnostics for detecting in situ star formation in AGN discs through velocity-resolved reverberation mapping campaigns.

3. An Extra-nuclear Water Maser in NGC 1106 Likely Driven By An AGN Shock: Implications for Maser-based Black Hole Mass Measurements[2610.31753]
Abstract

We present Very Large Array A-configuration ($0.13$”) 22 GHz spectral-line and continuum observations of the Swift-BAT detected Seyfert 2 galaxy, NGC 1106, aimed at localizing a water maser line offset from the host systemic velocity by 541 km s$^{-1}$. The 22 GHz continuum emission reveals a 352 pc extended structure within the nucleus of NGC 1106, and we spatially resolve the water maser emission to a compact region $\sim1$ kpc ($3.12$”) from the nucleus. Comparing our data with previous 22 GHz 1” observations, we found that the water maser lies near the boundary of a radio extension aligned with the jet axis, likely arising from the interface between the active galactic nucleus (AGN) outflow and the host galaxy's ISM. To verify this, we compared the water maser in NGC 1106 to a known water maser in a star-forming region within the Milky Way, along with known extragalactic water masers associated with star formation and AGN driven outflows. Overall, we found that the maser position, velocity offset, flux density, and alignment with the jet axis are consistent with an origin at the interface between the AGN outflow and the host ISM. Our results have important implications for maser-based black hole mass measurements in which disk geometry is ambiguous. Since single-dish observations cannot spatially separate disk masers from outflow-driven masers, high-velocity maser features attributed to the accretion disk may also include contributions from kpc-scale shocks.

4. Collapsars Are Not Heavy $r$-Process Factories[2610.31758]
Abstract

Collapsar disks are proposed sources of $r$-process elements, but neutron-rich disk ejecta require gas to be both neutronized in the disk and ejected before accretion. The magnetic field that enables ejection also thickens the disk and accelerates the inflow, lowering both the density and the time available for weak processing. To compute the resulting neutronization, we evolve the electron fraction of the inflowing gas, adopting at each radius the temperature that maximizes the net neutronization rate for the local density and instantaneous composition. This yields a lower limit on $Y_{\rm e}$ that is independent of the disk's thermal history. We adopt an optimistic unbinding radius of $R_{\rm unb}=10\,r_g$ for all magnetic fluxes capable of launching outflows. Combining this ejection condition with favorable assumptions of low black hole mass, ejection of midplane gas, and the upper end of the $\gamma$-ray burst (GRB) engine-power scale, which limits the accretion rate to $\dot M_{\rm BH}\lesssim0.4\,M_\odot\,{\rm s}^{-1}$, gives a fiducial lower bound $Y_{\rm e}\gtrsim0.33$, above the characteristic $Y_{\rm e}\approx 0.25$ threshold for heavy $r$-process production. Stronger magnetic flux, toward which jet-launching engines evolve, raises the lower bound to $Y_{\rm e}\gtrsim0.49$. We conclude that collapsar disk outflows cannot supply the heavy $r$-process yields invoked for a dominant enrichment channel. Only engines with unusually high accretion rates, potentially including exceptionally luminous GRBs, such as GRB 221009A, and direct-horizon collapsars, may produce heavy $r$-process ejecta.

5. The Galactic Center Reference Frame: A 30-Year Solution[2610.31766]
Abstract

Stellar orbits probe the metric of and mass around the Sgr A* massive black hole. To enable study of the black hole, the surrounding mass, and tests of gravity, a precise reference frame must be established and maintained. We present an updated stellar maser-based reference frame for the inner parsec of the Galactic Center, referenced to the radio continuum of Sgr A*. This work synthesizes 30 years of astrometric observations of 15 SiO stellar masers and the Sgr A* continuum at 43 GHz. The uncertainty in the mean offset of the maser ensemble from Sgr A* is 0.08 mas in the current epoch and will decay to 0.10 mas by 2030. At a distance of 8.2 kpc, these correspond to position uncertainties of 0.6 AU and 0.8 AU, respectively, which is similar to or smaller than the stellar SiO-emitting region. The mean maser proper motion uncertainties are 0.05 mas yr$^{-1}$ in RA and 0.08 mas yr$^{-1}$ in Dec, corresponding to 1.8 km s$^{-1}$ and 3.2 km s$^{-1}$. The reference frame stability between IR and maser astrometry is 10 $\mu$as yr$^{-1}$ (0.40 km s$^{-1}$) in RA and 15 $\mu$as yr$^{-1}$ (0.57 km s$^{-1}$) in Dec. The masers show substantial variability in flux density and velocity structure, most notably IRS 7, which shows a 30-fold brightening in a blueshifted component since 2022. This variability may set a floor on future astrometry improvements and especially measurements of radial acceleration.

6. AMICO galaxy clusters in KiDS-1000: Cosmological constraints from the two-point correlation function[2610.31767]
Abstract

In this paper we study the clustering properties of an optically identified photometric galaxy cluster catalogue from KiDS-1000. The sample was identified by the AMICO algorithm and consists of 9049 clusters with $S/N\geq3.5$, in the redshift range $0.1\leq z<0.8$, spread over an effective area of 838.78 deg$^2$. Clusters are selected according to their observed intrinsic richness, with thresholds of $\lambda^*{obs}\geq 20,25,30$ within $0.10\leq z<0.30$, $0.30\leq z<0.45$ and $0.45\leq z<0.80$, respectively. We measure the multipoles of the two-point correlation function over different ranges of scales from 5 $h^{-1}$ Mpc to 150 $h^{-1}$ Mpc. We model the clustering signal in the context of the $\Lambda$CDM cosmology, accounting for the main observational effects, such RSDs and photo-$z$ uncertainties. The effective bias of the cluster sample is estimated via a mass-richness scaling relation. For the MCMC analysis, we adopt Gaussian priors on the parameters of the mass-richness scaling relation, and broad, uniform priors to derive cosmological constraints on the cold matter density parameter, the power spectrum amplitude and the structure growth parameter, $S_8\equiv\sigma_8 \sqrt{\Omega{m}/0.3}$. Specifically, we find $\Omega_{m}=0.24^{+0.02}{-0.02}$ and $\sigma_8=0.86^{+0.13}{-0.10}$ representing an improvement in precision over both the 2D and 3D AMICO clustering analyses performed on the previous KiDS data release. Moreover, we obtain $S_8=0.77^{+0.10}_{-0.08}$ in $1\sigma$ agreement with the AMICO KiDS-1000 number counts constraints estimated from the same catalogue, as well as with some results from external early- and late-Universe data sets. Our work strengthens the potential of cluster clustering as a complementary and competitive probe, to be included and further developed with the final KiDS-Legacy data release, and in other future Stage-IV photometric redshift surveys.

7. The SDSS-V Local Volume Mapper (LVM): Technical Overview[2610.31936]
Abstract

The Sloan Digital Sky Survey V (SDSS-V) Local Volume Mapper (LVM) is an ultra-wide integral field spectroscopic survey of the Milky Way, the Magellanic Clouds, and a sample of galaxies in the local volume. The LVM spatially resolved spectroscopy covers the bulk of the optical range (3600-9800 A) at medium spectral-resolution (R = 2200-5500), and maps the disks of these galaxies at high physical spatial resolution (< 1 pc in the MW and  10 pc in the Magellanic Clouds). Observations are carried out with the LVM Instrument (LVM-I), a custom-built robotic telescope, instrument, and facility located at Las Campanas Observatory (LCO) in Chile. In this article we present a technical overview of the LVM project. We describe the hardware, the survey observing strategy, and the details of how science and calibration observations are carried out. We provide a high-level overview of the survey data flow, including the reduction and calibration of the LVM spectra, and the production of the main data products that are released by the project. We also present an assessment of the LVM data quality and instrumental stability based on science observations. The main goal of this article is to serve as a primer for LVM users, providing the technical information required for the adequate analysis, modeling, and interpretation of the LVM data.

8. Tilt, warp, and dissipation flare in hydrodynamic disks around kicked compact objects[2610.31990]
Abstract

We study the dynamical response of a hydrodynamic disk to an impulsive kick imparted to a central massive object, focusing on the global properties of the material that remains gravitationally bound. We construct a simple analytic model and show that the bound material can acquire a significant tilt—or even a retrograde rotation—if the kick is directed close to the disk midplane. We perform 3D numerical simulations and show that (1) the bound material relaxes to a torus-like circularized flow of which total mass and angular momentum is in fair agreement with the analytic estimate, and (2) in spite of nonlinear hydrodynamic effects, a highly tilted or retrograde disk can be sufficiently formed. The simulations additionally reveal a transient warp in the kicked disk, suggesting a kick as a formation mechanism of tilted or warped disks around compact objects. About 10% of the kinetic energy the bound material acquired from the kick is converted to the internal energy, while the exact conversion efficiency depends on the kick angle and the initial surface mass density profile. The luminosity of this kick-driven dissipation flare can reach $\sim 10^{43}\,{\rm erg\,s^{-1}}$ for strong kicks ($V_{\rm kick} \sim 1000$km/s). We discuss the implications of our results for recoiling supermassive black holes, recoiled stellar-mass black holes in the disks of active galactic nuclei, and natal kicks on compact objects.

9. SMMAN Studies of AGN Variability. I. Long-term Spectral Evolution and a Candidate $\sim1000$-day Quasi-periodic Modulation in 4C+72.28[2610.32168]
Abstract

The SMMAN project has carried out nine years of radio flux monitoring in the C and K bands of the blazar 4C+72.28. The two bands show markedly different secular evolution, with a substantially stronger K-band decline producing a gradual decrease in the C-K spectral index. After accounting for this secular evolution, multiple timing diagnostics reveal a candidate quasi-periodic modulation with a characteristic timescale of $\sim1000$ d, most clearly in the C band. The C-band GLSP peak reaches a local significance of $3.29\sigma$, while its global significance remains modest at $\sim2\sigma$ and the monitoring baseline covers only about three cycles. Independent and joint MCMC fits support a phenomenological decomposition for both bands into a long-term linear trend and a recurrent component. Under the shared-period assumption, the joint fit returns a characteristic timescale of approximately 1027 d and a similar fractional recurrent amplitudes of $\simeq5.45\%$. Information-criterion comparisons favor the combined linear-plus-sinusoidal model in both bands. Cross-correlation analysis shows a nominal K-leading offset in the original light curves but a centroid consistent with zero lag after detrending, although the lag distributions overlap substantially. These results are consistent with a common recurrent modulation superposed on slower frequency-dependent jet evolution. Geometrical Doppler-factor modulation provides one plausible interpretation, although intrinsic and opacity-related processes cannot be excluded.

10. Galaxy Luminosity Functions at z 7-10 from Four Years of JWST Observations[2610.32307]
Abstract

We present galaxy luminosity functions at $z\approx 7.4$ and 10 based on a comprehensive search for candidate galaxies at $6.4\lesssim z\lesssim11.3$ in JWST's public NIRCam data four years after its operation. Our work utilises nine fields covering $\sim$1,045 arcmin$^2$ in total, including seven blank fields and two galaxy cluster fields. Using the dropout technique, we selected 3,733 dropouts from the F090W passband and 2,396 dropouts from the F115W passband, which constitute the largest samples of candidate galaxies at $z\approx 7.4$ and $z\approx 10$ to date. Among these objects, 338 F090W-dropouts and 99 F115W-dropouts already have secure redshifts from JWST NIRSpec spectroscopy over four years, which we use to form two large spectroscopic subsamples to assess the magnitude-dependent contamination rates of their parent dropout samples. Our luminosity functions are based on the results after statistically correcting for both incompleteness and contamination. We find that the luminosity function at $z\approx 7.4$ can be well described by a conventional Schechter function but with a faint-end slope of $\alpha=-1.70\pm0.16$ that is significantly shallower than most of the previous studies at $z\approx 7$–8. To verify this result, more imaging and spectroscopy at $\sim$30 mag will be needed. The luminosity function at $z\approx 10$, on the other hand, can hardly be fitted by a Schechter function but is more consistent with a power law in the form of $\Phi(M)\propto 10^{-0.4(\beta+1)M}$, with the best-fit slope of $\beta=-2.36\pm0.16$. While such a behavior is largely driven by the high number densities at $M_{\rm UV}\lesssim -21.8$ mag, it is supported by the currently existing spectroscopic confirmations. Further spectroscopy at the bright end($\lesssim 26$ mag) will be needed to strengthen this result.

11. Differential and integral scaling laws of the mass density of molecular clouds[2610.32344]
Abstract

In this work we investigate the mass density - size scaling law in molecular clouds (MCs). This relation reflects the fractal nature of MCs and plays a key role for understanding the physics, structure and evolution of these objects. We make use of the notion "ensemble of MCs", introduced in our previous work (Donkov, Veltchev & Klessen, 2017), in which all MCs with the same probability density function (PDF) of mass density and effective size are represented by an abstract spherical cloud with the same PDF and size. In this spirit, the model is built on the base of abstract scales of the clouds' substructures (which are simply the radii of the spherical object). We consider two forms of the mass density - size scaling law: differential and integral, which in turn reveal the local and the global fractal clouds' structure. Both scaling functions are characterized by their scaling exponents, which can be explicitly expressed by the PDF of mass density, in the general case. Moreover, we derive a first order linear differential equation connecting the two scaling exponents and obtain its exact solution. As examples we apply this abstract construction to two PDFs: the so called power-law tail and the log-normal. Both have great importance for MC structure and evolution, as the latter corresponds to the earlier stages of clouds' evolution, when supersonic turbulence dominates the physical processes, while the former describes the latest stages of evolution, when star-formation takes place. The obtained results for the scaling exponents in both examples are qualitatively and numerically consistent with respective observations and simulations of MCs.

12. Binary Dynamics and Mergers in a Stellar Disk and the Formation of Dusty Objects in the Galactic Center[2610.32418]
Abstract

For more than a decade, observers have been tracking a population of enigmatic dusty objects orbiting the central supermassive black hole of the Milky Way, Sagittarius A$^\star$ (SgrA$^\star$). In one interpretation, these objects are products of stellar mergers enshrouded in gaseous-dusty envelopes, which account for their infrared excess, emission lines, and stability against the tidal forces of SgrA$^\star$. Recent near-infrared observations of D9, one of the dusty objects, revealed that it embeds a spectroscopic stellar binary. In this work, we present full-fledged N-body simulations of an eccentric disk of binary stars similar to the young stellar disk observed around SgrA$^\star$. We find that some of the binaries in the disk are naturally drawn towards SgrA$^\star$ and merge there due to Kozai-Lidov (KL) oscillations in their orbits. Together with the observed nature of D9, these findings represent a self-consistent picture of how the population of the observed dusty objects could have been brought into existence. The binary fraction in our N-body model evolves to increase with distance from SgrA$^\star$, which is also in agreement with the observational constraints. Our simulations also suggest that beside the dusty objects, stellar mergers are present within the young stellar disk, which could be identified as chemically peculiar or fast-rotating stars. Our results show that dynamical evolution of binary stars in the young stellar disk plays a crucial role in shaping the stellar content in the Galactic Center.

13. Local Analogs of Little Red Dots: Clues to the X-ray Weakness[2610.32430]
Abstract

A major question about JWST's little red dots (LRDs) at $z\sim4-8$ is the origin of their apparent X-ray weakness. Even deep Chandra stacks yield only tentative detections or broad-band limits and cannot distinguish among super-Eddington accretion, intrinsic X-ray weakness, and Compton-thick (CT) obscuration. Local analogs offer a powerful complementary route. At $z\sim0.3$, similarly luminous sources require $\sim10^{3-4}$ less exposure for comparable signal-to-noise, suffer less source confusion, and directly probe the diagnostic rest-frame soft X-rays. Here we present a pilot Einstein Probe/FXT study of six "local little red dots" (LLRDs), V-shaped broad-line green pea galaxies selected as low-redshift counterparts of LRDs. These sources show X-ray diversity: "X-normal" LLRDs resemble type 1 AGNs in X-ray luminosity and optical variability, whereas the "X-weak" stack lies $\sim0.5-0.7$ dex below standard unobscured-quasar scaling relations while remaining compatible with the representative LRD stack and 95% of published individual limits. The X-weak LLRDs also lack detected optical variability and have redder optical continua than X-normal LLRDs, making their V-shaped SEDs closer to those of high-redshift LRDs. The detection of the X-weak stack at $\sim3.3\sigma$ with the soft-X-ray-sensitive EP-FXT provides rest-frame $\sim2-3$ keV constraints difficult to reconcile with a typical unobscured super-Eddington model or a local-AGN-like CT geometry examined here, although modified variants remain possible. The simplest alternative is a mildly X-ray-weak corona ($\Delta\alpha_{\mathrm{ox}}\sim-0.1$ to $-0.2$) with Compton-thin absorption ($N_{\mathrm{H}}\sim10^{22-23}$ cm$^{-2}$), consistent with the independent Balmer decrement/break diagnostics. Together with growing samples of local LRD analogs, our results offer a practical path for testing the physical nature of LRD X-ray weakness.

14. Star clusters at cosmic dawn I: the emergence of the initial cluster mass function[2610.32497]
Abstract

JWST observations of lensed galaxies reveal unusually bright star-cluster candidates within the first billion years after the Big Bang. However, the physical processes shaping the initial cluster mass function (ICMF) remain uncertain. We study the formation of a galaxy embedded in a dark matter halo whose virial mass reaches $10^{10} \mathrm{M}_\odot$ at $z=6$, using cosmological radiation-hydrodynamics zoom-in simulations. Its star clusters, represented by sink particles, accrete gas and form stellar populations that supply radiation and supernova feedback, thereby regulating their own growth. As we do not prescribe an efficiency per free-fall time or a fixed star-cluster formation duration, the ICMF emerges self-consistently. We assess the effects of numerical resolution and stellar radiation. Schechter fits to the central-galaxy ICMF give truncation masses near $6 \times 10^6 \mathrm{M}_\odot$, with substantial uncertainty. At fixed resolution, including stellar radiation makes the low-mass slope of the ICMF shallower, from $-1.60$ to $-1.34$, while doubling the spatial resolution steepens it back to $-1.54$. Stellar radiation also reduces the abundance of low-mass clouds, particularly those with lower gas densities, and shortens the formation time of star clusters to about a Myr, i.e. well before their first supernovae go off. On average, more rapidly fed star clusters accrete for longer, making their final masses rise faster than linearly with the initial accretion rate and contributing to a flatter ICMF. At fixed host-galaxy star formation rate, our brightest simulated clusters reach UV luminosities comparable to those of observed lensed star-cluster candidates.

15. The Galactic rotation curve from the ALS III catalogue of OB Stars[2610.32537]
Abstract

The Galactic rotation curve reveals the radial mass distribution of the Milky Way, but its determination depends on the tracer population. Young massive stars are the least dynamically evolved tracer, retaining the kinematics of the gas from which they formed, yet previous determinations from OB stars were confined to the solar neighbourhood. We determine the rotation curve and the solar motion from 14017 spectroscopically confirmed OB stars from the ALS III catalogue, using Gaia DR3 five-dimensional astrometry with revised distances and corrected proper motions. The curve is parameterised as a fourth-degree Chebyshev polynomial in Galactocentric radius and fitted to the proper motions by weighted least squares, solving simultaneously for the solar motion. Uncertainties are derived from 1000 bootstrap resamplings. We obtain a circular velocity at the solar radius (8.178 kpc) of 236.3 +/- 1.1 km/s, solar motion components U = 8.06 +/- 0.41, V = 10.91 +/- 0.86 and W = 7.53 +/- 0.14 km/s, and Oort constants A = 16.35 +/- 0.21 and B = -12.54 +/- 0.19 km/s/kpc. The curve rises inside the solar circle and declines gently beyond it, with a local gradient of -3.81 +/- 0.38 km/s/kpc, and is constrained between 5.5 and 11 kpc. Because the tracer is dynamically cold and the proper motions are fitted directly, no asymmetric drift correction or Jeans modelling is required, so the systematics differ from those affecting older tracers. The gentle decline beyond the solar radius agrees with determinations from older tracers where they overlap, making it unlikely to be a methodological artefact. Only the solar radial motion is poorly constrained by tangential data, without affecting the curve. The stars departing most from the curve (1474 to 1662, depending on the selection criterion) form the largest sample of OB runaway candidates to date, to be analysed in a forthcoming paper based on Gaia DR4.

16. Forward model of the Gaia DR3 selection function[2610.32551]
Abstract

The completeness of an astronomical catalogue depends on its selection function, which is a combination of the observing strategy, instrumental characteristics, and intrinsic properties of the sources being observed. This is particularly complex for Gaia, whose non-uniform scanning law and onboard prioritisation of bright sources in dense areas make its selection function vary strongly with celestial position and source brightness. We estimate the Gaia DR3 source catalogue selection function across the whole sky and over the full magnitude range, using only data from the Gaia catalogue and knowledge of the nominal scanning law as provided by the Gaia Observation Forecast Tool. We develop a hierarchical Bayesian model that estimates the number of times Gaia scanned each celestial position jointly with the source detection efficiency, and fit it via Markov chain Monte Carlo to the 1 806 196 447 sources in Gaia DR3 with reported celestial positions, G magnitudes, and astrometric matched transit counts. Gaia DR3 is essentially complete for G in (5, 19.5) in the on-sky median. After that, the completeness decreases with magnitude according to the scanning law pattern combined with the imprint of crowded regions. The completeness is also lower at the very bright end of the survey, consistent with the known limitations on Gaia observations of bright stars. We find an unexpected completeness increase beyond G   21, which we conclude is an artefact caused by extended sources, as confirmed by cross-matching against Euclid's Q1 catalogue. Treating the scanning law as part of the inference process, and anchoring it to all Gaia DR3 sources of all magnitudes on a fine-grained grid on the sky, removes the need for an explicit crowding correction. The resulting selection function, valid everywhere where Gaia data are available, is released as part of the GaiaUnlimited Python package. [abridged]

Abstract

We present new continuum and polarization observations of radio galaxy NGC 315 obtained with the Five-hundred-meter Aperture Spherical radio Telescope (FAST) over 1.0-1.5 GHz. 18. Spatial distribution of star formation in dwarf galaxies: Merger-driven and secular starburst[2610.32624]
Abstract

Galaxy interactions can induce bursts of star formation (SF), while internal or secular processes can also trigger starbursts (SB). Recent studies of the spatial distribution of SF in high-mass (M$_{*}>10^{10}M_{\odot}$) SB galaxies show that SF is centrally concentrated in merger-driven SBs, whereas in secularly driven SB systems, SF is uniformly enhanced across the system. However, it is not clear whether such distinct spatial signatures are present in low-mass (M$_{*} < 10^{10} M_{\odot}$) SB galaxies. In this context, we studied the SF properties of a sample of 272 isolated SB (i.e., with no massive galaxy, within a 1 Mpc$^3$ volume) dwarf galaxies consisting of 17 post-merger and 255 non-interacting SB galaxies, spanning a stellar mass range of $10^{7}-10^{9.6} M_{\odot}$ and a redshift range of 0.01-0.12. For this study, we utilise FUV imaging data from GALEX to estimate the star formation rates of our sample galaxies. We investigated the spatial variation of SB activity by comparing star formation rate surface density (SFRD) across radial bins for post-merger and non-interacting samples of dwarfs. We find an enhancement in SFRD across all radial bins for both samples, and that the enhancement is more or less uniform across the galaxy, within uncertainties. This indicates that SF in low-mass SB galaxies is uniformly enhanced throughout the galaxy, regardless of the mechanism that triggers the SB. This contrasts with observations in massive galaxies, where SF is centrally concentrated in merger-driven SB systems. The lack of central concentration of SF in low-mass merger systems could be due to their shallow gravitational potentials and the compression of extended gaseous envelopes during interactions, which trigger SF across these galaxies. Deeper, higher-spatial-resolution observations are essential for better understanding the spatial distribution of SF in low-mass galaxies.

19. Magnetic Reconnection Modifies Cosmic-Ray Transport in the Interstellar Medium[2610.32716]
Abstract

We present numerical magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) simulations of the interstellar medium in gravity-stratified boxes with the Piernik MHD code. The system contains thermal gas, spectrally resolved cosmic-ray (CR) nuclei within the CRESP algorithm, and spectrally unresolved CR protons. CR protons drive gas outflows and undergo Coulomb and hadronic losses. CR nuclei undergo Coulomb losses. We also include the spallation of primary Carbon by thermal gas atoms, thereby decaying Car- bon into secondary stable Boron isotopes. We use the resistive-MHD approximation as a subgrid model for unresolved turbulent magnetic dissipation in current sheets. We perform a parameter study of the magnetic diffusivity {\eta}. The Boron-to-Carbon ratio B/C and hadronic {\gamma}-ray luminosity density are computed as diagnostics. We find that non-zero magnetic diffusivity modifies the magnetic topology, leading to a more horizontal field in the halo, thereby accumulating CRs in the disk region and signifi- cantly increasing the CR escape time. As a consequence, the local turbulent conditions and turbulent reconnection in the interstellar medium should be considered when interpreting B/C and hadronic {\gamma}-ray luminosity observed at Earth. Our results suggest that the escape time and diffusion coefficient of CRs deduced from observational data in the vicinity of the Solar System cannot be extrapolated to the whole Galaxy.

20. Research of signs of star formation in three S0 galaxies - members of isolated pairs[2610.32826]
Abstract

We obtained and analyzed long-slit spectra of three lenticular galaxies with optical rings: PGC 48114, ESO 234-011, and ESO 444-076. Ionized gas was detected in all of them. In PGC 48114, the excitation mechanism of the ionized gas differs along the ring, despite the consistent kinematics of the ionized gas and stars, as we showed earlier. Narrow-band images of PGC 48114 in the H$\alpha$ and [NII]$\lambda$6583 emission lines revealed a star formation arc in the northwestern part of the ring. In ESO 234-011 and ESO 444-076, the ionized gas along the long slit is shock-excited and have kinematic discrepancy with the stars. This implies accretion of matter at an angle to the main disk of the S0 galaxies, which likely inhibits ongoing star formation. The companions of these S0 galaxies are late-type galaxies with extensive ongoing star formation. These companions may be the sources of the gas, or each pair may be feeding from a common gas reservoir.

21. The Physics of the Cold and Warm Interstellar Medium Through Radio Recombination Lines[2610.32834]
Abstract

The interplay between star formation and the interstellar medium (ISM) gives rise to a feedback cycle that is one of the key drivers of galaxy evolution. The coldest gas in the ISM provides the fuel for star formation, and, in turn, the ISM is affected by feedback in the form of photons, shocks, and cosmic rays.
