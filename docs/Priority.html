1. The [Y/Mg] chemical clock and the stellar ages of solar-type stars in the solar neighbourhood[2610.35917]
Abstract

Stellar ages are an important parameter for studying the chemical evolution of the Galaxy. To improve age estimates, various complementary methods to the conventional isochrone-fitting method have been implemented in the last decade. The [Y/Mg] ratio has been proposed as a chemical clock for solar-metallicity stars, due to its strong dependence on stellar age. In this work, we study the behaviour of the abundance ratio [Y/Mg] in metal-poor stars, along with other solar-type disk stars, as a function of stellar age determined through isochrones. The analysis is based on the line-by-line differential technique, applied to a sample covering a wide metallicity range ($-$0.70 $\leq$ [Fe/H] $<$ +0.34), using high-resolution (R = 115,000) and high S/N spectra (= 270 - 1000) taken with the HARPS spectrograph at the ESO La Silla Observatory. We find that the stellar sample follows a metallicity-dependent [Y/Mg]-Age relation.

2. The SIRMOS mission concept: a space-based near-IR spectroscopic survey of 100 million galaxies in the Euclid Wide Survey footprint[2610.35945]
Abstract

SIRMOS is a mission concept for a future NASA Astrophysics Explorer opportunity that aims to revolutionize our understanding of the universe by mapping the large-scale structure of galaxies in unprecedented breadth and detail to explore cosmic origins and evolution. Specifically, the primary science goals of SIRMOS are to discover how cosmic inflation seeded large-scale structure, reveal the nature of the accelerating expansion of the universe, and measure the sum of neutrino masses to exceptionally high precision. Its secondary science goals include determining how environment shapes galaxy evolution at cosmic noon and beyond, and probing the Milky Way's dust-enshrouded regions, reaching the far side of our Galaxy. To accomplish its transformative science goals, SIRMOS executes a 3-year cosmology survey over the Euclid footprint of  14,000 deg$^2$, obtaining slit spectra for  100 million galaxies at 1<z<4. SIRMOS is the first space mission designed specifically to leverage Euclid's wide near-infrared (IR) imaging survey for target selection and employs a digital micro-mirror device (DMD) to acquire up to 4,000 simultaneous spectra over a wide 0.9 deg$^2$ field of view. The instrument's excellent spectral resolution of $R=1300$ and continuous wavelength coverage from 1.25–2.5 $\mu$m capture the redshifts of distant galaxies with no gaps, reducing systematic biases to achieve ground-breaking science.

3. Field-level modeling of halo and galaxy alignments[2610.36027]
Abstract

We develop an effective field theory (EFT) of intrinsic alignments at the level of spin-2 field fluctuations of large-scale structure tracers, such as the shapes of galaxies and dark matter halos. Our perturbative model includes the non-linear treatment of the Zel'dovich displacement, and contains all necessary shape operators through the third order. We apply our field-level model to the full 3D distribution of galaxy shapes, whose sky projections are a known contaminant of cosmic shear measurements. Our field-level EFT model achieves a better description of the helicity fields of dark matter halo shapes than the standard non-linear alignment (NLA) and tidal torquing + tidal alignment (TATT) models in the literature, as evidenced by improved correlations with the simulations' initial conditions. We perform precision measurements of field-level bias parameters by means of a generalized least-squares problem, for which we derive an optimal estimator and analytic covariance matrices. We measure the full set of linear, quadratic, and cubic shape bias coefficients as a function of halo mass in the $\texttt{Quijote}$ N-body simulations. We then study the intrinsic alignments of galaxies in the $\texttt{FLAMINGO}$ hydrodynamical simulation, focusing on observationally relevant samples: spirals, ellipticals, luminous red galaxies (LRG) and emission-line galaxies (ELG). We detect linear and nonlinear alignment coefficients at high significance for LRG, spiral and elliptical galaxies. We also find evidence for non-zero density-weighted tidal bias coefficient of the simulated ELG. This work supports the notion that EFT-based models of alignments will be paramount in optimally extracting constraints from future shape correlations, and lays the foundation for the development of simulation-based priors for future galaxy shape samples.

4. The Stellar Populations of Resolved Galaxies[2610.36040]
Abstract

The study of galaxies outside the Milky Way using observations of resolved stellar populations has experienced extraordinary growth over the past two decades. There now exist panchromatic photometry and precise astrometry for hundreds of millions of individually resolved stars in hundreds of galaxies that span $\sim10^9$ in luminosity, $\sim10^3$ in metallicity, and sample a wide range of environments. Here, we review advances in extragalactic astronomy enabled by resolved stellar population imaging. We summarize major observational programs and focus on four interconnected science areas: the star formation histories of galaxies, 'bursty' star formation, the stellar initial mass function, and the orbital histories of Local Group galaxies. We review the literature in each area, synthesize key findings, identify emerging trends, and discuss how new and future facilities (e.g., JWST, LSST, Euclid, Roman, CSST, ELTs, HWO) will enable further progress. Finally, we highlight shortcomings in current datasets and discuss promising directions for new areas of investigation.

5. Detection of star formation in a near-pristine pocket of the Universe[2610.36042]
Abstract

We detect two high-confidence star-forming sources in the environment of a chemically near-pristine ([Fe/H] $<-3.66~;2\sigma)$ Damped Ly$\alpha$ system (DLA) using Keck/KCWI. These are found via the hydrogen Ly$\alpha$ emission line at $\Delta v=-1200$ kms$^{-1}$ (LAE1) and $\Delta v = -200$ kms$^{-1}$ (LAE2) relative to the DLA redshift ($z_{\rm abs} = 3.078$) at an impact parameter of $b=66$ physical kpc and $b=137$ physical kpc. We measure Ly$\alpha$ fluxes of $F(\rm Ly\alpha)_{\rm LAE1} =4.4\pm 0.7\times10^{-18}~\rm erg\,s^{-1}\,cm^{-2}$ and $F(\rm Ly\alpha)_{\rm LAE2} =3.8\pm 0.9\times10^{-18}~\rm erg\,s^{-1}\,cm^{-2}$. This marks the first detection of star formation in the environment of a chemically near-pristine DLA. We use red channel Keck/KCRM data to place limits on the associated He II 1640 A emission flux of these sources, $F(\rm He\,II)<3.8\times10^{-19}~\rm erg\,s^{-1}\,cm^{-2}~(3\sigma)$ and $F(\rm He\,II) <3.7\times10^{-19}~\rm erg\,s^{-1}\,cm^{-2}~(3\sigma)$. The weak and narrow properties of the Ly$\alpha$ emission, combined with the inferred position on the mass-metallicity relation, suggest a metal-paucity of these galaxies. Future rest-frame optical observations are required to pin down the metallicity of LAE1 and LAE2 and test if chemically near-pristine DLAs can be used as a signpost to search for primitive, metal-poor galaxies 2 Gyrs after the Big Bang.

Abstract

Context. The star-formation rate (SFR) is one of the key quantities that characterizes the star formation process and has been extensively investigated both observationally and theoretically. Aims. We aim to provide a coherent multi-scale view of star formation by connecting Galactic-scale conditions (kpc) to the properties of individual star-forming clumps at sub-parsec scales. Methods. We perform a set of numerical simulations with large-scale turbulent forcing designed to reproduce physical conditions typical of the solar neighbourhood. 7. Does the Escape Fraction Prescription Shape Reionization History and Morphology?[2610.36046]
Abstract

The escape fraction of ionizing photons, $f_{\rm esc}$, is one of the least constrained quantities of the Epoch of Reionization (EoR), and the opacity of the intergalactic medium prevents its direct measurement at high redshifts. We constrain three power-law parameterizations, $f_{\rm esc}(z)$, $f_{\rm esc}(M_{\rm h})$, and $f_{\rm esc}(z, M_{\rm h})$, against JWST UV luminosity functions and EoR observables (the ionizing emissivity, the neutral hydrogen fraction, and the Thomson optical depth) using Markov Chain Monte Carlo, with priors that allow $f_{\rm esc}$ to either increase or decrease with redshift and halo mass. We then propagate the best-fit models into the semi-numerical simulation SimFast21 and compare their ionization morphology and 21 cm power spectra with those of a constant-$f_{\rm esc}$ model. All models reproduce the observations with nearly identical global reionization histories. The data favor an escape fraction that decreases with increasing redshift, from $\approx 0.8$ at $z = 5$ to $\approx 0.35$ at $z = 15$. The halo-mass-only model prefers $f_{\rm esc}$ increasing with halo mass, but this trend acts as a proxy for redshift evolution. When we vary both dependencies jointly, the data prefer a mass-independent $f_{\rm esc}$. At matched global neutral fraction, the ionization morphology is closely similar for all four prescriptions, and the 21 cm power spectra agree to within $\approx 10\%$. These results suggest that, once calibrated against the same observations, the details of the $f_{\rm esc}$ prescription, which are set by the physics of the interstellar medium, largely average out in both the global observables and the 21 cm power spectrum. Distinguishing them may require statistics beyond the power spectrum, such as sightline statistics of the Ly$\alpha$ and 21 cm forests, or forecasts that exploit their small differences in reionization timing.

8. Dependence of Merger-driven Star Formation and Gas-phase Metallicity Evolution on Merger Type and Stellar Mass[2610.36055]
Abstract

Galaxy mergers play a fundamental role in driving gas inflows, altering star formation, and redistributing metals, yet how these processes depend on merger type, merger stage, and progenitor galaxy properties remains incompletely understood. In this work, we investigate the resolved impact of mergers on star formation rate (SFR) and gas-phase metallicity using MaNGA integral-field spectroscopy and the MaNGA Galaxy Merger Catalog of Comerford et al. (2024). By comparing merger galaxies to matched controls, we quantify local offsets in SFR and metallicity as functions of merger type, merger stage, stellar mass, and galactocentric radius. We find that major mergers generally exhibit stronger SFR enhancement and greater metallicity dilution than minor mergers. In major mergers, SFR enhancement is often spatially extended, whereas in minor mergers it is weaker and more centrally concentrated. The SFR response in major mergers varies non-monotonically with merger stage, with stronger enhancement in the early (first-passage) and post-coalescence stages and weaker enhancement in the late (between-passage) stage. Metallicity dilution is generally strongest in early-stage major mergers and weaker at later stages, although this stage dependence is not monotonic across all stellar-mass bins. We also find stellar-mass dependence that lower-mass systems show stronger metallicity dilution in both major and minor mergers, while the strongest SFR enhancement occurs in intermediate- and high-mass major mergers. These results show that the resolved star formation and metallicity responses depend jointly on merger type, merger stage, and stellar mass and exhibit different behaviors across the merger sequence.

9. Prospects for Near-Far Constraints on Reionization from Simulated LSST Observations of Milky Way Dwarf Galaxies and Stellar Streams[2610.36056]
Abstract

The NSF-DOE Vera C. Rubin Observatory's Legacy Survey of Space and Time (LSST) will detect resolved stellar populations of intact dwarf galaxies and disrupted stellar streams in the Milky Way (MW) halo and beyond, opening a new avenue for near-field cosmology, particularly for constraining the faint galaxies during reionization. To interpret these data, we present and publicly release mock LSST catalogs of satellites, stellar streams, phase-mixed galaxies, and field galaxies around 13 MW-mass hosts in the FIRE-2 zoom-in simulations, generated with \textsf{py-ananke}. Using a surface brightness limit of $\mu_{g} \leq 34$ $\rm{mag}$ $\rm{arcsec^{-2}}$, we find that $\approx29\%$ of streams with $M_{\star} \gtrsim 5 \times 10^5$ $M_{\odot}$ are detectable with tidal tails, while another $\approx38\%$ are detectable only through their bound progenitors and would appear as satellites. We estimate that $\approx96\%$ of satellites and $\approx64\%$ of field galaxies (out to $\sim$3.5 Mpc) with $M_{\star} \gtrsim 10^{5}$ $M_{\odot}$ are detectable, decreasing to $\approx$59\% and $\approx$42\% for $M_{\star} \gtrsim 10^{4.5}$ $M_{\odot}$ owing to resolution limits. Including streams detectable as satellites reproduces the radial distribution of observed MW satellites, suggesting that some observed satellites may be bound remnants of disrupted systems. Our mock galaxies match the sizes of observed satellites with $M_{\rm{V}} \lesssim -6$. Star-formation histories computed from only the detectable stars broadly recover the simulated star-formation histories. As an application, we test the near-far technique for reconstructing the proto-MW ultraviolet luminosity function (UVLF) during reionization ($z \gtrsim 6$). Adding the detectable streams improves the recovered faint-end slope at $z = 7$ and reduces the halo-to-halo scatter by $\approx33\%$.

11. The Cosmic Ultraviolet Baryon Survey X: Density and Pressure Profiles of Clumpy Circumgalactic Gas at $z=0.4-0.7$[2610.36088]
Abstract

The thermodynamic state and turbulent properties of the cool circumgalactic medium (CGM) are investigated for 23 galaxies and galaxy groups at intermediate redshift ($z=0.4$–0.7) in the Cosmic Ultraviolet Baryon Survey (CUBS) midz sample using background QSOs within the virial radius. Kinematically resolved absorption-line measurements from Hubble Space Telescope/COS and STIS spectra, combined with photoionization modeling, yield gas densities of $n_\mathrm{H}\approx10^{-4}$–$10^{-2} \ \mathrm{cm}^{-3}$, temperatures of $T\approx(1$–$5) \times 10^4\,\mathrm{K}$, and non-thermal line broadening of $b_\mathrm{NT}\approx2$–$30 \ \mathrm{km \ s^{-1}}$ for individual cool clumps. Combined with published samples at similar redshifts, the results indicate that photoionization equilibrium accurately describes the cool CGM and that turbulent motions are predominantly subsonic. At fixed projected distance, cool-gas density decreases systematically with halo mass, while inferred cool-gas pressures in $M_h\approx10^{12}$–$10^{13}\,\mathrm{M}_\odot$ halos are consistent with hot-gas pressures inferred from stacked X-ray observations. The analysis uncovers a near constant total cool gas mass across a broad halo mass range, which directly translates to a declining baryon mass fraction in the cool phase from $\approx 6$–$19\%$ in halos of $M_h \approx 10^{12}\, \mathrm{M}_\odot$ to $\approx 0.6$–$1.5\%$ in halos of $M_h \approx 10^{13}\, \mathrm{M}_\odot$. Finally, several CUBS galaxies exhibit kinematically aligned low- and high-ionization species spanning up to two orders of magnitude in density, reflecting local fluctuations in the halo gas pressure.

12. Heavy Seeds and the Multi-scale Fueling of Early Supermassive Black Holes and Little Red Dots[2610.36089]
Abstract

The formation of supermassive black holes (SMBHs) in the early Universe has been a longstanding mystery due to the brief period available to assemble them. More recently, the unexpected discovery with the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) of Little Red Dots (LRDs), compact sources exhibiting signatures of active galactic nuclei (AGN), has accentuated this challenge. Cosmological simulations investigating their formation and evolution confront the limitation that the compact scale of SMBH/LRD physics cannot be simultaneously resolved with the larger-scale cosmological environment. We address this limitation by utilizing a cutout technique, selecting a targeted region around an SMBH seed from a cosmological parent simulation and re-running the cutout at higher (sub-pc) resolution for shorter periods. We explore two SMBH formation scenarios: the standard, light seed stellar-remnant case, and the heavy-seed, direct collapse black hole (DCBH) channel. Within the high-resolution achieved by the cutouts, the DCBH, due to its larger mass, is able to induce a buildup of high density gas nearby and accrete gas in bursts of super-Eddington phases, growing in mass and producing an LRD-like spectrum. In contrast, the stellar remnant cannot do so and remains dormant, unable to affect the nearby gas or significantly evolve by gas accretion alone. Therefore, we find that observed high-redshift SMBHs and LRDs require the existence of heavy seeds to assemble dense gas around themselves, enabling efficient growth and producing red optical spectra with strong Balmer breaks resembling LRDs.

13. Protostellar Outflows at the EarliesT Stages (POETS) X. Preliminary statistics of 6.7 GHz masers near POETS sources[2610.36096]
Abstract

So far, the POETS survey has focused on studying at high angular resolution the properties of both 22.2 GHz H2O masers and the radio continuum emission near young stellar objects (YSOs). Now, we want to add the information coming from another star formation tracer, the Class II 6.7 GHz CH3OH (methanol) maser, towards the same POETS targets. We seek to probe the existence of 6.7 GHz maser emission near POETS sources with high sensitivity observations and study their kinematic properties with respect to local radio continuum and 22.2 GHz maser emission at high angular and spectral resolutions. 14. The inner galaxy as a window on its early assembly: lessons from a cosmological Milky Way analogue[2610.36164]
Abstract

Characterising the assembly history of the inner Milky Way is a key goal of upcoming spectroscopic surveys, yet disentangling its overlapping stellar components remains challenging. We investigate the chemodynamics of the centre of a Milky Way analogue in the Auriga Superstars cosmological hydrodynamical simulation suite, focusing on a high-resolution halo (  800 M_sun star particles) with a realistic galactic bar and accretion history. We classify stars within the inner 4 kpc into proto-galaxy, subsequent in-situ, and accreted components to assess their distinguishability using kinematics, dynamics, ages, and metallicities. Stellar net rotation declines sharply between [Fe/H]=0 and [Fe/H]=-1, marking the transition from disc-like in-situ stars to the proto-galaxy. While significant chemical and dynamical overlaps complicate the search for structures in energy-angular momentum (E-Lz) space, metal-poor stars ([Fe/H]<-1) at low energy form a remarkably pure (>97%) sample of proto-galaxy stars. Conversely, the high-energy, metal-poor tail offers the best chance of detecting accreted debris. However, at high energies the inner accreted halo is dominated by low-mass systems (M_star   10^7 - 10^9 M _sun), not the earliest massive mergers. Dynamically, few clues remain from early mergers, and early enrichment is rapid and similar across progenitors. In-situ contamination is an issue for [Fe/H]>-1 throughout E-Lz space. Overall, while accurate stellar ages and high-precision chemical abundances may help further constrain the Milky Way's history, we should be cautious when associating stars with individual merger events in the inner galaxy.

15. Unveiling the nonthermal environments of NGC 3411 and NGC 5903 through new MeerKAT observations[2610.36170]
Abstract

Galaxy groups are key laboratories for investigating the interplay between active galactic nucleus (AGN) outbursts and the dynamical processes of the intra-group medium. We aim to characterize the morphology, spectral properties, and magnetic field structure of the diffuse nonthermal radio emission in two peculiar galaxy groups, NGC 3411 and NGC 5903, selected from an originally larger sample, in order to constrain its origin and its connection with the dynamics of the intra-group medium. We present new MeerKAT UHF- and L-band observations of the two groups, complemented by archival Chandra and XMM-Newton data already analyzed in previous works. In NGC 3411, we detect extended radio emission surrounding the central AGN over scales of  70 kpc, and resolve previously undetected arc-shaped substructures on scales of  20-30 kpc. This extended component exhibits a relatively uniform steep spectrum ($\alpha$   -1.7). We discuss possible scenarios for its origin, including mini-halos, aged AGN plasma, weak shocks, and the possibility of a radio galaxy observed along the line of sight. In NGC 5903, MeerKAT reveals a complex filamentary morphology, including a previously unknown ring-like structure surrounding the central AGN. Spectral-index mapping shows localized flattening ($\alpha$   -0.9) along the north arc, while polarization data reveal ordered magnetic fields and a patchy rotation measure distribution across the source. Together with the disturbed X-ray morphology, these results support a scenario in which fossil AGN plasma has been redistributed and locally reenergized by group-scale dynamical processes such as turbulence, shear, and weak compression. Our results demonstrate that galaxy groups can host a rich variety of nonthermal phenomena, whose properties can only be fully revealed through the combination of sensitive, high-resolution, and multifrequency radio observations.

16. Testing Pre-main-sequence Evolutionary Models with Gaia and SPHEREx Observations of Canis Major North[2610.36223]
Abstract

Pre-main-sequence (PMS) evolutionary models can assign different ages across stellar mass within the same population. We use joint Gaia and SPHEREx spectral energy distributions to infer effective temperatures, radii, and luminosities for members of Canis Major North (CMaN), a nearby association with independent lithium and kinematic age constraints near 23 Myr. The SPHEREx fluxes agree with 2MASS and WISE photometry to 3–6\%, and we anchor the absolute flux scale to Gaia, 2MASS, and WISE. Using the revised temperatures, we obtain a lithium age of $22.4^{+2.8}_{-1.5}$ Myr. For a 29-star PMS sample, population-wide HR-diagram fits yield $20.5^{+1.5}_{-1.4}$ Myr for unspotted SPOTS models, consistent with the external constraints, $18.4^{+1.4}_{-1.3}$ and $18.7^{+1.3}_{-1.2}$ Myr for the Feiden nonmagnetic and BHAC15 models, and substantially older ages of $32.2^{+2.3}_{-2.1}$ and $55^{+5}_{-5}$ Myr for the Feiden magnetic and PARSEC models. No grid returns a temperature-independent age. The Feiden, SPOTS, and BHAC15 models assign older ages to stars above $4000$ K, whereas PARSEC assigns increasingly older ages toward lower temperatures. The intrinsic luminosity dispersion is also mass dependent, approximately $0.14$ dex below $3450$ K but $0.05$ dex at intermediate temperatures. At a fixed age of 23.0 Myr, a single SPOTS filling factor cannot reproduce the width of the low-temperature sequence, whereas the empirical filling-factor distribution of young stars overpredicts it. Spotted isochrones can enclose every cool member only at ages of 37–50 Myr, inconsistent with the lithium and kinematic ages.

17. NOCTURNE. VI. Hour-timescale radio variability in early-stage active galactic nucleus SDSS J122844.81+501751.2[2610.36225]
Abstract

Recently, a sample of radio-weak active galactic nuclei (AGN) was discovered to exhibit extreme Jy-level radio flares at 37 GHz. The origin of these extraordinary flares remains unknown. All sources host low-mass supermassive black holes, and most of them can be classified as narrow-line Seyfert 1 galaxies. We employed multiwavelength observations to investigate one of these sources, SDSS J122844.81+501751.2, during a period of high 37 GHz activity. Our data include radio observations from Metsähovi Radio Observatory (MRO) at 37 GHz, OVRO at 15 GHz, and JVLA at 15, 22, and 33 GHz, as well as optical, UV, and X-ray observations from \textit{Swift}/XRT and UVOT. We studied the variability of the multiwavelength light curves (LC) using statistical tests, as well as derived the $e$-folding timescales of the flares. Utilising the timescales and the observed flare properties, we estimated the variability brightness temperatures and Doppler factors associated with the flares. Most individual LCs do not show statistically significant variability, which is likely due to the low number of measurements. The shortest timescale we estimated is only 1.1 hours, measured between MRO 37 GHz and JVLA 33 GHz detections. This is the fastest intrinsic radio variability reported in an AGN so far. This yielded an extreme variability brightness temperature of $T_\mathrm{B}$ = 4.20 $~\times$ 10$^{18}$ K and a variability Doppler factor of 438. We discuss magnetic eruptions near the black hole as a possible explanation for the extreme radio flares, and show that both incoherent and coherent processes can produce flares at these timescales. These flares challenge our current understanding of AGN radio variability. Whereas magnetic eruptions offer a viable explanation for the flares, more observations, preferably broadband and including polarisation, will be required to confirm their origin. (abridged)

18. Impact of Gaia-Sausage-Enceladus Debris on Milky Way Escape-velocity Estimates in TNG50[2610.36246]
Abstract

The escape velocity is a key probe of the Milky Way (MW) potential, but standard estimates can be biased by unrelaxed substructures. Using MW analogs from the TNG50 cosmological simulation, we investigate the impact of the dominant, highly radial Gaia-Sausage-Enceladus (GSE) debris on escape-velocity measurements. We introduce an adaptive selection strategy that balances statistical reliability with sample size to identify the power-law decline regime of the local halo velocity distribution. Applying this strategy to 16 MW analogs with prominent GSE-like structures and continuous high-velocity tails, we find that retaining GSE-like stars yields a systematic underestimate, with a recovery fraction of $97.5^{+2.1}_{-2.9}\%$, while removing GSE-like stars alone increases the recovery fraction to $99.4^{+2.1}_{-2.5}\%$. The $\sim 2\%$ underestimation of the escape velocity corresponds to an $\sim 6\%$ underestimation of the total mass. This improvement persists in LAMOST-like magnitude and footprint selection. GSE-like debris is therefore a key contributor to the systematic underestimation of escape-velocity measurements, with direct implications for the inferred MW mass.

19. The formation of silicon-carbon chain molecules in IRC+10216[2610.36369]
Abstract

Linear silicon-carbon chain molecules, SiC$_n$ (n = 3–6), have been detected in the circumstellar envelope of the carbon-rich AGB star IRC+10216. Here, we discuss the mechanisms likely to be at work in the synthesis of chains up to SiC$_8$ and present for the first time a detailed comparison between observations and model calculations. Using a combination of measured and postulated rate coefficients, we show that the column densities of the silicon carbide molecules observed in IRC+10216 can be reproduced to with a factor of four. Furthermore, our model calculations suggest that the column densities of SiC$_7$ and SiC$_8$ are a factor of 2–4 less than that of SiC$_6$. We discuss the influence of neutral-neutral reactions involving parent SiC$_2$ on chain growth and show that their neglect leads to a reduction in column densities of carbides up to a maximum of about six for SiC$_6$ and SiC$_7$. Our results suggest that the detection of SiC$_7$ and SiC$_8$ in IRC+10216 will be challenging but that astronomical searches should be undertaken.

20. ASKAP Discovery of a Giant Odd Radio Circle at $z=0.15$[2610.36512]
Abstract

Odd radio circles (ORCs) are a recently recognized class of faint, roughly circular diffuse radio sources, typically a few hundred kiloparsecs across, whose origin is still debated. We report the serendipitous discovery of a giant ORC, ORC J1928$-$5056, in 856 MHz continuum data from the ASKAP observations. The emission forms a single, mildly elliptical structure $4^{\prime}$ across, corresponding to $0.63$ Mpc at the redshift $z=0.1507$ of the central galaxy LEDA 140850, the brightest cluster galaxy of Abell 3639, which makes it one of the largest single ORCs known. After subtracting the radio nucleus associated with the host galaxy, the ORC has a flux density of 73 mJy integrated within an area of $\sim 12$ arcmin$^2$, i.e.\ a mean surface brightness of only $1.7$ $\mu$Jy arcsec$^{-2}$, and a rest-frame luminosity of $L_{1.4}=2\times10^{24}$ W Hz$^{-1}$, comparable to those of the most luminous ORCs. Including its nucleus, the system reaches $L_{1.4}=6.6\times10^{24}$ W Hz$^{-1}$, higher than that of any other ORC reported so far. The host is a massive ($M_{\ast}\sim10^{11}\,M_{\odot}$), passive elliptical galaxy with negligible star formation. It contains an AGN jet with a power-law spectrum of index $\alpha=-0.877\pm0.089$ between 856 and 1656 MHz. Finally, we discuss the implications for ORC formation models and the observations needed to establish the nature of this system.

21. A Hierarchical Bayesian Differential Calibration of the Gaia DR3 Parallax Zero Point Using Galactic Globular Clusters[2610.36543]
Abstract

Globular clusters (GCs) occupy a broad range of stellar colors and magnitudes while sharing nearly the same distance. Gaia Data Release 3 parallaxes retain systematic zero-point residual variations for GCs, even after the correction by the widely used standard correction model (L21). We derive an empirical residual correction by the Hierarchical Bayesian Model, separating cluster-level offsets from the differential information carried by the member sequences.
