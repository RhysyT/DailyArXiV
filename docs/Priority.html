1. A Composite Rest-Frame UV Spectrum of Galaxies at $5.7 < z < 6.9$: Ionization, Outflows, and Implications for Intergalactic Metal Enrichment[2610.33154]
Abstract

We present a stacked JWST/NIRSpec spectrum of 104 galaxies at $\lambda_{\rm rest}=1200\unicode{x2013}2000\;\mathrm{\mathring{A}}$ to study nebular emission and absorption from the interstellar medium (ISM) and stellar winds. The sample is drawn from known [O III] emitters with mean redshift $z=6.50$, absolute UV magnitude $M_\mathrm{UV}=-20.7$, and stellar mass $M_{\star}=10^{8.93}\;\mathrm{M_\odot}$. The stacked continuum closely resembles lower-redshift Lyman Break Galaxy composites, with a few notable differences. Ly$\;\alpha$ has comparable strength and is redshifted relative to the [O III] systemic frame, consistent with production in gaseous outflows. High-ionization emission lines including O III], C III], and He II are slightly stronger than in lower-redshift stacks but exhibit narrow widths, disfavoring ionization from a broad-line AGN. Emission-line ratios slightly favor ionization by a hard stellar continuum, though a contribution from a narrow-line nuclear source cannot be strictly excluded. Numerous strong absorption lines at both high and low ionization are blueshifted by $\sim200\;\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$, again indicating outflows driven by stellar winds or entrained metal-enriched ISM. A simple model suggests that at $z\sim6.5$ most star-forming galaxies are too young for winds at these velocities to propagate beyond $\sim3\unicode{x2013}9$ proper kpc, implying that the observed [O III] emitter population alone cannot explain the frequency of heavy-element absorbers seen in quasar spectra. In this picture, early enrichment of the intergalactic medium likely arises from undetected low-mass galaxies ($M_\star\sim10^5\unicode{x2013}10^6\;\mathrm{M_\odot}$) clustered around the more massive systems detected in JWST/NIRCam WFSS surveys.

2. $El\ Ascua$: An ultra-luminous, radio-loud, Compton-thick AGN at $z\sim 3.8$ hiding in plain sight revealed by MINERVA multi-band MIRI observations[2610.33155]
Abstract

Heavily dust-obscured active galactic nuclei (AGN) are crucial for understanding supermassive black hole growth. Using multi-band MIRI photometry from the MINERVA survey, we report the discovery in the UDS field of an extremely mid-infrared bright Compton-thick AGN (CT AGN) at $z \sim 3.8$, \textit{el Ascua} (the Ember). The AGN and/or its host galaxy is detected at 870 $\mu$m with ALMA and at 1.4 GHz with the VLA. With UV-to-radio spectral energy distribution modeling, we estimate that the dusty host galaxy has a stellar mass of $\log\left( M_\star / M_\odot \right)=10.82 \pm 0.57$ and a star formation rate of ${\rm{SFR}} =157 \pm 8 \,M_\odot\,{\rm yr^{-1}}$, and we confirm the presence of a powerful dusty AGN with an intrinsic accretion luminosity of $\log\left( L_{\rm{acc}} /{\rm erg\,s^{-1}} \right)=47.12 \pm 0.58$. CT obscuration is revealed by comparing the upper limit on the X-ray luminosity over $2-10 \,{\rm{keV}}$ with the $6 \,{\rm\mu{m}}$ mid-IR AGN luminosity. We additionally find evidence for a radio excess by comparing its total IR-to-radio ratio to those of typical star-forming galaxies and radio-quiet AGN, implying that \textit{el Ascua} could be a rare radio-loud CT AGN. With an extremely high ratio between black hole accretion rate and SFR, the AGN is likely nearing the peak of a deeply obscured phase of black hole growth before AGN feedback blows away the obscuring material. Our work demonstrates the power of MIRI to reveal heavily obscured AGN missing from our supermassive black hole census.

3. Dark Matter and MOND successes and degeneracies in reproducing SPARC rotation curves and weak lensing data[2610.33157]
Abstract

The observed rotation velocities of spiral galaxies at large scales exceed those predicted by their baryonic components, motivating the need for dark matter (DM) or Modified Newtonian Dynamics (MOND). We fit the rotation curves of 147 late-type galaxies from the SPARC database, using a suite of models including Cold Dark Matter (CDM) models with and without baryonic feedback, Warm DM, Fuzzy DM, and MOND. We also combine SPARC kinematics with weak-lensing data to extend the mean rotation curves to  1 Mpc. On a galaxy-by-galaxy basis, the inner SPARC rotation curves marginally favour CDM models with baryonic feedback, although accounting for the uncertainties in distances and inclinations increases the degeneracy among DM-based models and MOND. All DM models predict host halo masses larger than abundance-matching expectations, with only the CDM model with baryonic feedback reducing the scatter around the mean luminosity-halo mass relation. MOND is able to reproduce the flatness of the mean galactic rotation curves on scales up to 1 Mpc, providing stellar mass-to-light ratios within observational uncertainties. In contrast, all DM models, without the contribution of mass external to the bound halo, struggle to explain the flat rotation curves. The inclusion of the current weak-lensing data tends to favour MOND over DM models, at least at the largest scales. Future observations from BIG-SPARC, the Square Kilometre Array, and most importantly, upcoming weak-lensing surveys such as EUCLID and Rubin will provide more stringent and independent tests of these competing frameworks

4. Assessing the detectability of a realistic massive black hole binary population in LSST[2610.33159]
Abstract

Electromagnetic signatures of massive black hole binaries (MBHB) are expected to be found in quasar light curves. Specifically, MBHB light curves are expected to show periodic modulations which may originate from different physical processes. The detection of such periodicities requires algorithms suitable to model modulations with arbitrary shapes embedded in quasar red noise, in massive datasets. With the advent of the Vera C. Rubin Observatory Legacy Survey of Space and Time (LSST), such sources may finally be within reach in the next decade. In this work, we assess the performance of a recently proposed flexible detection algorithm for periodicities of arbitrary shapes with simulated LSST-like data that span its 10-year baseline. For the first time, we examine a physically motivated population of binaries and assess the effect of assumed priors on the period on the periodicity search. We study two methods to set the detection threshold: the first based on the posterior odds ratio, which assumes knowledge of the expected fraction of binaries in quasar light curves, and the second, which limits the detection of false positives. We find that the expected distribution of detected periods shows a clear peak between 5 and 10 years, depending on the specific LSST band considered. We recover a detected binary fraction ranging from   20% to   3% depending on the detection threshold assumed and the band analysed, with the peak of the observed period distribution depending on the underlying binary period distribution. Therefore, the detection of a population of binaries in LSST can constrain the binary population and shed light on their evolution.

5. SN Ares: A Strongly Lensed Type IIb Supernova at $\mathbf{z=1.276}$ with a Predicted 70-Year Time Delay[2610.33161]
Abstract

We present the discovery of supernova (SN) Ares, a strongly lensed supernova at $z_{\rm spec}=1.276\pm0.002$ in a massive, triply imaged host galaxy behind the MACS J0308.9+2645 galaxy cluster. JWST/NIRSpec spectroscopy spanning the rest-frame optical to NIR of Ares reveals helium features at $5876\,\mathring{\mathrm{A}}$, $7065\,\mathring{\mathrm{A}}$, and $10830\,\mathring{\mathrm{A}}$, as well as H$\alpha$ and Pa$\beta$. A comparison to local core-collapse SN spectra and light curves leads to a classification of SN Ares as a Type IIb supernova, the highest-redshift member of its class spectroscopically identified. A comparison to local SN IIb light curves provides a phase estimate for the spectrum of $34_{-9}^{+10}$ rest-frame days post-radioactive peak, providing a unique view into a high-redshift stripped-envelope supernova in its late-photospheric evolution. A lens model of the cluster constructed using recent JWST data indicates that SN Ares is the first image to arrive and will reappear in two future images near the center of the cluster plane in $\gtrsim 70$ years, with predicted magnifications of $\sim5\times$ and $\sim1.5\times$. Planned JWST/NIRSpec Multi-Object Spectroscopy of the cluster will allow for more detailed modeling of the lensed system for tighter constraints on the time delay and magnifications, as well as a template spectrum at the location of SN Ares.

6. The Hosts and Mass Distribution Function of the Most Massive Black Holes: Implications for the nHz Gravitational Wave Background[2610.33162]
Abstract

We use the core-radius, $r_\gamma,$ of a galaxy's central core along with a volume-limited sample of brightest cluster galaxies (BCG) to determine the $z=0$ mass distribution function of the most massive super-massive black holes (SMBH). We recover a $M_{BH}-r_\gamma$ relationship between the SMBH mass, $M_{BH},$ and $r_\gamma$ of form $M_{BH} \approx1.3\times10^{10}~(r_\gamma/{\mathrm{kpc}})~M_\odot,$ with $\sigma = 0.25\pm0.03$ dex scatter in $M_{BH}.$ We derive a core distribution function (CDF), based on HST and DLS images of the BCGs. The CDF falls steeply as $N(r_\gamma)\sim r_\gamma^{-3}$ for $r_\gamma>0.50$ kpc. Only $\sim5\%$ of BCGs have $r_\gamma>1$ kpc. Combining the CDF and $M_{BH} - r_\gamma$ relationship yields the BH mass distribution function (MDF). For $M_{BH} \geq 7\times10^{9}~\mathrm{M_\odot}$ (the mean $M_{BH}$ at $r_\gamma=0.50$ kpc) we derive: $\log\left(\frac{dN(M_{BH})}{d{M_{BH}/10^{10} M_\odot}}~[\rm Mpc^{-3}]\right)=(-3.05\pm0.42)~\log\left(\frac{M_{BH}}{10^{10}M_\odot}\right)~-~5.11\ (+0.41, -0.36)$. Cores with $r_\gamma \geq 0.50$ kpc are found almost exclusively in BCGs, implying that SMBHs with $M_{BH} \geq7\times10^{9}~\mathrm{M_\odot}$ may also be exclusive to BCGs. Core-radius correlates with $\alpha,$ a parameter that characterizes the growth of BCG envelopes by mergers, suggesting that the most massive SMBHs grew in tandem with their BCG hosts. The BH MDF embodies a significant population of SMBH with $M_{BH} >7\times10^{9} M_\odot.$ When combined with the Liepold & Ma (2024) BH MDF to account for SMBH with $M_{BH} < 7\times10^{9} M_\odot,$ it can comfortably explain the nHz gravitational wave background.

7. How Steep is your Dust? Direct Measurements of Nebular Attenuation Curves at Cosmic Noon with JWST[2610.33166]
Abstract

Tracing the shape of the nebular attenuation curve, which is crucial for accurately measuring intrinsic emission-line fluxes, has proven challenging both in the local universe and at high redshift. Here we present nebular dust attenuation curves measured directly for 24 star-forming galaxies ($9.3<\log(\rm M{\star}/M_\odot)<11.1$) at cosmic noon ($1.8<z<3.3$) from the Blue Jay survey. Using deep JWST/NIRSpec spectra, we measure up to eight hydrogen recombination lines from H$\delta$ to Pa$\beta$ per galaxy and find little diversity in the curve shape, with most galaxies agreeing with the \citet{calzetti_dust_2000} attenuation curve. For over $90\%$ of the sample, the nebular reddening from the Balmer lines is consistent with that from the Paschen lines. Using \texttt{Prospector} spectral energy distribution (SED) fits, we obtain the stellar reddening and find that the difference between stellar and nebular reddening increases with stellar mass, and that each reddening measurement is independent of inclination, which disagrees with the two-component dust model. We then measure the impact of using the derived nebular attenuation curve or a literature curve when determining metallicity using the strong-line method, and find that for some diagnostics (e.g., S32, S3, S23, and S3O3), the difference can be as large as $5\sigma$. Caution is needed when using these diagnostics if the nebular attenuation curve cannot be directly measured, since the resulting uncertainty can exceed the measurement uncertainty on the emission-line fluxes even with high signal-to-noise Balmer decrement measurements. These results show that the shape of the nebular attenuation curve is poorly understood and that constraining it is important for reducing systematic uncertainty in galaxy measurements.

8. Recently launched AGN-driven outflow in a lensed quiescent galaxy at Cosmic Noon[2610.33176]
Abstract

We analyze JWST/NIRSpec IFU spectroscopy of MRG-M0150, a gravitationally lensed, massive quiescent galaxy at $z=2.6$ from the INQUEST-JWST survey. Thanks to the combination of lensing magnification with JWST's angular resolution, we are able to build spatially resolved maps of the ionized gas spatial distribution, kinematics, and ionization properties in this system. We detect ionized gas emission throughout the galaxy: the lack of residual star formation inferred from line-ratio diagnostic diagrams, together with the decoupling between the gas and stellar kinematics, suggest that the ionized gas is in a wind. We use the 3D gas kinematics modeling code \texttt{MOKA3D} to constrain the geometry and physical properties of the ionized gas wind. We find that the observations are consistent with a biconical wind driven by the low-luminosity AGN at the center of the galaxy, which is detected in X-rays. The wind reaches high velocities of $1000-1400$ km/s, implying that part of the gas may escape the halo. The ionized gas mass outflow rate, $(0.6 \pm 0.2)$ ${\rm M}_{\odot}$ yr${}^{-1}$, is modest but exceeds the upper limit on the residual star formation rate, and it is likely that the wind is multiphase and carries more mass. We show that typical unresolved observations would not detect the wind, suggesting that the incidence of such outflows may be higher than previously recognized. Simple geometric arguments show that the observed outflow must have been launched $\approx 0.45$ Myr ago from the time of observation. This evidence suggests that low-level AGN activity is an important mechanism for maintaining quiescence in massive galaxies at cosmic noon, potentially over cosmological timescales.

9. Initial Conditions of Magnetized AGN Accretion Disks from Cloud Capture and Disruption[2610.33297]
Abstract

Accretion onto supermassive black holes (BHs) from the ISM can occur via capture of cold gas 'clouds' (GMCs, clumps, or overdense structures). Unlike material in stellar tidal disruptions or X-ray binaries, or classical Bondi accretion, ISM clouds are strongly magnetized (plasma $\beta \equiv P_{\rm thermal}/P_{\rm magnetic} \ll 1$), with supersonic turbulence, and cool rapidly. We derive analytic scalings for the initial conditions of accretion disks formed by capture/disruption of such clouds, in three regimes: (1) low-mass BHs ($M_{\rm BH} \ll M_{\rm cl}$), where turbulent Bondi-Hoyle or hierarchical 'leapfrog' growth via sub-cloud encounters dominates; (2) comparable-mass encounters ($M_{\rm BH} \sim M_{\rm cl}$); and (3) massive BHs tidally disrupting small clouds ($M_{\rm BH} \gg M_{\rm cl}$). In each, the resulting disk is predicted to be initially toroidally-magnetically-dominated ($|B_{\phi}| \gg |B_{z}|$, $\beta \ll 1$), geometrically thick ($H/R \sim 0.1-1$), and gravitationally stable ($Q \gtrsim 1$), with super-sonic, trans-Alfvenic turbulence. These conditions differ qualitatively from those assumed in classical accretion/$\alpha$ disk theory, 'chaotic cold accretion', stellar tidal disruption, or Bondi-Hoyle accretion. At least initially, the toroidal field should be sustained by flux-freezing on timescales shorter than Parker or turbulent resistive losses. We examine how cloud mass spectra drive accretion rate distributions, duty cycles, and fueling rates, with integrated BH growth dominated by clouds of mass comparable to the BH. Given known cosmological evolution, we suggest a scenario linking (a) inefficient growth and wandering BHs in dwarf galaxies, (b) hyper-Eddington growth in little red dots, (c) peak quasar activity in massive gas-rich systems at cosmic noon, and (d) lower-luminosity AGN in gas-poor galaxies at late times.

10. Exploring the UV Fe III Emission along the Quasar Main Sequence[2610.33339]
Abstract

The diversity of Type I AGNs can be organized along the Quasar Main Sequence (MS), a powerful framework primarily driven by the accretion rate and defined in the optical plane by the FWHM of $\mathrm{H\beta}$ and the Fe II strength ($R_{\rm FeII}$). Previous studies suggested that the UV Fe III evolves along this MS. However, a systematic analysis is still missing. We aim to characterize the UV Fe III emission across the MS, assess its potential as a tracer, and investigate how its properties depend on the conditions of the emitting gas. We analyzed the UV iron emission in 69 low-$z$ $(z \lesssim 1)$ AGNs observed with the Hubble Space Telescope. We fitted the 1700-2200 Angstrom range and investigated the main correlations using principal component analysis. We complemented with $\tt{CLOUDY}$ photoionization simulations to explore the dependence of the iron emission on gas density, ionization parameter, metallicity, microturbulence, column density, and spectral energy distribution. We find a positive correlation between the strengths of the UV iron emission and the optical $\mathrm{R_{FeII}}$ parameter. With this, we define a UV MS diagram using the FWHM of $\mathrm{Al III\,\lambda1860}$ and the equivalent width of $\mathrm{Fe III}$ + $\mathrm{Fe II}$. $\tt{CLOUDY}$ simulations support this result, predicting stronger Fe III emission at high $\mathrm{R_{FeII}}$. Additionally, we found object-to-object variations in the $\mathrm{Fe III}$ multiplets. Our results indicate that UV Fe III emission and $\mathrm{Al III}\,\lambda 1860$ can serve as proxies for optical Fe II and $\mathrm{H\beta}$, extending the Quasar MS into the UV domain. The variations in the Fe III multiplets demonstrate that a single template may not represent all quasars. Thus, characterizing the Fe III emission and its dependence on physical conditions is essential for improving the modeling of UV AGN spectra.

11. Late Epoch Infrared Spectroscopy and Two-Component 3D Photoionization Modeling of Classical Nova V5668 Sgr[2610.33376]
Abstract

V5668 Sgr is an Fe II classical nova (CN) that exploded on 15 March 2015. We present near-infrared (NIR) spectroscopy of the ejecta from the Mt. Abu observatory which exhibits emission lines of species H, He, N, S, Ti, and Si. We also present SOFIA (+FORCAST) spectroscopy which shows two forbidden emission lines, [Ne II] at 12.8 $\mu$m and [O IV] at 25.9 $\mu$m, as well as two emission features attributed to polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAH) at 6.2 $\mu$m and 11.3 $\mu$m. 1D Cloudy photoionization simulations require two components to model the system, which suggests a multifaceted, morphologically complex ejecta. 12. Emission line stacking on cosmological scales: equivalence to two-point statistics and modelling of the observational effects[2610.33603]
Abstract

Emission line stacking measurement for cosmological line intensity mapping (LIM) contains not only the average emission line profile of the stacked sources, but usually also the clustering signal, due to the limited angular resolution of the observations. We provide an explicit derivation of the equivalence between the emission line stacking, the two-point cross-correlation function in configuration space and the power spectrum in Fourier space, similar to the case between the angular stacking signal for the kinematic Sunyaev-Zel'dovich effect and the angular power spectrum in harmonic space ( arXiv:2512.14625 , arXiv:2605.15947 ). Using the quadratic estimator formalism, we establish that the emission line stacking signal measurement is equivalent to a quadratic estimator for the cross-correlation function. The formalism allows for the explicit modelling of the stacked signal, including the clustering statistics as well as observational effects such as the galaxy selection function and the LIM survey footprint. In particular, using mock HI intensity mapping data, we show that we can accurately model the effects of foreground removal on the stacked signal. The correlation function estimator provides significant speed-up for the stacking measurement and enables modelling of the signal and its covariance for large LIM datasets with MeerKLASS and future SKAO.

13. Effects of Synchrotron Self-Absorption on Polarization Diagnostics of MHD Turbulence[2610.33696]
Abstract

Synchrotron polarization is a powerful tool for diagnosing magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) turbulence, yet prior analyses are confined to the optically thin regime, neglecting the effects of synchrotron self-absorption (SSA). Here, we incorporate SSA into the radiative transfer of polarized synchrotron emission and employ 3D MHD turbulence simulations to investigate its impacts on polarization statistics. We show that SSA strongly modulates polarized intensity by suppressing its emission amplitude, steepening its low-frequency power-law slopes, and reducing scale-dependent anisotropy. Notably, SSA does not impede the recovery of the scaling slope of the magnetic turbulence. Our results extend the applicability of synchrotron polarization diagnostics to optically thick astrophysical environments, providing a theoretical foundation for interpreting multi-frequency radio polarization observations. This confirms that polarization statistical techniques remain effective for diagnosing MHD turbulence properties in astrophysical environments where SSA is present.

14. On the Evolution of Circumstellar Disks Modified by the Presence of a Binary[2610.33820]
Abstract

Stars form within circumstellar disks, and some binary stars develop circumbinary disks surrounding the pair. Observations suggest that disks that form around binary star systems are less common than those around single stars. One possible explanation is that binary stars may disrupt or shorten the lifespan of their surrounding disks relative to those around single stars. To investigate this, we used the DISCO hydrodynamics code to compute the lifetimes of circumstellar disks with and without a binary. Our results indicate that binary systems can reduce the lifetime of a circumbinary disk by a modest factor of two to three compared to disks around single stars. This effect is present when the binary separation is at least one-tenth of the disk radius. While our findings show that binaries can shorten circumbinary disk lifetimes, this alone might not be enough to fully account for the scarcity of these disks.

15. Properties of Oxygen-rich Asymptotic Giant Branch stars in the LMC and the Milky Way[2610.33991]
Abstract

We investigate the properties of oxygen-rich asymptotic giant branch (OAGB) stars in the LMC and the Milky Way using infrared (IR) color-magnitude diagrams, two-color diagrams, spectral energy distributions (SEDs), and variability data. The observed SEDs are analyzed with radiative transfer models to constrain the properties of the central stars and their circumstellar dust shells and to estimate distances. For OAGB stars in the LMC, whose distance is well established, a set of representative OAGB models is derived by fitting theoretical SEDs to the observations. These models are subsequently applied to Galactic OAGB stars, for which Gaia DR3 parallaxes are often uncertain, to estimate distances from SED fitting. For Galactic Mira variables, distances are also determined using a period-magnitude relation calibrated with Mira stars in the LMC. We find that OAGB stars in the two galaxies exhibit broadly similar IR properties, and that the theoretical models successfully reproduce the observed IR characteristics, despite the extreme rarity of heavily obscured objects with optically thick silicate dust envelopes in the LMC. We further examine Galactic OAGB stars associated with OH, H2O, and SiO masers, including the relationship between absolute OH maser fluxes and IR colors. In addition, we investigate the IR properties of Mira-type OAGB stars and characterize their long-term variability using archival observations together with 16 years of WISE photometric data. Finally, we present a new catalog of 70,877 Galactic OAGB stars, providing a more reliable and comprehensive dataset for future studies.

16. Observational evidence of the Gaia-Enceladus-Sausage merger's influence on the opposite side of the Galactic Center[2610.34001]
Abstract

The Gaia-Enceladus-Sausage (GES) merger, which occurred approximately 8-11 Gyr ago, is a key event that shaped the Galactic inner halo. Its remnants have been studied extensively and are known to dominate the halo on the Near-side of the Galactic Center (GC), i.e. the side facing the Sun, but systematic evidence for whether the Far-side (the side beyond the GC as seen from the Sun) is similarly affected has been lacking. Using 14,056 red giant stars selected from SMSS DR4 and Gaia DR3 through stringent quality cuts, we compare the metallicity distributions, velocity-space distributions, velocity dispersions, anisotropy parameters, and action-space properties of the Near- and Far-sides. We find clear observational evidence for the influence of GES on the Far-side: it hosts the same metal-rich component peaked at [Fe/H]   -1.4, and its metal-rich population (-1.7 < [Fe/H] <= -1.3) shows GES-typical features consistent with the Near-side, namely a "sausage"-shaped velocity distribution and a velocity anisotropy as high as beta   0.8-0.9. Action-space analysis shows that 10.86% +/- 0.23% of Far-side stars carry the GES dynamical imprint, consistent with 11.56% +/- 0.16% on the Near-side. The Far-side shows a residual prograde rotation that persists under increasingly aggressive disk-removal cuts, indicating a genuine kinematic signal rather than disk contamination, and a battery of robustness tests confirms that the symmetry is not an artifact of measurement uncertainties, residual disk contamination, or incomplete Sagittarius-stream removal. These results demonstrate that GES shaped the entire inner halo (r < 28 kpc) globally and symmetrically, with its imprint clearly present on the Far-side as well as the Near-side.

17. Polar-structure galaxies beyond the local Universe: first results from Euclid[2610.34049]
Abstract

Galaxies hosting strongly misaligned stellar and gas structures offer a direct window into external accretion and the buildup of angular momentum through multiple spin components. Such polar-structure systems are intrinsically rare and, until now, have been identified almost entirely at low redshift. The Euclid Quick Data Release (Q1), with its uniform, space-based VIS imaging, provides an unprecedented opportunity to locate these objects over wide areas and at much greater distances. We present the first visual search for polar-structure galaxy (PSG) candidates in Euclid VIS data, spanning approximately 10% of the Q1 survey footprint. This search yields the largest high-redshift PSG candidate sample assembled to date, including roughly 100 high-confidence systems with over 50 at $z \gtrsim 0.3$, along with additional populations of face-on rings and polar tidal structures (PTS). Robust candidates are detected out to redshifts of $z\sim1$, demonstrating that polar structures can be identified well beyond the local Universe. Although the present sample is not yet sufficient for quantitative occurrence rate or evolutionary measurements, it enables initial classification and basic structural characterization of PSGs at intermediate redshift. These results lay the groundwork for future statistical studies with forthcoming Euclid data releases and illustrate Euclid's power for rare-object science across cosmic time.

19. A MAGNHIFFIC cold-gas filament fuelling the nuclear activity of NGC 3100[2610.34071]
Abstract

MAGNHIFFIC (MeerKAT AGN HI Feeding & Feedback Investigation close-by) leverages on sensitive MeerKAT 21-cm and VLT Survey Telescope (VST) observations of nearby Active Galactic Nuclei (AGN) to study feeding and feedback from the circum-nuclear ($\lesssim 1$ kpc) to the circum-galactic scales ($\gtrsim 10$ kpc). In nearby ($D\sim38$ Mpc) radio galaxy NGC 3100, these observations reveal, for the very first time, a starless filament of neutral atomic hydrogen ($M_{\rm H {\hskip 0.02cm \tt I}}\sim 3.4\times 10^8 {\rm M}_\odot$) extending from its outskirts ($\sim 340$ kpc) down to its innermost kiloparsecs. The kinematical properties of the filament show it likely formed by gas stripped from dwarf galaxy MKT095931-312323, flying by NGC 3100 $\gtrsim 0.5$ Gyr ago. The filament appears to replenish the cold-gas reservoir of NGC 3100's circum-nuclear disk traced by archival $^{12}$CO images as well as by HI absorption. The kinematics of the HI absorption and of the $^{12}$CO(3–2) show that, in the very centre, the jets are currently stirring the multi-phase gas while dense clouds may still fall into the SMBH. The coexistence of a surviving cold reservoir, compact inner atomic/molecular clouds, and localised jet-driven heating indicates that AGN feeding and feedback operate simultaneously rather than as separate evolutionary phases.

20. Post-fragmentation Morphological and Broadband Color Evolution of C/2025 K1 (ATLAS)[2610.34100]
Abstract

Comet C/2025 K1 (ATLAS) underwent major fragmentation after its 2025 October 8 perihelion passage at q   0.33 au. We present four epochs of Johnson-Cousins BVR imaging obtained with the Mount Laguna Observatory 40-inch reflector on 2025 November 26, November 29, December 5, and December 8, sampling the subsequent evolution of the integrated fragment-coma complex. The images were homogenized to common circular two-dimensional effective point-spread functions before morphology was measured along the projected fragment axis. We evaluated 324 profile-extraction configurations and removed solutions whose localized peak landed on the search-window boundary. The fragment-axis surface-brightness enhancement relocates inward between November 26 and 29 and outward between December 5 and 8; the sign of both changes is recovered in every valid configuration and at all tested common target PSFs from 5.3 to 6.0 arcsec. Calibrated BVR photometry using Gaia DR3 synthetic Johnson-Kron-Cousins standards independently shows a repeatable November 26-29 change in broadband spectral shape: B-V decreases while V-R increases. The curvature index S'_(V-R) - S'_(B-V) changes by +3.47 (+1.50/-1.65) percent per 1000 Angstrom, with 99.1 percent of Monte Carlo realizations positive, while the full B-R reflectivity gradient remains approximately constant. Repeating the color comparison at a fixed projected radius of 2205 km produces nearly identical changes. The combined results are consistent with continued restructuring of the unresolved post-fragmentation inner coma, while broadband imaging alone cannot uniquely separate dust-color evolution, molecular emission, and changing contributions from unresolved fragments.

21. EP241021a as An Off-nuclear Month-duration Transient in a Dwarf Starburst Galaxy[2610.34217]
Abstract

EP241021a is a peculiar fast X-ray transient exhibiting a long X-ray plateau and an optical rebrightening, both challenging the traditional jet afterglow model. Whether its nature is a stellar explosion or a tidal disruption event (TDE) remains debated. Here we present late-time HST/WFC3 and Gemini-N/GMOS observations of its host galaxy at $z=0.748$. After $\sim300$ days post-trigger, the transient emission had faded below detection, revealing a dwarf disk galaxy with an effective radius $R_e=1.7$ kpc, a stellar mass $M_\star\sim3\times10^8M_\odot$, and a high star formation rate of $\sim5\ M_\odot\ {\rm yr}^{-1}$. Combined with previous very long baseline interferometry measurements, the transient can be located in the galactic disk with a projected physical offset of $0.95\pm0.13$ kpc from the galaxy center.
