1. Chemical abundance patterns at z=1-7: Carbon, Nitrogen, Neon, Sulphur, and Argon abundances from JWST spectroscopy[2610.28717]
Abstract

The relative abundances of chemical elements in the interstellar medium (ISM) trace the history of star formation and chemical enrichment in galaxies. We investigate the abundance patterns of carbon (C), nitrogen (N), neon (Ne), sulfur (S), and argon (Ar) in star-forming galaxies at z=1-7, combining ultra-deep JWST/NIRSpec observations from the MARTA survey with archival spectroscopy. We assemble 244 galaxies with [OIII]4363 detections and derive direct Te-based abundances. New empirical strong-line calibrations for N/O, Ne/O, S/O, and Ar/O extend the analysis to an additional 514 galaxies. We find distinct abundance-metallicity trends. N/O is enhanced by 0.18 dex in median relative to local galaxies, with a stronger enhancement at low metallicity. Direct measurements reveal decreasing S/O and Ar/O with increasing metallicity, while strong-line measurements suggest a flattening at higher metallicities, especially for S/O. We also identify a stellar-mass dependence in the O/Ar-Ar/H plane, with lower-mass galaxies exhibiting lower O/Ar at fixed Ar/H, consistent with differences in star-formation efficiency and delayed SNe Ia enrichment. Ne/O remains approximately constant up to 12+log(O/H)=8.2, with a mild increase at higher metallicities and no significant redshift evolution. C/O tentatively increases with metallicity, broadly following the local relation. Finally, a forward model shows that observational selection affects the abundance ratios differently: it mainly restricts the accessible metallicity range for Ne/O and Ar/O, but preferentially selects galaxies with enhanced N/O at low metallicity, with a weaker effect for S/O. These results provide new constraints on chemical enrichment at high redshift and highlight the importance of accounting for selection effects when interpreting abundance-metallicity trends.

2. The Balmer Absorber Strength Problem of Little Red Dots[2610.28720]
Abstract

Little Red Dots (LRDs) commonly show Balmer absorption features. Some observed LRD spectra have weaker absorption in H$\alpha$ than in H$\beta$, in apparent contradiction to atomic physics according to standard attenuation models. This tension is naturally resolved when one considers emission from the absorbing medium itself, which is ignored in the standard model: when the Balmer absorption is optically thick, a non-negligible source function could fill in the H$\alpha$ absorption trough and reduce its observed absorption equivalent width (EW). With proof-of-concept photoionization models we show that the source function of H$\alpha$ could be systematically stronger than that of H$\beta$. This is due to radiative trapping preferentially populating the $n=3$ level of neutral hydrogen, which operates efficiently when the medium is optically thick to H$\alpha$ but not very optically thick to Paschen-$\alpha$. Such line-dependent source functions could lead to significant corrections to the expected EW and selectively weaken H$\alpha$ absorption, consistent with observations. Quantitatively, this depends on the distance of the absorbing medium and its internal structure, providing a new diagnostic of the environment around LRDs. Future modeling of the absorbers may also potentially open a new avenue for estimating the mass of the central engine.

3. Arepo-CD: Coupling Collisional $N$-Body Dynamics with Moving-Mesh Magnetohydrodynamics[2610.28727]
Abstract

Star formation is a hierarchical, clustered process in turbulent giant molecular clouds (GMCs), where close stellar encounters can strongly affect cluster evolution and feedback. However, gravitational softening in many hydrodynamic simulations of GMCs limits the treatment of these encounters. We present Arepo-CD (Collisional Dynamics), a framework that couples star-by-star collisional $N$-body dynamics to the moving-mesh magnetohydrodynamics (MHD) code Arepo. Star-star interactions use unsoftened gravity, while interactions involving gas or collisionless components remain softened. Compact stellar subsystems are identified on the fly and evolved with a fourth-order forward symplectic integrator and slow-down algorithmic regularization coupled to the native Arepo timestep hierarchy. Benchmark tests against the collisional $N$-body code PeTar show good agreement in cluster core collapse and subsequent expansion, radial density profiles, and stellar velocity distributions. Coupled to the Rigel star formation and feedback model, Arepo-CD follows cluster formation and early evolution in radiation MHD simulations of a $10^5~M_\odot$ turbulent molecular cloud. In the fiducial GMC comparison, the collisional and softened-gravity runs yield similar star formation histories and integrated star formation efficiencies. Nevertheless, close gravitational encounters during the embedded phase generate a substantial runaway population, with a velocity tail extending beyond 100 km/s. Ejected massive stars redistribute radiative feedback into the surrounding lower-density medium and produce a more extended distribution of heated gas. Arepo-CD provides a practical and computationally efficient framework for studying collisional stellar dynamics and its coupling to gas evolution and feedback during star cluster formation in realistic galactic environments.

4. Discovery of a Broad-Line Active Galactic Nucleus at $z = 9.98$[2610.28735]
Abstract

The James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) has uncovered a previously unexplored population of faint active galactic nuclei (AGNs) in the early Universe. We present MoM-BL-z10 at $z=9.9837\pm0.0009$, identified through a systematic search for broad-line AGNs at $z\geq6$ using public JWST/NIRSpec PRISM spectra from the DAWN JWST Archive. The PRISM spectrum exhibits a broad component in H$\beta$ at 5.35 microns. A narrow+broad Gaussian fit yields a broad H$\beta$ detection at S/N = 3.87, with FWHM = $3480^{+2040}_{-950}$ km s$^{-1}$ and $L_{{\rm H}\beta,{\rm broad}}=1.25^{+0.33}_{-0.31}\times10^{42}$ erg s$^{-1}$. The detection of [Ne III] $\lambda3870$ and the high [Ne III]/[O II] and [O III]/H$\beta$ ratios further support the AGN interpretation. Its rest-frame UV and optical continuum slopes, compact NIRCam morphology, and a tentative MIRI F770W detection suggest similarities to Little Red Dots (LRDs). The broad H$\beta$ detection enables the first virial SMBH mass estimate at $z\sim10$. We derive $M_{\rm BH}=3.6^{+5.8}_{-1.7}\times10^7 M_\odot$ and $\lambda_{\rm Edd}=0.11^{+0.10}_{-0.07}$ using the standard single-epoch virial calibration and quasar bolometric correction. We also consider an alternative scenario adopting the LRD-specific bolometric correction and assuming $\lambda_{\rm Edd}=1$, which yields $M_{\rm BH}=2.1^{+0.6}_{-0.5}\times10^6 M_\odot$. Under either scenario, the inferred SMBH mass at a cosmic age of only 470 Myr highlights the challenge of rapid BH growth in the early Universe, suggesting super-Eddington accretion from a stellar-mass seed or a more massive seed. As the highest-redshift broad-line AGN identified to date, MoM-BL-z10 provides a new observational benchmark for understanding the early growth of supermassive black holes.

5. A combined HST and JWST analysis of the stellar population in Draco II[2610.28738]
Abstract

Draco II (Dra II) is one of the faintest and most metal-poor Milky Way satellites known, yet its nature as either an ultra-faint dwarf galaxy or an extended globular cluster remains debated. We perform a photometric analysis of the stellar population of Dra II reducing deep multi-band observations from the Hubble Space Telescope (HST) and the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST). We select Dra II member stars based on their proper motions and photometric quality criteria. For the first time, optical and infrared photometry are combined to produce a stellar catalogue reaching 50 percent completeness at $F814W = 26.5$ mag (corresponding to the absolute magnitude $M_{F814W} = 10.1$ mag). The colour-magnitude diagram confirms that Dra II hosts an old stellar population with an age of $13.5_{-1.5}^{+1.7}$ Gyr at a distance of $19.2\pm0.9$ kpc. We measure an $\alpha$-enhanced stellar population with a low mean metallicity of $[\rm Fe/H]_{CaHK} = -2.38 \pm 0.17$ dex and confirm the presence of an intrinsic metallicity spread of $\sigma_{[\rm Fe/H]} = 0.85 \pm 0.13$ dex. We identify 10 unresolved main-sequence binary stars in the HST catalogue with mass ratios $q > 0.5$ in the mass range $0.43<M_1/M_\odot<0.71$, corresponding to a binary fraction of $f_{bin}^{q>0.5} = 0.21 \pm 0.07$. This value is higher than those typically observed in globular clusters and lies near the upper range measured in open clusters. The stellar mass function is best modelled by a single power law with a slope of $1.53\pm0.26$ over the mass range $0.13$–$0.77\ M_\odot$. We find no evidence of mass segregation. Consistent results are obtained independently from the JWST catalogue. Combining these findings strongly suggests that Draco II is best classified as an ultra-faint dwarf galaxy rather than a globular cluster.

6. Unveiling the correlation between the UV 2175 Å dust attenuation bump, PAH emission, and gas-phase metallicity at cosmic noon[2610.28743]
Abstract

The UV 2175 Å attenuation bump is one of the most prominent yet poorly understood features of galaxy attenuation curves. Its main candidate carrier is polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbon (PAH) molecules, which are more prevalent in high-metallicity environments. We present the first systematic study of the relationship between PAH abundance and the UV bump in 49 galaxies at cosmic noon (1.5 < z < 2.7), examining its dependence on stellar mass, star formation rate (SFR), and V-band optical depth. For 19 galaxies, we also investigate gas-phase metallicity using oxygen abundances. We measure the bump amplitude from VLT/MUSE spectroscopy and derive galaxy properties, including the PAH-to-dust mass fraction $q_{\rm PAH}$, through spectral energy distribution fitting with Prospector, combining JWST/NIRCam and JWST/MIRI photometry. Oxygen abundances are derived from strong-line diagnostics using Keck/MOSFIRE and JWST/NIRSpec data. We find a positive correlation between bump amplitude $E_{\rm bump}$ and $q_{\rm PAH}$ (Spearman $\rho = 0.60$). UV bumps are rarely detected at low $q_{\rm PAH}$, supporting PAHs as their primary carriers. Both $E_{\rm bump}$ and $q_{\rm PAH}$ correlate with stellar mass, oxygen abundance, and optical depth, and anticorrelate with specific SFR, suggesting that PAHs and the UV bump are suppressed in low-metallicity, dust-poor, and/or intensely star-forming environments. This is consistent with PAH destruction in environments with limited dust shielding and/or strong radiation fields. A principal component analysis of $E_{\rm bump}$, $q_{\rm PAH}$, optical depth, specific SFR, and oxygen abundance explains 97% of the variance, suggesting that previously reported correlations with stellar mass reflect an underlying dependence on metallicity. Finally, variations in dust geometry may explain the wide range of bump amplitudes (0-0.5 mag) at high $q_{\rm PAH}$.

7. Ammonia Fitting of Multiple Velocity Components in High-Mass Star-Forming Regions[2610.28746]
Abstract

We perform a multiple velocity component analysis of ammonia (NH$_3$) gas from KEYSTONE, a Green Bank Telescope survey covering 11 giant molecular clouds (GMCs) at distances from 0.7 to 2.7 kpc. We extend existing codes to enable simultaneous fitting of up to three velocity components in NH$_3$ (1,1) with radiative transfer. Using our multiple component fitting technique, we produce the best maps of the centroid velocity and velocity dispersion in these clouds to date. Over the entire survey, multiple velocity components were seen in 13% of pixels where we detect and fit NH$_3$ emission, primarily in clusters at the heart of the GMCs. In these multiple component pixels, the median measured NH$_3$ linewidth decreased by a factor of 1.4 compared to equivalent single-component measurements, but there are also significant cloud-to-cloud variations. Regions with multiple velocity components tend to be found in the densest zones of the GMCs and at the sites of high-mass star formation, indicative of multiple filaments, cores, or complex velocities associated with the formation of massive stars. Disentangling these velocity features will allow future quantitative analysis of coherent motions, such as infall and outflow, in these complex environments. The multi-component data products are available on the CADC.

8. Link between the properties of circumgalactic outflows and the recent star formation history of their host galaxies[2610.28752]
Abstract

Galactic outflows play a key role in galaxy evolution, yet their connection to the star formation history of their host galaxies remains poorly constrained. Building on previous work modeling extended Mg II emission of galaxies observed with VLT/MUSE to infer outflow properties, we investigate how these properties depend on the recent SFH. We derive non-parametric SFHs by fitting spectroscopic and photometric data with linear combinations of single stellar population models, and compute star formation rates over different timescales sensitive to recent stochastic bursts. We find moderate correlations between these SFRs (and sSFRs) and the kinematic properties of the outflows, namely maximum velocity and radial acceleration rate. Such correlations are not recovered using SFR tracers based on [O II] luminosity or parametric SFHs, highlighting the importance of capturing bursty star formation on timescales consistent with the traveling time of ejected gas into the circumgalactic medium. Using the extent and kinematics of the Mg II halos, we estimate each galaxy's outflow launching time and compute the SFR averaged over the relevant timescale. We identify a correlation between this SFR and the maximum outflow velocity, although it is not significantly stronger than those obtained using fixed averaging timescales. This suggests that the properties of Mg II halos are shaped by a series of star formation events rather than by a single dominant burst. Lastly, we find faster outflows in galaxies whose recent star formation is dominated by one or a few concentrated episodes, whereas galaxies with star formation spread more evenly over time exhibit slower outflows. These results indicate that the circumgalactic medium acts as a dynamical archive of recent star formation, linking bursty galaxy growth to outflow kinematics and providing a direct observational constraint on feedback models.

9. The MIni-Halos in MIni-Clusters (MIMIC) project: New insights into the origin of mini-halos from a LOFAR, uGMRT, and MeerKAT study of diffuse radio emission in Abell 496 and ZwCl 235[2610.28756]
Abstract

We aim at investigating the presence of diffuse radio emission in low-mass (M$_{500}\leq3\times10^{14}$ M$_{\odot}$), relaxed galaxy clusters. We present a pilot study for the MIMIC (MIni-halos in MIni-Clusters) project, focusing on Abell 496 and ZwCl 235, using LOFAR (54, 144 MHz), uGMRT (400, 650 MHz), and MeerKAT (1.28 GHz) data, as well as Chandra X-ray data. We detect diffuse radio emission in the central 100 kpc of both Abell 496 and ZwCl 235, spatially confined within sloshing-induced cold fronts seen in X-ray images. The diffuse sources satisfy the classification criteria for radio mini-halos and represent the least powerful known to date, with 1.4 GHz radio powers of $(2.2\pm0.2)\times10^{22}$ W/Hz in Abell 496 and $(1.3\pm0.3)\times10^{22}$ W/Hz in ZwCl 235. The mini-halos have steep spectra ($-1.9\leq\alpha\leq-1.4$), and in Abell 496 we find outward steepening of the spectral index. In both clusters, the radio and X-ray surface brightness are correlated over the diffuse source extent, with sub-linear or nearly linear slopes depending on frequency. Separately, we present the properties of two extended (>200 kpc) tailed radio galaxies in both clusters. The steep spectra, sub-linear radio-X-ray correlations, and confinement within cold fronts suggest that contribution from fossil plasma advected from the central radio galaxy and plausible turbulent re-acceleration are at play in Abell 496 and ZwCl 235. These two systems allow us to extend mini-halo scaling relations toward the low-power regime. We confirm a strong correlation between the mini-halo radio power and the cool core X-ray luminosity, with $P_{1.4} \propto L_{X,70}^{1.5\pm0.2}$. We find, for the first time, tentative indications that mini-halos with steeper radio spectra may preferentially be associated with lower core X-ray luminosities and lower radio power. (abridged)

10. PIANISTpy: A JAX-based Python package for velocity-anisotropic astrophysical equilibrium models[2610.28758]
Abstract

The hierarchical assembly of dark matter haloes and galaxies via mergers and anisotropic mass accretion naturally imprints non-trivial velocity anisotropy onto haloes and galaxies, an empirical fact emerging from cosmological simulations. However, dynamical studies based on idealised numerical simulations typically assume isotropic velocity distributions for the systems studied. This is mainly because no tools exist to construct anisotropic initial conditions with fully self-consistent (i.e., physical and instability-free) truncation boundary conditions for finite spatial extents. Here we present the first such analytical framework that performs truncation in phase space, with two distinct approaches to guarantee either the monotonicity of output distribution functions or identical truncated density profiles. We additionally present the open-source JAX-based GPU-accelerated Python package PIANISTpy (Particle Initial conditions with ANISoTropy) for constructing spherical, non-rotating initial conditions, supporting arbitrary double-power-law analytical radial density profiles and a library of analytical anisotropy profiles. This framework further permits the construction of systems with non-analytical anisotropy profiles via an arbitrary linear superposition of all implemented analytical anisotropy models as basis functions. PIANISTpy also outputs 3D velocity profiles and line-of-sight-integrated observables with end-to-end differentiability for rapid gradient-based Monte Carlo inference, and self-consistent local dark matter velocity distributions of anisotropic haloes beyond the unphysical Maxwell-Boltzmann simplification adopted in terrestrial detection experiments. We discuss use cases and demonstrate PIANISTpy's orders-of-magnitude higher numerical solver accuracy and superior dynamical stability of particle initial conditions relative to existing tools, including galpy and AGAMA.

11. Constraints on Reionization from Planck Low-$\ell$ EE Polarization and the Lyman-Series Forests[2610.28765]
Abstract

CMB measurements tightly constrain the Thomson scattering optical depth ($\tau_{\rm es}$), but they still permit a broad range of degenerate reionization histories. Incorporating Lyman-series forest constraints on the end of reionization can alleviate the degeneracy and narrow the range of allowed histories. We explore this effect using forest measurements combined with full Planck likelihoods. We apply upper limits on the neutral fraction at $4.9\lesssim z\lesssim6.9$ derived from the dark pixel fraction, as well as a lower limit at $z=5.8$ from damping-wing-like features on the edges of long dark gaps in stacked forest spectra. We find that neither the choice of parametric vs. non-parametric reionization model, nor the choice among different forest data combinations, produces a significant shift in $\tau_{\rm es}$ or the other cosmological parameters. Instead, forest constraints primarily redistribute $\tau_{\rm es}$ in redshift. Combining dark pixel, damping-wing-like, and CMB measurements, we find $z_{50}=7.7^{+0.7}_{-0.8}$ and a 95\% upper limit of $\Delta z_{90}<11.4$ for the midpoint and duration of reionization, respectively. The asymmetry of the reionization history ($A_z$) is not well constrained by these data. Stronger constraints can be obtained using Ly$\alpha$ forest measurements of the mean IGM photoionization rate, at the cost of requiring more detailed modeling. Using a new semi-analytic reionization model, we obtain (model-dependent) constraints of $z_{50} = 7.4^{+0.2}_{-0.3}$, $\Delta z_{90}=6.0^{+0.8}_{-1.0}$, and $A_z = 2.4^{+0.3}_{-0.7}$. We also find evidence that the ionizing emissivity of reionization sources declines after $z=7-8$, perhaps signaling a decline in the escape fraction and/or the onset of a feedback process. Our results motivate the development of forest likelihood products and fast forward models for its transmission statistics.

12. FAUST XXXII. Chemically layered structures and ionisation in the disk and envelope of NGC 1333 IRAS 4C[2610.28795]
Abstract

Recent molecular line observations with high spatial resolutions have revealed vertical chemical stratification in Class I/II disks (i.e. chemical composition changing with height in the vertical direction of the disk), as well as a radial variation of ionisation rates. However, such chemical layered structures in Class 0 disk and envelope systems have been investigated only for limited objects, such as L1527. In the context of the ALMA Large Program FAUST, we report the results of molecular line observations toward the disk and envelope around the low-mass Class 0 protostar NGC 1333 IRAS 4C in the Perseus Molecular Cloud. Previous observations have shown that the disk rotation axis of this object is largely tilted to the line of sight, making it suitable for studying the molecular abundance distribution as a function of disk height. We found that the molecules known as gas density tracers such as C18O and H2CO are distributed around the disk midplane. In addition, molecules known as tracers of photochemical reactions such as CCH and c-C3H2 are distributed in the low-density upper layers of the disk, unlike the distribution in L1527. Moreover, the column density ratio of HCO+/CO derived from the H13CO+ and C18O line emission suggests that the ionisation degree and the ionisation rate vary with the disk radius and they are larger in the outer region of the disk and envelope.

13. Not so Fast: Revised Calibration of SIDM Core Collapse[2610.28890]
Abstract

Self-interacting dark matter (SIDM) haloes undergo gravothermal evolution whose late-time behaviour is central to predictions for dense substructure and black-hole seed formation. We revisit the calibration of this evolution using a new spherical Direct Simulation Monte Carlo code, SphDSMC, which follows spherically symmetric SIDM systems without force softening and to substantially higher central densities than standard N-body simulations. We compare SphDSMC calculations with conventional N-body simulations and with one-dimensional gravothermal fluid models. We show that N-body results converge to SphDSMC only for softening lengths significantly smaller than those normally adopted for collisionless simulations, and that allowing multiple scatterings per particle per time step does not introduce significant errors when implemented appropriately. Calibrating the gravothermal fluid equations against SphDSMC, we find that the commonly used harmonic interpolation between the long- and short-mean-free-path conductivities is inaccurate. A gentler transition, however, yields excellent agreement for NFW haloes over a range of concentrations and truncation radii, with a single long-mean-free-path conductivity parameter. The revised calibration gives a long-mean-free-path collapse time that is somewhat shorter than previous estimates, but we show that collapse slows dramatically once the core becomes optically thick. We provide fitting functions for the late-time evolution and argue that black-hole seed formation by SIDM gravothermal collapse has generally been underestimated in duration, making it unlikely to explain the massive high-redshift black holes inferred from JWST observations without additional physics.

14. KROME-X: a GPU-ready library for chemistry and microphysics in astrophysical simulations[2610.28917]
Abstract

KROME is a Python pre-processor that writes a complete Fortran solver of chemistry and microphysics for each chemical network, widely used in astrophysical simulations. We introduce here KROME-X, a complete rewrite of KROME in modern C++, which incorporates updated physics. A code generator translates a declarative network description into a compact core (rates, right-hand side, analytic Jacobian, thermal terms, and tables), which a fixed, header-only runtime integrates with a C++ port of the `backward differentiation formula' method of CVODE (SUNDIALS). The physics is validated against legacy KROME and independent references. On the legacy test cases KROME-X reproduces legacy KROME to the accuracy of the solvers, wherever the physics is the same, and is up to 13.5 times faster at equal tolerance. On NVIDIA A100 GPUs, it can outperform its share of a computing node from a few thousand cells per call, with the highest throughput (5 to 30) obtained for $10^5$ cells per batch. Networks of hundreds of species still run faster on the CPU due to the GPU's limited memory capacity. The new version also includes, among other changes, on-the-fly co-evolution of a photon field alongside chemistry, which conserves photons exactly; the secondary ionizations using the photoelectron energy of each absorption; dust evolution in size and temperature with a true grain energy equation; line cooling rates rebuilt from current atomic and molecular data, and incorporating optical thickness effects. KROME-X therefore represents the natural successor of KROME and provides a portable, extensible framework for astrochemical kinetics on both CPUs and GPUs for future hydrodynamic simulations.

15. The H IX galaxy survey IV: What does the molecular gas reveal in HI eXtreme galaxies[2610.28970]
Abstract

Galaxies with extreme amounts of neutral atomic hydrogen (HI), known as HI-eXtreme (HIX) galaxies, paradoxically exhibit normal or low star formation rates. To investigate the bottleneck in their star formation, we present ALMA CO(1-0) observations for five HIX survey galaxies: ESO208-G026, ESO378-G003, ESO381-G005, ESO417-G018, and ESO055-G013. While these systems possess massive, extended HI discs, their molecular gas discs are compact with normal H$_{2}$ masses relative to their stellar mass. Dynamical modelling with 3DBarolo reveals that the HI and molecular gas are kinematically consistent. Both gas phases show high velocity dispersions, indicating enhanced turbulence compared to regular late-type galaxies. We calculate the specific angular momentum for the stellar, atomic, and molecular components alongside the global stability parameter ($q$). Most of the system angular momentum is stored in the HI discs, rendering them globally stable against collapse. This stability suppresses the atomic-to-molecular gas conversion, as evidenced by extremely long HI depletion times contrasted with typical H$_{2}$ depletion times. The high angular momentum and HI excess likely originate from past gas accretion or recent tidal interactions; this is especially apparent in ESO378-G003 and ESO381-G005, which exhibit extended HI structures and positive tidal indices.

16. The MAGE Project: Size Evolution and Disk Formation in Massive Galaxies at Cosmic Dawn[2610.29070]
Abstract

We investigate size evolution and disk formation with a new suite of eleven cosmological zoom-in simulations, Massive Assembly of Galaxies in the Early universe (MAGE), run with RAMSES at $\sim10\,\mathrm{pc}$ resolution until $z\simeq9$. The target galaxies span stellar masses of $M_\star\simeq2\times10^8$-$3\times10^{10}\,M_\odot$ at $z=9$. The stellar half-mass radius grows steeply with stellar mass at low masses, turns over near $M_\star\simeq5\times10^9\,M_\odot$, and evolves weakly with redshift. The intrinsic UV sizes in MAGE overlap the compact end of observed distributions, including the compact strong-line systems, but not the more extended population at $z>9$. Dust, PSF-limited size measurements, and our overdense environments may contribute to this offset. In the simulations, stars dominate the mass within the half-mass radius above $M_\star\simeq5\times10^8\,M_\odot$, where the central circular speed exceeds a characteristic stellar-feedback scale. Outflows are then increasingly retained within the galaxy, consistent with gas recycling on galactic rather than halo scales, which sustains central gas supply and drives a compaction-like central buildup. The turnover in size at $M_\star\simeq5\times10^9\,M_\odot$ coincides with rapid growth of rotational support to $v/\sigma\simeq1.5$-$3$ in star-forming gas and stars younger than 10Myr, as the central circular speed exceeds the halo virial speed, which the star-forming gas velocity dispersion tracks. The subsequent low central gas fraction, declining central star formation, and younger outskirts suggest the onset of inside-out quenching.

17. NGC 3227 Blinks: An Extreme Changing-Look Event in a Seyfert Galaxy[2610.29137]
Abstract

We report a rapid optical changing-look event in the Seyfert galaxy NGC 3227: the optical continuum faded by more than 70% and broad H-beta emission nearly disappeared within weeks, before recovering within two months. Damped random walk simulations calibrated to the source's normal variability show that such a decline is unlikely for stochastic accretion fluctuations (p = 0.002). Simultaneous Swift monitoring reveals X-ray spectral hardening, pointing to enhanced line-of-sight absorption. During the dim state, reverberation mapping yields a persistent 6-8 day lag between the continuum and broad H-beta, showing that the broad-line region continued to reverberate and placing the obscurer interior to the broad-line region. Photoionization calculations require a fully ionized, Compton-thick column with N_H   1.5 x 10^24 cm^-2 to reproduce the optical continuum suppression. Together, these observations demonstrate that compact, high-column-density obscurers interior to the broad-line region can produce extreme optical changing-look behavior on week timescales.

18. Unveiling Accretion-Disk Reverberation Beneath BLR Contamination with TILDE[2610.29173]
Abstract

We present a Bayesian framework, Two-stage Inference for Light curve DEcomposition (TILDE), for mapping the accretion disk and the broad line region (BLR) from active galactic nuclei (AGN) multi-band light curves. By inferring delayed BLR contamination from the underlying disk variability, TILDE provides the delay measurements of the disk as well as the BLR. We validate TILDE using synthetic data, finding that TILDE recovers the known time delays of the disk and BLR. By applying TILDE to the Seoul National University AGN Monitoring Project sample, we find that the inferred BLR light curves match the observed H$\beta$ emission light curves, providing consistent BLR lags compared to the reported H$\beta$ lags. Using the lag spectrum, effectively corrected for the BLR contamination, we find that the disk size is broadly consistent with the standard thin-disk model, suggesting that BLR contamination can significantly bias the disk size derived from the cross-correlation analysis of the broad-band photometry. These results demonstrate that the new inference method is a powerful tool for determining both disk and BLR sizes from broad-band photometry in the era of time-domain surveys.

19. B-fields In STar-forming Region Observations (BISTRO): Technical Foundations for Quantitative 450/850 $μ$m JCMT SCUBA-2/POL-2 Polarimetry[2610.29273]
Abstract

We present five technical validations of SCUBA-2/POL-2 polarimetry on the James Clerk Maxwell Telescope at 450 and 850 $\mu$m, using 174 archival fields in 69 molecular cloud regions and subsets appropriate to each test. Extended-source calibration avoids a deterministic shift of $\Delta\beta=-1.05$ in the dust emissivity index $\beta$ that would result from calibrating extended emission using peak brightness. We modeled Stokes $I$ noise with additive and signal-dependent components. The measured median ratio of surface-brightness noise at 450/850 $\mu$m is 25. Independent tests of Stokes $Q,U$ noise support approximately equal additive variances and weak error correlations, providing a basis for propagating polarization uncertainties. Corrections determined for individual fields reduce additive zero-level offsets of order $1$ mJy arcsec$^{-2}$ in the 450 $\mu$m Stokes $Q$ and $U$ intensities. The three-step regression leaves a median combined residual amplitude of $0.07$ mJy arcsec$^{-2}$. Independent comparisons with SOFIA/HAWC+ at 154 and 214 $\mu$m support the correction in strongly affected fields. We matched the 450 $\mu$m maps to the modeled 850 $\mu$m beam and the common $12.6''$-$300''$ range of angular scales. We compared three reductions of the same 450 $\mu$m observations across 30 sources. Taking the median angular residual relative to the final product for each source, we find that beam matching reduces the median over sources from $6.3^\circ$ to $4.7^\circ$. After matching, 29 of the 30 sources have median residuals below $10^\circ$. We also quantified how residual $Q/U$ offsets propagate in ratios of polarization fractions, differences in spectral indices, and polarization angles. These validations provide procedures and quantitative references for interpreting POL-2 measurements and combining them with polarimetry at other wavelengths.

20. B-fields In STar-forming Region Observations (BISTRO): Dual-Wavelength Constraints on Size-Dependent Grain Alignment in Star-Forming Regions[2610.29285]
Abstract

We compared 450 and 850 $\mu$m POL-2 observations from the James Clerk Maxwell Telescope at $12.6''$ resolution. The median absolute difference in polarization angle between bands is $6.7^\circ$ across 21 regions. For total intensity $I\propto\nu^\alpha$ and polarized intensity $PI\propto\nu^\gamma$, 24-27 of 35 regions have $\gamma<\alpha$, allowing for $3\sigma$ limits on weak polarized intensities. The median $\gamma-\alpha$ is $-0.51$, ranging from $-0.57$ to $-0.45$ under these limits. The ratio of polarization fractions is $P_{450}/P_{850}=0.69\pm0.02_{\rm sys}\pm0.03_{\rm stat}$, with no detected dependence on the angle difference. Recent JWST observations suggest that galaxies at z > 9 may be dominated by low-opacity SNe-produced dust before efficient ISM grain growth is established. This transition in dust origin and opacity could explain both the prevalence of galaxies with extremely low dust attenuation and the excess of UV-bright galaxies relative to most pre-JWST predictions. We investigate whether this transition, combined with evolving star-formation efficiency, can reproduce these observed properties. We develop a physically motivated attenuation framework combining (i) extinction laws for reverse-shock-processed SNe dust, (ii) metallicity- and dust-to-metal-dependent opacity scalings, and (iii) porous radiative-transfer geometries allowing partial UV-photon leakage. Unlike outflow-driven scenarios requiring large-scale gas evacuation, our approach preserves gas reservoirs while reducing effective UV opacity through dust composition and geometry. We introduce extinction-based, gas-based, and hybrid attenuation prescriptions linking SNe-dominated and ISM grain-growth dust regimes. We find that the observed A_FUV-M_star relation at z > 9 is best reproduced for an intrinsic FUV dust opacity kappa_UV(dust)=10^3 - 10^4 cm2/g, characteristic of low-opacity SNe dust, naturally producing very low attenuation even in gas-rich galaxies. This regime reproduces galaxies with extremely low dust attenuation (GELDAs), which dominate observed samples at z > 9. Applied to intrinsic UV luminosity function models, our SNe-dominated and hybrid prescriptions mainly suppress the brightest galaxies, bringing predictions into agreement with JWST measurements without requiring extreme star-formation efficiencies or dust-free interstellar media. Our results suggest that the UV-bright galaxy excess at z > 9 reflects a transition in dust origin and opacity during the earliest phases of galaxy evolution.

43. The role of major mergers in triggering super-Eddington accretion[2606.04081]
Abstract

JWST observations have opened a new era in the exploration of the high-redshift Universe, revealing black holes (BHs) with masses of several million solar masses already at $z>8$, challenging our understanding of their growth mechanisms. In this context, super-Eddington (SE) accretion has emerged as a promising solution and has been widely adopted in both numerical simulations and semi-analytical models. In this work, we investigate whether a major merger between two relatively low-mass halos ($M_{\rm halo}\sim10^9\,\mathrm{M_\odot}$) at high redshift can trigger episodes of sustained SE accretion, with particular focus on the role of BH feedback. We employ state-of-the-art, high-resolution cosmological zoom-in simulations of a major merger at $z\sim11$. We explore different prescriptions for BH seeding and feedback, including physically motivated radiative and kinetic models (winds and jets) across the three main accretion regimes: advection-dominated accretion flows (ADAF), radiatively efficient sub-Eddington accretion, and SE accretion. We find that, although the merger drives gas inflows towards the central regions, this is not sufficient to trigger sustained SE accretion. Post-merger SE accretion episodes are observed only when BH feedback is entirely switched off. Amongst the feedback channels considered, kinetic feedback is the primary mechanism regulating BH growth. Moreover, the only significant SE accretion episodes occur immediately after BH seeding, while the merger itself does not produce a substantial enhancement of the accretion rate.

Abstract

Abstract

Abstract
