1. Impact of Gaia-Sausage-Enceladus merger revealed by a single cosmic clock: thick-to-thin disc transition, gas disc shrinking and bar formation[2610.30237]
Abstract

Whether the formation of the Galactic bar was connected to the Gaia-Sausage-Enceladus (GSE) merger, the last significant merger experienced by the Milky Way, remains an open question. The bar formation epoch has been inferred from the star formation history of the nuclear stellar disc (NSD) traced by Mira variables, and the GSE merger epoch from the age-metallicity relation of red giants. However, these epochs rest on different stellar tracers with different age scales and cannot be compared directly. We recalibrate the Mira period-age relation onto the age scale of APOGEE red giants, with ages from a Bayesian neural network trained on Kepler asteroseismic ages. We fit the Gaia DR3 proper motions of 2394 O-rich disc Mira variables with an action-based quasi-isothermal distribution function whose parameters vary with period, and derive the period-age relation by matching their vertical velocity dispersion as a function of period to that of the APOGEE red giants as a function of age in Funakoshi et al. (2025). This places the NSD star-formation rise and peak at 8.5-10.5 Gyr ago on the red giant age scale, overlapping with the final stage of the GSE-induced starburst, thick-to-thin disc transition and the contraction of the star-forming gas disc. If NSD star formation traces bar formation, these coincidences suggest a possible scenario in which the GSE seeded the bar formation, while fresh gas supplied by the GSE and outer disc gas driven inward by its tidal torques, increased the baryonic dominance of the inner Galaxy.

2. The deepest X-ray view of a kiloparsec scale jet at z=6.1[2610.30259]
Abstract

The mechanism responsible for the high-energy emission observed in kiloparsec-scale jets across cosmic time remains poorly understood. The relativistic jet associated with the z=6.1 AGN PSO J030947.49+271757.31 is the highest-redshift large-scale jet currently resolved in X-rays, providing a unique opportunity to study these structures. We present new Chandra observations, combined with archival data, for a total exposure of 360 ks. The increased exposure allowed us to resolve the extended X-ray emission into two distinct, slightly resolved components and perform spatially resolved spectral analyses. The extended emission has a photon index of Gamma = 2.25 (+0.45, -0.44), steeper than that of the core (Gamma = 1.86 +/- 0.09), and an extended-to-core luminosity ratio of 18%. Simulations using the Chandra image as input show that an AXIS-like mission could increase the detected photon statistics by a factor of approximately 17. We resolve the kiloparsec-scale jet into two main components and constrain its spectral properties. This is the most distant and one of the most X-ray-luminous large-scale jets currently known. Future X-ray missions with arcsecond-scale angular resolution could enable more detailed studies of such systems and reveal a population of fainter high-redshift jets beyond Chandra's reach.

3. Charting the Galactic Underworld II: Strategies for Identifying BHs and NSs with LISA[2610.30262]
Abstract

Stellar-mass black holes (BHs) and neutron stars (NSs) observed by space-based gravitational-wave detectors offer constraints on binary stellar evolution that are complementary to those from ground- based detectors. The Laser Interferometer Space Antenna (LISA) is expected to detect hundreds of double compact objects (DCOs) containing a BH or NS, but distinguishing these sources from the far more numerous double white dwarfs (WDWDs) may prove challenging. We use adaptive importance sampling with COSMIC to synthesise large populations of BHBH, BHNS, NSNS, BHWD, and NSWD binaries. We combine these binaries with a Milky Way star-formation history and integrate their Galactic orbits with cogsworth, repeating our simulations for a grid of variations in uncertain (binary) stellar physics. We find that LISA detections are largely insensitive to the changes in Galactic position resulting from natal kicks. We then assess how well chirp mass, eccentricity, distance from the Galactic plane, and orbital frequency distinguish BH- and NS-hosting DCOs from WDWDs, accounting for LISA measurement uncertainties. Using chirp mass alone distinguishes only 50-60% of BHBHs and BHNSs, and  40% of NSNSs, but combining this with orbital frequency recovers 90-100% of BHBHs and BHNSs and around  50% of NSNSs, with eccentricity further improving the recovery of NSNSs to around 60-70%. Distance from the Galactic plane adds little discriminating power, though it can recover a handful of NSWDs that are missed otherwise. These methods will help identify a significantly larger number of BHs and NSs in LISA data that would be missed by a chirp mass cut alone.

4. AGB-star Mass Transfer Products in Stellar Streams as Tracers of Density in Dissolved Star Clusters[2610.30264]
Abstract

The least dense globular clusters (GCs) appear to host the highest fractions of stars with chemical signatures of mass transfer from asymptotic giant branch (AGB) companions. Recent observations suggest the fraction may be even higher in stellar streams that are debris of tidally disrupted (preferentially low-density) GCs. We explore whether this is attributable to lower encounter rates in less-dense GCs allowing more of the relevant binaries to survive dynamical hardening, or outright disruption, long enough to undergo AGB mass transfer. We run a small suite of GC simulations varying initial density using the code CMC with its latest observationally motivated binary prescriptions. Using CMC's built-in COSMIC code for binary stellar evolution, we first identify primordial binaries that would, if left unperturbed, likely undergo AGB mass transfer via either standard or wind Roche-lobe overflow. We then check how many of those binaries survive GC dynamics in CMC long enough to experience AGB mass transfer. We find that wind Roche-lobe overflow is necessary to reproduce the fraction of (sub)giant stars in Galactic GCs that are likely products of AGB mass transfer ( 1%) and that binary dynamics plausibly explain why this fraction appears lower in dense, intact GCs than stellar streams. The abundance of mass-transfer products (also collision products and binaries) in upcoming surveys of stellar streams without known progenitors will thus probe the densities of dissolved GCs—difficult to determine otherwise but key to improved stream modeling and predicted rates of many astrophysical transients.

5. The THESAN-ZOOM project: the delayed rise of Lyman continuum escape fractions in bursty high-redshift galaxies[2610.30265]
Abstract

The ionizing radiation that galaxies release into their surroundings depends on whether efficient Lyman continuum (LyC) escape coincides with strong photon production. Stellar feedback can open escape paths as the source population fades, linking the escaping radiation to a galaxy's recent history. We investigate this timing in thirteen high-redshift (z > 3) target haloes from the THESAN-ZOOM radiation-hydrodynamic simulations, post-processed with COLT radiative transfer and sampled at  10 Myr intervals. The clearest delayed relationship links $\text{SFR}_{10}/\text{SFR}_{100}$ to the LyC escape fraction. Selecting the nearest positive cross-correlation peak gives typical lags of  16 Myr and peak correlations of 0.4-0.6 in massive haloes, with longer and less coherent delays towards lower masses. The fitted delays scale approximately as $M_\star^{-0.19}$ and $M_\text{halo}^{-0.27}$, although the shortest lags approach the native snapshot spacing. Recurrent variations span multiple amplitude-dependent timescales, consistent with repeated episodes of star formation and feedback. Recent star formation closely tracks both intrinsic and escaping photon rates, while the escape fraction reflects the evolving gas transparency. The H$\alpha$-to-UV luminosity ratio retains a delayed association with escape, offering an observable connection between burst phase and leakage. High transparency during a fading phase can nevertheless yield little escaping radiation. The temporal overlap of photon production and transmission therefore shapes both the amount and timing of escaping radiation, informing the interpretation of observed leakers and their contribution to cosmic reionization.

6. Gone Without a Bang: A Search for Failed Core-Collapse Supernovae in ZTF and Predictions for Rubin and Roman[2610.30276]
Abstract

The core-collapse of massive stars (${\geq}10\ \rm M_\odot$) usually takes the form of a supernova, but in some cases may lead to a direct collapse, leading to a failed core-collapse supernova (FccSN). Wide-field time-domain surveys, such as the Zwicky Transient Facility (ZTF), are a useful tool to find such “disappearing" stars. We use a piecewise linear function model to search the entire ZTF optical photometric catalog for FccSNe, yielding 125 “faders" that dip significantly with a flat top and bottom flux. Although no candidates were definitively found, some of the sources exhibit promising behavior that may indicate a direct collapse event. Assuming a null result from the search, we calculate at 99\% credibility that the maximum statistical rate of FccSNe in the Milky Way is 147 per century. To contextualize these findings, we also simulate the formation of stellar populations in Milky Way-like galaxies to estimate the expected number of FccSNe observable by ZTF. This simulation predicts ZTF to observe $0.234\pm0.006$ FccSNe per century, which aligns with the lack of definitive observations in ZTF. However, we predict that similar monitoring using the Legacy Survey of Space and Time (LSST) and the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope's Wide Field Instrument (WFI) could enable population-level studies of nearby FccSNe.

7. The black hole-galaxy correlations of the 12 micron local AGN sample: a NuSTAR perspective[2610.30285]
Abstract

The M-sigma correlation of inactive galaxies plays a crucial role in estimating the black hole mass (MBH) in active galactic nuclei (AGN): 1) it is used to calibrate the reverberation mapping (RM) method; 2) the RM is the basis of the single epoch method, the most-used technique to determine MBH of broad-line AGN; and 3) in narrow-line AGN, MBH is often directly inferred from the M-sigma correlation. The main goal of this work is to test whether the M-sigma relation calibrated from inactive galaxies can be reliably applied to a local AGN population spanning Seyfert 1, Seyfert 2, and hidden broad-line region galaxies (HBLRs), and whether the resulting AGN relation has sufficient predictive power to be used as an indirect MBH estimator. To investigate this hypothesis, we used the 12 micron AGN sample, a local representative and largely unbiased sample. Since a substantial fraction of this sample has good-quality NuSTAR data, we estimated their MBH with the X-ray scaling method, which works for any AGN accreting at moderate or high level. We find that the M-sigma correlation of the 12 micron AGN sample is different from the one obtained with inactive galaxies and has a substantial scatter. Therefore, its predictive power for MBH estimates is fairly limited. We also find that the MBH-Mgal correlation based on the 12 micron AGN sample is consistent with the analogous correlation obtained from a large sample of nearby type 1 AGN and inconsistent with that of inactive galaxies. Finally, we find suggestive evidence that Seyfert 1 and HBLRs have similar and systematically higher Eddington ratios than Seyfert 2 galaxies.

8. Nuclear Millimeter Continuum Emission in Radio-Quiet Active Galactic Nuclei: an ALMA Spectral and Variability Study[2610.30286]
Abstract

Unresolved millimeter (mm) emission is observed in radio-quiet (RQ) Active Galactic Nuclei (AGN), but its physical origin remains debated. Self-absorbed synchrotron emission from the compact X-ray corona is one of the leading candidates. Previous support for this coronal synchrotron origin has mainly been based on variability and spectral indices in a few RQ AGN, with a lack of (quasi-)simultaneous broadband data for larger samples. We present Atacama Large Millimeter/sub-millimeter Array (ALMA) observations of nine nearby ($z \sim 0.008-0.064$) RQ AGN, obtained quasi-simultaneously at 100, 145, and 233 GHz. Based on power-law fits to the spectral energy distributions, we measure millimeter spectral indices ranging from $-0.05^{+0.09}_{-0.09}$ to $0.88^{+0.12}_{-0.11}$. Furthermore, across two 100 GHz observing epochs per source, separated by $4-11$ days, we detect flux density variability of $30-56$% (>$3\sigma$ significance level) in three out of nine sources. Spectral slopes alone are insufficient to constrain the origin of the millimeter emission; however, the observed short-timescale variability and inferred brightness temperature values are consistent with a non-thermal emission mechanism arising from a compact emitting region with a size of $R_{\rm mm}< 10^3 - 10^4 R_{\rm g}$, such as the corona or a compact jet. Furthermore, we find a clear correlation between the 100/233 GHz and X-ray flux in our sample, and establish a new linear correlation between 145 GHz and X-ray emission.

9. The LBT Y$_{\rm p}$ Project VI: Gas-Phase Chemical Abundance Patterns of Metal-Poor Nebulae[2610.30289]
Abstract

Through a combined rest-optical and NIR observing campaign, the Large Binocular Telescope Primordial He Abundance ($Y_{\rm p}$) Project has delivered a precision measurement of $Y_{\rm p}$. Given the stringent requirements to measure gas-phase He abundances, the optical spectra contain emission features from a plethora of metals in the Interstellar Medium (ISM). We present gas-phase N, O, Ne, S, Cl, Ar, K and Fe abundance patterns in 62 low-metallicity nebulae. We assess ionization correction factors for each element and select those that reproduce the observed ionic abundance trends. The Ne/O, S/O, and Ar/O abundances do not evolve with metallicity and agree with the solar ratios. Given the Type Ia enrichment of S and Ar, the solar S/O and Ar/O suggest extended star formation histories in local galaxies relative to high-$z$ galaxies at fixed O/H. We report the first ISM K abundances in low-metallicity extragalactic nebulae; both Cl/O and K/O are constant with O/H, indicating the production of these odd-Z elements follows that of $\alpha$ elements. Consistent with prior analyses, gas-phase Fe/O is observed to decrease with O/H due to dust depletion; correcting for this produces an average [Fe/O]$=$$-$0.80, implying Fe-deficient stars capable of producing hard ionizing spectra. Finally, the metallicity-insensitive log(N/O) plateau from 50 nebulae is $-$1.43$\pm$0.10, consistent with previous works. Five galaxies have N/O significantly enhanced relative to the primary plateau, and we discuss their properties, mechanisms that could bias measurements of N/O, and nucleosynthetic progenitors that can produce enhanced N at low metallicity.

10. The PAU Survey: Active galactic nucleus galaxies in galaxy pairs[2610.30309]
Abstract

Aims. Leveraging the high-precision photometric redshifts of the Physics of the Accelerating Universe Survey (PAUS), we aim to investigate the connection between active galactic nuclei (AGNs) and galaxy pairs up to redshifts of $\sim1.0$. Methods. We identified 16,824 galaxy pairs with projected separations of $r_p < 100$ kpc and radial-velocity differences of $\Delta V < 3500$ km s$^{-1}$. AGNs were selected by cross-matching PAUS galaxies with multi-wavelength AGN catalogues, yielding 270 AGNs in pairs out of a parent sample of 8,880 AGNs in PAUS. We constructed control samples of isolated AGNs; non-AGNs in pairs; and isolated non-AGNs matched in redshift, absolute magnitude, and environment. Results. Active galactic nuclei in pairs show progressively redder rest-frame colours with increasing redshift, particularly at redshifts of $z\gtrsim 0.5$, whereas isolated AGNs and non-AGNs in pairs show no significant colour evolution and remain at $\mathrm{M_g-M_r}\sim0.65$. Pairs hosting AGNs are also preferentially morphologically disturbed interacting systems found at smaller projected separations and velocity offsets, consistent with more dynamically advanced interactions. Conclusions. These results provide evidence of a redshift-dependent link between galaxy interactions and AGN activity, suggesting that interactions play an increasingly important role in triggering nuclear activity at intermediate redshifts. This study highlights the unique capability of PAUS to probe AGN-galaxy interaction connections beyond the local Universe with unprecedented statistical power.

11. Optical-Infrared Extinction Variation in Nearby Molecular Clouds[2610.30336]
Abstract

The dust extinction curve is an important probe of the properties of dust grains, particularly their size distribution. The properties of dust grains are expected to change significantly in dense molecular clouds due to shielding from interstellar radiation and increased frequency of grain-grain and grain-gas collisions. We measure the dust extinction curve shape parameter R(V) in 9 nearby, dense molecular clouds using data from the APOGEE spectroscopic survey, in combination with ten-band photometry from PS1, 2MASS, and WISE. We analyze the results to determine how the extinction curve varies in nearby molecular clouds. We find that most molecular clouds show a positive correlation between R(V) and E(B-V), but the strength of the correlation varies significantly between clouds. In addition, we find that the clouds with the strongest correlation tend to have very high dispersion in R(V) at fixed E(B-V), which tightens dramatically when clouds are subdivided into separate regions. This suggests that local environmental conditions beyond density shape the R(V)-E(B-V) relation.

12. Optical and near-infrared s-process abundance scales in the Galactic disc[2610.30346]
Abstract

A discrepancy has recently emerged between optical and near-infrared (NIR) s-process abundance scales, particularly for Neodymium (Nd). We investigated this issue using a direct cross-match between the optical Gaia-ESO Survey (DR5) and NIR SDSS-V/MWM (DR19). While atmospheric parameters and Cerium (Ce) abundances exhibit robust 1:1 correlations, Nd displays a systematic X-shaped vertical stretching. Our analysis reveals that the SDSS-V pipeline expands intrinsic Nd variations by a scaling factor of 5.54 relative to the stable optical baseline. This phenomenon is driven by baseline compression in the APOGEE H-band, where the growth of the molecular forest (CN and CO) at high metallicities depresses the local continuum. This forces the chi^2 minimisation engine in the NIR abundance determination to inflate derived abundances to reach mathematical convergence. We provide an empirical calibration framework to reconcile these disparate regimes. This correction effectively eliminates the contradictory negative radial [Nd/Fe] gradient of -0.052 dex/kpc reported in recent NIR surveys, bringing them into alignment with the rising trends established by the optical high-resolution Gaia-ESO survey. These results demonstrate that the observed scale expansion is a spectroscopic artefact of line-blanketing rather than a physical Galactic trend.

13. JWST observations of the planetary nebula NGC 6302 IV: PAH spectral characteristics, formation and evolution[2610.30441]
Abstract

Polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs) and carbonaceous dust have been observed in Oxygen-rich planetary nebulae (PNe), yet their formation and evolution in these environments is an open question. We aim to study the formation and evolution of the PAHs in the O-rich PN NGC 6302, by characterizing the PAH emission throughout this object. We obtained JWST MIRI IFU spectroscopic observations of NGC 6302 pointed at its central source. From these, we extract four template spectra that are representative of the variation in PAH emission throughout NGC 6302. We detect the major PAH features at 6.2, 7.7, 8.6, 11.2, and 12.7 $\mu$m, and several minor PAH features. The template spectra vary in terms of their PAH feature widths, peak wavelengths, and relative intensities. The 11.2 $\mu$m PAH feature is class B, typical for PNe. The 6-9 $\mu$m PAH features are class A, which is less common for PNe. We estimate the PAH charge to be 75% cations on average, based on the 7.7/11.2 PAH feature ratio. The relative strengths of the 11-15 $\mu$m PAH features indicate a range of PAH edge structures. To ensure consistency in our observations and explain variable PAH feature widths, we propose a variation in the size of the PAHs throughout NGC 6302, and suggest variation in the shielding of these PAHs. The PAHs range from large and non-compact, to small and compact. We find that NGC 6302 formed PAHs in-situ in the current PN phase, likely from small hydrocarbons formed by the photodissociation of CO.

14. A Potential New Dimension of Astrophysical Aromatic Functionalization: Phosphaethynyl (CP) Derivatives[2610.30509]
Abstract

Quantum chemical computations outline a thermodynamically viable, low-temperature, gas-phase formation pathway for phosphaethynyl cyclopropenylidene ($c$-C$_3$HCP), resulting from cyclopropenylidene ($c$-C$_3$H$_2$) and the phosphaethynyl (CP) radical. Given the detections of both $c$-C$_3$H$_2$ and the CP radical toward circumstellar shells along with the provided reaction pathway, $c$-C$_3$HCP is a potentially present astro-molecule which could be searched for in phosphorus rich environments. The reaction of CP and benzene (C$_6$H$_6$) to produce phosphaethynyl benzene (C$_6$H$_5$CP) is also explored. A marginally emerged, but tentative, transition state is identified for this reaction using certain quantum chemical approaches but not others. Consequently, the formation of CP-substituted benzoid polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs) in astrophysical environments from CP + PAH reactions is possible but currently uncertain. Additionally, explicitly correlated coupled cluster quartic force field and second-order vibrational perturbation theory rovibrational analysis of the vibrational frequencies and rotational constants of $c$-C$_3$HCP are provided, as well as density functional theory predictions of the same for CP-benzene (C$_6$H$_5$CP) and both isomers of CP-naphthalene (C$_1$$_0$H$_7$CP). Our results suggest that IR searches for these molecules would likely be unsuccessful due to the low intensities and coincidence with PAH features, but their large dipole moments could make them feasible to detect with radio astronomy. The barrierless formation of $c$-C$_3$HCP and possibly barrierless synthesis of C$_6$H$_5$CP stand as examples for future investigations of other CP-substituted aromatic molecules, marking the potential beginning of a new frontier of astronomical aromatic functionalization.

16. Multiwavelength Classification of Chandra Sources in Omega Centauri[2610.30604]
Abstract

We present a comprehensive study of X-ray sources in the globular cluster NGC 5139 to date. Over 200 Chandra sources are cross-matched to multiwavelength catalogs from HST, JWST, and MeerKAT. We employ a machine-learning classification pipeline (MUWCLASS) for the first time in such a crowded stellar environment and introduce the globular-cluster-specific training dataset, which compiles previously classified sources from 25 Galactic globular clusters. Given the challenges presented by the high-density environment, we compare the MUWCLASS results with traditional classification methods. We confirm counterpart associations suggested in previous works and also identify new confident and candidate associations. We discuss the implications of our classification results in light of models of cluster dynamical evolution and binary formation/destruction.

17. The Milky Way's Early Disk Was Built in Bursts. II. The Multi-component Age-Metallicity Structure of the High-$α$ Disk[2610.30659]
Abstract

Whether the high-$\alpha$ disk experienced a continuous or episodic star-formation history remains an open question. Here we investigate the age–metallicity distribution of the high-$\alpha$ disk using approximately 145,000 Gaia DR3 subgiants with $[\mathrm{Fe/H}]>-1$, 6.5 kpc $\lesssim R\lesssim9.5$ kpc, and $|Z|\lesssim1.5$ kpc. We adopt a forward-modelling framework that accounts for individual age and metallicity uncertainties and corrects for sample selection effects. The reconstructed distribution reveals two components: a broad, dominant population peaking at $\sim10.6$ Gyr and $[\mathrm{Fe/H}]\simeq-0.38$, and an older, more metal-poor population peaking at $\sim12.4$ Gyr and $[\mathrm{Fe/H}]\simeq-0.79$. These features suggest an episodic history of star formation and chemical enrichment in the high-$\alpha$ disk, qualitatively consistent with cosmological simulations in which recurrent gas accretion drives variations in the star formation rate. The age of the older component is close to the $\sim12.3$ Gyr epoch inferred for the proposed Low-energy–Kraken–Heracles accretion event, suggesting a possible connection between this early interaction and enhanced disk star formation. There is tentative evidence that the dominant population is possibly composed of two sub-components with age distributions peaking at $\sim11.1$ Gyr and $\sim10.0$ Gyr, respectively. Together, these results favour a picture in which the high-$\alpha$ disk was built through recurrent episodes of enhanced star formation rather than a single temporally smooth phase, even for the regime of ${\rm [Fe/H]}>-1$.

18. A 13 Year Black Hole Microlensing Survey in Omega Centauri[2610.30673]
Abstract

Star clusters are born with massive stars that leave behind a large population of stellar mass black holes. These black holes may account for a large fraction of binary black hole mergers seen in gravitational waves as the dense environment enables retention of black holes and the formation of binaries. However, direct measurements of stellar mass black holes in globular clusters are limited, with just a few found in binary systems thus far. In this paper, we examine the possibility of detecting black holes through microlensing in the Milky Way's most massive star cluster, Omega Centauri. We take advantage of a unique dataset of nearly 200 single-band images taken over 13 years with the Hubble Space Telescope to look for the $\sim$1 year long events expected from microlensing of cluster stars by stellar mass black holes. We develop a novel cost function to fit the lensing signatures that we verify using synthetic events, quantifying our completeness. These synthetic events are based on a recent Monte Carlo simulation of Omega Centauri. We also use these synthetic events to train a neural network to most efficiently distinguish observed lensing events. Application of this algorithm to our observed data, and subsequent visual follow-up, show no convincing candidate black hole lensing events. Based on analysis of the simulation data and our completeness, we find that no events would be expected in the data contrary to the expectations from earlier work. We release the photometric dataset used in this work for future use.

19. Lomb-Scargle Recovery of Periodic Binary Signals across Black Hole Mass and Cadence[2610.30772]
Abstract

Searches for periodic supermassive black hole binaries in quasar light curves rely on recovery fractions, the share of injected signals detected, which forecasts use as detection efficiencies. Lin, Charisi, & Haiman (2026) measured them for the Lomb-Scargle periodogram on damped-random-walk light curves matched to 12,400 bright, high-mass Palomar Transient Factory (PTF) quasars with Sloan Digital Sky Survey Data Release 7 (DR7) masses. To test them over four decades of mass, we inject their sinusoidal and sawtooth signals into light curves of mergers in the TNG100-1 cosmological simulation, with magnitudes drawn from DR7 quasars of similar mass (extrapolated at low mass). We apply their threshold in each frequency bin, set by $10^5$ noise-only light curves per binary, on observed PTF cadences, one Stripe 82 cadence and an LSST-like cadence (Legacy Survey of Space and Time). At fixed absolute magnitude $M_i=-23$, sinusoid recovery falls toward high mass, and with DR7 magnitudes it climbs above about $10^8\,M_\odot$, so the magnitude assignment sets the sign of the trend. Above $10^8\,M_\odot$, where their sample lies, PTF sinusoid recovery is 39.8 against their 44.7 per cent. At LSST-like cadence sinusoid recovery is 76.9 per cent, about three times their 22.6 per cent, for reasons mostly unidentified. Denser sampling of the same PTF baselines did not lower recovery in any test. Even at LSST-like cadence we miss most sawtooth signals above $10^8\,M_\odot$. We tabulate recovery per 0.5-dex mass bin and cadence, with low-mass values likely too high for a deeper survey.

20. The ALMA-QUARKS survey: A Keplerian Circumbinary Disk Candidate in the High-mass Star-forming Region IRAS 08303-4303[2610.30811]
Abstract

We present the ALMA Band 6 continuum and molecular line observations of IRAS 08303-4303, a high-mass star-forming region, with an angular resolution of $\sim 0.3''$ ($\sim 700$ au at the distance of 2.35 kpc). Six dense condensations are identified in the 1.3 mm continuum, and at least four of them are associated with molecular outflows. The estimated outflow parameters, particularly mass outflow rates $\geq 10^{-4}$ $M_{\odot}$ yr$^{-1}$, suggest that the driving sources are high-mass young stellar objects. Two of the condensations are forming a wide protobinary system with a separation of $\sim 900$ au. They reside within a single rotating structure, identified as a circumbinary disk which has a mass of about 2 $M_{\odot}$. This structure is traced by multiple molecular lines and exhibits a Keplerian rotation pattern. Based on the Keplerian rotation assumption, the enclosed mass is derived to be between 32 and 61 $M_{\odot}$: 22-43 $M_{\odot}$ for the primary and 10-18 $M_{\odot}$ for the secondary. The circumbinary disk is on the whole gravitationally stable, yet some parts with Toomre $Q < 2$ indicate potential fragmentation. Various molecular lines reveal a streamer-like structure connecting to the circumbinary disk, suggesting transfer of material from the envelope to the protobinary system through it. Despite the observational rarity of such circumbinary disks around high-mass YSOs, the high binary fraction among massive stars underscores their significance. This source, therefore, serves as a key laboratory for understanding the formation of high-mass binary systems.

21. A recurring changing state AGN discovered in the SAMI Galaxy Survey and SDSS[2610.30816]
Abstract

We present a detailed analysis of the AGN in galaxy J085337.26+014303.6 at z=0.0582. A combination of SDSS and SAMI Galaxy Survey data shows that this object has gone through at least one full changing-look AGN cycle, from type 1.9 in 2001 to type 1 in 2015 and 2021, and then type 1.9 again in 2022. While the broad lines in this object show evidence of dust obscuration, detailed spectral analysis plus WISE photometry demonstrate that the changes in this object are related to a physical state change, not changing obscuration. In fact, the WISE light curve suggests that this object may have briefly returned to a type 1.9 state in 2020. In the optical, the host galaxy dominates the light (e.g. only  10 per cent contribution from the AGN at 4000 Angstoms within a central aperture), even in the high state. In contrast, the brightest WISE W2-band measurements place the AGN at a similar luminosity to the entire host galaxy. The broad Balmer lines show evidence of multiple components, with a weak very-broad component (FWHM=5000-6000 km/s) visible in the low type 1.9 state, and a stronger broad component of FWHM 2000 km/s dominating in the high type 1 state. We propose that this is caused by two spatially distinct regions within the broad line region.

22. Discovering supernovae in the Virgo Cluster during the LSST era[2610.30840]
Abstract

69. Efficient Eccentric Effective-One-Body Dynamics via Near-Identity Averaging Transformations[2606.30594]
Abstract

Next-generation gravitational-wave detectors, such as LISA, the Einstein Telescope, and Cosmic Explorer, will require accurate and efficient models of long-lived black hole binary signals, including those with significant eccentricity. A challenge for eccentric effective-one-body models is the cost of resolving rapidly oscillating orbital dynamics over the inspiral, particularly for low-mass and large-mass-ratio systems. We address this by recasting the nonspinning eccentric effective-one-body equations of motion in terms of osculating orbital elements and then applying near-identity averaging transformations to eliminate the fast orbital-timescale structure during the inspiral. Each order in this procedure suppresses the oscillatory behavior by one factor of the ratio of orbital to radiation-reaction timescales. The resulting averaged dynamics are evolved on the radiation-reaction timescale, and then the system is mapped back to the full EOB dynamics for the final transition to plunge. This reduces the cost of the inspiral dynamics by up to two orders of magnitude, eliminating this as the primary bottleneck for long waveforms. The overall waveform-generation speedup spans $1.5 - 8 \times$, motivating the development of more efficient waveform-generation methods. We also validate the accuracy of the method by comparing waveforms generated from the averaged and full effective-one-body dynamics across a broad region of parameter space for moderate to large eccentricities. Carrying out this averaging procedure to next-to-next-to-leading order is needed to accurately model comparable-mass binaries, yielding mismatches $\leq 8.05 \times 10^{-5}$. These results establish near-identity averaging as a practical route to efficient eccentric effective-one-body inspirals, and provide a foundation for further extensions to low-eccentricity and spinning waveform models.

70. Automated identification and subtraction of gravitational-wave glitches using boundary refinement[2608.29295]
Abstract

Transient noise artifacts, or glitches, in gravitational wave strain data elevate the false alarm rate of astrophysical searches and degrade parameter estimation when overlapping a signal. No method previously identified a glitch's time boundary: existing detection and classification tools flag and label glitches without resolving their extent, forcing subtraction to run over padded windows that cost time and erase signal beyond the glitch itself. We present three boundary identification methods, AMPS (Amplitude-based Multi-glitch Pulse Segmenter), FLARE (Fitness-based Localization And Refinement Extractor), and CRISP (connected-region identification via spectrogram power), paired with three subtraction techniques, adaptive spline fitting, wavelet shrinkage, and their combination, across five glitches from the GravitySpy database spanning Advanced LIGO's first three observing runs. AMPS sets a boundary from a robust amplitude threshold, FLARE from the best fitness of a segmented spline fit, and CRISP from spectrogram power. Injecting a chirp signal on four broadband glitches, the combined technique recovers 95 to 97 percent of the injected signal-to-noise ratio, against roughly 64 percent for wavelet shrinkage alone. CRISP gives the most uniform boundary width across glitches; AMPS and CRISP both identify a boundary in a fraction of a second, two to three orders of magnitude faster than FLARE. For the glitch overlapping GW170817, a low-frequency residual persists under the default boundary, a deliberate tradeoff against removing signal power. Scattered light glitches remain untested and are the primary direction for future work.

71. Do Tabular Foundation Models Know Physics? Contamination, Units, and the Deterministic Limit[2609.02766]
Abstract

Tabular foundation models (TFMs) learn to fill in tables the way language models fill in text — and tables are arguably the format in which most physical measurement arrives. Did they learn any physics in the process? They are Bayesian by construction, so the question is what their prior contains. We probe it directly, evaluating four of them ($\texttt{TabPFN-3}$, $\texttt{TabICLv2}$, $\texttt{TabDPT}$ and $\texttt{Real-TabPFN-2.5}$) against six baselines on datasets sampled from 316 physical equations, in and out of domain. TFMs dominate, out of the box and after tuning. But they struggle to represent noiseless deterministic mechanisms in their uncertainty, and show no sign of exploiting physical units: they interpolate physics without yet being able to act as physical models.
