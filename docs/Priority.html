1. MAHA-z: A Mahalanobis distance kNN estimator for photometric redshifts[2611.14582]
Abstract

We present \texttt{MAHA-z}, a photometric-redshift (photo-$z$) estimator that combines $k$-nearest neighbour ($k$NN) regression with a Mahalanobis distance metric learned from the covariance of a spectroscopic reference sample. For each target galaxy, \texttt{MAHA-z} performs a neighbour search in a colour-magnitude feature space with optional morphological parameters, and returns both a point estimate and a redshift posterior $p(z\mid\mathbf{f})$ obtained by kernel density estimation over the neighbour redshifts. The Mahalanobis metric accounts for correlated features and heterogeneous units, eliminating ad hoc normalisation, and its subspace restriction allows \texttt{MAHA-z} to handle missing bands on a per-galaxy basis without imputation. We validate the method on four datasets spanning simulations and survey photometry: the Euclid Flagship 2 mock, the Legacy-KiDZ calibration sample, VIPERS, and the DES Year 3 BAO sample. On Euclid Flagship 2 (LSST+NISP) we achieve $\sigma_{\rm NMAD}=0.0148$ with a $0.27\%$ outlier fraction, and on DES Y3 we obtain $\sigma_{\rm NMAD}\approx0.023$ with $<1\%$ outliers and posterior calibration comparable to the \texttt{ANNz2} catalogue. \texttt{MAHA-z} processes millions of galaxies in minutes and provides competitive PDFs at substantially lower computational cost than trained machine-learning models, making it a practical option for iterative photo-$z$ production and validation in Stage-IV surveys.

2. galpy with differentiable, accelerated backends: galactic dynamics in jax and PyTorch[2611.14614]
Abstract

We describe an extension of galpy, the Python library for galactic dynamics, that adds jax and PyTorch as computational backends alongside its existing numpy and C implementation. With it, galpy's gravitational potentials, orbit integration, action-angle transformations and their inverses, and distribution functions, including those for tidal streams, run on GPUs, can be compiled as part of a user's program, and can be differentiated with respect to their inputs and parameters, from the force at a point to the samples drawn from a distribution function. The user-facing design is kept simple: the active backend follows from the type of the arrays that are passed in, the galpy interface is unchanged, and the numpy path gives the same results as before. We describe the design of the backend framework and its implementation in each of galpy's modules, with examples of use, and we discuss its limitations. On a GPU, batches of orbits, samples from distribution functions, and stream models run one to five orders of magnitude faster than on a single CPU core. To illustrate the new capabilities, we present simple examples of fitting the Galactic potential with a hypervelocity star and of learning the inverse action-angle transformation with a neural network trained through galpy. An AI coding agent wrote essentially all of the code under the detailed direction and review of the human author, and we describe how we organized this work and how we established the correctness of the result. This work is the start of galpy's version 2, which is available at this https URL and documented at this https URL .

3. Beyond classification: regressing physical merger properties using solid harmonic wavelet bispectrum[2611.14630]
Abstract

Galaxy mergers play an important role in galaxy evolution; they enhance star formation, transform morphologies, and may drive accretion onto black holes. Due to their complex morphological features, identification of mergers poses a significant challenge. Convolutional neural networks (CNNs) have been successful at identifying mergers with good accuracy. However, similar to other deep learning techniques such as Vision Transformers, they require large training samples. Current methods are also limited to classification, rather than regression. Here, we apply the solid harmonic wavelet scattering bispectrum, a method that encodes invariances and symmetries in the data which need to be learned by CNNs, as an alternative to CNNs. We apply our methodology to a simulated dataset of galaxies with a wide range of merger properties. We show that this method outperforms standard methodologies, while requiring fewer training samples and being robust to noise. Most importantly, we show that for the first time this method is able to regress on both merger mass ratio and time since merger simultaneously, with uncertainties   0.33 dex uncertainty in merger mass ratio and   0.66 Gyr uncertainty in time since merger. We have therefore shown that solid harmonic wavelet bispectrum presents an alternative to currently used machine learning techniques.

4. FRIDA: Finding Rotation In ultra-Diffuse gAlaxies. I. Programme overview, resolved gas density profiles, and HI mass-size relation[2611.14635]
Abstract

Gas-rich ultra-diffuse galaxies (UDGs) provide powerful tests of galaxy formation models at low surface densities. However, the structure of their neutral atomic hydrogen (HI) remains poorly constrained, hampering our understanding of this population. We present FRIDA (Finding Rotation In ultra-Diffuse gAlaxies), a MeerKAT and VLA programme providing high-resolution HI data of 12 gas-rich UDGs. In this paper, we introduce our sample and delve into the associated observations and data reduction. We derive HI fluxes, masses ($M_{\rm HI}$), and resolved surface density profiles ($\Sigma_{\rm HI}(R)$). We find extended ($\sim 7-30\ \rm{kpc}$ in radius) and regular HI emission in our UDGs, and we report the discovery of a satellite around one of them. Most UDGs in FRIDA have $\Sigma_{\rm HI}(R)$ profiles with flat inner parts reaching $\Sigma_{\rm HI}(R)\approx 4\ M_\odot\ \rm{pc^{-2}}$ near their centres, which then decline exponentially in their outer parts. By modelling the HI morphology of the UDGs, we find a median inclination of $43^\circ$, in agreement with theoretical expectations for disc galaxies under UDG selection effects. Moreover, we place our UDGs in the $M_{\rm HI}-R_{\rm HI}$ and $M_{\rm HI}-R_{\rm 50,HI}$ mass-size relations, where $R_{\rm HI}$ is radius at which $\Sigma_{\rm HI}(R)=1\ M_\odot \rm{pc^{-2}}$, and $R_{\rm 50,HI}$ is the radius enclosing $50\%$ of $M_{\rm HI}$. While our UDGs lie within the scatter of the relations, they systematically fall on their upper side, being slightly (by $\sim 15-25\%$) more extended and of lower mean $\Sigma_{\rm HI}$ (by $\sim 25-35\%$) than average galaxies at similar $M_{\rm HI}$. Moreover, we find that $\Sigma_{\rm HI}(R/R_{\rm HI})$ and $M_{\rm HI}(<R)/M_{\rm HI}$ for UDGs follow an intermediate trend between late-type discs and early-type galaxies, with a large overlap with Sa galaxies.

5. Dark matter alters dwarf galaxy sizes and morphologies[2611.14638]
Abstract

Dwarf galaxies are the most dark-matter-dominated systems in the Universe, serving as unique laboratories for exploring the fundamental nature of the dark sector. Although the observed diversity in dwarf galaxy sizes and morphologies suggests an interplay between dark matter and baryonic physics, this relationship has remained largely unquantified in existing models. Here, we utilize a set of novel cosmological simulations with baryons to demonstrate how dark matter physics directly drives the structural evolution of these galaxies. By comparing the same systems across cold dark matter and varied self-interacting dark matter models, we find that the evolution of the central density of dark matter significantly impacts a galaxy's half-light radius, but the signal is dependent on morphology. This causal link establishes galaxy size as a direct signature of the underlying dark matter microphysics. Our findings provide a predictive framework for decoding dark matter particle properties from galactic shapes, offering a roadmap for upcoming wide-field surveys, such as the Legacy Survey of Space and Time, to probe the dark sector through the statistical analysis of galaxy populations.

6. Variations in the Extinction Curve at High Galactic Latitudes as seen by the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument[2611.14639]
Abstract

The dust extinction curve, characterized by the total-to-selective extinction ratio, $R(V)$, varies with grain size and composition. Accurate $R(V)$ values are crucial for dereddening astronomical colors and understanding the interstellar medium. We measure extinction toward stars with Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI) spectroscopy using a machine-learning-based relative of the "pair method," matching reddened and unreddened stars. We use XGBoost to predict stars' intrinsic colors from their spectroscopic parameters ($T_\mathrm{eff}$, $\log g$, $[\mathrm{Fe/H}]$) and subtract them from observed colors to measure reddening in combinations of the $g$, $r$, $z$, $W1$, and $W2$ bands. This approach directly measures reddening, unlike the common thermal-emission-based method of Schlegel et al. (1998; SFD). The reddening is accurate to roughly 0.02 mag in $g-r$ and $r-z$. We develop a forward model to map $g-r$ and $g-W1$ reddenings to $R(V)$ and find significant variation in $R(V)$, with $\sigma_\mathrm{NMAD}(R(V)) = 0.23$, in dusty regions ($E(g-r) > 0.1$) at high Galactic latitudes observed by DESI. In this footprint, $R(V)$ is bimodal, with modes at 2.9 and 3.4, likely due to large-spatial-scale correlations in the shape of the extinction curve combined with the relatively small number of distinct sightlines with substantial reddening. We find no strong relationship between $R(V)$ and other tracers of the interstellar medium, such as thermal emission, dust temperature, spectral emissivity index $\beta$, and $H\alpha$. However, the error in the SFD-predicted extinction correlates well with $R(V)$, implying that a single optical reddening combined with the far-infrared dust brightness can predict variations in the extinction curve.

7. Tracing Galactic Dust Reddening with Polycyclic Aromatic Hydrocarbon Emission: SPHEREx Observations of the North Ecliptic Pole[2611.14640]
Abstract

We investigate the ability of SPHEREx observations of the $3.3$ $\mu$m polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbon (PAH) emission feature to trace Galactic dust reddening. Applying a spectral template-based method to SPHEREx data, we measure the strength of the $3.3$ $\mu$m PAH feature in each $6''.2$ pixel of a $22^{\circ}$ by $22^{\circ}$ region centered on the north ecliptic pole (NEP). We convert the PAH feature strength to reddening by applying a radiation field correction and a linear scale factor. The resulting $E(B-V)$ map is consistent with far-infrared, stellar, and HI-based reddening maps at arcminute scales, exhibiting a typical $E(B-V)$ difference of $\lesssim 7$ mmag. A galaxy count test finds that all reddening maps have a dispersion of $\approx11$ mmag in $E(B-V)$ relative to what would be needed to produce spatially uniform galaxy counts. While there is some evidence that reddening is overestimated by our map at high PAH abundances, this effect is smaller than the uncertainties on the applied radiation field corrections and limited to $\sim$10 mmag in the most extreme regions of the NEP. For a diffuse, high Galactic latitude region like the NEP and at the sensitivities of our validation metrics, the SPHEREx PAH-emission based reddening map matches the accuracy of existing reddening maps while being capable of accessing a higher angular resolution of $\sim6''$.

8. Revisiting the distance to 4U 1543-475 through X-ray dust-scattering rings[2611.14643]
Abstract

We derived a geometrical distance of the black-hole X-ray binary 4U 1543-475 based on X-ray dust-scattering rings associated with its 1992 and 2002 outbursts. We analyzed archival ROSAT and XMM-Newton observations and identified an X-ray dust-scattering ring in each observation, produced by two different dust layers along the line of sight. By combining the angular radii of the rings with the independently determined distances of the scattering dust layers and the epochs of the corresponding outbursts, we derive a best-constrained distance to 4U 1543-475 of 4.6 $\pm$ 0.2 kpc. This value is substantially smaller than the distance of 7.5 kpc commonly adopted in the literature and has important implications for the inferred physical properties of the system. In particular, the revised distance favors the presence of a low-mass extended-horizontal-branch donor star over a main-sequence star, calling for a reassessment of the dynamical parameters of the binary and, consequently, of the black-hole mass. The smaller distance also affects the inferred properties of the relativistic jet and the estimate of black-hole spin derived from the spectral continuum-fitting.

9. DIISC-VIII: Chemical Signatures of Gas Accretion in Star-Forming Galaxies[2611.14701]
Abstract

We investigate anomalously low-metallicity (ALM) star-forming regions in 11 galaxies from the Deciphering the Interplay between the Interstellar Medium, Stars, and the Circumgalactic Medium (DIISC) survey. We combine optical spectroscopy of 542 H$\alpha$-bright regions from MMT/Binospec with imaging from the Vatican Advanced Technology Telescope and H I 21 cm imaging from the Very Large Array. We find 75 ALM regions: 19 identified using deviations from each galaxy's radial metallicity gradient, 27 using the resolved mass–metallicity relation (rMZR), and 29 via both methods. Regions identified by both methods are metal-poor relative to their galactocentric positions and local stellar mass surface densities by $>$ 0.1 dex, making them the strongest candidates for localized metallicity dilution. We estimate accreted masses of $5.6<\log(M_{\rm acc}/{\rm M_\odot})<7.5$ for the 48 radial-gradient-selected regions, comparable to observed and simulated high-velocity cloud masses. We calculate a mean hydrogen accretion rate of $1.02\ {\rm M_\odot yr^{-1}}$ per galaxy, which is comparable to the mean star formation rate of the sample. The strongest ALM candidates lie within or near $R_{25}$, while rMZR-only regions dominate the outer population. We find no evidence that circumgalactic Ly$\alpha$ absorption along a single sightline traces disk chemical structure, as quantified by either the radial metallicity gradient or the fraction of radial-gradient-selected ALM regions. These results suggest that localized accretion of low-metallicity gas can sustain star formation in galaxies and demonstrate that ALM regions provide a spatially resolved indirect view of ongoing gas cycling.

10. La Silla-QUEST RR Lyrae Survey III: The Shape of the Outer Virgo Overdensity[2611.14734]
Abstract

Using the La Silla-QUEST and Gaia surveys, we identify 256 RR Lyrae stars (RRLS) in the Outer Virgo Overdensity (OVOD). These show that the OVOD has a signifcantly larger and more complex shape than previously recognized. The OVOD lies at an average distance of $\sim 85$ kpc, has a long dimension of $\sim 57^\circ$ ($\sim 85$ kpc), and a width that varies from $\sim 10^\circ $ ($\sim 15$ kpc) to $\sim 35^\circ $ ($\sim 50$ kpc) in a prominent bulge. Because it bends by $\sim 35^\circ$ from a great circle, it does not appear to be a stellar stream. The type ab RRLS (RRab) span a large range in period, with a mean of $0.59$ days, and only $19\pm 3 \%$ lie near the locus of Oosterhoff II variables in the Bailey diagram. The overall large number of RRLS along with a mean RRab metallicity ([Fe/H]) of -1.60 and $1 \sigma$ dispersion of 0.32, as inferred from the RRab periods, suggest that the OVOD progenitor was a large dwarf galaxy. The hypothesis that the OVOD may be a shell produced by the Gaia-Sausage-Enceladus merger \citep{chandra2023} is supported by the $\sim 70^\circ$ angle that the southern extension of the OVOD makes with the orbits of red giants in its central part. The complex shape of the overall RRLS distribution, including a small north-south distance gradient, may also indicate, however, that we are seeing a blended, composite structure projected on the sky.

11. How Intensity Threshold Shapes the Velocity Dispersion-Size Relation in Molecular Clouds[2611.14852]
Abstract

The velocity dispersion-size relation of molecular clouds, $\sigma_v = C\,(R/1\,{\rm pc})^{\gamma}$, has been reported with a power-law index ranging from $\gamma \approx$ 0.38 to 0.5 or higher, and a correspondingly varying normalization coefficient $C$, but the role of the intensity threshold in producing this scatter has not been quantitatively characterized. Using 4916 $^{13}$CO clouds identified with GDCluster (Liu & Du 2025) in the Milky Way Imaging Scroll Painting (MWISP) survey, we derive $\gamma$ and $C$ as functions of the intensity threshold $T_{\rm th}$. The index $\gamma$ increases monotonically with threshold, reaching a saturation value $\gamma_{\rm sat} \approx$ 0.56 for the full sample; splitting the sample by spiral arm gives higher values, $\gamma_{\rm sat} \approx$ 0.62 and 0.70. The normalization $C(T_{\rm th})$ declines monotonically, saturating at $C_{\rm sat} \approx$ 0.35 km/s and implying a surface density of  46 Msun/pc^2 under a virial approximation. On the $\gamma(T_{\rm th})$ curve, $\gamma \approx$ 0.38 (Larson 1981) and $\gamma \approx$ 0.5 (Solomon et al. 1987) correspond to a low threshold ($T_{\rm th} \approx$ 1.2 K) and a near-saturation one (2.6 K), respectively, roughly consistent with the $^{13}$CO-equivalent boundary temperatures of these two studies. These two historically debated values, together with the broader $\gamma \approx$ 0.38-0.7 range reported in the literature, may largely reflect different positions along this single threshold-dependent curve and differences in sample composition. This threshold effect should therefore be considered when using Larson-type relations to assess the roles of turbulence and gravity. As an independent check, DBSCAN-identified $^{13}$CO and $^{12}$CO samples show the same monotonic trend in $\gamma$, though the curve shape differs quantitatively.

12. Moving Beyond the DRW Model: Learning Quasar Variability with Generative Models[2611.15040]
Abstract

The optical continuum variability of quasars is often described by a damped random walk (DRW) model, with characteristic damping timescales $\tau_{\rm DRW}$ of hundreds of days and amplitudes $\sigma_{\rm DRW}$ of $\mathcal{O}(0.1)$ magnitudes. This model has been widely used as a null hypothesis to assess the significance of periodic signals in quasar light curves in the search for supermassive black hole binaries, the significance of extreme quasar variability like tidal disruption events (TDEs) and changing-look (CL) quasars, or to model time lags in reverberation mapping. However, the DRW model is known to be incomplete, and an incorrect null hypothesis can lead to biased significance estimates. We investigate the impact of such null-hypothesis mis-specification on the detection of periodic signals in synthetic quasar light curves and introduce the generative model Nullasar, capable of learning quasar variability directly from time-series data. We demonstrate that Nullasar, trained with flow matching, can learn delicate statistical properties of stochastic light-curve variability without explicitly assuming any noise model. We further demonstrate that Nullasar can learn correlated distributions of noise parameters and generate new light-curve realizations from a range of deviations from the Gaussian DRW noise model, approximating parametric descriptions of stochastic processes. When used as a null hypothesis for detecting periodic signals in synthetic light curves, Nullasar performs comparably to fitting the correct DRW model to each light curve, and outperforms parametric fitting when the variability follows a more flexible AR(2) model. Finally, we identify practical limitations, including sensitivity to contamination of the training set by non-null signals, and show that projecting the conditioning vector of the model onto its leading principal components largely mitigates it.

13. Prospects for probing supermassive binary black holes in strongly lensed active galactic nuclei via microlensing effects[2611.15056]
Abstract

Gravitational microlensing in strongly lensed active galactic nuclei (AGNs) provides a powerful probe of accretion-disk structure and may offer a unique opportunity to identify sub-parsec supermassive binary black holes (SMBBHs) through distinctive signatures. Upcoming wide-field surveys are expected to discover a large sample of strongly lensed AGNs, a fraction of which may host SMBBHs detectable via microlensing. In this paper, we employ semi-analytic models to assess the detection prospects of sub-parsec SMBBHs in lensed AGNs, focusing on systems with resolvable multiple images suitable for microlensing analysis. Accounting for galaxy-scale strong-lensing statistics, and including the cluster-lensing contribution as an optimistic statistical extension, we estimate that up to approximately 87 such systems could be spatially resolved by individual upcoming surveys, including the Vera C. Rubin Observatory (Rubin), Euclid, China's Space Survey Telescope (CSST), and the Roman. For the Rubin WFD survey, we estimate that periodic oscillations in microlensing light curves could identify $\sim0.12-0.68$ short-period ($<5$ yr) binaries, whereas diagnostics based on the reconstructed size-wavelength relation apply to a broader population, potentially identifying $\sim0.4-5.3$ systems, depending on the model assumptions. CSST, Euclid, and Roman may also reveal up to $10.6$, $8.9$, and $1.2$ systems exhibiting size-wavelength features, and $2.3$, $1.9$, $0.3$ systems showing periodic oscillations, which should be regarded as imaging-selected candidates whose microlensing-based identification requires adequate time-domain follow-up, including cadence, monitoring baseline, photometric precision, and reliable time-delay measurements. Our results show that strongly lensed AGNs provide a promising, though observationally demanding, pathway for finding candidate SMBBHs via microlensing.

15. Non-Gaussian matter fluctuations from primordial magnetic fields and galaxy luminosity function[2611.15188]
Abstract

Primordial magnetic fields (PMFs) represent a promising candidate for the origin of large-scale magnetic fields observed in astrophysical environments. Through the Lorentz force, PMFs generate matter density perturbations, leaving distinct imprints on the formation of cosmic structures. While previous studies have primarily characterized these imprints via the matter power spectrum — a two-point statistic — we demonstrate that the one-point statistics of PMF-induced density fluctuations reveal an intrinsically non-Gaussian probability distribution function (PDF), even when the PMFs themselves are statistically Gaussian. In particular, we find that the high-density tail of the PDF is enhanced by over two orders of magnitude relative to the Gaussian case. Adopting the Press-Schechter formalism, we show that this non-Gaussianity significantly boosts the abundance of rare objects, especially massive halos and those at high redshifts. We further compute the ultraviolet luminosity function of galaxies at $z=6$, $12$, and $16$, and compare our predictions with recent James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) observations that have reported an excess of high-redshift galaxies at $z\sim 12$ – $16$ relative to standard $\Lambda$CDM expectations. Intriguingly, in the limiting case where the total density field inherits this PDF, our results suggest that the non-Gaussian density fluctuations sourced by PMFs can explain the bright-end ($M_\mathrm{UV}\lesssim -18$) excess at $z\gtrsim 12$, without requiring additional astrophysical enhancements to star-formation efficiency.

16. Diffuse interstellar bands in nearby galaxies with MaNGA: environmental trends with metallicity, extinction, ionization, and star-forming activity[2611.15272]
Abstract

Diffuse interstellar bands (DIBs) are absorption features of uncertain origin observed in the optical and near-infrared spectra of stars and galaxies. Although their correlation with dust extinction is well established, the physical conditions governing their strength across different interstellar medium (ISM) environments remain poorly understood. We investigate the environmental dependence of the DIB $\lambda5780$ using spatially resolved MaNGA spectroscopy for 837 nearby galaxies spanning a broad range of stellar masses, star-formation rates, and ISM properties. Stellar and nebular parameters are derived via spectral fitting, while DIB absorption is measured from high-signal-to-noise spatially binned residual spectra. We detect the DIB $\lambda5780$ in 159 galaxies and report the most distant spatially resolved DIB detection to date, at 232 Mpc. The DIB $\lambda5780$ equivalent width correlates positively with both stellar and gas-phase extinction. DIB $\lambda5780$ is preferentially detected in regions of high extinction, near- or super-solar metallicity ($12+\log({\rm O/H})\gtrsim8.5$), and moderate specific star-formation rates ($-10.5\lesssim\log({\rm sSFR}/{\rm yr}^{-1})\lesssim-9$). By contrast, it falls below our detection threshold in metal-poor or strongly ionized environments. It is rarely detected in regions with little or no recent star formation, with the few detections in such environments occurring primarily in regions driven by composite ionization. Our results indicate that DIB $\lambda5780$ is preferentially detected, within the sensitivity limits of the MaNGA data, in dust-rich, chemically evolved, and moderately ionized environments.

17. The Distant Milky Way Halo from the Southern Hemisphere: Spatially broad and closer stellar debris associated to the Magellanic Stream[2611.15301]
Abstract

The Magellanic Stream (MS) is a gaseous structure extending over $150^\circ$ across the sky, likely formed through interactions between the Large and Small Magellanic Clouds (LMC and SMC) as they fell into the Milky Way (MW). Its predicted stellar counterpart would provide a collisionless record of these interactions, but few candidate members have been identified. To expand this sample, we constructed a catalog of 87,763 distant K-giant candidates using DELVE DR2 and VHS DR5 photometry and Gaia DR3 astrometry, and analyzed VLT and Magellan spectra of 51 targets along the trailing MS. Combining radial velocities, spectrophotometric distances, and proper motions, we identify five new MS candidates at 58–95 kpc. Their metallicities are consistent with the outskirts of the SMC, while orbit integrations in a time-dependent MW–LMC–SMC potential support a possible Magellanic origin. Combined with previously identified candidates, these stars span a substantially broader range of Magellanic latitude than predicted by existing models, suggesting an extended debris field rather than a narrow stream. The candidates also lie predominantly closer than the simulated debris. More recent, less eccentric SMC orbits may help explain this distance discrepancy, although new simulations are required to test their implications for the stellar debris, along with larger spectroscopic samples.

18. VISTA Magellanic Clouds deep observations of Classical Cepheids: Precision distances and a collision-driven SMC[2611.15303]
Abstract

The central regions of the Magellanic Clouds are crucial for understanding galaxy interactions and calibrating the cosmic distance scale, yet they remain observationally challenging due to extreme stellar crowding. We utilised deep VISTA observations (VMCDeep) to determine the highest-precision near-infrared Period-Luminosity (PL) and Period-Wesenheit (PW) relations to date for Classical Cepheids (delta Cep class, DCEPs) in the dense central regions of the Large and Small Magellanic Clouds (LMC and SMC). We analysed J and Ks time-series data covering the central 1.77 deg^2 of each galaxy. Utilising approximately 40 epochs per filter and PSF-based photometry, we derived high-quality light curves for 573 and 2090 DCEPs in the LMC and SMC, respectively. We derived exceptionally precise PL and PW relations, exhibiting the lowest photometric dispersion published so far for the Magellanic Clouds. Crucially, we discovered a striking line-of-sight spatial dichotomy within the young SMC DCEP population (t < 116 Myr), which is bifurcated into two distinct structures separated by  4 kpc. This provides independent stellar evidence tracing a recent direct collision with the LMC. Finally, by unifying fundamental and first-overtone pulsators, we derived a highly constrained relative distance between the Clouds. Comparing this to detached eclipsing binary distances, our data suggest a moderate metallicity correction for the Leavitt Law with gamma   -0.2 mag/dex.

19. Discrete Supernovae and the Limits of Continuous Models in Low-Mass Dark Matter Halos[2611.15335]
Abstract

Many galaxy formation models represent supernova feedback by a continuous energy injection rate averaged over the stellar initial mass function (IMF). This approximation can fail in low-mass dark matter halos, where single, discrete supernovae can unbind more gas than the reservoir holds. We investigate this using a one-zone model that characterises continuous and discrete supernova feedback at redshifts $z\geq5$. Both feedback modes inject the same expected energy per unit stellar mass formed. In the discrete model, individual supernovae are resolved and energy is injected instantaneously; in the continuous model, supernovae are IMF-averaged and energy injection is spread over a finite time interval. Once stars form, a supernova can unbind 1.5-2.7 times the gas present. Continuous feedback begins removing gas after $\sim 4$ Myr, whereas the first discrete supernova occurs after $\sim 10$ Myr, which produces a stellar mass excess before feedback takes effect. The excess persists because the reservoir responds nonlinearly: an event removes at most the gas present, and star formation halts once it is empty. It depends strongly on halo mass: from 135 per cent at $10^7\, \rm{M}_\odot$, to 15 per cent at $10^8\, \rm{M}_\odot$, to negligible at $10^9\, \rm{M}_\odot$ by $z=5$. Fixing the number and timing of supernovae does not remove the stellar excess, which indicates that it is not caused by stochastic IMF sampling. Instead, it vanishes when the same energy is divided among more, smaller events. Matching the energy released per unit stellar mass therefore does not guarantee equivalent feedback in halos that retain gas until the first supernova.

20. 3D dust attenuation effects in galaxies: structural biases and induced negative color gradients[2611.15355]
Abstract

Radial surface brightness profiles (SBPs) and color gradients are useful tools for studying galaxy structure, formation, and evolution. While it is usually assumed that they are primarily driven by stellar properties, such as mass, age or metallicity, their dependence on 3D dust attenuation is not fully understood or accounted for. We analyze how 3D dust attenuation affects galaxy observations by hindering the estimation of intrinsic properties. We examine dust-induced shifts in surface brightness and color. Using SKIRT radiative transfer models, we simulated simple Sérsic galaxies, with spherically-symmetric stellar densities, constant stellar-to-dust ratios, and a spatially constant spectral energy distribution (SED). We find that attenuated SBPs differ significantly from dust-free profiles, particularly in the central galaxy regions. These differences become more pronounced at bluer wavelengths and for higher core dust densities, leading to variations in the fitted Sérsic index of up to  25%. This can lead to incorrect conclusions about the intrinsic stellar distribution and mimic a central mass deficit. For galaxies with very centrally-dense dust distributions, a core-Sérsic model results in a better fit of the attenuated SBP. For dust masses as low as  2*10^6 $M_{\odot}$, dust-induced negative color gradients reach the same order of magnitude as those commonly observed in galaxies, which a dust-screen fails to account for. This occurs even for a spatially invariant SED and can lead to wrong conclusions about intrinsic stellar populations and related astrophysical questions, such as their formation histories or the mechanisms of inside-out star formation quenching. A full 3D approach is necessary to properly model attenuation effects, which, if not accounted for, can bias inferences made about stellar populations, their evolutionary histories, and spatial distributions.

21. LHAASO's Peanut source is a laboratory for studying magnetic fields in the Milky Way[2611.15473]
Abstract

Recently, the LHAASO Collaboration reported gamma-ray emission from the Peanut-shaped source (Peanut), which extends over $4.5^\circ$ parallel to the Galactic plane at $b=-17.5^\circ$. The source is associated with the millisecond pulsar PSR J0218+4232, located at the eastern edge of the Peanut, at a distance of 3 kpc from the Earth and 0.9 kpc from the Galactic plane. Its location and bright gamma-ray emission above 25 TeV make it a natural laboratory for studying the Galactic magnetic field (GMF) in the transition region between the Galactic plane and halo. We interpret the observed emission as inverse-Compton scattering of CMB photons by electrons propagating along the large-scale GMF, accounting for turbulence through two models: Kolmogorov and Goldreich-Sridhar (GS).
