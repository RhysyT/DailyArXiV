1. The Local Star Formation Environment Predicts Young Star Cluster Radii: Evidence from PHANGS-MUSE H-alpha Maps[2610.23876]
Abstract

The effective radii of young star clusters correlate with cluster mass, but the large scatter about that relation is not well understood. Theory predicts that a cluster's initial radius depends on the gas surface density of its natal environment, so the star formation environment should leave a measurable imprint on cluster structure. In a companion analysis of 5,105 clusters across 31 LEGUS galaxies, we found that galaxy-wide specific star formation rate (sSFR) carries no predictive power for cluster radius. Here we test whether that null result reflects the spatial scale on which the environment was measured. We cross-match the LEGUS cluster catalog of Brown & Gnedin with H-alpha maps from the PHANGS-MUSE survey in the four galaxies common to both surveys, and measure the local star formation rate surface density, Sigma_SFR, at the position of every cluster by aperture photometry. For the 325 clusters younger than 10 Myr with reliable radius and mass measurements, local Sigma_SFR significantly predicts effective radius (beta = 0.079, p = 2 x 10^-4) in a model containing mass and age, while the host galaxy's sSFR does not (beta = 0.042, p = 0.40) for those same clusters. The local model generalizes nearly three times better under cross-validation (CV R^2 = 0.061 versus 0.022). The effect operates among clusters within a single galaxy: it survives galaxy fixed effects (beta = 0.068, p = 0.001) and appears independently in each of the two galaxies large enough to fit alone. Including local Sigma_SFR also reduces the mass coefficient by 26%. The birth environment measurably shapes cluster structure, but only when it is measured on the scale on which clusters actually form.

2. Beyond the dust: JWST reveals extinction and ice content in local (U)LIRGs[2610.24069]
Abstract

We investigate the spatially resolved extinction and ice column densities in four nearby interacting (U)LIRGs using JWST/NIRSpec integral field spectroscopy (R$\sim$2700; 0.97–5.14 $\mu$m). This wide range encompasses numerous (>25) H recombination and H$_2$ ro-vibrational emission lines, as well as the prominent H$_2$O ($\sim$3.02 $\mu$m) and CO$_2$ ($\sim$4.26 $\mu$m) ice absorption features. We develop three methods to derive phase-specific extinction for ionized (A$_{\text K}^{\text HII}$) and hot molecular gas (A$_{\text K}^{\text H_2}$), and H$_2$O and CO$_2$ ice column densities (N$_{\text H_2O}$ and N$_{\text CO_2}$), exploiting H and H$_2$ emission lines and ice absorption features. Nuclear regions (0.2" radius) exhibit higher extinction and ice column densities than extended regions, with median A$_{\text K}^{\text HII}$ and A$_{\text K}^{\text H_2}$ of 1.25 and 0.75 in the nuclei, compared to 0.61 and 0.50 in the extended regions. The ionized gas is more extincted than the hot molecular gas, with A$_{\text K}^{\text HII}\approx1.5$ A$_{\text K}^{\text H_2}$, while H$_2$O ice column densities are comparable between phases. In some nuclei, continuum-derived N$_{\text H_2O}$ reaches $\sim10^{18.81}$ cm$^{-2}$, likely due to compact obscured components. These results suggest that ionized gas originates in more deeply embedded regions, whereas water ice envelopes both gas phases, with additional ice-rich material around the most obscured nuclei. The median N$_{\text CO_2}$/N$_{\text H_2O}$ abundance ratio is $\sim0.10$, a factor of 2–3 below typical Galactic values.

3. Beyond the Disc–Spheroid Dichotomy at $0.6 < z < 8.0$: Evidence for a Morphological Indeterminacy Regime from JWST[2610.24078]
Abstract

Galaxy morphology is a fundamental tracer of galaxy assembly, but its characterization becomes increasingly uncertain in the early Universe. We investigate morphological classifications across different methodologies over the redshift range $0.6 < z < 8.0$, using JWST observations of 15,567 galaxies with 6,099 synthetic galaxies from IllustrisTNG. To quantify morphology without relying on visual labels or prescribed light profiles, we use an unsupervised-supervised hybrid machine-learning approach based on the non-parametric MEGG system, which combines the second moment of the light distribution, Entropy, Gini, and Gradient Pattern Analysis. We find that parametric and non-parametric classifications remain broadly consistent at $z < 2.5$, but diverge systematically at higher redshift. We identify this epoch as the Morphological Indeterminacy Regime (MIR), in which classical disc and spheroidal categories no longer provide a unique description of the galaxy population. The fraction of mixed-interpretation galaxies increases with redshift, reaching $F_{\rm MIR} \sim 0.55$ at $z > 2.5$. Cross-domain and projection-invariance tests show that the classifications are stable and insensitive to data set and viewing angle. In the interval $2.5 < z < 8.0$, MIR galaxies occupy an intermediate stellar-mass regime (median; $\log(M_\star/M_\odot) \sim 9.7$), between low-mass discs (median; $\log(M_\star/M_\odot) \sim 9.5$) and massive spheroids (median; $\log(M_\star/M_\odot) \sim 10.1$), while irregular systems have stellar-mass distributions similar to those of MIR galaxies. We interpret these results as evidence that morphological indeterminacy is a characteristic feature of galaxy assembly in the early Universe, reflecting a population of dynamically young systems that have not yet settled into the classical Hubble sequence.

4. Can Blue UV-Bright Galaxies Drive Reionization at Cosmic Dawn? An $M_{\rm UV}=-19.6$ Galaxy with $f_{\rm esc}\approx0.85$ at $z=9.244$[2610.24092]
Abstract

We revisit EBG-1, a gravitationally lensed galaxy at $z = 9.244$, using newly obtained deep (14 hr) JWST/NIRSpec G235M ($R \sim 1000$) spectroscopy. EBG-1 is one of the bluest galaxies observed at $z > 9$. Along with its remarkably blue continuum of $\beta = -2.99 \pm 0.15$, we find very weak nebular emission from EBG-1. We obtain a $3\sigma$ upper limit of EW $< 8.4$ Angstrom for C III], a marginal H$\beta$ measurement (EW $= 256.2 \pm 122.2$ Angstrom), and a $5\sigma$ detection of [O III] $\lambda 5007$ (EW $= 882.8 \pm 180.6$ Angstrom). These lines suggest an oxygen abundance of $12 + \log(\mathrm{O/H}) = 7.16 \pm 0.25$ ($\simeq 3\% Z_{\odot}$) and $\log(\mathrm{C/O}) < -0.88$ ($3\sigma$), placing EBG-1 on the C/O-metallicity sequence traced by lower-redshift galaxies. To constrain the escape fraction ($f_{\rm esc}$), we compare the observed UV slope and emission-line equivalent widths with synthetic spectra generated using BEAGLE. The models favor $f_{\rm esc} \gtrsim 0.75$, with $f_{\rm esc} \approx 0.85$ providing the best overall agreement with the observations. These results suggest that relatively UV-bright galaxies like EBG-1 can achieve the exceptionally high ionizing efficiencies supported by late reionization scenarios.

5. The evolution of 3C 306 in the galaxy cluster RXCJ1454.4+1622: linking spectral ageing, ICM conditions, and jet dynamics through MHD simulations[2610.24097]
Abstract

We present a methodology for analysing radio sources in galaxy clusters by combining multi-frequency radio observations, X-ray analysis of the intracluster medium (ICM), and three-dimensional magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) simulations, and apply it to the galaxy cluster RXCJ1454.4+1622 and its associated FR II radio galaxy 3C 306 (IC 4516). The radio emission exhibits a curved structure connecting the lobes, likely indicating a complex dynamical evolution influenced by environmental effects. Spectral index and break-frequency maps reveal a relatively homogeneous radiative ageing distribution, with characteristic spectral ages of $\sim220$ Myr and a smooth spatial gradient. From equipartition arguments, we estimate an equivalent jet magnetic field of $2.1 \pm 0.3\,\mu$G, consistent with values observed in FR II radio galaxies. These observational constraints are used to initialise non-relativistic MHD simulations exploring three scenarios: jet precession, bulk motion of the source through the ICM, and a combined model including both effects. Neither pure precession nor pure source motion alone fully reproduces the observed morphology, whereas the combined model provides a more consistent representation of the jet curvature and lobe asymmetries. Nevertheless, the simulations indicate that source motion is the dominant mechanism shaping the large-scale radio morphology, with jet precession playing a secondary role. The inferred source velocity ($\sim280$ km s$^{-1}$) and precession timescale ($\sim250$ Myr) are compatible with the spectral ageing constraints. These results suggest that the morphology of 3C 306 is influenced by the interplay between jet precession and dynamical interaction with the ICM, highlighting the role of environmental effects in radio-galaxy evolution in clusters.

6. A DECADE of Giants: A catalog of 42,000 optically identified galaxy clusters from 13,000 $\mathrm{deg}^2$ of the sky[2610.24104]
Abstract

Galaxy clusters are sensitive to a rich variety of physics spanning a wide dynamic range, from microphysical to cosmological scales. Optical datasets are efficient at identifying clusters and studying their properties. We leverage 13,000 $\rm deg^2$ of the sky imaged by the Dark Energy Camera (DECam) and extract over 40,000 clusters using the redMaPPer algorithm. Our catalog spans $0.2 < z < 0.9$ and $M_{\rm 200c} \gtrsim 10^{14} {\rm M}_{\odot}/h$, with four-band ($griz$) photometric redshifts that have a precision $\sigma_z/(1 + z) \leq 0.008$ below $z < 0.65$, and $\leq 0.011$ between $0.65 < z < 0.9$ and that exhibit an outlier rate of $f_{\rm out} < 0.2\%$. Our cluster finding is calibrated using spectroscopic data, but we find that a larger spectroscopic sample does not improve the performance of our cluster redshift estimation. We cross-match our catalog to those produced from millimeter-wave and X-ray data, and extract scaling relations between various cluster observables. There is excellent agreement between our estimates for different subsets of the 13,000 ${\rm deg}^2$ dataset, and also with results from prior catalogs and simulations. We use our joint analysis of millimeter-wave and optical data to estimate the halo masses of individual clusters. We also provide lensing profiles, measured for each cluster using overlapping galaxy shape catalogs, to enable additional mass-calibration work from the community. We demonstrate from a suite of tests that spatial inhomogeneity in the quality of the imaging data does not have an appreciable impact on the properties of this catalog. We also test a cylindrical richness observable, which is defined such that it is significantly simpler to model, and show that it has precision competitive with the traditional optical richness observable. All data products and measurements are made publicly available.

7. Modeling Multi-band Active Galactic Nuclei Lightcurves Using an Self-supervised Attentive Neural Process Network[2610.24195]
Abstract

With the the Legacy Survey of Space and Time (LSST), observations of tens of millions of quasars are expected. We present a machine-learning framework based on an attentive neural process (ANP) that models stochastic quasar variability and jointly performs two tasks: (i) probabilistic reconstruction of quasar light curves in all six LSST bands, and (ii) inference of physical parameters of the AGN system — black hole mass, Eddington ratio, and intrinsic variability timescale — with joint, full-covariance posteriors. The model processes all six bands within a single masked attention mechanism, so that observations in any band constrain the shared driving process, and natively handles irregularly and asynchronously sampled light curves with realistic heteroscedastic noise. We train and evaluate on full ten-year light curves simulated with a relativistic accretion-disk reprocessing model, observed through the fielded Rubin baseline cadence with per-visit photometric noise. The light-curve model is trained with self-supervised, learning from the sparse noisy observations alone and never accessing the underlying noiseless light curves. On a held-out test set, we show that our ANP model reach comparable performances to that of the near-optimal baseline Gaussian process model, while exceeding it in power spectrum reconstruction and damped-random walk timescale estimation. The compact learned latent representation supports future downstream applications, and the fully observation-driven training objective enables direct fine-tuning on real LSST light curves.

8. Spiraling into the `snail' cluster MCXC J0157.4-0550[2610.24360]
Abstract

We present a deep Chandra (130 ks) study of a striking, tidally induced cold front in a subcluster merging with the galaxy cluster MCXC J0157.4-0550. The subcluster, nicknamed the `snail' (J0157), displays an extensive spiral structure that extends to $\sim 260$ kpc — one of the largest spiral cold fronts identified to date. Surface brightness and spectral analysis across four sectors reveal a consistent density jump of a factor of $\sim 3$ across the front, together with significant temperature discontinuities, and thermodynamic mapping further delineates the coherent, low-entropy structure of the stripped gas. Unlike classical sloshing cold fronts, the spiral in J0157 arises not from oscillation of a displaced cool core, but from subcluster gas that has been ram pressure stripped and tidally wound into a spiral by orbital rotation. Applying Gaussian Gradient Magnitude filtering and azimuthal surface-brightness analysis, we identify quasi-periodic corrugations along the front consistent with candidate Kelvin-Helmholtz (KH) instabilities, with a characteristic scale of $\sim 90$ kpc. The presence of these KH rolls constrains the effective ICM viscosity to $\lesssim 0.59$ times the Spitzer value and the magnetic field strength at the interface to $\lesssim 5.9\ \mu$G. These results establish J0157 as a useful laboratory for probing macro-scale merger-induced gas dynamics and micro-scale plasma transport processes in the intracluster medium.

9. Statistical Strong Lensing V. Inference with Microlensed Quasars[2610.24423]
Abstract

Microlensing of strongly lensed quasars provides direct information on the stellar component of lens galaxies, and can help break the degeneracy between the stellar initial mass function (IMF) and the dark matter. However, a statistical framework to forward model microlensing data while accounting for strong lensing selection effects is still missing. We incorporated microlensing effects into the hierarchical model of Paper III, and investigated to what extent flux measurements of strongly lensed quasars can break the degeneracy between the stellar IMF and the dark matter distribution. We tested the method on a mock sample of 219 doubly imaged quasars, lensed by circularly symmetric lenses. When microlensing effects are modelled, the inference using image fluxes recovers the input foreground population parameters and provides tighter constraints than the inference without flux constraints. The improvement is clearest in the $\mu_\alpha-\mu_5$ plane, where $\mu_\alpha$ describes the mean stellar population synthesis mismatch parameter $\log\alpha_{\rm SPS}$ and $\mu_5$ describes the mean projected dark matter mass within $5\,{\rm kpc}$. The 68% credible interval on $\mu_\alpha$ shrinks from 0.16 dex to 0.09 dex, with the degeneracy between $\mu_\alpha$ and $\mu_5$ significantly reduced. By contrast, fitting the image fluxes without accounting for microlensing leads to large biases on the inferred parameters. This work is a first step towards an accurate modelling of microlensing data. Application to real lens samples will require dropping the circular symmetry assumption and adopting more realistic models for the microlensing magnification.

10. Newly Identified Planetary Nebula Candidates in M31 using Deep-Learning Search of Hubble Space Telescope Images[2610.24554]
Abstract

The Hubble Space Telescope has imaged approximately a quarter of a billion stars in M31's disc, yet only slightly over a thousand planetary nebulae (PNe) are known, mostly from ground-based surveys. We show that deep learning can find them in broad-band images alone. In what is the first such search, we scan the Panchromatic Hubble Andromeda Treasury (PHAT) and the Panchromatic Hubble Andromeda Southern Treasury (PHAST) mosaics, nearly one square degree of the disk, where a PN is a faint blue point among crowded stars. Because the existing catalogs are incomplete, we train four Faster R-CNN detectors with partial supervision, so that uncataloged PNe are not taught as background, and combine their rankings into one candidate list, an ensemble. On spatially separate test fields, this list places 73% of the known PNe in its top 200, 4 to 6 times the purity of classical color-magnitude selection. Newer detection transformers and segmentation networks trained on the same data do not surpass our detectors. The ranking also follows classifications that the detectors never saw: emission-line objects that the literature calls PNe enter the list at 47%, against 0.6% for those called H II regions, which makes them candidates for reclassification. Among the top 400 candidates, 175 appear in no published catalog, 12 of them flagged as cosmic-ray residuals. One of them, a point source with [O III] emission in nine independent exposures, meets the PN criteria of the three major PN studies of M31, so by those criteria it is a PN. Most of the others lack strong [O III], as expected if narrow-band surveys have found the bright PNe, although some may be PNe with weak [O III]. These results support the near completeness of ground-based surveys for bright PNe and extend it from the PHAT area to PHAST. Finally, we identify evaluation pitfalls for detectors of rare objects and recommend simple remedies.

11. Forecasting the Resolvability of Stellar Velocity Dispersions in Ultra-faint Dwarf Galaxies[2610.24563]
Abstract

Ultra-faint dwarf galaxies (UFDs)—the smallest and most dark-matter dominated galactic systems—push the boundaries of our understanding of galaxy formation in the cold dark matter paradigm. As current and near-future imaging surveys continue to expand the known population of extremely faint systems, spectroscopic resources have become a major limitation in our ability to understand the physical nature of these systems. We present projections for the capability of Keck/DEIMOS, Magellan/IMACS, and future spectroscopic instruments (e.g., Magellan/Via and GMT/GMACS) to kinematically distinguish dark-matter-dominated UFDs from dark-matter-free star clusters in the ultra-faint regime. We simulate ensembles of mock observations of ultra-faint dwarf galaxies and apply a formal statistical framework, calibrated to observations, to systematically characterize the ability to resolve velocity dispersions as a function of satellite properties. Our projections suggest that current instruments will struggle to resolve many of systems that will be discovered by the Rubin Observatory. Near future instruments will be capable of resolving a majority of these systems, though they will fail for the faintest and most distant systems. Ultimately, we will require even more advanced instruments, with enhanced stability and velocity precision, to classify the full population of ultra-faint Milky Way satellites.

12. Galactic warps constrain equivalence-principle violation in the dark sector[2610.24618]
Abstract

Current measurements of the expansion of the Universe allow for the presence of evolving dark energy, distinct from a cosmological constant, with an equation of state that seems to violate the null energy condition. One notable explanation for this behaviour is that the dark energy interacts with the dark matter, violating the equivalence principle yet evading conventional laboratory and astrophysical constraints. Such models produce fifth forces acting only on dark matter. We show how such forces separate halo and stellar disk centres, creating a potential gradient across the disk that warps it into a cup shape. Measuring the warps of $4\times10^3$ edge-on disks in Sloan Digital Sky Survey images and modelling the fifth-force signal together with competing baryonic effects, we find no sign of the predicted correlation between the measured warps and the fifth-force field. This constrains the fifth-force strength relative to gravity to $\Delta G/G_N<7.7\times10^{-4}$ at 95% credibility (or $<1.7\times10^{-3}$ once systematic uncertainties are included) for force ranges of 8 Mpc and above. In the infinite-range limit of a scalar rolling today, even the conservative bound is three times stronger than the tightest cosmological bound (which, unlike ours, depends on the scalar's cosmological evolution) and about two orders of magnitude below other astrophysical bounds. It disfavours at 2-3$\sigma$ the couplings preferred by recent fits of interacting dark energy to cosmological data unless gravitational screening is invoked.

13. Clumpy Outflows from Super-Eddington Accreting Black Holes. II. Conditions for Clump Formation[2610.24660]
Abstract

Active galactic nuclei (AGNs) commonly exhibit ultra-fast outflows (UFOs) with velocities of $0.03$–$0.3~c$. Recent high-resolution X-ray observations with XRISM/Resolve suggest that UFOs may have a clumpy structure. However, the formation and evolution of these clumpy outflows remain poorly understood. In the first paper of this series, we investigated the formation of clumpy outflows using numerical simulations and find that sufficiently high spatial resolution is required to reproduce clumpy structures numerically. In this second paper, we perform a series of numerical experiments to identify the key physical processes governing clump formation. We find that spatial fluctuations of physical quantities at the disk surface are essential for the formation of clumpy outflows, whereas spatial and temporal fluctuations at the inner boundary have only a minor effect. Furthermore, density structure-function analysis suggests that the high-density regions have an elongated morphology with characteristic transverse widths of several tens of $R_{\rm g}$, possibly produced by radiation Rayleigh–Taylor instability. A more comprehensive investigation of the instability in general-relativistic radiation magnetohydrodynamic simulations, where accretion disk structures emerge self-consistently, is expected to provide deeper insights into the physical origin of clumpy outflows.

14. The Impact of an AGN on PAH Spectral Features in Galaxy Evolution Simulations: Continuum Dilution and Excitation of PAHs[2610.24778]
Abstract

Galaxies hosting an active galactic nucleus (AGN) show diminished power and non-standard spectral feature ratios from polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs) compared with normal star-forming galaxies. Proposed mechanisms for this difference include a combination of AGN-driven PAH grain processing, differential heating from the surrounding radiation field, and continuum dilution. These potentially competing factors are difficult to disentangle observationally. In this paper, we investigate the impact of the AGN radiation field on the excitation properties and potential dilution of the mid-IR emission features via constrained numerical experiments of idealized galaxies in simulations that include a self-consistent model for the evolution of dust. We find that, for the integrated spectrum of our simulated Milky Way analog and independent of grain-processing physics, the apparent PAH emission is progressively diminished as AGN luminosity is increased. For spectra within the central half kiloparsec, PAH features are entirely obfuscated when an AGN of any sampled luminosity is present. We additionally find that the UV radiation from an AGN is capable of impacting PAH band ratios, though note that we do not yet include a model for potential photodestruction of PAHs. Our model suggests that the inferred lack of PAHs via featureless mid-IR spectra from galaxies hosting AGN may simply owe to the direct continuum from the AGN overpowering mid-IR features from PAHs in the ISM of the host galaxy. Future work will investigate the relative role of AGN continuum dilution and PAH heating vs photodestruction/ionization of PAHs.

15. The Star Formation Histories of Different Components of M31's Halo from Photometric Estimates of Fe and $α$ Abundances[2610.24915]
Abstract

We present the chemical-abundance distributions and star-formation histories of the M31 halo components using photometric Fe and $\alpha$ abundance estimates derived from the PAndAS broad-band $g$ and $i$ filters and the Subaru/HSC $NB515$ narrow-band filter. We selected about 76,400 candidate M31 giant stars and constructed a synthetic photometric training dataset based on model isochrones. We estimated elemental abundances and errors using random forest regression models. We identify various halo components, including about 32,500 stars in the “smooth" halo (where “smooth" refers to the complete sample with all the known substructures removed), and infer their star-formation histories from comparisons of their abundance distributions. From the radial abundance gradients, we find that [Fe/H] decreases while [$\alpha$/Fe] increases toward the outer halo. Including substructures further enhances the radial abundance contrast, suggesting that the smooth halo stars are accreted at relatively early times, before the progenitor galaxies produce large numbers of Type Ia supernovae. Both M31 and the Milky Way show similar radial gradient trends, but M31 exhibits higher [Fe/H] and lower [$\alpha$/Fe] values overall, suggesting that the smooth halo of M31 may possess a larger fraction of relatively younger stars associated with more recent merger events. Future photometric observations with additional narrow-band filters and larger spectroscopic samples are expected to provide a better understanding of the formation and evolution history of M31.

16. IMF-aware N-body forward modelling of membership selection functions for young open clusters[2610.25200]
Abstract

We present an IMF-aware N-body forward-modelling framework, in which the stellar initial mass function (IMF) is an explicit input, to quantify the membership selection functions of open clusters, namely completeness and contamination as functions of magnitude. Accurate derivation of the IMF requires correcting for these selection effects, which otherwise distort the inferred present-day mass function. Our framework simulates clusters with IMF slopes $\alpha_2 = -2.7, -2.3, -1.9$, converts them into synthetic Gaia observations, adds mock field populations, and recovers members with HDBSCAN to mimic observational membership selection. Applying this framework to 35 young open clusters (ages $\lesssim100$ Myr, distances $\lesssim600$ pc), we find declining completeness towards the faint limit; older, more expanded, and more diffuse models have lower completeness and higher contamination. For a given cluster, models with flatter IMFs yield modestly but systematically lower global completeness and higher contamination, despite initial cluster masses calibrated to the same observed member count. These models undergo more stellar evolution mass loss and expand more, resulting in lower projected stellar densities; their lower completeness is consistent with more diffuse clusters being harder to separate from field stars. Corrections derived from a mismatched IMF largely recover the total star counts but leave a larger distortion in the shape of the present-day luminosity function than those derived with the correct IMF. Because membership recovery is tied to a cluster's structural evolution and the specific membership algorithm used, our findings emphasize that selection functions should be inferred jointly with the IMF rather than assumed in advance.

17. Dynamical response of a protoplanetary disk to an FU Ori-type outburst[2610.25435]
Abstract

Episodic accretion events onto young stars can significantly change the global structure of their surrounding protoplanetary disks. Using radiation hydrodynamical simulations with the HURAKAN code, we study the response of the outer passive disk to a powerful FU Ori-type outburst. Disk heating initiates a factor-of-two increase in the vertical scale-height, which develops on a local keplerian timescale. This effectively launches an outward travelling wave with a bump whose radial position changes with time as $r_{b}\sim t^{2/3}$. After the outburst ends, new waves are launched as the disk structure re-equilibrates toward a quiescent state. The vertical expansion of the disk introduces significant time-variable extinction of the central source emission. Disks with inclinations in the range $40^{\circ}<i<60^{\circ}$ go from weakly ($A_{V}<1$) to a strongly embedded state ($A_{V}\sim10^2$), with the extinction decaying on a $\sim10^2$ yr timescale after the outburst. A fingerprint of the outburst-launched shadowing wave is a long-lived drop or a transient dip in the luminosity curve occurring several years after the outburst begins. This apparent luminosity variation is relatively large, up to $1^m-3^m$ in the V band, and is not related to the intrinsic accretion variability.

18. Connecting galaxy structure, quenching, and halo environments during cosmic noon[2610.25446]
Abstract

The connection between galaxy structure, star formation activity, and large-scale environments provides important insights into the evolution of massive galaxies. However, the relative importance of different galaxy properties in predicting quenching and the environmental dependence of structurally distinct galaxy populations remain poorly constrained at cosmic noon ($1\leq z<3$). In this work, we investigate massive galaxies with $\log M_*/{\rm M}_{\odot}>10.5$ at $1\leq z<3$ using deep JWST/NIRCam imaging from the COSMOS-Web survey, combining measurements of central stellar mass surface density ($\Sigma_1$), star formation activity, and galaxy clustering. We find that $\Sigma_1$ is the most informative predictor of quenching among the considered properties, including stellar mass, effective radius, formation redshift, and local overdensity. The quenched fraction shows a strong dependence on $\Sigma_1$, increasing rapidly above a characteristic $\Sigma_1$. We further show that compact quenched galaxies exhibit a tendency toward stronger clustering than compact and extended star-forming galaxies, while compact and extended star-forming galaxies show similar clustering properties. These results highlight the importance of $\Sigma_1$ in tracing quenching and reveal that structural diversity among star-forming galaxies is not strongly reflected in their large-scale environments, providing new constraints on the interplay between galaxy structure, quenching, and halo environments during cosmic noon.

19. The Next Generation Virgo Cluster Survey (NGVS). XXIX. Demography of Nuclear Star Clusters[2610.25527]
Abstract

Nuclear star clusters (NSCs) provide insights into processes shaping galaxy cores. We use deep $u^*giz$ CFHT imaging from the Next Generation Virgo Cluster Survey (NGVS) to study NSCs in Virgo cluster galaxies. Our parent sample consists of 3680 certain, likely or possible cluster members spanning more than six orders of magnitude in stellar mass. Using a self-consistent classification framework, we identify 1144 candidate nucleated galaxies: 804 high-confidence NSCs and 340 lower-confidence candidates. This is the largest and most uniform NSC catalog for any environment studied to date. We explore the nucleation fraction, $f_{\rm nuc}$, as a function of stellar mass, morphology and environment. Consistent with previous studies, we find stellar mass a key factor driving nucleation probability. However, host-galaxy morphology is also important, with the peak of the $f_{\rm nuc}$ distribution occurring at progressively lower masses as one moves from later to earlier morphological types. We interpret this shift as evidence that the more extended structures of late-type galaxies reduce the efficiency of GC inspiral at fixed stellar mass, while gas-rich secular processes and central star formation may contribute increasingly to NSC growth at higher masses. Environment plays a smaller role, with only a slight enhancement in high-density regions. We characterize the nonlinear relationship between NSC stellar mass and host-galaxy mass, and assess the role of selection effects. We conclude that NSC formation is best explained by a hybrid scenario, with inspiraling star clusters and in situ star formation contributing, and their relative importance dictated primarily by galaxy mass.

20. Reading Between the Lines: How Much Information Remains After Fitting a Stellar Spectrum?[2610.25583]
Abstract

What if we could model 100% of a stellar spectrum? Modern surveys infer stellar properties by comparing observed spectra with synthetic models, yet even the best fits leave structured residuals. We analyse 7087 GALAH survey spectra and ask what information remains after subtracting each best-fitting model. Across 13927 pixels, 4.5% of residuals exceed $3\sigma$, far above 0.3% expected from Gaussian noise. We map the amplitude, significance, sign, and recurrence of these residuals and identify 380 significant regions. Comparison with stellar atlases, line lists, diffuse interstellar bands, and sky diagnostics links most regions to plausible causes: 62% are candidate line-strength or line-profile mismatches, while others trace missing or unidentified lines, interstellar absorption, sky emission, or broader reduction structure. We then train data-driven models on the residual spectra themselves. Residual-only models recover $T_\mathrm{eff}$ to 80-150 K and [Fe/H] to 0.059-0.086 dex, depending on sample quality, and retain weaker, partly indirect information about elemental abundances. Extinction-related coefficients recover the strongest known interstellar features, whereas chromospheric activity leaves plausible Balmer-line signatures but is not recovered robustly out of sample. Most strikingly, the residual flux in 6% of wavelength pixels that were synthesised but excluded from the GALAH DR4 optimisation recovers stellar labels almost as well as the full residual spectrum and better than the remaining 94% alone. Residuals are therefore not merely imperfections to discard: they map missing line data, incomplete physics, unfitted astrophysical components, and observational systematics. Reading between the lines turns one spectroscopist's trash into another one's treasure, and provides a practical route towards extracting more of the information already present in stellar spectra.

21. Velocity-resolved Ionization Mapping of the Broad-line Region. II. Signatures of Eccentric Broad-line Regions from a Quantitative Photoionization-Based Model[2610.25718]
Abstract

Velocity-resolved reverberation mapping (RM) is one of the most powerful tools for probing the geometry and kinematics of the broad-line region in active galactic nuclei. However, its interpretation may be non-unique, as similar velocity-delay signatures can arise from different combinations of BLR geometry and kinematics. To address this degeneracy, this series of studies develops velocity-resolved ionization mapping (IM) as a complementary technique. In this second paper of the series, we construct photoionization-based BLR models to quantitatively demonstrate the diagnostic power of the joint RM+IM framework. We calculate local \ha\ and \hb\ emissivities with Cloudy, couple them with disk-like BLR models, and generate synthetic velocity-dependent line fluxes, lags, and Balmer decrements.
