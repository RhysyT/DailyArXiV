1. Unveiling the Origins of Local Group Galaxies with ELT-SHARP: An Overview of Science Cases[2611.06963]
Abstract

The $\Lambda$CDM paradigm successfully describes large-scale cosmic structure, yet tensions possibly persist on galactic and sub-galactic scales. The Milky Way and the Local Universe offer a uniquely accessible laboratory to probe these discrepancies through chemo-dynamical studies of resolved stellar populations. Understanding disk assembly, dynamical heating, and galactoseismology requires wide-field coverage and high-angular-resolution near-infrared spectroscopy capable of penetrating dust-obscured regions and resolving crowded stellar environments. We propose science cases exploiting \textsc{SHARP}, a multi-mode near-infrared spectrograph for Extremely Large Telescopes and future space facilities. Operating over $0.95$–$2.45\,\mu$m in a single exposure, \textsc{SHARP} enables efficient access to key stellar and gas tracers across the Galactic bulge, disk, halo, and nearby galaxies. The \textsc{NEXUS} mode provides adaptive-optics-assisted multi-object spectroscopy over a $1.2'\times1.2'$ field with spectral resolutions up to $R\sim17{,}000$, enabling gas and stellar kinematics. Complementarily, the \textsc{VESPER} multi-IFU mode delivers spatially resolved spectroscopy in crowded regions, allowing reconstruction of internal kinematics and stellar population gradients on sub-arcsecond scales. Combined with surveys such as \textit{Gaia}, APOGEE, LAMOST, LSST, 4MOST, and ELT–MOSAIC, this project will map six-dimensional phase-space structures of disk and bulge stars, stellar streams, and nearby disk galaxies (e.g. LMC, SMC). The data will enable investigations of bars, spiral arms, warps, flares, and satellite interactions, providing critical constraints on disk evolution, merger history, and dark matter substructure, and providing stringent tests of $\Lambda$CDM on the smallest accessible scales.

2. SHARP(ening the) view of unresolved globular clusters in ultra diffuse galaxies[2611.06966]
Abstract

Ultra-Diffuse Galaxies (UDGs) challenge current galaxy formation models within the {\Lambda}CDM framework, particularly regarding their dark matter content and formation histories. Globular Clusters (GCs) serve as excellent tracers for probing the halos of these faint systems; however, current spectroscopic facilities struggle to reach the depth required to fully characterize GC populations beyond the local neighborhood. In this work, we present the scientific potential of NEXUS@SHARP, a near-infrared multi-slit spectrograph proposed on the upcoming Extremely Large Telescope (ELT). We demonstrate that NEXUS is uniquely positioned to overcome current observational limitations by combining high spatial resolution with efficient multiplexing. We show that NEXUS can efficiently help to characterize the GC population in UDGs across diverse environments out to 60 to 70 Mpc - with a few hours of ELT time. These capabilities will allow for precise measurements of radial velocities and stellar populations, enabling robust dynamical mass estimates and distinguishing between competing UDG formation scenario.

3. Black Hole Seeds and Little Red Dots: Connecting Formation and Growth with a JWST Multi-Cycle Treasury Program[2611.07006]
Abstract

JWST has revealed an abundant population of faint active galactic nuclei and a new class of compact, high-redshift sources known as Little Red Dots (LRDs), whose physical nature remains debated, with accreting massive black holes among the leading explanations. The emerging demographics of early black holes, with their high inferred number densities and, in some systems, the apparent excess of black hole mass relative to stellar mass, challenge simple scenarios of black hole seeding and growth. Despite these observational breakthroughs, the origin of early massive black holes remains uncertain, because "black holes have no memory": current samples cannot separate the imprint of seeding from subsequent growth. This white paper identifies the multi-cycle JWST observations needed to revolutionize our understanding of early black hole growth by: (i) distinguishing seeding pathways, (ii) constraining early black hole growth mechanisms, (iii) resolving the physical nature of broad-line emitters and LRDs, and (iv) testing black hole-galaxy co-evolution. These tasks require uniformly selected samples extending to low black hole and host-galaxy masses, allowing self-consistent measurements of black hole mass functions, Eddington-ratio distributions, and host properties. Grating spectroscopy, deep MIRI imaging, and variability analysis through repeated observations of the same fields would provide complementary tests of black hole formation and growth, their connection to host-galaxy evolution, and the nature of newly discovered populations of LRDs. In this white paper, we argue that these joint tests require a coordinated JWST Multi-Cycle Treasury program; such a program could provide the depth, uniformity, and time baseline needed to place substantially stronger, complementary constraints on early black hole formation, growth, and their connection to galaxy evolution.

4. The obscured nucleus of NGC3256 S. I. Anisotropic AGN near-IR radiation field traced by CO v=1-0 with JWST/NIRSpec[2611.07020]
Abstract

The southern nucleus of NGC3256, a nearby luminous infrared (IR) interacting system (d=40 Mpc; L(IR)=10^11.6 Lsun), hosts a deeply obscured low-luminosity active galactic nucleus (AGN) associated with a massive and fast bipolar collimated molecular outflow. JWST/NIRSpec observations revealed bright spatially resolved ro-vibrational CO v=1-0 4.67um emission at the base of this molecular outflow extending out to  300pc, which is excited by the  4.7um IR radiation from the southern dusty core. Using Monte Carlo radiative transfer (MCRT) models, we analyzed the CO v=1-0 band emission to determine the intensity of the  4.7um IR radiation field across the outflow base. We find that the core spectral luminosity density at  4.7um, as seen by the material in the outflow base, is  2-4 times higher than that inferred along our line of sight, indicating an anisotropic near-IR radiation field. Moreover, the slower than d^-2 decrease of the near-IR flux density across the outflow base suggests a collimated radiation field, for which we estimate a small half-opening angle (15-20deg) and a diameter of the collimating structure of 15 +- 5 pc. This is slightly smaller than the diameter of the obscuring envelope (25 +- 5 pc). Combined with the expansion of this obscuring envelope, traced by the blueshifted CO v=1-0 absorption toward the nucleus, this suggests that the AGN is beginning to clear its environment. We estimate N_H 10^22-23 cm^-2 for NGC 3256 S, which is similar to that of Type 2 AGN. However, in contrast to Type 2 AGN, a high covering factor of the obscuring material, possibly sustained by inflows linked to the galaxy merger, and similar to that expected in deeply obscured compact obscured nuclei (CONs), is needed to explain the absence of extended high-ionization emission. Therefore, NGC 3256 S shares properties with CONs and Type 2 AGN.

5. Why a Multi-Cycle Treasury Program is Essential to Maximize JWST's Scientific Contributions to Galaxy Formation[2611.07027]
Abstract

The Genesis Initiative is an extragalactic community-wide effort to coordinate white papers in response to the STScI Multi-Cycle Treasury (MCT) concept call. Two of JWST's primary science goals are to discover and characterize the first stars and galaxies, and to understand how they evolved into the galaxies we see today - questions whose fundamental importance has been affirmed by the past three decadal surveys. JWST's first five years have surpassed expectations, revealing a bounty of unexpected discoveries about the early universe. Yet these discoveries have raised as many questions as they have answered, and key mission goals remain unmet. Transforming this excitement into a quantitative physical understanding of early galaxy formation requires a uniform, statistically controlled legacy dataset that the current portfolio of smaller, heterogeneous programs cannot provide. The history of the Hubble Space Telescope offers a clear precedent. Its most transformative extragalactic results came from large, coordinated surveys far exceeding what the standard time-allocation process typically approves, and these uniform datasets remain scientifically productive decades later. We argue that JWST is now poised for the same leap. Answering the field's central open questions demands coordinated, community-led surveys of  1000-2000 hr, with uniform and well-characterized selection functions that are structurally impossible within single-cycle programs. Such an investment is time-critical while JWST's instruments remain at peak performance, and would maximize synergy with ALMA, Euclid, Roman, Rubin, and future facilities. We therefore urge STScI to open a MCT channel, enabling the bold, ambitious surveys needed to understand how galaxies evolve from first light to the present day.

6. AGN Archaeology: A Systematic Search for Fading AGN in MaNGA[2611.07028]
Abstract

Active galactic nuclei (AGN) vary on broad timescales, but are primarily thought to "flicker" on and off during episodes of high and low accretion. Supermassive black holes (SMBHs) which are actively accreting material often show optical spectra dominated by AGN emission in their nuclei and beyond. In this work we develop an empirical model to quantify the fractional AGN contribution to optical spectra using the Baldwin-Phillips-Terlevich (BPT) emission line diagnostic diagram. We leverage this model against integral field unit (IFU) data from the Mapping Nearby Galaxies at Apache Point Observatory (MaNGA) survey to select 34 galaxies with weak nuclear AGN activity relative to their AGN signatures at large radii; this signifies past AGN activity which is fading with time (i.e., fading-AGN). The majority of our fading-AGN reside in edge-on systems undergoing minor mergers. We construct a simple model consisting of a disk galaxy with ionization cones and find that the orientation of such a system will impact its BPT classification in both single-fiber and IFU observations. The mean distance to the extended AGN emission in our sample corresponds to a light-travel time of $1.1\times10^4$ years. We use this to set a lower-limit on the typical lifetime of the AGN phase ($t_{\text{AGN}}>1.3\times10^5$ years) which is consistent with SMBHs growing via brief accretion bursts. These fading-AGN allow us to probe the elusive period of post-AGN galaxy evolution, and they give a $\sim10^4$ year baseline for understanding the accretion history in these systems.

7. A multi-epoch VLBI study of compact symmetric objects as contaminants in the search for milli-lenses[2611.07029]
Abstract

The Lambda Cold Dark Matter model predicts a high abundance of low-mass dark matter halos, which can be probed through milliarcsecond-scale gravitational lensing (milli-lensing) of background radio-loud active galactic nuclei (AGNs). The Search for MIlli-LEnses (SMILE) project aims to find these rare systems with Very Long Baseline Interferometry (VLBI). However, compact symmetric objects (CSOs) can closely resemble milli-lenses in VLBI images and are the main contaminant in such searches. We aim to determine the nature of the nine remaining milli-lens candidates of the SMILE pilot study and to characterize their physical properties. We analyzed all available multi-epoch, multi-frequency archival VLBI data, new HSA and EVN observations, and single-dish and connected-element interferometer measurements. We tested the lensing hypothesis through the morphology and spectra of the sources, and characterized their kinematics, spatially resolved spectral indices, magnetic fields, and integrated spectra, using synchrotron self-absorption (SSA) and free-free absorption (FFA) models. We identify eight of the nine candidates as CSOs, four of them for the first time. Spectral index asymmetries between components and previously undetected emission connecting them rule out the lensing hypothesis. Only J0923-3435 remains ambiguous. For the CSOs with known redshifts, the lobes have equipartition magnetic fields of $\sim$20-320 mG and projected separations of $\sim$60-240 pc, and show no measurable expansion, implying kinematic ages of at least $\sim$50-950 yr. The integrated spectra of J0210-2213 and J2347-1856 favor an inhomogeneous external free-free absorber over homogeneous and inhomogeneous SSA models. Since CSOs will be the main contaminant in the full SMILE survey, applying this framework will provide the same constraints for a much larger sample of compact radio-loud AGNs.

8. DOWSING, Dust Observations With SCUBA-2 In Nearby Galaxies: I. Dust in M81[2611.07044]
Abstract

We present a resolved far-infrared/sub-millimetre study of dust in the nearby spiral galaxy M81 using Herschel PACS/SPIRE and JCMT SCUBA-2 observations from 70 to $850\,\mu\mathrm{m}$. We model the dust emission using both single-temperature modified blackbody (SMBB) fits and point process mapping (PPMAP), comparing pixel-by-pixel SED-fitting with image cubes of dust surface density as a function of position and temperature. For fixed $\beta = 1.9$ and a dust opacity of $\kappa_{350\,\mu\mathrm{m}} = 1.92\,\mathrm{cm}^{2}\,\mathrm{g}^{-1}$, SMBB gives an integrated dust mass of $(1.77 \pm 0.09) \times 10^{7}\,M_\odot$, while PPMAP gives $(2.65 \pm 0.13) \times10^{7}\,M_\odot$. The higher PPMAP mass is expected because real lines of sight contain dust over a range of temperatures, whereas SMBB assumes a single effective temperature and can underestimate the dust mass. Both methods show enhanced dust surface densities along the spiral arms and a radially declining dust temperature, with the warmest dust in the central region. Variable-$\beta$ fits indicate only moderate spatial variation in $\beta$ for well-constrained pixels, but the strong $T$-$\beta$ degeneracy motivates a fixed emissivity index for the main analysis. Combining the dust maps with HI data, we find a positive radial gas-to-dust ratio gradient, with $d\log_{10}(\mathrm{GDR})/dR = 0.065\,\mathrm{dex}\,\mathrm{kpc}^{-1}$. Comparison with M81's metallicity gradient implies a declining dust-to-metals ratio, potentially consistent with more efficient grain growth in the inner disc. The star formation rate is modest, $\sim 0.27\,M_\odot\,{\rm yr}^{-1}$, and is strongly concentrated along the spiral arms. These results suggest that the dust, gas, and star-forming structures of M81 are strongly shaped by its spiral arms, while the central region hosts the warmest dust in the disc.

9. The HST-Hyperion Survey: X-ray AGN Enhancement in the Hyperion Proto-supercluster[2611.07047]
Abstract

Dense environments can influence both galaxy evolution and supermassive black hole growth, but whether they enhance or suppress (or both) active galactic nuclei (AGN) activity at high redshift ($z\gtrsim2$) remains uncertain. As one of the most massive structures identified in the early universe, the Hyperion proto-supercluster at $z\approx2.5$ provides a unique opportunity to investigate this connection during an epoch near the peak of both cosmic star formation rate and AGN activity density. To examine the impact of galaxy environment on AGN activity, we measure the fraction of X-ray-selected AGN in Hyperion as a whole, its highly overdense peaks, and its outskirts, comparing these measurements to a coeval field sample. We identify X-ray AGN using the deep Chandra COSMOS-Legacy Survey and combine optical/near-infrared (NIR) photometry and photometric redshift information from COSMOS2020 with extensive ground-based spectroscopy and HST-grism observations. Redshift information is incorporated statistically using a Monte Carlo framework. We find a higher X-ray AGN fraction in Hyperion ($7.4^{+3.2}_{-1.8}\%$) than in the coeval field ($3.2^{+0.6}_{-0.5}\%$), corresponding to a $\sim2.3\sigma$ difference. Strikingly, this enhancement is concentrated in the overdense peaks, where the X-ray AGN fraction reaches $12.3^{+5.5}_{-3.4}\%$ ($15.0^{+6.9}_{-4.4}\%$ in the most massive peaks versus $6.2^{+11.9}_{-2.1}\%$ in the less massive peaks), while the lower-density Hyperion outskirts ($2.1^{+3.6}_{-1.1}\%$) show no evidence of enhancement relative to the field. These results add to the limited but growing evidence for enhanced AGN activity in high-redshift protostructures, suggesting that the dense environments at these epochs may preferentially trigger AGN during the early stages of cluster assembly.

10. The ALPINE-CRISTAL-JWST Survey: The Tully-Fisher Relation at $z = 4-6$[2611.07064]
Abstract

The Tully-Fisher relation provides a fundamental link between galaxy mass and their kinematics. Although its evolution is relatively well constrained up to cosmic noon, observational probes at higher redshifts remain scarce. In this work, we investigate the stellar and baryonic Tully-Fisher relations (TFRs) at $z=4-6$ by analyzing JWST/NIRSpec IFU observations of 18 main-sequence star-forming galaxies belonging to the ALPINE-CRISTAL-JWST survey. We apply a morpho-kinematic disk classification scheme, finding five disk-like galaxies and a survey disk fraction of $0.28\pm0.11$. These disk galaxies span stellar masses of $10^{9.5-10.5}\,M_\odot$, intrinsic rotational velocities of $91-127\,{\rm km\,s^{-1}}$, and intrinsic velocity dispersions of $66-129\,{\rm km\,s^{-1}}$. For both versions of the TFR, we find that the ALPINE-CRISTAL-JWST disks lie below the local relations and are likely offset from those reported at cosmic noon ($z\approx2.3$). By fixing the TFR slope to the $z=0$ value, we measure zero-point offsets for the stellar-mass TFR of $-0.96\pm0.18$ dex and $-0.50\pm0.18$ dex relative to the local Universe and cosmic noon, respectively. For the baryonic TFR, we find zero-point offsets of $-0.57\pm0.20$ dex and $-0.30\pm0.20$ dex with respect to the local Universe and cosmic noon relations. Our measurements are consistent with the redshift evolution of the zero-points of both TFRs being driven by the evolution of dark matter halo properties with cosmic time, modulated by a reduced contribution of dark matter to the dynamical mass on the galaxy scale.

11. On the importance of medium-resolution continuum spectroscopy for JWST's extragalactic legacy[2611.07070]
Abstract

We argue for a significant investment of JWST time into an extremely deep medium-resolution NIRSpec MSA survey targeting a large, representative sample of high-redshift $(z>1.5)$ galaxies, specifically intended to obtain high-SNR continuum detections. This is only possible with JWST, and no planned future instrumentation has similar capabilities, meaning that obtaining such observations with JWST must be urgently prioritised. The vast majority of existing archival NIRSpec observations are not deep enough for continuum science, being focused on the detection of emission lines. Deeper continuum data opens up the ability to constrain a much wider range of key galaxy physical parameters, including stellar population ages and metallicities, stellar kinematics and neutral gas physical properties, inflows and outflows. Such measurements will provide crucial insight into quenching physics, galaxy dynamical masses, merger histories, pristine early star-formation, reionization and the physics of Little Red Dots, as well as enabling all of the emission-line science that can be achieved with shallower observations. To facilitate future survey planning, we present an empirically derived relationship between rest-optical continuum SNR in JWST NIRSpec medium-resolution data, rest-optical apparent magnitude and exposure time, which we show to be independent of redshift (and hence the grating used). We then provide a 1000-hour sketch survey plan based on achieving the continuum SNR $=20$/pixel necessary to constrain key physical properties and scaling relations for a large galaxy sample at $z>1.5$. We finally discuss NIRSpec sky subtraction strategies, which are of critical importance for continuum SNR.

13. Detection of Differential Chromatic Refraction with LSST Camera data[2611.07161]
Abstract

The Earth's atmosphere acts like a prism, deflecting blue light towards the zenith more than red light. Airmass-dependent changes in astrometry can therefore be used to infer additional information about the wavelength distribution of light within a photometric passband. The Legacy Survey of Space and Time (LSST) will observe each point in the southern sky several hundred times, with observations spread across a range of airmasses. Using commissioning data from the LSST camera released in LSST Data Preview 2, we report the detection of this differential chromatic refraction (DCR) effect in observations of galaxies and quasars in the COSMOS field. We show how information from DCR can complement the six-band colour information from LSST and help break degeneracies when selecting extragalactic samples and estimating their photometric redshifts. DCR will be a valuable resource for scientific studies and care should be taken not to remove useful DCR information from future LSST data releases.

14. A Machine Learning Approach to Coupling Photoionization & Multi-Frequency Radiation Hydrodynamics[2611.07162]
Abstract

We use machine learning (ML) to couple photoionization microphysics to radiation hydrodynamic simulations of line-driven winds. ML models are trained on outputs from the photoionization code XSTAR to predict the line-driving force multiplier as a function of gas ionization, optical depth, and the blackbody temperature of the irradiating spectral energy distribution (SED). We implement the trained models within Athena++, enabling rapid runtime evaluation of the radiative acceleration without directly solving the photoionization problem during the hydrodynamic evolution. We find that random forest models provide the highest accuracy and reproduce the force multipliers over a three-dimensional parameter space more accurately, and with less training data, than traditional precomputed tables using trilinear interpolation. Because the framework treats the force multiplier as a function of the evolving local SED, it can be generalized to more complex radiation fields and arbitrary spectral shapes. More broadly, this work demonstrates how ML can serve as a computational framework for embedding expensive microphysical calculations within large-scale dynamical simulations.

15. Formamide formation on forsterite and Mg(OH)$_2\cdot$H$_2$O[2611.07172]
Abstract

Context. Formamide formation in the Universe has been the subject of debate due to its importance as a precursor to amino acids and RNA. Current studies focus on the gas-phase and icy grain formation routes. However, formamide formation on bare silicate surfaces or hydrated Mg-bearing molecular models remains largely unexplored in environments relevant to protoplanetary disks. Aims. This study presents a quantum-chemical investigation of formamide formation on an Mg center and a silicate surface. $Mg(OH)_2\cdot H_2O$ and the bare (010) forsterite ($Mg_2SiO_4$) surface, used as an analog of a bare dust-grain surface, may promote formamide formation via $NH_2$ addition to $H_2CO$. Methods. I employed density functional theory (DFT) to study the addition of $NH_2$ to $H_2CO$ with an isolated $Mg(OH)_2\cdot H_2O$ molecular model and on the forsterite (010) surface. First, I benchmarked various theoretical methodologies. I then examined the ability of $Mg(OH)_2\cdot H_2O$ and the forsterite (010) surface to promote formamide formation. Finally, for the case with an entrance barrier, I computed unimolecular rate constants and half-lives to assess its kinetic behavior. Results. $Mg(OH)_2\cdot H_2O$ promotes proton transfer within the $NH_2$-$CH_2O$ intermediate via the O-H group coordinated with Mg following $NH_2$ addition to $H_2CO$, whereas on the forsterite, a barrier-less addition pathway is found when $NH_2$ is initially physisorbed close to the formaldehyde center, followed by proton transfer leading to formamide formation. Conclusions. The Mg-bearing model systems considered here can promote rapid proton transfer following $NH_2$ addition to $H_2CO$. For the $Mg(OH)_2\cdot H_2O$ model, the reaction step from the pre-reactive complex is predicted to occur on nanosecond timescales at temperatures approaching 300 K. The present work provides kinetic data for astrochemical models.

Abstract

We present CosmicGrain, a dust physics module built on Gadget-4 that models explicit dust superparticles that experience resolved gas-dust drag and radiation pressure, undergo collisional heating and cooling with gas, and are further shaped by subgrid treatments for grain growth, thermal sputtering, coagulation, shattering, supernova shock destruction, and astration. We run an eleven-rung physics ladder (S0-S10) at 1024^3 resolution to isolate the contribution of individual processes to a Milky Way-mass halo's dust population. 17. GetGaia 2.0: automatic membership and systemic motions of stellar systems with Gaia[2611.07227]
Abstract

We present GetGaia 2.0, a public Python package that retrieves the Gaia data around a stellar system and selects its member stars automatically. The membership is a probabilistic model fitted by expectation maximization that combines the astrometry of every source, with its full covariance, the color-magnitude diagram, the sky distribution and, when available, the line-of-sight velocities, from Gaia or from an external catalog. The local systematic errors of the Gaia reference frame are measured on the quasars of each field. The code returns the systemic proper motion (PM) and parallax with their full covariance, a detection significance, a contamination estimate and the structural parameters of the system. We validate it on synthetic catalogs, where it recovers the members with 90-100 percent purity, and run it on Gaia DR3 for the known dwarf galaxies of the Local Group within 850 kpc and for 95 Galactic globular clusters, in tiers that add one piece of literature information at a time. It measures the systemic PMs of 55 dwarf galaxies and 93 globular clusters at a significance of at least $3\sigma$; 29 of the dwarfs and 86 of the clusters are recovered from their position alone. The dwarfs include every Milky Way satellite within 300 kpc brighter than $M_V=-5.4$, ultra-faint systems down to $M_V=-0.8$, and nine galaxies at 370-820 kpc. The PMs agree with the published values within 0.1 mas/yr for 47 of the dwarfs and for all the clusters (median differences of 0.032 and 0.009 mas/yr), with uncertainties equal to those of dedicated studies, and none deviates by more than $3\sigma$. The Gaia line-of-sight velocities alone give the systemic velocity of 71 clusters, within 1 km/s of spectroscopy. GetGaia 2.0 delivers, without manual intervention, the systemic motions and the members of the stellar systems within the reach of Gaia, and is ready for the forthcoming Gaia data releases.

18. Dust Survival in Lensed Quiescent Galaxies at $z>1$ Depends on How They Quench[2611.07237]
Abstract

The interstellar medium of massive quiescent galaxies holds key clues to how they quench, yet remain poorly understood. Although dust is expected to be depleted rapidly ($\lesssim$100 Myr) after quenching, some quiescent galaxies host substantial dust reservoirs, raising the question of whether dust survives quenching or is replenished afterward. We present ALMA Band 6 continuum observations ($\lambda_{\mathrm{rest}}$$>$250$\mu$m) of eight strongly-lensed, spectroscopically-selected quiescent galaxies at z$\sim$1-2.5 from the COOL-LAMPS and REQUIEM surveys, plus five archival REQUIEM systems. The sample spans more than two orders of magnitude in dust-to-stellar-mass ratio ($f_{\mathrm{dust}}<10^{-5}$ to $>10^{-3}$), and two galaxies host extended, resolved dust. Comparing $f_{\mathrm{dust}}$ with time since quenching, roughly half the sample, preferentially at higher redshift, is consistent with rapid quenching and harsh dust destruction, while lower-redshift, more slowly quenched systems are consistent with milder destruction, possibly reflecting an evolving dominant quenching mode. The two most dust-rich galaxies exceed even the mildest model destruction tracks, with J1201-3952 retaining the highest $f_{\mathrm{dust}}$ $\sim$1.8 Gyr after quenching. If these systems are truly quiescent, their dust masses approach maximum stellar yields, pointing to ISM grain regrowth and/or TP-AGB dust production. However due to our single-band continuum data, $f_{\mathrm{dust}}$ is uncertain ($\pm$0.3 dex systematic uncertainty from the unknown dust temperature) and we cannot rule out obscured star formation in these two galaxies. Our results suggest that dust survival depends on how galaxies quench: rapid, harsh quenching efficiently destroys dust, while slower, milder quenching can leave dust reservoirs that persist or are replenished long after star formation ceases.

19. The 10 Mpc Volume[2611.07284]
Abstract

Accurate distances are now known for most unobscured galaxies brighter than MB = -13 mag within 10 Mpc from Hubble Space Telescope observations of their luminous stars. Complementary mid-infrared observations with the Wide-field Infrared Explorer provide global stellar mass estimates for these galaxies. Consequences are detailed knowledge of the properties of the Universe in a volume-limited space. Galaxies tend to lie within groups, within filaments. The filaments experience bulk motions in response to well defined gravitational attractors such as the Virgo Cluster and repulsors such as the Local Void. The groups are inevitably dominated by a single galaxy, with 10 galaxies between one and nine Mpc containing most of the stellar mass within the sample. There is a curious lack of galaxies in an interval fainter than these dominant galaxies. The dominant galaxies in halos tend to have gas reservoirs and ongoing star formation, a property extending to dwarf galaxies in separate halos. Secondary galaxies in halos are frequently gas depleted and lack evidence of star formation. With one notable exception, all galaxies in the complete sample have well formed red giant branches indicative of early-onset star formation.

20. Magnetic contribution to outer-disk rotation in the spiral galaxy NGC 5055[2611.07332]
Abstract

We test whether fields of galactic strength enhance NGC 5055's outer gas rotation. Finite analytic disk decompositions yield consistent rotation–density pairs and a blended gravitational reference approximately retaining inner rotation while its density approaches the measured outer HI+He profile. This reference is modelled, not an independently measured stellar curve. Its difference from fitted gas rotation gives the required purely toroidal field for circular, axisymmetric midplane motion. The prescribed field strength at the reconstruction's inner radial boundary is $B_s$. With hydrogen inertia, no pressure and $B_s=0$, constant exponential heights of 1 kpc and 250 pc give approximate peaks of 5.47 and 10.95 $\mu$G near 34.9 kpc; 0.2 kpc gives approximately 12.24 $\mu$G. Extrapolated flaring exponential and Gaussian profiles peak at approximately 5.25 and 5.96 $\mu$G, respectively, near 33.8 kpc. For $B_s=$8 $\mu$G, their fields decline overall to approximately 5.73 and 6.30 $\mu$G near 39.7 kpc. These estimates are conditional on the gravitational reference, vertical structure and prescribed boundary field. Comparison with total radio fields supports the field scale without establishing a magnetic origin for the rotation excess.

21. The Milky Way-like radial structure in nearby galaxies[2611.07338]
Abstract

The Milky Way exhibits a distinctive plateau in its inner surface brightness profile around one effective radius, challenging the traditional view of a purely exponential disk. To explore the origin of this feature, we identify 65 nearby galaxies ($<50$ Mpc) with similar plateau structures and analyze their multi-band surface brightness and color profiles using SDSS, DESI, 2MASS, and Mephisto data. Of the 65 galaxies with Milky Way-like plateaus, 42 have bar classifications (25 barred, 17 non-barred). Barred galaxies show radial color variations more consistent with stellar population ageing, while non-barred galaxies align more with the dust reddening direction, suggesting dust attenuation contributes to plateau formation in non-barred systems. The broad range of color gradients among barred galaxies suggests bars influence plateaus through multiple pathways, including stellar population variations and dynamical stellar-mass redistribution. Environment may also contribute: both barred and non-barred galaxies tend to reside in denser environments than a comparison sample without plateaus. The low occurrence rate ($\sim$3.6\%) may indicate that plateau structures mark a relatively uncommon evolutionary stage. The Milky Way, with both strong stellar population dependence and a persistent stellar mass profile feature, may represent an intermediate case where both stellar population variations and dynamical processes associated with the bar shape its plateau.

Abstract

The next generation of galaxy surveys will bring a plethora of new observations, a vast majority of which will be observed with a few photometric broadbands, limiting the information we can infer from traditional SED fitting methods. To maximize the scientific value of these surveys, techniques that can go beyond traditional summary statistics in extracting information from galaxy observations are needed. Moving beyond integrated photometry, we introduce the Katachi-PRIME method to do spectral energy distribution (SED) based inference that jointly incorporates morphological and SED information. Katachi-PRIME uses implicit likelihood inference (ILI) to incorporate realistic morphological priors from gri broadband images, to make predictions of: stellar mass (M$_*$), specific star formation rate (sSFR), redshift (z), age (t$_{50}$), dust attenuation (Av), and gas-phase metallicity (12+log(O/H)) of the galaxy. We find that this improves constraints compared to traditional 5-band photometry. We then use the learned morphological priors to examine the extent to which morphology encodes information about past star formation, finding a stark dichotomy in how morphology encodes star formation history. While the stable morphologies of main-sequence and quiescent galaxies can predict past star formation out to $\sim 4-7$ Gyr and $\sim3-5$ Gyr, respectively, starburst and green-valley populations above and below the main sequence tend to experience structural disruptions that obscure all but the most recent star formation activity. Methods like Katachi-PRIME maximize the scientific value of future telescopes by providing broad-baseline SED-level property estimates by incorporating morphological information.

26. Violent relaxation in hierarchical stellar systems: pathways toward massive black hole formation in the early Universe[2611.07546]
Abstract

Massive stellar systems have been detected in the high-redshift Universe via the James Webb Space Telescope using gravitational lensing techniques. Such stellar systems may evolve on short dynamical timescales, despite a formally large 2-body relaxation time. If the stellar system consists of a large number of gravitationally bound clusters, a 2-body relaxation time can be defined for the star clusters in analogy to the 2-body relaxation time for stars, implying a faster evolution determined by the number of stars per cluster. At their formation, clusters will go through a process of "cold collapse" as part of their early, non-collisional, evolution. Abstract

We present the results of multi-epoch very long baseline interferometry (VLBI) observations of the sub-parsec-scale northern jet component of 3C 84 (known as the N1 component) at 7 mm from 2016 to 2021. The peak position of the N1 component exhibits positional variations of approximately 0.2 mas at a distance of about 2.5 mas from the central radio core (C1) throughout the observation period. These results provide new constraints on the physical origin of N1 and on the circumnuclear environment of 3C 84.

We present a large language model (LLM) system that monitors arXiv, extracts limit curves, integrates them as code, and opens pull requests for human review. Extraction uses a tool-using agent that interprets physical conventions and checks its results, with a fixed multi-channel pipeline evaluated as an alternative. We evaluate both approaches on a 329-paper benchmark of measured limits graded against the upstream-curated repository. An extraction is considered successful when the extracted and reference mass ranges overlap by more than 90%, with at least 90% of compared values agreeing within a factor of two. For Fable 5, 52.6% of eligible curve comparisons meet this criterion with the agent, compared with 20.9% with the pipeline. Deployed, the pipeline has generated limit proposals, but none have merged: governance of AI-generated scientific data is itself unsolved.
