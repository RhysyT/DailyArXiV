1. Interpreting JWST UV luminosity functions and angular correlation functions via a "universal language" for high-redshift galaxies[2611.18850]
Abstract

The James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) found more bright $z\gtrsim10$ galaxies than expected before its launch. No consensus has emerged on whether difficulties in reproducing them stem from missing physics, simplifying assumptions that break down at the highest redshifts, or model mis-calibration. This is complicated because most galaxy models cannot be compared like-to-like, and models invoking different physical processes can make similar predictions for compressed summaries such as ultraviolet luminosity functions (UV LFs). Here we describe galaxy populations in a universal language: a chain of physically and empirically motivated conditional distributions linking halo mass, stellar mass, star formation rate and UV magnitude, defined consistently across models and data. We build a broad prior from fits to five hydrodynamic simulations, which predict very different high-redshift galaxy populations. We infer the parameters of this flexible galaxy model from $z>6$ JWST observations of UV LFs and angular correlation functions (ACFs). Including the mass-complete ACFs disfavors a large scatter around the stellar-to-halo mass relation (SHMR). With simulation-based priors, the star-forming main sequence (SFMS) is described by a star-formation timescale of about one halo dynamical time, and the scatter around the SFMS increases toward lower stellar masses. The SFMS scatter is the main source of stochasticity in UV magnitude at fixed halo mass, accounting for a median $\sim$61% of the variance at $M_{\rm UV}=-20$ (SHMR scatter: $\sim$36%). Bayesian model selection finds no evidence for redshift evolution of the SHMR or for an SFMS slope different from unity. At $z\sim20$, our fiducial model predicts UV LFs orders of magnitude below estimates from photometric candidates. A consensus on the discrepant UV LF estimates at $z\geq14$ is needed to determine whether model extensions are required.

2. Mid-Infrared Variability as a Probe of Hidden AGN Activity: A Comparison of Seyfert 1 and Seyfert 2 AGNs in the BAT 58-Month Sample[2611.18904]
Abstract

In accordance with the AGN Unification model, the optical variability of Seyfert 2s is expected to be suppressed because the central engine is obscured by a dusty torus. The absorbed UV/optical radiation is reprocessed and re-emitted in longer wavelengths including the mid-infrared (MIR), making MIR variability a promising probe of obscured AGNs. We investigate the optical ASAS-SN and MIR NEOWISE variability of Seyfert 1 and Seyfert 2 AGNs from the \textit{Swift} 58-month BAT catalog, comparing both populations against non-variable galaxies. One of the first systematic studies of MIR AGN variability, we detected significant variability for Sy1s and the majority of Sy2s. 65% of Sy2s exhibit variability as significant as Sy1s in the MIR, especially in the W2 band, indicating torus dominated MIR variability. Additionally, 22% of Sy2s show higher variability compared to non-variable sources in the W2 band with p-value of $7.1\times 10^{-15}$. Demonstrating MIR variability as an important method to probe the variability of torus and select obscured AGN. Finally, we perform dust reverberation mapping on the Sy1 sample between the optical and W1 or W2 light curves, with measured time delays between 22-786 days (0.02-0.61 pc) for the hot dust in the inner regions of the torus. Our measured torus radius-luminosity slope, $R \propto L^{0.42\pm0.05}$, agrees with literature results. Combining the W1 and W2 band lags with archival NIR measurements, we measured the temperature profile of the torus with $T \propto R^{-\alpha}$, where $\alpha$ values range from 0.3-1.1.

3. Quantifying the Dominance and Emergence of Brightest Cluster Galaxies in SPT and Planck Clusters: A Novel Application of the Gini Index[2611.18929]
Abstract

We introduce the Gini index (G) as a metric for studying the emergence of brightest cluster galaxies (BCGs) by quantifying the disparity in the light distribution of galaxy cluster cores. The Gini index collapses the skewness of the bright-end luminosity function to a single statistic, and provides a measure for the light dominance of the BCG over other cluster members that is more robust than the magnitude gap. We argue that the Gini index is far less sensitive to short-term changes in the cluster dynamical state than the magnitude gap and solves its known degeneracies by taking the entire cluster membership into account, making G a more sensitive metric to long-term BCG assembly. We apply the magnitude gap and Gini index to a progenitor-selected sample of 92 South Pole Telescope- and Planck-selected cluster cores at 0 < z < 1 with corresponding Hubble Space Telescope F110W and F200LP and Sloan Digital Sky Survey g and r photometry. Based on both parametric and empirical tests, we find no statistical evidence for evolution in G or $\rm M_{1,2}$ over 0 < z < 1, and find that BCGs have not grown significantly (2${\times}$) faster than the cluster population for z < 1 at 3${\sigma}$ confidence. This implies that BCGs achieve dominance and establish defining properties early (z > 1) in massive clusters. In general, we find that the Gini index is a valuable and relatively straightforward measurement, with well-controlled systematics, that could be applied to much larger samples to study BCG dominance and emergence.

5. Gradients of Turbulent Fluctuations as New Tracers of Astrophysical Magnetic Fields[2611.18980]
Abstract

Magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) turbulence is ubiquitous in astrophysical plasmas and produces scale-dependent anisotropy with respect to the local magnetic field. Because turbulence cascades preferentially perpendicular to the field, its spatial gradients acquire preferred orientations that can be used to probe magnetic-field structure. This property underlies a family of gradient techniques based on spectroscopic, synchrotron-intensity, and synchrotron-polarization observations. In this review, we summarize the theoretical foundation of these techniques, including the anisotropy of MHD turbulence and the corresponding behavior of velocity, magnetic-field, and density fluctuations. We then describe how these fluctuations are encoded in spectroscopic data and synchrotron observables, and review the implementation of Velocity Centroid Gradients, Velocity Channel Gradients, Intensity Gradients, Synchrotron Intensity Gradients, and Synchrotron Polarization Gradients. We also discuss the effects of isotropic fast modes, shocks, self-gravity, stellar outflows, thermal broadening, and self-absorption. Finally, we review applications to the diffuse interstellar medium, star-forming regions, the Galactic center, external galaxies, and galaxy clusters.

6. Nearby M&M Binaries Uncovered with the Gaia Multipeak Fraction and Speckle Interferometry[2611.19048]
Abstract

Multipeak fraction is a strong indicator of stellar multiplicity with subarcsecond angular separation in the Gaia DR3 catalog. We select 18 sources with multipeak fraction above 5% among stars possibly in the 20 pc solar neighborhood with as yet unconfirmed binarity. We successfully resolve eleven systems of M-type dwarf components in speckle interferometric observations with a 2.5 m telescope at the Caucasian Mountain Observatory of Sternberg Astronomical Institute, Moscow State University. The tentatively estimated companion masses lie in the 0.09 – 0.23 $M_\odot$ range. All six sources with a multipeak fraction of 6–8% are unresolved, suggesting an observational bias that affects nearby stars.

7. OGLE-2018-BLG-0829L: A Microlensing System with a Planet Orbiting a Star Possessing a Brown-Dwarf Companion[2611.19134]
Abstract

We analyze the microlensing event OGLE-2018-BLG-0829, whose light curve exhibits a weak short-term anomaly in addition to the prominent caustic-crossing features of a binary lens. The anomaly cannot be explained by a binary-lens single-source (2L1S) model. We therefore investigate both binary-lens binary-source (2L2S) and triple-lens single-source (3L1S) interpretations and find that both models reproduce the additional anomaly, with the 3L1S model being favored by $\Delta\chi^2=15.1$. Under the favored 3L1S interpretation, the anomaly arises from a caustic induced by a planetary-mass companion, whereas the prominent caustic-crossing features are produced by the underlying binary lens. Combining the measured event timescale, $t_{\rm E}=7.55\pm0.30$ days, and angular Einstein radius, $\theta_{\rm E}=0.325 \pm 0.059$ mas, with a Bayesian analysis, we infer a lens system consisting of an M dwarf of mass $0.193^{+0.300}_{-0.098}\,M_\odot$, a possible brown-dwarf companion of mass $0.068^{+0.105}_{-0.034}\,M_\odot$, and a Saturn-mass planet of mass $0.337^{+0.522}_{-0.171}\,M_{\rm J}$. The companions have projected separations of approximately 3.6 au and 1.6 au from the primary, respectively. However, the physical interpretation remains tentative because potentially important higher-order effects have not been considered, and the dynamical stability of the proposed configuration remains uncertain. If confirmed, this would be the third star–brown dwarf–planet system identified through microlensing, highlighting the potential of microlensing to detect planets in complex multiple systems.

8. From Non-parametric Statistics to Images: Generating Galaxy Morphologies[2611.19211]
Abstract

Non-parametric statistics characterize complex galaxy morphologies but do not specify how to construct images that reproduce those statistics. We present Flow-VAE, which combines a variational autoencoder with a conditional normalizing flow to generate images conditioned on concentration, asymmetry, smoothness (CAS), Gini coefficient (G), and M20. A Gaussian mixture model completes unspecified descriptors and measures the training-distribution support of requested combinations. Using a dataset of 240,000 DESI Legacy Imaging Surveys r-band images, we evaluate fidelity by remeasuring morphological statistics from model outputs and comparing them with the inputs. With a tolerance of +/-10% of each descriptor's adopted range, descriptor-specific recovery rates are 96.62-99.97% for reconstruction and 87.81-99.83% for single-parameter generation among valid measurements. Generated descriptors show regression toward typical training values, while less-supported joint conditions produce broader residuals and systematic offsets. These results quantify the extent to which Flow-VAE can generate galaxy images consistent with prescribed non-parametric morphological statistics.

9. Unveiling Hidden X-ray Broad Line Region in Centaurus A: Broadband Analysis with XRISM and NuSTAR[2611.19327]
Abstract

We present a broadband X-ray spectral analysis of the radio galaxy Centaurus A, utilizing simultaneous \textit{XRISM} (Resolve and Xtend) and \textit{NuSTAR} observations. Unlike the previous phenomenological analysis of the Fe K$\alpha$ line (Bogensberger et al. 2025), we employ physical reflection models developed with the radiative transfer code SKIRT. They self-consistently predict the continuum and fluorescent lines from a clumpy torus and an X-ray broad line region (xBLR). The Fe K$\alpha$ complex is well reproduced with a three velocity component model. We interpret this as two narrow torus components (FWHM $= 310\pm50$ and $1900\pm400$ km s$^{-1}$) and an xBLR component (FWHM $= 6000_{-800}^{+1100}$ km s$^{-1}$). The inner torus radius of 0.13 pc is 10 times the dust sublimation radius (0.013 pc), implying that the torus in a low-luminosity AGN is not determined by dust-related physics. The xBLR has a substantial covering factor of 0.11–0.36, a hydrogen column density ($5 \times 10^{23}$ cm$^{-2}$), a radius of 0.014 pc from the SMBH, and a mass of $>0.9 M_\odot$. Its mass supply rate is comparable to the accretion rate inferred from the bolometric luminosity, implying efficient xBLR formation even at low Eddington ratios. The xBLR line width also provides a novel estimate of the black-hole mass, $\log{(M_{\rm BH}/M_{\odot})} = 7.00\pm0.34$, via the optical BLR mass-scaling relation to the Fe K$\alpha$. This agrees with dynamical measurements within a factor of $\sim5$, consistent with the scatter of the optical relation. We also infer the abundance ratios of $A_{\rm Ca}/A_{\rm Fe} = 1.1_{-0.1}^{+0.2}$ and $A_{\rm Ni}/A_{\rm Fe} = 1.4_{-0.2}^{+0.1}$ solar.

10. Lindblad and Corotation Resonances as the Common Origin of Moving Groups, Diagonal Ridges, and Velocity Waves in the Milky Way Disc[2611.19391]
Abstract

The Galactic disc is a complex dynamical system showing various kinematic structures, including moving groups (MGs), diagonal ridges (DRs), and velocity waves. To investigate their possible dynamical connection, we analyzed the phase-space distribution of stars from the extit{Gaia} DR3 catalog. We used angular momentum $L_z$ as a key dynamical tracer and examined stellar densities and velocities as functions of $L_z$. We identified stellar groups at specific $L_z$-values and associated them with extrema in the median radial velocity $\left<V_R ight>$, known as a velocity wave. By cross-matching the extrema of the $\left<V_R ight>$-wave with structures in the $V_R$–$L_z$ and $R$–$L_z$ planes, we established a clear connection between the velocity wave, the main MGs, and the DRs. A similar connection was partially identified for corrugations in the median vertical velocity $\left<V_z ight>$. Both velocity waves are strongest near the Galactic mid-plane and decrease in amplitude with increasing vertical distance. Our results demonstrate that MGs, DRs, and velocity waves in the solar neighborhood are different projections of the same dynamical structures associated with Lindblad and corotation resonances. These resonances arise from coupling between the radial and azimuthal stellar motions and are particularly prominent in the thin Galactic disc. Numerical simulations including perturbations from the Galactic bar and spiral arms confirm these conclusions. Our findings indicate that the local stellar disc is currently evolving in a state of dynamical equilibrium. We also show that in-plane velocity oscillations must be considered when interpreting vertical stellar distributions, as they may produce artifacts in vertical profiles.

11. Unbound matter in the circum-halo medium: a common mediator of halo secondary bias[2611.19491]
Abstract

Halo secondary bias describes the dependence of halo clustering on properties beyond mass. Here we report that a new environmental metric, the unbound matter fraction ($f_{\rm un}$) in the $2$–$3\,R_{\rm vir}$ shell around a halo, can largely account for secondary bias. Using haloes with $M_{\rm vir}=10^{10.5}$–$10^{14.5}h^{-1}{\rm M_\odot}$ at $z=0$ obtained from TNG300-1-Dark, we find that controlling for $f_{\rm un}$ reduces the secondary bias of formation time, concentration, spin, shape and velocity anisotropy to a residual correlation of $\lesssim 4\%$, outperforming other environmental metrics such as the tidal anisotropy for spin and velocity anisotropy. Applying a stacking method to the circum-halo medium (CHM), we identify two distinct flow patterns: low-$f_{\rm un}$ haloes co-move with coherent flows, with the CHM converging onto the haloes; in contrast, high-$f_{\rm un}$ haloes move through dynamically hot, partially virialized structures, with the CHM passing by rather than being accreted. This dichotomy points to two distinct regimes of secondary bias, a `cold' regime and a `hot' regime, in which tidal and inertial forces act as the main drivers, respectively, with the dominant mechanism migrating from the former to the latter as haloes assemble into massive structures. At fixed large-scale environment, $f_{\rm un}$ further indicates the presence of nearby neighbours and the competitive accretion. Our results highlight the role of unbound matter as a mediator of environmental effects on haloes, and reveal the physical mechanisms that shape the CHM pattern through which these effects are mediated.

Abstract

Inspection of deep JWST images centered on the lensing cluster of galaxies MACS J0454.1-0300 reveals a small, well resolved stellar system that is clearly in the foreground. 13. Stripped White Dwarfs as Spectroscopic Clocks: Abundance Transitions in Partial Tidal Disruptions[2611.19867]
Abstract

We investigate partial tidal disruptions of He and CO white dwarfs (WDs) by a $10^{4}\,M_{\odot}$ intermediate-mass black hole (IMBH) using three-dimensional hydrodynamic simulations. We construct realistic WD structures and abundance profiles with MESA and map them into FLASH to follow the remnant response and the time-dependent composition of the fallback debris in single pericentre passages on tightly bound orbits ($e=0.9$). The element-specific fallback rates exhibit pre-peak compositional transitions that trace the WD's stratified layers. In the grazing encounter ($\beta=0.55$), hydrogen dominates the earliest returning debris, followed by helium, showing a H$\rightarrow$He transition. Deeper encounters ($\beta=0.7$ and $0.8$) penetrate the CO core and produce a He$\rightarrow$C transition, that occurs earlier relative to peak fallback and is stronger in the deeper encounter. These signatures provide a potential spectroscopic diagnostic for identifying CO-WD tidal disruption events (TDEs). We then develop a semi-analytic model for repeated tidal stripping and predict passage-to-passage compositional evolution, in which the stripped material becomes progressively enriched in heavier elements as deeper layers are exposed, leading to stronger and earlier abundance transitions in later flares. We find that WDs can survive several dozen ($\sim 30$-$80$) passages, depending on their initial mass and compactness. Our results link time-dependent abundance signatures to repeating WD disruptions and provide testable observable predictions for identifying IMBH-powered transients.

14. Mock Observations: Morphological Analysis of Galactic Bars in IllustrisTNG Simulations[2611.19884]
Abstract

Galactic bars are important tracers of disk-galaxy evolution, yet reported bar fractions vary among observational studies and between observations and cosmological simulations. We compare bar statistics and properties in the IllustrisTNG simulations(TNG100 and TNG50 at z 0.1) with Sloan Digital Sky Survey(SDSS) galaxies at0.1<=z<=0.1264. We generate synthetic r-band TNG images,match SDSS observational conditions,and analyze mock TNG and real SDSS images using a common image-based framework of source segmentation,morphological classification, and isophotal ellipse fitting. For face-on late-type galaxies with log(M*/Msun)>10,the global bar fractions are 26.68+/-0.23%,24.96(-.76/+.78)%, and 13.42(-1.07/+1.15)% for SDSS,TNG100,and TNG50,respectively. TNG100 broadly agrees with SDSS over 10^10< M*< 10^11.2Msun but shows a higher fbar at higher masses, whereas TNG50 remains lower than SDSS over most of10^10< M*< 10^11.7Msun. Bulge-disk decompositions show fewer high-n bulges in TNG100 than in SDSS,particularly at high masses,but more in TNG50. Our SDSS measurements may underestimate the bar fraction relative to observations with higher spatial resolution.

15. Magnetic fields in blazar jets derived from synchrotron self-absorption[2611.19906]
Abstract

Context. Magnetic fields are believed to play a crucial role in the launching, acceleration, and evolution of blazar jets. However, direct measurements of the magnetic field strength in the radio-emitting regions of blazar jets remain limited. Aims. We aim to estimate the magnetic field strengths in the synchrotron self-absorption (SSA) regions of blazar jets and investigate their connections with the properties of the jet and central engine. Methods. We compiled multifrequency radio data for 143 blazars and fitted their radio spectra with a combined power-law and SSA model to derive the turnover frequency and turnover flux density. The magnetic field strengths were then calculated by combining the SSA parameters with the very long baseline interferometry (VLBI) measured angular sizes and Doppler factors compiled from the literature. Results. The SSA-derived magnetic fields are systematically lower than those obtained from broadband spectral energy distribution modeling, suggesting that the two methods probe different regions of the jet. We further investigated the correlations between the SSA magnetic field strength and the apparent knot speed, {\gamma}-ray luminosity, black hole mass, and accretion disk luminosity. Only a weak correlation is found between the SSA magnetic field strength and {\gamma}-ray luminosity, while the best-fit relations with the other investigated parameters also exhibit negative slopes, suggesting a common tendency that is qualitatively consistent with magnetic energy being progressively converted into bulk kinetic energy during jet propagation. Conclusions. Our work presents a large statistical studies of SSA-derived magnetic fields in blazars and provides new observational constraints on the magnetic evolution of relativistic jets.

16. The mass density slope of early-type galaxies: newest results from the redshift distribution of strong gravitational lenses[2611.20141]
Abstract

The mass distribution of early-type galaxies (ETGs) encodes key information about baryonic processes and dark-matter assembly. In this paper, we propose a new framework to investigate the mass-density profiles of ETGs and their evolution with redshift, based on the redshift distribution of strong gravitational lenses. We compile 200 galaxy-scale strong lensing systems with complete information, including lens and source redshifts, Einstein radii, and spectroscopic velocity dispersions. To account for the heterogeneous construction of the compiled sample, we model a lens-redshift-dependent selection function for each survey category. By combining the lens-redshift distribution with velocity dispersion function (VDF) and spherical Jeans-based mapping, we obtain population-level constraints on the total mass-density slope gamma in three lens-redshift intervals, marginalizing over the luminosity-density slope delta and the stellar orbital anisotropy beta. We find that the total mass density of ETGs remains close to isothermal at low and intermediate redshifts ($\gamma=2.00^{+0.07}_{-0.15}$ at 0 < z_l < 0.5 and $\gamma=1.96^{+0.16}_{-0.26}$ at 0.5 < z_l < 0.6), with a shallower effective slope at higher redshift ($\gamma=1.81^{+0.17}_{-0.21}$ at $0.6<z_l\lesssim1.0$). The marginalized posteriors of delta and beta remain broad, consistent with $\delta\simeq2$ and with isotropic orbits in all three redshift intervals. These results are consistent with the redshift trend predicted by Horizon-AGN simulations, in which active galactic nucleus (AGN) feedback, together with stellar assembly and merging, contributes to the structural evolution of ETGs. Applied to the much larger lens samples expected from future missions such as the Chinese Space Station Telescope (CSST), this method will enable tighter tests of density-slope evolution and its connection to galaxy formation processes.

17. Euclid Data Release 1 (DR1). Double-source-plane lens candidates[2611.20166]
Abstract

Strong gravitational-lensing systems with multiple source planes are powerful tools for probing the stellar and dark matter content of galaxies. Lens modelling can measure the relative Einstein radii of the two source planes, which encode a ratio of angular-diameter distances determined by the lens and source redshifts and the dark energy equation of state, independently of the Hubble constant. Galaxy-scale double-source-plane lenses (DSPLs) are extremely rare. We present a sample of 84 galaxy-scale DSPL candidates in Euclid Data Release 1 (DR1), of which 80 are newly identified. These systems were selected using machine-learning lens-finding models followed by visual inspection by citizen scientists and experts. We then used lens modelling to test the DSPL interpretation. Among our lens candidates, 11 form the 'Gold' sample: each shows two sets of multiple images at different radii, and the observed configuration is reproduced by a DSPL lens model. The remaining 73 'Silver' candidates have at least two lensed arcs from visual inspection, but either lack multiple images for at least one arc, have a best-fitting DSPL distance ratio close to the single-source-plane limit, remain compatible with a single-plane model with multiple sources, or have not yet been modelled. Even if all 84 candidates are DSPLs, the sample falls below the approximately 90 systems expected from extrapolating the Euclid Quick Data Release 1 (Q1) yield and the approximately 200 obtained by scaling the LensPop forecast to the DR1 area, indicating that many DSPLs remain undetected in DR1. Spectroscopy or higher-resolution imaging is required before we can be confident that the Silver candidates are DSPLs.

19. Population structure of the velocity distribution in normal shock waves: Gaussian representations, Mach-number similarity, and prediction[2611.20302]
Abstract

Normal shock waves are commonly described as mixtures of upstream and downstream Maxwellians. Discrete-velocity solutions of three relaxation models up to Mach 12 and direct simulation Monte Carlo (DSMC) of hard-sphere and variable-hard-sphere gases up to Mach 25 are used to examine the population structure, Mach-number similarity and upstream precursor of their velocity distributions. Three anisotropic Gaussian populations reproduce the streamwise reduced distributions of the Bhatnagar–Gross–Krook (BGK) model and of DSMC to within 4\% up to Mach 12, and of DSMC to within 6\% at Mach 25, whereas two Maxwellians incur errors up to 50\% (BGK) and 37\% (DSMC). Collision histories identify the hard-sphere beam with unscattered stream molecules, which carry half of the peak viscous stress; the other populations are effective descriptions, not collision classes. Above Mach 5, Rankine–Hugoniot-scaled distributions are nearly Mach-independent, with corrections linear in $1/M^2$, consistent with the scaled kinetic equation. At equal normalised density, Mach 8 and 12 give the heat flux and stress at Mach 25 within 1\%, comparable to the accuracy of the reference; with one moment equation for position, physical-space profiles follow within 5\%. Comparing three prediction methods shows that the accuracy comes from the scaling; the Gaussian form adds compactness. Ahead of the shock, the mass of upstream-moving molecules decays as a stretched exponential with effective exponent 0.68–0.87. Collisions regenerate upstream-moving molecules, which explains a decay slower than free-flight survival; a linear cascade model with a downstream-Maxwellian source and no input from the shock interior reproduces the decay rates within 1.2\%.

20. Tracing the Luminosity Evolution of Globular Cluster Systems with JWST: MACS J1149.5+2223 at z=0.544[2611.20358]
Abstract

We present a study of the compact-source population associated with the massive galaxy cluster MACS J1149.5+2223 at z=0.544 using deep JWST/NIRCam imaging in F115W, F150W, and F200W. After photometric and morphological selection, we identify 1,202 unresolved compact-source candidates. The observed luminosity distribution samples only the bright side of the expected globular-cluster luminosity function (GCLF), remaining several magnitudes above the turnover. Based on published GCLF turnovers for Abell 2744 and MACS 0417, we adopt an expected turnover magnitude of m_0=33.66 mag for MACS 1149. Together with recent JWST studies of globular cluster systems across a range of redshifts, MACS 1149 extends the emerging observational sequence of compact stellar systems and supports stellar population fading as an important contributor to their luminosity evolution across cosmic time.

21. Independent interferometric observable confirms black hole shadow in M87*[2611.20418]
Abstract

A new epoch for direct visualisation of accretion dynamics near a black hole's event horizon began when the Event Horizon Telescope Collaboration (EHTC) synthesised an Earth-sized aperture with millimeter radio interferometry and published the first reconstructed image of a black hole shadow at the core of radio galaxy Messier 87 (M87). The image appeared as a ring-like structure, representing the gravitationally lensed and redshifted emission around the central supermassive black hole, M87*, located at the galactic nucleus. However, application of various data analysis and imaging heuristics resulted in divergent representations of M87* fine-scale features, with some external analyses even challenging the fundamental ring-like morphology of the source. Such difficulties arise as a consequence of the inherent sparsity of the very-long baseline interferometry (VLBI) measurements, rendering interferometric image reconstruction an inadequately constrained problem. Moreover, the solution is sensitive to instrument calibration, post-processing algorithms, and imaging hyperparameters. Here, we apply a newly-developed independent imaging algorithm to the M87* dataset, which is free from prescriptive morphological priors and founded on observables immune to instrument calibration errors. We reconstruct the black hole shadow image, and unequivocally reproduce a ring-like morphology, with angular diameter 42.2 +/- 1.5 microarcseconds corresponding to a black hole mass of 6.5 +/- 0.5 billion solar masses assuming nominal scaling parameters, observed at an inclination angle of  20 deg from the approaching jet.

22. Model-Driven Stellar Spectroscopy with the Total Differential Spectral Model (TDSM): Framework and Its Application to 11 Million LAMOST Low-Resolution Spectra[2611.20633]
Abstract

Deriving stellar astrophysical parameters from large-scale spectroscopic surveys requires accurate modeling of spectra across a wide wavelength coverage and a broad range of stellar parameter space, which remains a great challenge. In this work, we present \texttt{TDSM}, a physically motivated neural network architecture that self-consistently emulates both the flux and its first-order derivative of the ab initio stellar spectra. The method enables accurate and precise emulation of Kurucz synthetic spectra with severely blended features at $R \simeq 2000$ in a high-dimensional parameter space, achieving a typical pixel-wise flux precision of 0.05\% in the wavelength range $3700<\lambda<9000$Å over the parameter range of $3000 < T_{\rm eff} < 45,000$ K, $-1 < \log g < 5$, and $-4.0 < \mathrm{[Fe/H]} < 0.5$. The resultant catalog provides eight stellar labels – $T_{\rm eff}$, $\log g$, $v_{\rm mic}$, [Fe/H], [$\alpha$/Fe], [C/Fe], [N/Fe], and [Mg/Fe] – for 8 million unique stars (11 million spectra including repeat observations) across the full parameter space. This adds a valuable dataset for studies of Galactic structure and archaeology, stellar populations, and related areas of astrophysics.

23. Ca II IRT activity indices from $Gaia$ spectroscopy: a baseline method for photospheric contribution estimates[2611.20636]
Abstract

The $Gaia$ high-resolution spectroscopic observations using Radial Velocity Spectrometer offers a unique opportunity to study stellar Ca II infrared triplet (IRT) activity. Radio antennas are often used to form arrays, such as phased arrays and interferometer arrays. For most applications the beam patterns of these instruments must be accurately known. For phased arrays the array patterns are formed by coherently adding the embedded element patterns (EEPs) of the individual antennas in the array. For interferometers, the fundamental measurement is of visibilities, which depend on the products of pairs of EEPs. Unfortunately, in either case, for closely-spaced arrays, the EEPs can be distorted by mutual coupling between the antennas and can be very different from the isolated element patterns (IEPs), measured when the antennas are far apart. Mutual coupling is characterized by the scattering parameters for the array. Full-wave electromagnetic simulations can simulate the EEPs, including mutual coupling effects, but these simulations can be computationally expensive and require accurate knowledge of the design of the array elements, their placement, and the environment in which they are deployed. Directly measuring either the EEPs or the array patterns for instruments in the field is often impractical as well. In this paper we show that in cases where the scattering parameters between array elements can be measured, they can be used to infer the array patterns of a phased array. We demonstrate the training of a deep neural network to simulate array patterns when given: the design of the antennas, the locations of the antennas, the frequency of operation, and measurements of the scattering parameters of the array. As a demonstration for a simple model, we apply this approach to the simulation of the array patterns for a small phased array of three dipole antennas and evaluate the sensitivity of this procedure to uncertainties in measurements of the antenna positions and scattering parameters.

Abstract

We present a systematic comparison between arbitrary precise arithmetic and integration, IEEE-754 compliant floating point arithmetic (fp16, bfp16, fp32, double precision fp64, and quadruple precision fp128), and two implementations of Posits (type III unum) for solving Newton's chaotic N-body problem. Abstract
