1. Evidence of galaxy cluster rotation and intrahalo gas motions in the cosmic microwave background[2512.10951]
Abstract

We report the first robust evidence for the rotational kinematic Sunyaev-Zel'dovich (rkSZ) effect: the dipolar signal produced by the Thomson scattering of cosmic microwave background (CMB) photons off intracluster gas in dynamically active systems (including rotations and mergers). By combining CMB intensity and polarization measurements from the \emph{Planck} satellite with spectroscopic member-galaxy redshifts from the Sloan Digital Sky Survey in a sample of 25 X-ray cross-matched, low-redshift ($0.02< z< 0.09)$, massive ($10^{13.9}\lesssim M_{\rm 500c}/M_\odot \lesssim 10^{14.6}$) galaxy clusters, we detect a dipolar rkSZ signature aligned with the estimated angular momentum of each cluster, ruling out a chance fluctuation at 99.98\% confidence (3.6$\sigma$). The significance of this measurement is enhanced by several new methodological improvements for isolating the rkSZ signal from primary CMB fluctuations and noise. The amplitude and shape of the signal are qualitatively consistent with predictions from state-of-the-art hydrodynamical simulations. These results establish a new tool with which to probe the dynamical state of galaxy clusters using CMB data.

2. The stellar-to-halo mass relation of central galaxies across three orders of halo mass[2602.10193]
Abstract

The stellar content of galaxies is tightly connected to the mass and growth of their host dark matter halos. Observational constraints on this relation remain limited, particularly for low-mass groups, leaving uncertainties in how galaxies assemble their stars across halo mass scales. Accurately measuring the brightest central galaxy (BCG) stellar-to-halo mass relation (SHMR) over a wide mass range is therefore crucial for understanding galaxy formation and the role of feedback processes. Here we present the SHMR spanning $M_{\rm halo} \sim 10^{12}$-$10^{15}\,M_\odot$, using halo masses derived from eROSITA eRASS1 X-ray data and BCG stellar masses based on SDSS photometry. By stacking X-ray spectra of optically selected groups, we recover robust average halo gas temperatures for each bin, which are then converted to halo masses via the $M$-$T_X$ relation. We find that the SHMR peaks near $M_{\rm halo} \sim 10^{12}\,M_\odot$, with a declining stellar fraction at higher masses. This trend reflects a combination of processes that reduce the efficiency of stellar mass growth in massive halos, such as AGN feedback, reduced cooling efficiency, and the increasing dominance of ex-situ assembly, while halos continue to grow through mergers and accretion. Our measurements are consistent over the full mass range with previous observational studies, including weak lensing, X-ray analyses of individual clusters, and kinematical and dynamical methods. Comparisons with hydrodynamical simulations show good agreement at low masses but reveal significant discrepancies in the normalization at cluster scales, highlighting the sensitivity of BCG stellar growth to feedback prescriptions and halo assembly history. These results provide the first X-ray-based observational SHMR covering three orders of magnitude in halo mass, establish a robust benchmark for testing galaxy formation models.

3. Identifying heating processes in simulations with an entropy-based scheme: A single jet episode in a galaxy cluster[2603.26882]
Abstract

Understanding heating processes in galaxy clusters is essential for predicting the regulation of radiative cooling and star formation, and for clarifying the mechanisms underlying active galactic nucleus (AGN) feedback in cool-core clusters. We investigate the processes through which AGN jets deposit heat into the intracluster medium (ICM) by tracking passive entropy scalars in magneto-hydrodynamic (MHD) simulations. This enables us to systematically disentangle the contributions from different heating channels. We successfully validate this method with several idealized tests, including turbulent heating, heating by anisotropic Braginskii viscosity, dissipative and adiabatic heating by shocks using in-situ shock-detection methods, and cosmic ray (CR) heating through the excitations and damping of Alfvén waves. Using this methodology, we simulate single-epoch outbursts of high-power jets with varying densities in a cluster environment. Light jets produce wider bubbles, displacing a larger fraction of the gas in the cluster's core, whereas comparatively denser jets propagate more efficiently to larger distances without significantly disturbing the central region. During early evolution, shock heating of the ICM prevails irrespective of jet density. At later times, light jets primarily heat the ICM through turbulently induced heating (which includes dissipation of vortices emerging from a turbulent cascade and mixing gas of different temperatures) and compressible dissipation of sound waves and very weak shocks, while denser jets continue to dissipate most of their energy via shocks. Particularly, turbulently induced heating by solenoidal-shear motions prevails inside the cocoon, whereas shocks and acoustic compressions dominate outside. In light jets, the forward shock weakens rapidly, whereas dense jets can sustain strong bow shocks to large distances.

4. The DESIRED strong-line calibrations: I. New empirical metallicity relations for the local and high-redshift universe[2604.16273]
Abstract

We present the most comprehensive set of empirical optical strong-line metallicity calibrations to date, based on the DEep Spectra of Ionised REgions Database (DESIRED), the largest compilation of HII regions and galaxies with direct electron-temperature determinations. We construct a high-quality calibration sample of 2392 spectra: 1029 extragalactic HII regions, 1296 local star-forming galaxies, and 67 high-redshift ($z > 2$) galaxies, drawn from 201 independent literature references, spanning $12+\log({\rm O/H}) \in [6.79, 9.07]$. Physical conditions and chemical abundances are derived homogeneously using up-to-date atomic data. We derive 27 strong-line calibrations covering oxygen-, nitrogen-, sulphur-, argon-, and neon-based line ratios, including 4 previously uncalibrated diagnostics, with reported validity ranges and intrinsic dispersions (typically $\sim0.15-0.35$ dex). For the first time in a systematic calibration framework, all relations are presented for both the homogeneous temperature case ($t^2 = 0$) and a scenario including temperature inhomogeneities ($t^2 > 0$), thereby reconciling abundances from recombination lines (RLs) and collisionally excited lines (CELs) and directly tackling the abundance discrepancy problem. A comparison with previous calibrations shows that the DESIRED relations span the broadest validity intervals while remaining anchored to the empirical data. Comparisons with $z>2$ galaxies show that several DESIRED calibrations remain consistent with independent high-redshift relations within their intrinsic scatter, whereas others display systematic offsets associated with elevated N/O or harder ionising conditions. Thus, DESIRED calibrations can provide reliable metallicity estimates at high redshift for a subset of diagnostics, while highlighting the need to account for diagnostic-specific redshift evolution.

5. Astrophysical Parameters of 5056 Open Star Clusters from Bayesian Nested Sampling with PARSEC Isochrones[2605.23802]
Abstract

We present a homogeneous catalogue of ages, metallicities, distances, and reddenings for 5056 open clusters in the Unified Cluster Catalogue (UCC), derived uniformly from Gaia Data Release 3 (DR3) colour-magnitude diagrams via Bayesian nested sampling with PARSEC isochrones. Unlike previous large-scale catalogues, metallicity is fitted as a free parameter for every cluster rather than fixed or adopted from the literature: where available, Gaia XP spectrophotometric metallicities inform the fit (1201 clusters), while the remaining clusters are constrained by broad priors from Gaia parallaxes and the Schlegel-Finkbeiner-Davis dust map. No cluster is anchored to an external open-cluster parameter catalogue, and no per-cluster manual intervention is applied. A fit-quality metric, $\eta_{\rm fit}$, identifies 3807 clusters (75 per cent) with reliable colour-magnitude solutions, spanning ages of 0.004-5.9 Gyr and distances out to 17 kpc. We validate the resulting parameters against six literature catalogues and, more critically, against independent high-resolution spectroscopic metallicities from OCCAM DR19: for 132 clusters in common we find a median absolute deviation of only 0.054 dex ($-0.021 \pm 0.132$ dex mean offset), comparable to the intrinsic systematic uncertainty of isochrone-based metallicities. As a direct consequence of this fidelity, the radial metallicity gradient recovered from our XP-informed clusters ($-0.064$ dex kpc$^{-1}$) steepens towards the spectroscopically calibrated value ($-0.079$ dex kpc$^{-1}$) for the highest-quality subset - demonstrating that a purely photometric pipeline, built without reference to spectroscopy, recovers a physically meaningful abundance scale. The full catalogue and posterior chains are publicly released.

6. Validation of Satellite Lifetime Predictions at Leonid Space[2601.02453]
Abstract

This paper validates Leonid Space's satellite lifetime prediction pipeline through a backtesting campaign of 878 deorbited satellites spanning six decades of spaceflight, showing that it accurately recreates their last year of flight. The pipeline combines a custom high-speed orbit propagator, ballistic coefficient estimation from on-orbit TLE data, atmospheric density corrections from data assimilation, custom space weather forecasts, and probabilistic Monte Carlo analysis. Validation proceeds in four steps that progressively remove the benefit of hindsight: starting from a "perfect knowledge" scenario using actual space weather data and ephemerides, we gradually replace historical data with contemporary predictions to arrive at a fully predictive scenario analogous to a deorbit prediction performed for a spacecraft flying today. These forecasts are scored using the continuously ranked probability score and root mean square calibration error, yielding a median one-year prediction accuracy of 3.9 days (1.1%) with perfect knowledge and 44.9 days (12.3%) in the fully predictive scenario. Solar flux forecasts contribute 89% of the variance in our predictions, dominating all other sources of error. To our knowledge, this is the first comprehensive backtesting of long-term satellite lifetime predictions that reports quantitative results for the fully predictive case, and it establishes a new framework for scoring solar cycle forecasts by grounding forecast accuracy in operationally-relevant satellite lifetime predictions.

7. CHRONOS Science Program[2603.10070]
Abstract

Cryogenic sub-Hz cROss torsion-bar detector with quantum NOn-demolition Speed meter (CHRONOS) is a proposed cryogenic torsion-bar interferometer for precision measurements in the 0.1-10 Hz band. A Sagnac speed-meter readout, power and signal recycling, and balanced homodyne detection provide control of the optical response and quantum measurement correlations. This White Paper describes the science program, detector design, calibration, and staged experimental development. The reference sensitivity uses a multi-input quantum model, including rear-mirror vacuum fields and measurement-back-action correlations, combined with classical noise. Two operating points are optimized for complementary power-law-integrated sensitivity objectives. Gravitational-wave forecasts use this common noise budget and state their source and observing assumptions. The program includes intermediate-mass black-hole binaries, stochastic backgrounds, quantum measurement, and terrestrial gravity perturbations. Dark-matter applications are discussed as response and feasibility studies. A differential gravitational calibrator provides a proposed Yukawa-gravity test whose systematic floor is improved by subtracting auxiliary harmonics. These are conditional projections: experimental reach requires validation of the noise, transfer functions, environmental backgrounds, and systematic uncertainties described here.

8. Probing the magnetic field of a coronal mass ejection with PSR J1022+1001[2603.18051]
Abstract

We investigate whether low-frequency pulsar observations can provide LoS magnetic field estimates and whether these are consistent with synthetic LoS signatures extracted from a three-dimensional CME reconstruction constrained by Solar Orbiter data. We analyze a CME occultation of the LoS to PSR J1022+1001 on 20 August 2021 at a projected heliocentric distance of 24.6 R_sun, observed simultaneously with LOFAR and NenuFAR. From LOFAR, we derive time-resolved dispersion measure (DM) and rotation measure (RM) and isolate the CME contributions using background estimates for interstellar, solar wind and ionospheric components. We then infer the density-weighted LoS-averaged magnetic field component <B||>_PSR from the ratio delta-RM/delta-DM. In parallel, we reconstruct the CME using a semi-empirical 3DCORE model fitted to Solar Orbiter in-situ magnetic field observations at 0.65 au. We sample the modeled magnetic field along the pulsar LoS using fixed spatial sampling points and compute synthetic LoS-averaged signatures <B||>_3D for different flux rope configurations. The derived <B||>_PSR increases from approximately -9 nT to a peak near 63 nT during the observed interval. Comparison with synthetic signatures shows that the polarity and temporal evolution of the LoS signal are strongly dependent on the flux rope configuration and only a South-West-North (SWN) configuration (confirmed by Solar Orbiter in-situ data) reproduces the observed sign and overall evolution, whereas alternative configurations are incompatible. The modeled amplitudes, however, are systematically larger than the pulsar-derived values by roughly a factor of five. We show that simultaneous low-frequency pulsar DM and RM measurements can provide LoS magnetic field estimates for a CME and can be used to test CME magnetic structure against data-constrained three-dimensional reconstructions.

9. Searching for Ultra-Light Axions with Twisted Cavity Resonators of Anyon Rotational Symmetry with Bulk Modes of Non-Zero Helicity[2208.01640]
Abstract

Möbius-ring resonators stem from a well-studied geometrical structure with a one-sided topology, the Möbius strip, and have been shown to exhibit fermion rotational symmetry with respect to an untwisted ring resonator, which exhibits boson rotational symmetry [1]. Here, we present twisted hollow resonators with equilateral triangular cross-sections, leading to cavities with anyon rotational symmetry. Unlike conventional cavity resonators, these structures permit bulk resonant modes with a non-zero complex electromagnetic overlap $\mathcal C_p=\int_V\mathbf{E}_p\cdot\mathbf{B}_p^*\,d\tau$. The real and imaginary parts of this overlap couple to different observable quadratures. Although the negative-frequency component of a real axion field is immaterial for haloscopes employing a static DC magnetic field, it must be retained for a pumped resonator because the complex forms of Maxwell's equations differ for positive and negative frequencies and produce distinct upper and lower mixing branches. Retaining both branches shows that the real overlap produces amplitude-modulated sidebands, whereas the imaginary helicity of the modes studied here produces phase-modulated sidebands through the axion-photon chiral anomaly. A sensitive ultra-light dark-matter experiment may therefore be realised with an ultra-stable oscillator and a phase-sensitive readout. The sensitivity curves calculated here require the assumed noise floor to be interpreted as phase-modulation noise rather than amplitude-modulation noise, and consequently require sufficiently low residual oscillator phase noise at the axion Fourier frequency. The scheme removes the usual requirement for an external magnetic field and permits superconducting resonators with reduced surface loss and enhanced axion sensitivity.

10. Cosmic Rays as an Interdisciplinary Earth Observation Tool: From Particle Physics and Atmospheric Processes to Geosciences and Urban Science[2601.19265]
Abstract

The exploration of cosmic rays, which are high-energy particles originating from space and the atmosphere, has historically been associated with particle physics and astrophysics. In the last 20 years, these particles have evolved into valuable tools for observing Earth's systems. This review compiles the use of cosmic rays in three primary areas: (1) particle physics and atmospheric processes, which include cosmic- ray-induced cascades, ionization, and their impact on atmospheric chemistry and radiation; (2) geosciences, where cosmogenic radionuclides assist in the dating of geological materials and cosmic-ray neutrons are used for large-scale monitoring of soil moisture and snow water equivalents; and (3) urban science, where cosmic- ray muons are employed for non-invasive subsurface imaging and, when paired with distributed sensors, serve as the basis for smart city monitoring. This review places particular emphasis on integrating these methods with remote sensing and geographic information systems (GIS), which helps close the persistent scale gap between point measurements and satellite observations, thereby enabling three-dimensional digital representations of subsurfaces. This review concludes by discussing data standards, their integration into operational Earth observation workflows, and future research directions.
