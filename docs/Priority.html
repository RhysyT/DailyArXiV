1. A Parsec-scale Circumstellar Shell Around VX Sgr: Mass Loss and RSG/TŻO Nature[2610.20689]
Abstract

A thin, arc-shaped shell is discovered surrounding VX Sagittarii (VX Sgr), the largest known CSM shell around an RSG at a scale $r\sim0.59\,\mathrm{pc}$. The nebula has an extinction-corrected H$\alpha$ intensity $I_{\mathrm{H}\alpha}\sim38\,\mathrm{R}$ and an ionized mass $\sim0.1\,\mathrm{M_\odot}$. The slow $12\,\mathrm{km\,s}^{-1}$ peculiar velocity of VX Sgr and the lack of far-infrared emission in the arc indicate that it is not produced by a bow shock, but is likely instead steady RSG wind or ejecta that is photoionized by a nearby $\mathrm{O}$- or $\mathrm{B}$- type star. The nature of VX Sgr is debated in the literature as a red supergiant/hypergiant (RSG/RHG), super-AGB star, or Thorne–Żytkow Object (TŻO). Using the kinematic age of the Sgr OB1 association, the super-AGB model is disfavored. A spectroscopic test of the arc is proposed to potentially distinguish an RSG from a possible TŻO. The mass-loss rate is estimated from the geometry and emission measure of the shell, finding $\dot{M}\approx4\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm{M}_\odot\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}$. The future interaction of the supernova (SN) ejecta of VX Sgr and the surrounding circumstellar material (CSM) is briefly explored through the mass ratio $\Lambda$ of the shell and SN ejecta; the interaction would likely not have a significant effect on the SN forward shock.

2. JWST Observations of RR Lyrae in Leo P[2610.20692]
Abstract

We analyze co-spatial HST/ACS and JWST/NIRCam time-series photometric observations of RR Lyrae in the low-metallicity dwarf galaxy Leo P. This study provides a new opportunity to explore the properties of and potential for metal-poor RR Lyrae studies with JWST using well-understood relationships from past HST work. We identify 20 RR Lyrae in the HST imaging and 15 RR Lyrae in the JWST imaging. We determine the periods, amplitudes, and mean magnitudes for each of the RR Lyrae. We fit the Period-Wesenheit-Metallicity (PWZ) function to the HST RR Lyrae population, and obtain a Gaia-consistent RR Lyrae-based distance to Leo P of $\mu = 26.04\pm0.07$ ($1.61\pm0.06$ Mpc), consistent with existing distance measurements. We determine a JWST-based PWZ using Leo P data along with previous JWST RR Lyrae observations in WLM. The simultaneous fit decreases the mean fractional uncertainty by 31% on the slope and by 11% on the intercept, increasing the constraining power as compared to the existing calibration.

3. The impact of nonlinear Landau damping on cosmic ray streaming and the role of external turbulence[2610.20695]
Abstract

Cosmic rays (CRs) streaming faster than the Alfvén speed generate Alfvén waves that scatter them. The saturated wave amplitude is regulated by a damping mechanism. This damping controls the CR scattering rate and drift velocity, and thus the rate at which CRs transfer momentum and energy to the ambient plasma, which sets the strength of CR feedback. In the ionized interstellar medium, nonlinear Landau damping (NLLD) is commonly invoked as the dominant damping mechanism for Alfvén waves. We test this assumption with fluid-PIC simulations in which thermal ions and electrons are modeled as fluids with optional Landau closures, allowing us to compare otherwise identical runs with and without NLLD. When waves are self-generated by monoenergetic CRs, runs with and without Landau closures are indistinguishable: NLLD is inefficient and the CRs isotropize in the wave frame. Once high-amplitude Alfvén waves mimicking external turbulence are seeded, NLLD curbs wave growth and equalizes forward- and backward-propagating waves. We show that the saturated drift velocity, which falls below the Alfvén speed, can be accurately predicted from the wave spectra alone. If the CR distribution is undersampled, a numerical instability corrupts the largest resonant scales: unphysical, unresolved pitch-angle gradients resonantly generate backward-propagating waves, driving an inverse cascade that should be absent from purely streaming-driven waves. While power-law CR distributions may permit stronger NLLD, our results indicate that NLLD is unlikely to be an intrinsic saturation mechanism of the streaming instability; instead, its effectiveness hinges on the interplay of streaming-generated modes with those of external origin.

4. A Vision for Little Red Dots in the Low-Redshift Universe with ALMA2040[2610.20697]
Abstract

The discovery of Little Red Dots (LRDs) at high redshifts with JWST has revealed a previously unknown phase of black-hole growth. However, their black-hole (BH) masses, BH-to-stellar mass ratios, cold-gas reservoirs, and depletion times remain uncertain. The recent discovery of low-redshift LRDs at $z\simeq0.1 - 0.9$ creates a decisive opportunity to access their low-$J$ CO lines for measuring molecular gas reservoirs, and the nearest systems can in principle be spatially resolved. We use J1025+1402 ("The Egg") at $z=0.1007$, the best-studied local LRD, as a benchmark to explore the scientific opportunities with ALMA2040. Assuming a molecular gas mass of $10^9\,M_\odot$, the CO(2-1) detectability strongly depends on the assumed CO-to-H$_2$ conversion ($\alpha_{\rm CO}$). With a large $\alpha_{\rm CO} \simeq 315 - 725\,M_\odot\,({\rm K\,km\,s}^{-1}\,{\rm pc}^2)^{-1}$ at a low gas-phase metallicity, the CO line would remain very challenging for the current ALMA to detect, while ALMA2040 will likely deliver a robust CO detection and thus a gas-reservoir constraint for The Egg. Furthermore, with the best angular resolution at its CO(2-1) frequency (14.6 mas, or 27 pc), ALMA2040 may spatially resolve the CO emission from The Egg. Assuming an optimistic, SMC-like $\alpha_{\rm CO}$ with a 50-hour deep integration, we produce mock position-velocity diagrams that may distinguish a $10^7\,M_\odot$ black hole from the no-BH model through its steeper central velocity structure. We conclude that ALMA2040 has the potential to directly measure the gas masses, depletion times, and excitation of local LRDs, while the CO-bright subset may ultimately yield dynamical constraints on the mass of their central black holes.

5. The Impact of High-Mass Clusters on the Binary Black Hole Merger Rate over Cosmic Time[2610.20698]
Abstract

We present a cosmological calculation of the binary black hole merger rate from dense star clusters, using a $\texttt{Cluster Monte Carlo}$ ($\texttt{CMC}$) model grid spanning the full observed parameter space of cluster mass, metallicity, and virial radius. For the first time, we incorporate $N=10^{7}$-particle $\texttt{CMC}$ models into this calculation, including a new solar-metallicity model, extending the $\texttt{CMC Cluster Catalog}$ to the most massive clusters observed in the local Universe. Using an analytic cluster formation model which convolves the cluster formation rate with delay-time distributions from our model suite, we find that the local cluster BBH merger rate is dominated by low-mass ($M_1 \lesssim 20\,M_\odot$) mergers originating from recently formed, high-metallicity clusters. While high-mass clusters are the primary sites of hierarchical (second-generation and higher) mergers, their contribution to the overall merger rate is suppressed by the exponential cutoff of the cluster initial mass function. The hierarchical merger rate is therefore highly sensitive to the exact value of the Schechter mass, $M_S$, suggesting that gravitational wave constraints on the hierarchical merger fraction can independently constrain the high-mass end of the cluster initial mass function. Our cluster formation model reproduces the observed present-day GC number density without fine tuning, and we find $M_S = 10^7\,M_\odot$ provides the best match to the observed Virgo GC mass distribution. For this value, $27\%$ of local dynamical mergers are hierarchical, compared to $18\%$ for the canonical value of $M_S = 10^{6.26}\,M_\odot$. Our merger weights, which we make publicly available, can be used for direct comparison with LVK distributions and other formation models.

6. Efficient numerical evolution of self-gravitating systems with dark-matter-baryon scattering[2610.20702]
Abstract

We present a fast algorithm to numerically evolve a spherically symmetric system consisting of dark matter and gas, including dark-matter-baryon scattering. The method augments the spherical N-body code NSphere, which collapses the six-dimensional phase space of a spherical collisionless system to three dimensions, with a one-dimensional Lagrangian treatment of gas. Dark-matter-baryon scattering is implemented as a Monte-Carlo process between the dark-matter numerical particles and the gas shells. We also implement an algorithm for radiative gas cooling that is valid for weak cooling. The gas, as well as dark-matter-baryon scattering and cooling, adds only incrementally to the run time of the code, allowing systems to be evolved on a laptop computer, a tiny fraction of the computational resources required for a full 3D N-body/hydro simulation. To illustrate, we show the evolution of a $\sim 10^{13}\,M_\odot$ halo with a dark-matter-baryon scattering cross section $\sigma_{\rm tot}/m_\chi=0.1\,{\rm cm}^2\,{\rm g}^{-1}$ but no gas cooling. We then show examples with radiative gas cooling where the cooling is altered through interaction with dark-matter particles. The resulting code, NSphere-IDM, allows the implications of dark-matter interactions for galaxy evolution to be explored quickly over large volumes of the dark-matter parameter space, and other new physics (e.g., dark-matter self-interactions or anisotropy in the distribution function) is easily incorporated. The code can also provide useful calibrations and benchmarks for fully 3D N-body or hydrodynamical simulations.

7. CoRGI in MaNGA - II. NGC 5717: cold-gas accretion and disk growth in a BGG[2610.20704]
Abstract

Large-scale stellar counter-rotation, in which two coplanar stellar components rotate in opposite directions, is usually interpreted as a signature of external gas accretion. Such systems are most often found in relatively sparse environments, where cold gas can be acquired and remain dynamically undisturbed long enough to settle into a disk. It is less clear whether this channel can operate in massive central galaxies, whose group or cluster environments can heat, strip, or perturb cold gas. Here we present NGC 5717, one of the most massive galaxies known to host a counter-rotating stellar disk and the first identified case in a brightest group galaxy. Using integral-field spectroscopy from SDSS-IV Mapping Nearby Galaxies at Apache Point Observatory (MaNGA), together with CFHT imaging, ancillary HI data, and SDSS/DESI spectroscopy of the surrounding group, we show that the galaxy contains a dynamically cold counter-rotating disk embedded in an old dynamically hot elliptical component. The disk is young ($\sim 1.3$ Gyr), metal-poor ([Fe/H] $\sim -0.7$ dex), extended ($h \sim 2.4$ kpc), and massive ($\log M_\star/M_\odot \sim 9.9$); the associated ionized gas co-rotates with it, while the available HI data indicate no significant remaining reservoir of neutral atomic gas ($\log M_{\mathrm{HI}}/M_\odot \lesssim 9$). These properties support an in situ origin of the secondary component from externally accreted gas. The chemical evolution model favors gas accretion from a cosmic filament, which is consistent with the surrounding cosmic-web geometry, and indicates highly efficient burst-like star formation with substantial metal-enriched outflows. NGC 5717 may therefore represent a rare example of disk growth in a massive central galaxy, enabled by external cold-gas accretion during an earlier phase of the group assembly history.

8. How Little Red Dots Turn Blue: Evolution of Kelvin-Helmholtz Contracting Black Hole Envelopes and Observational Diagnostics[2610.20709]
Abstract

James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) observations have revealed compact, red-colored, broad-line emitters known as little red dots (LRDs), while a bluer counterpart population has been referred to as little blue dots (LBDs). Their physical connection remains unclear: they may represent different evolutionary stages of the same phenomenon, distinct populations, or different phases of the broader evolution of early galaxies and their black holes (BHs). We propose a model in which a rapidly growing BH is embedded in a geometrically thick gaseous envelope accompanied by concurrent nuclear star formation. The model reproduces the observed spectral energy distributions (SEDs) of LRDs and LBDs, and naturally connects the two phases through Kelvin-Helmholtz contraction of the envelope and exposure of the nuclear accretion disk, as the gas supply declines due to external stellar activity or feedback. We predict the corresponding evolutionary tracks in the UV-optical slope ($\beta_{\rm UV}$-$\beta_{\rm opt}$) plane which bridge the apparent gap between LRDs and LBDs. These evolutionary tracks start from the LRD regime and consistently exhibit a red excursion that extends toward $(\beta_{\rm UV},\beta_{\rm opt})\sim(2,-1)$ before turning toward the LBD regime. This feature is largely insensitive to variations in the model parameters that govern the envelope structure, providing a robust signature of the transitional population. The transition lasts $\sim5$-$50\,\mathrm{Myr}$, depending primarily on the BH feedback efficiency and the gas-retention efficiency of the envelope. The abundance ratio of transitional systems to LRDs therefore provides a constraint on BH feedback and gas retention during the rapid growth of early supermassive BHs.

9. Tracing small-scale structure formation through cosmic time with strong gravitational lensing: A case for a next-generation sub-millimetre/millimetre interferometer[2610.20710]
Abstract

The nature of dark matter and the growth of supermassive black holes (SMBHs) remain major open questions in modern astrophysics. Both leave a key signature in an overlapping, largely unexplored mass range: $<10^7$ M$_{\odot}$ dark haloes and wandering black holes. These may be undetectable directly, but detectable through gravitational perturbation of strongly lensed images, and partly or entirely distinguishable from one another through lensing data alone. We demonstrate through realistic simulations that a 255-element sub-mm/mm interferometer could probe this critical mass regime, detecting gravitational perturbations in strongly lensed dusty star-forming galaxies (DSFGs) from objects down to at least $10^5$ M$_{\odot}$. Combined with lens-galaxy characterisation from the European Extremely Large Telescope (ELT), which strengthens the physical interpretation of detected perturbers, and the unprecedented lens samples expected from Euclid and the Square Kilometre Array (SKA), this facility could deliver a census of low-mass dark matter haloes and wandering massive black holes across cosmic time.

10. Probing ordered magnetic fields in galaxies across cosmic time[2610.20711]
Abstract

Magnetic fields are dynamically important across many astronomical environments, yet their origin, strength, and evolution over cosmic time remain poorly understood. Ordered magnetic fields are ubiquitous in the nearby Universe, as revealed by a range of observational techniques, but evidence for their existence at cosmological distances is scarce. Recent ALMA detections of polarised dust emission indicate that galactic-scale ordered magnetic fields, spanning up to kiloparsec scales, were already present in massive star-forming galaxies at redshifts up to $z\sim5.6$. This white paper argues that the ALMA2040 concept – with its nearly tenfold improvement in sensitivity and angular resolution, and full-polarisation capability extended to higher-frequency bands – will open up the study of cosmic magnetism in the early Universe. We identify four science drivers: constraining the dynamos that generate galactic-scale ordered fields, measuring the strength of early magnetic fields, connecting magnetic fields to galaxy evolution across cosmic time, and constraining dust grain properties through the polarised spectral energy distribution. We summarise the corresponding requirements in angular resolution, spectral coverage, and sensitivity, and discuss synergies with next-generation telescopes.

11. The Pristine survey XXIX. Data Release 2: Photometric metallicities for 15 millions stars down to $G\sim 20.5$[2610.20714]
Abstract

Tailored narrow-band photometric observations are an efficient way to measure the metallicity of Milky Way stars. The Pristine survey has been mapping the northern sky since 2015 using narrow-band $CaHK$ photometry obtained with the MegaCam imager on the Canada-France-Hawaii Telescope (CFHT). Data Release 1 (DR1) provided a high-precision absolute calibration of the survey observed before October 2022 using \Gaia DR3 XP synthetic photometry, yielding photometric metallicities over $\sim$6,500 deg$^2$, but was limited in depth to the availability of \Gaia spectro-photometry ($G \lesssim 17.65$). We present the Pristine Data Release 2 (DR2) that includes all Pristine observations until January 2025, corresponding to an additional 1,500 deg$^2$ of coverage, and including all faint sources down to $G\sim20.5$. This release aims to exploit the full depth of the Pristine observations to uncover the faint metal-poor halo. We process all images observed from the start of the survey in March 2015 through to January 2025 following the methodology presented in DR1. The core of the calibration utilizes the subset of bright stars with \Gaia synthetic $CaHK_\mathrm{syn}$ magnitudes to train the \texttt{PhotCalib} neural network. We then derive photometric metallicities using the established Pristine ($CaHK, G, G_{\rm BP}, G_{\rm RP}$) to [Fe/H] model. The resulting catalogue covers approximately $\sim$ 8,000 deg$^{2}$ and provides photometric metallicities for 15 millions FGK stars, which show good agreement with recently published, independent Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI) metallicities. With its increased coverage and access to a significantly fainter regime compared to DR1, DR2 significantly increases the volume of the survey. We report more than 200,000 and 6,500 very and extremely metal-poor star candidates, respectively.

12. HI rotation curves and mass-size relation for 568 galaxies from the Apertif Wide-area Extragalactic Survey[2610.20718]
Abstract

The extended HI rotation curves of galaxies are one of the most powerful tools for studying galaxy evolution and for testing different dark matter and modified gravity models. However, their sample sizes have historically been limited to a few hundred galaxies, mostly due to the survey speed of radio interferometers. Here, we present a first data release of HI kinematic models from the Apertif Wide-area Extragalactic Survey (AWES). Starting from 1924 galaxies detected in HI, we constructed 568 reliable kinematic models, which provide rotation curves, velocity dispersion profiles, surface density profiles, and HI disk geometries. This dataset constitutes the largest homogeneous HI rotation curve sample to date. The HI disks of the modeled galaxies span three orders of magnitude in HI mass, have outer rotation velocities between  20 and  300 km/s, and extend from 2 to more than 60 kpc. The number of data points of the rotation curves ranges from 3 to 23, with a peak value at 4 and a broad tail to larger values. The rotation curves display a variety of inner shapes, but in general, they tend to become flat at large radii. Based on the HI surface density profiles derived during the kinematic modeling, we studied the HI mass-size relation with a large homogeneous sample of 213 galaxies. The relation is very tight, with an observed scatter along the mass direction of  0.101 dex and an inferred intrinsic scatter along the same direction of only 0.067 +/- 0.007 dex. The best fitting slope is 1.96 +/- 0.03 when fitting for mass as a function of size, indicating that HI disks have a constant average surface density.

13. Wandering intermediate-mass black holes and off-nuclear tidal disruption events from early star clusters[2610.20719]
Abstract

Off-nuclear tidal disruption events (TDEs) reveal evidence for massive black holes outside galactic centers, but the origins of this wandering population remain uncertain. We investigate wandering intermediate-mass black holes (IMBHs) formed through runaway stellar collisions in dense, high-redshift star clusters and predict their TDE signatures across cosmic time. Our semi-analytic model samples clusters associated with low-mass subhalos at $7<z<20$ and uses merger trees from the IllustrisTNG TNG50 simulation to follow the assembly of 15 elliptical galaxies with stellar masses of $10^9-10^{11}~M_\odot$. We compare two models for high-redshift cluster formation: a maximal model taken from a high-resolution simulation and a conservative model calibrated from local cluster relations. Off-nuclear TDEs produced through this mechanism occur in bursts within 10-100 Myr of IMBH formation as the loss-cone surrounding the black hole is depleted. We estimate the rate of off-nuclear TDEs expected across time per galaxy, and characterize these events as typically occurring between $10-10^3$ kpc from their host galaxy centers. Event rates increase with galaxy mass; in the maximal model, the progenitors of the most massive galaxies reach peak off-nuclear TDE rates of approximately $5\times10^{-4}-4\times10^{-2}~\mathrm{yr}^{-1}$ per galaxy at $7\lesssim z\lesssim13$. This work provides a framework for constraining the early population of star clusters that form IMBHs through runaway collapse via their contribution to the rate of off-nuclear TDEs in the high redshift Universe.

14. An Attenuation Bump at z=9.3: Probing Dust Diversity at Cosmic Dawn[2610.20722]
Abstract

We report the identification of a UV attenuation bump in the Black Pearl, a galaxy at a spectroscopic redshift of 9.3335 observed with JWST/NIRSpec as part of the ZENITH and EMBER surveys. This is the highest-redshift UV bump reported to date, probing dust properties only approximately 500 Myr after the Big Bang. The feature is directly detected as a broad depression in the rest-frame UV continuum and is well described by a local Drude profile, with a central wavelength of 2132+/-17 Angstroms, an FWHM of 413+/-54 Angstroms, and a peak attenuation of 0.21+/-0.02 mag. Joint spectrophotometric modeling with a flexible attenuation law independently recovers a pronounced UV bump without imposing its presence a priori, regardless of the adopted star-formation history. The modeling indicates a relatively low-mass, actively star-forming galaxy, with a logarithmic stellar mass of 8.35 (+0.09/-0.08) in solar units, a star-formation rate of 2.36 (+0.54/-0.42) solar masses per year, and a visual attenuation of 0.34 (+0.12/-0.11) mag. Rest-frame optical emission-line diagnostics indicate a hard ionizing spectrum and allow for a moderate AGN contribution, but provide no evidence for a powerful AGN capable of dominating the observed UV continuum. The bump is blueshifted by 43+/-17 Angstroms (approximately 2.5 sigma) relative to the canonical Milky Way 2175 Angstrom feature and by 127 +/- 17 Angstroms relative to the few UV bumps currently known at redshift approximately 7, while its width is consistent with the broad distribution measured along Milky Way sightlines. The displaced central wavelength therefore provides the clearest evidence for a nonstandard UV-bump profile at redshift 9.3335, pointing to substantial diversity in the properties of UV-bump carriers at early cosmic times, with both PAHs and small graphite grains remaining plausible candidates.

15. A model for the ongoing subcluster interaction in Centaurus[2610.20726]
Abstract

Intracluster globular clusters (GCs) preserve the hierarchical and chemical history of galaxy cluster assembly. The nearby Centaurus cluster is believed to be undergoing an interaction between two subclusters, Cen 30 and Cen 45, which should leave an imprint on its intracluster GCs. We aim to characterise the GC population in Centaurus and constrain its recent assembly history, ultimately constructing an interaction scenario consistent with the observational evidence. Using deep VLT/FORS imaging in the $V$ and $I$ bands, we select GC candidates around the cluster centre based on morphology and colour criteria. We use a Voronoi tessellation approach to study the GC spatial distribution and density profiles, demonstrating its advantages over traditional annuli-based methods. We then constrain the evolution of the galaxy luminosity function using the GC specific frequency and colour distribution. The GC spatial distribution shows significant substructure. As in other clusters, red GCs are concentrated on the brightest galaxies, whereas blue GCs trace a more extended structure. We confirm and quantify the previously reported 'bridge' of GCs connecting NGC 4696 and NGC 4709, showing that it hosts twice as many GCs as expected from the projected GC systems of the two galaxies alone. The inferred evolution of the galaxy luminosity function agrees with previous results for Hydra I, yielding a faint-end slope of $\alpha=-1.87_{-0.06}^{+0.06}$ for the galaxy population that assembled the Centaurus core. We propose a nearly head-on collision scenario in which Cen 45 recently passed through Cen 30, with the two subclusters moving approximately along the line of sight. This scenario explains the large line-of-sight velocity difference between the subclusters, the hot X-ray-emitting gas between them, the GC bridge, and the puffed-up GC system of NGC 4709.

16. Has JWST Seen Everything? Hunting for "NIRCam-dark" Dusty Star-Forming Galaxies at $z \simeq 6 - 9$ with ALMA2040[2610.20727]
Abstract

JWST/NIRCam has recovered the stellar counterparts of many ALMA-detected, formerly HST-dark galaxies, yet ALMA has started identifying dusty star-forming galaxies (DSFGs) at $z>6$ that remain extremely faint or undetected even with NIRCam. These "NIRCam-dark" DSFGs may trace a missing rapid-growth phase connecting UV-luminous galaxies at $z>10$ to massive quiescent galaxies at $z\gtrsim4$. So far, the small, heterogeneously selected NIRCam-dark sample is insufficient to determine their abundance or role in the evolution of massive galaxies. We show that a blind 1.3-mm continuum survey with ALMA2040 can provide the first high-significance census of this elusive NIRCam-dark DSFG population across $z\simeq 6 - 9$. We conduct sensitivity and survey-speed calculations requiring a $10\sigma$ detection of a NIRCam-dark DSFG with a dust-obscured star-formation rate of $60\,M_\odot\,{\rm yr}^{-1}$ at $z = 8$. At a like-for-like 7.5-GHz normalization, ALMA2040 will reach the required 1.3-mm RMS of $21\,\mu$Jy beam$^{-1}$ about 90 times faster than current ALMA. With 100 hr on-source, ALMA2040 can cover an area of $\sim0.5$ deg$^2$ and detect $\langle N \rangle\sim16$ NIRCam-dark DSFGs over $7.5<z<9$, assuming a fiducial number density $n=3\times10^{-6}$ cMpc$^{-3}$. Dividing the survey among independent fields with deep JWST coverage would mitigate cosmic variance and enable immediate NIRCam, MIRI, and spectroscopic characterization of their environments. The $\geq512$-GHz instantaneous bandwidth expected for ALMA2040 will also detect [C II] line at a wide redshift range, supplementing the continuum discovery with redshift confirmation. Such a survey will test whether NIRCam-dark DSFGs trace a missing rapid-growth phase between the earliest UV-luminous galaxies at $z>10$ and the rapid emergence of massive quiescent systems at $z\gtrsim 4$.

17. ALMA2040: Understanding the Early Assembly of Galaxies - The Formation of Star-Forming Clumps and their Role in Shaping Galaxy Evolution[2610.20730]
Abstract

Astrophysics seeks to explain how galaxies form and evolve through the interplay of stars, gas, and dust. Giant molecular clouds (GMCs), the main reservoirs and sites of star formation, link the cold interstellar medium to the birth of stars and stellar structures. Their properties reflect galactic disk conditions, and their evolution is shaped by stellar feedback and host-galaxy dynamics. Studying GMCs across cosmic time is therefore key to understanding how gas turns into stars and drives galaxy evolution. Hubble Space Telescope and James Webb Space Telescope observations reveal compact star-forming clumps in galaxies from Cosmic Noon to the Epoch of Reionization ($z\approx6$), but their link to the molecular gas reservoir is still unclear. We do not yet know how GMC properties vary with redshift and environment, how molecular gas, dust, and young stars are arranged, or whether clumps form in unstable disks or disturbed systems. Addressing these questions requires spatially resolved observations of the cold interstellar medium at the scales probed by optical/near-infrared facilities. ALMA2040, a transformational next-generation sub-mm facility, will provide the sensitivity and angular resolution needed to detect individual GMCs in representative lensed and unlensed galaxies at $z>1$. CO and [CII], complemented by dust continuum and molecular-gas kinematics, will measure the molecular gas reservoir, its physical conditions, and its dynamical state. Together with JWST and the ELT, ALMA2040 will connect cold gas, dust, and stellar structures across cosmic time. ALMA2040 will revolutionize our understanding of (i) the properties and evolution of GMCs and their connection to star-forming clumps, (ii) the spatial interplay between molecular gas, dust, and young stars, and (iii) the dynamics, survival, and formation of GMCs and stellar clumps.

18. The dawn of the first galaxies[2610.20733]
Abstract

Understanding how the first galaxies assembled their stellar mass at cosmic dawn is a central frontier of modern astrophysics. JWST has now revealed an unexpected population of ultraviolet-bright galaxies at z>10, whose intense emission points towards a rapid stellar mass growth that challenges current models of early galaxy assembly. Yet, the cold gas fueling this growth remains hidden: current ALMA sensitivity restricts routine detection to only the brightest ionized-gas tracers such as [OIII] 88$\mu$m, while [CII] 158$\mu$m, a key coolant and gas-mass proxy of the neutral interstellar medium (ISM), is undetected beyond $z\sim8$. This leaves fundamental questions unanswered: how quickly primeval galaxies gathered cold gas and turned it into stars; what physical conditions (density, temperature, radiation field) characterized their ISM; how fast metals and dust built up; and how feedback regulated early gas assembly. Only a transformational sub-millimeter facility like ALMA2040 can address these questions, enabling systematic surveys of [CII] in the first galaxies and providing a representative census of gas fractions and depletion times. A simulated galaxy at $z=11.3$ shows that ALMA2040 not only will detect, but also spatially resolve its merging cold-gas structure in [CII] on $\sim$200 pc scales in 5 hours (below 100 pc in 10 hours). ALMA2040 will also spectrally resolve multiple lines simultaneously, akin to integral-field spectroscopy, providing the key diagnostics needed to break density-metallicity-radiation-field degeneracies and map the multi-phase ISM of z>10 galaxies. The combination of dramatically increased line sensitivity and wide instantaneous bandwidth will push the search for the first galaxies out to the earliest cosmic times, with [OIII] detectable up to $z\sim20$. ALMA2040 will thus be transformative for understanding how the first galaxies assembled at cosmic dawn.

19. A Search for Dust in the Outflow of the HH212 Class 0 Protostellar System[2610.20735]
Abstract

Perhaps the most visually striking phenomenon during star formation is the ejection of material along the direction of the protostar's rotational axis. Observed in the form of molecular outflows and/or atomic jets, these features carry away mass, energy and angular momentum from the system. Given the known dust content of protoplanetary disks, and the energetics of outflows, it has been suggested that it should be possible to lift dust from disks via winds, yet limited observational evidence has been presented in the literature. Any detection of dust within protostellar outflows not only puts constraints on the dust content in outflows, but if a sufficient amount of dust can be removed from the system, the mass budget for planet formation could also be affected. We present re-imaging of archival, multi-frequency dust continuum observations taken with the Atacama Large Millimeter/sub-millimeter Array (ALMA) towards the HH212 protostellar system. We detect an extension in the dust emission, and using multi-frequency data in Bands 7 and 9, we estimate the spectral index of the emitting dust and the dust mass in the wind launching region. We find a small but still significant amount of dust mass coincident with the molecular outflow cavity. This finding is assessed in relation to the hypothesis that the outflow could be capable of transporting dust away from the HH212 protoplanetary disk.

20. The ALMA-QUARKS Survey. IV. Core Mass Function and Evolutionary Trends in Core Formation Efficiency and Chemistry[2610.20738]
Abstract

The ALMA-QUARKS (Querying Underlying Mechanisms of Massive Star Formation with ALMA-Resolved Gas Kinematics and Structures) survey targets 139 massive protoclusters selected from the ATOMS survey. The targets were observed at 1.3 mm wavelength with an angular resolution of $\sim0.3^{\prime\prime}$, corresponding to $\sim10^3$ au at the sample's median distance of $\sim3.8$ kpc. Using \texttt{getsf}, we identify 3374 dense cores and classify them as starless, protostellar, hot core, or H{\sc ii} region candidates based on spectral-line diagnostics (e.g., H30$\alpha$, CH$_3$CN, and CH$_3$OH) and CO outflow associations. The resulting catalog provides core physical properties and enables statistical investigations of dense core populations in massive protocluster environments. The derived global core mass function (CMF) after excluding H{\sc ii} region candidates, which is biased by distance effects, has a relatively flat power-law slope at the high-mass end ($\mathrm{d}N/\mathrm{d}\log M \propto M^{-\gamma}$, with $\gamma=0.98\pm0.03$). In contrast, the measured high-mass slopes in individual distance bins are approximately Salpeter-like, while their fitted lower mass limits increase with distance. HERMES started its scientific operations in June 2009 and has been the workhorse instrument since then. Regular instrument updates have been crucial. They have helped to ensure the continued provision of high-quality spectra. This will continue both now and in the future. Aims. With this contribution we review over 16 years of HERMES operations and we launch and introduce the archive to the interna- tional community at large. We describe the overall performances of the HERMES spectrograph and list the most important instrument upgrades since the start of the operations. We release about 150 000 high-resolution spectra to the community and describe global properties of the datarelease. We use a standard propriety period of 1 year with some exceptions. Methods. We used the German Astrophysical Virtual Observatory (GAVO) DaCHS platform to present the archive in a Virtual Ob- servatory (VO) compatible form with both an access via a dedicated webinterface, and via VO-compliant tools like PyVO, TOPCAT, Aladin etc. We illustrate the use in several worked-out examples to search and download the fully wavelength-calibrated, cosmic- cleaned and barycentric corrected 1D spectra. Results. With this contribution we provide the needed information of the data product and offer a reference paper for the users of the HERMES archive.

113. XuYi Interferometer Observation Simulator[2610.07042]
Abstract

The XuYi Interferometer is the first Chinese long-baseline optical interferometer currently under development by the National Institute of Astronomical Optics and Technology (NIAOT) of the Chinese Academy of Sciences. In preparation for future scientific operations, we developed the XuYi Interferometer Observation Simulator (XIOS), a lightweight MATLAB-based software package designed to assist observers in planning interferometric observations. The simulator combines target observability calculations, interferometric baseline geometry, delay-line constraints, (u,v)-coverage prediction, and visibility estimation into a single graphical user interface. It incorporates astronomical routines from the MAAT toolbox and adopts visualization concepts inspired by ASPRO2. The software allows users to evaluate observing opportunities, predict interferometric performances, and generate realistic observing configurations before telescope operations. We present the architecture of the simulator, its principal modules, and representative simulation results obtained for the current three-telescope XuYi interferometric configuration operating in the near-infrared H band.
