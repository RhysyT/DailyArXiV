1. JWST Observations of Starbursts: Polycyclic Aromatic Hydrocarbons in the NGC 253 Superwind[2611.23367]
Abstract

We present the first spatially resolved study of polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbon (PAH) evolution in the galactic superwind of the nearby (d 3.5Mpc) galaxy NGC 253. Using JWST MIRI and NIRCam, we image  $\pm$2kpc along the galaxy minor axis, capturing both sides of the starburst-driven wind at  0.05”-0.375” ( 0.85-6.4pc) resolution and providing one of the most detailed views of the wind to date. We target PAH emission at 3.3um, 7.7um, and 11.3um, revealing PAH-bright filamentary streamers along the limb-brightened edges of the outflow cones out to at least a deprojected distance r 0.5kpc, beyond which they likely become confused with the spiral arms. In the southwest (SW) and northeast (NE) streamers, PAH intensity declines faster than $r^{-2}$, suggesting either PAH destruction or a decrease in gas column density or dust-to-gas ratio rather than a response to declining illumination alone. Band ratios trace changes in PAH properties, with the 11.3/7.7um ratio increasing with distance in the SW and northwest (NW) streamers and 3.3/7.7um and 3.3/11.3um decreasing in the SW and NE streamers, indicating larger, more neutral PAHs farther from the starburst. Given the thermal and dynamical properties of the gas in the SW streamer, cool gas is expected to survive acceleration in the outflow and enable PAH shielding. Compared with those of the prototypical starburst M82, the NGC 253 band ratios indicate faster evolution in PAH size. Thus, the NGC 253 wind may represent an earlier stage of outflow evolution in which small PAHs are destroyed near the starburst within the first few Myr.

2. Seeing through the fog: astrophysical priors and the evidence for new physics in pulsar timing arrays[2611.23371]
Abstract

Pulsar timing arrays (PTAs) have found evidence for a gravitational wave background at nHz frequencies. Inspiralling supermassive black hole binaries (SMBHBs) likely contribute to at least part of the signal, but there may be additional cosmological sources from new physics in the early universe. Disentangling the two is complicated by the prohibitively slow modelling of SMBHB spectra based on realistic population models. Here we introduce binaryfog, a surrogate of up-to-date population models also used by the NANOGrav collaboration, that returns the statistical distribution of bent spectra $\mathcal{O}(10^6)$ faster than direct computation. We use binaryfog to profile over the six astrophysical parameters in fits of four extensively discussed new-physics models: a dark sector phase transition, cosmic superstrings, inflationary gravitational waves, and domain walls. Compared to the widely adopted power-law description of the SMBHB background, the preference for a phase transition drops from $2.8\sigma$ to $1.5\sigma$. The other three models follow a similar pattern. We also recommend updated astrophysical priors based on galaxy surveys published since 2014, which for current PTA data still only give a marginal preference of $1.7\sigma$ for a phase transition. An interpretation of the signal in terms of SMBHBs alone favours binaries that merge within about $2\,$Gyr after their host galaxies have merged, requiring an efficient mechanism to overcome the final parsec of separation. If binaries need longer than about $6.5\,$Gyr to merge, the preference for a phase transition exceeds $3\sigma$ under our recommended updated prior set. binaryfog can help resolve these questions, as it allows users to study the impact of updated astrophysical priors and a realistic SMBHB population model on new-physics searches with PTAs.

3. Gaia's promise to detect compact-object binaries II: Predictions for dormant neutron star binaries in DR4 and beyond[2611.23372]
Abstract

With the fourth data release (DR4) from Gaia imminent, we predict neutron star (NS) binaries with luminous companions (LCs) expected to be detectable in DR4 and the final data release (DR5). We create realistic synthetic populations of NS–LC binaries in the Milky Way using COSMIC, assuming (a) observationally motivated initial distributions of binary properties, (b) metallicity and location dependent star formation history of the MW adopted from the FIRE-2 galaxy m12i, and (c) all relevant binary interaction physics, with the rapid and delayed supernova prescriptions. We estimate the detectable NS–LC population using an approximate Gaia\ selection function based on projected orbital size of the LC as well as gaiamock, which emulates Gaia's multi-epoch astrometry and orbit-fitting pipeline- both after taking into account extinction and reddening. Across supernova models and statistical fluctuations, the former (gaiamock) pipeline predicts $\sim14$–$29$ ($\sim1$–$8$), $4500$–$4700$ ($100$–$110$), and $4800$–$5000$ ($300$–$380$) detectable NS–LC binaries in DR3, DR4, and DR5, respectively. While the orbital-size based pipeline brackets the DR3 yield of NS–LC candidates, the gaiamock prediction falls short, suggesting uncertainties in the modeled binary evolution or contributions from other formation channels. DR4 is expected to directly shed light on the the relative importance of detectable NS–LC binaries from isolated binary evolution vis a vis other channels such as dynamical formation. Interestingly, our models suggest that future releases will also include $22^{+7}_{-5}\ (13^{+5}_{-4})$ and $124^{+14}_{-15}\ (53^{+10}_{-6})$ gaiamock-detectable pulsar–MS binaries in DR4 and DR5, respectively, for the rapid (delayed) model, which are prime targets for radio follow-up.

4. Are AGN Required? Updated Photoionization Models for Rest-UV Line Diagnostics in Reionization-era Galaxies[2611.23391]
Abstract

Rest-frame ultraviolet (UV) emission lines in galaxies provide important probes of the ionizing radiation, chemical abundances, and interstellar medium conditions. Here, we consider UV emission-line diagnostics for galaxies with the observed physical properties of young, metal-poor stellar populations in the epoch of reionization. We study eight $z \sim 7$–$9$ galaxies using deep JWST/NIRSpec G140M and G395M spectroscopy from the Carbon-3 plus Oxygen survey. We measure rest-frame UV and optical emission lines, $\mathrm{C\,IV}$ $\lambda\lambda1548,1551$, $\mathrm{He\,II}$ $\lambda1640$, $\mathrm{O\,III]}$ $\lambda1666$, $\mathrm{C\,III]}$ $\lambda\lambda1907,1909$, $\mathrm{[O\,II]}$ $\lambda\lambda3727,3730$, H$\gamma$, $\mathrm{[O\,III]}$ $\lambda4364$, H$\beta$, and $\mathrm{[O\,III]}$ $\lambda\lambda4960,5008$. The galaxies have low oxygen abundances, $12+\log(\mathrm{O/H})=7.50$-$7.90$, low C/O ratios, $\mathrm{C/O}=0.10$-$0.32$, and high electron densities, $n_e(\mathrm{C\,III]})=(1.9-4.9)\times10^4\,\mathrm{cm}^{-3}$. We compute Cloudy photoionization models using BPASS and BC03 stellar populations to test how lower C/O and higher gas density affect the UV diagnostics. We find that reducing C/O and increasing the gas density shift the star-forming grids into regions conventionally classified as composite or AGN-like. We therefore conclude that positions within these regions alone do not establish the presence of an AGN in the galaxies studied here. However, some lines remain enigmatic, including strong $\mathrm{He\,II}$, which is not reproduced by the updated models. These results highlight the need for UV diagnostic boundaries that account for the abundance patterns and gas conditions of galaxies during the epoch of reionization.

5. Orbital parameters for 50 million stars observed by Gaia and spectroscopic surveys[2611.23411]
Abstract

Together with ground-based spectroscopic stellar surveys, the Gaia mission has changed Galactic archaeology over the past decade. Nevertheless, attempts to combine these datasets remain scarce, even though precise orbital parameters for as many stars as possible are crucial to unraveling the Galaxy's formation and evolutionary history. This work aims to construct the largest homogeneous catalog of stellar orbital parameters ever compiled to date, using the most up-to-date data from nine large-scale spectroscopic stellar surveys (Gaia RVS DR3, APOGEE DR19, BOSS DR19, LAMOST DR11, Gaia-ESO DR5, GALAH DR4, RAVE DR6, SEGUE DR12, DESI DR1), combined with Gaia DR3 astrometric data and employing an orbital integration tool for orbital modeling. Our catalog includes orbital parameters, such as orbital distances, eccentricity, inclination, energy, angular momentum, and actions, for 52,423,233 stellar sources from Bailer-Jones distances, and 40,355,569 stellar sources from recomputed StarHorse distances, of which 41,100,866 and 30,777,133 are unique objects, respectively. We also included probabilities of belonging to specific Galactic components based on kinematic classification and chemical-abundance classification. Using this catalog, we recover the known kinematic substructures of the Galactic disk while extending their mapping to Galactocentric distances of $\sim17$ kpc, demonstrating the potential of the combined dataset for studies of Galactic dynamics and evolution. We expect that this catalog will benefit a broad range of research areas, from stellar astrophysics to Galactic archaeology.

6. Excess intergalactic heating around a dual quasar system 2.8 Gyr after the Big Bang[2611.23487]
Abstract

Galaxy formation models necessitate that actively growing supermassive black holes, or quasars, strongly impact their environments. Theory suggests that quasar winds produce very hot gas bubbles that extend through the host galaxy and into the circumgalactic medium. Because this gas component is so hot and diffuse, the only way to measure its total energy is through the thermal Sunyaev-Zel'dovich (tSZ) effect. Here we report the discovery of a dual quasar system at z=2.37 which is embedded in a bubble of hot gas as detected (at 6 $\sigma$) by a tSZ flux decrement at millimeter wavelengths. The total thermal energy of the hot gas is 9.7 x 10$^{60}$ erg. This energy exceeds the amount expected from all of the hot gas in a 10$^{13}$ M$_\odot$ dark matter halo by a factor of 3.6, and constitutes at least 0.6% of the quasar's lifetime energy output. We also find evidence for hot gas on larger (100-600 kpc) scales that may also originate in quasar outflows. This considerable excess energy is more than sufficient to shut off star formation and heat the intergalactic medium, implying a significant impact from quasar-driven winds on their surroundings. Such feedback plausibly explains the steep decline of the bright end of the luminosity function of galaxies across the observable Universe, and thus provides evidence to further support leading galaxy evolution theory.

7. JEPA for astronomy: multi-survey spectral alignment puts stellar parameters on the leading principal axes[2611.23492]
Abstract

Spectra from different surveys carry different systematics and are not calibrated against one another. Multi-survey foundation models for stellar spectra are then built by aligning embeddings of the same star observed across surveys. Most in astronomy follow the recipes established for language and vision, aligning with a contrastive loss that separates every star from the others. However, this does not ensure that the stellar physics is encoded in a form that a simple read-out can recover. Holding the cross-survey architecture fixed and varying only the alignment loss, we measure what an objective encodes from the principal-component geometry of the Gaia BP/RP (XP) spectra and from a linear probe of the stellar parameters. In a properly aligned embedding, the leading two principal components form a plane that recovers an analog of the Kiel diagram, the third component tracks [Fe/H], and [alpha/Fe] varies coherently across the plane, such that the chemically defined Galactic disks and accreted populations occupy distinct loci. We show that legibility, the ease of reading the information out, is highest for a training objective that never pushes stars apart, and instead constrains only the overall distribution of each survey's embeddings (JEPA). JEPA also exceeds the contrastive alternative at the same architecture, and the performance improves further as more surveys (Gaia XP+LAMOST LRS+DESI+APOGEE) are included. Predicted abundances for 8.0 million XP giants reproduce the main [alpha/Fe]-[Fe/H] structure of the Milky Way. These results indicate that the choice of alignment objective, not alignment alone, determines whether stellar labels are linearly recoverable.

8. Stars and gas in the most metal-poor galaxies – II. Search for VMS and determination of stellar population and nebular properties at $Z\lesssim0.1\,Z_\odot$[2611.23689]
Abstract

Constraining the properties of OB-star populations and ionized gas at low metallicity is essential for understanding a broad range of astrophysical objects, from stellar black holes to the first galaxies. Rest-frame UV spectra of nearby metal-poor galaxies provide crucial constraints on theoretical predictions of low-metallicity stars and gas. However, such spectra are scarce at $Z\lesssim0.1Z\,\odot$, and candidate Very Massive Stars (VMS) remain elusive. We analyse optical and new UV spectra of nearby star-forming galaxies with $Z\lesssim0.1Z\,\odot$, selected for their nebular He II $\lambda4686$ emission and lack of Wolf-Rayet blue and red bumps, making them promising targets for VMS searches. Some galaxies exhibit stellar wind features (group 1), while others do not (group 2). For group 1, we compare STARBURST99, GALAXEV, and BPASS predictions with observed stellar lines to constrain OB-population ages and masses. The inferred ages are too old for VMS to be present. We also use pySTARBURST99 and GALAXEV photoionization models to investigate the ionized gas in a subsample, finding no evidence for VMS-dominated ionization. The absence of detected VMS may reflect their rarity, our limited sample size, or shortcomings in current models. Moreover, reproducing the extreme optical and UV line ratios simultaneously requires unrealistic N/O, ionization parameters, or Lyman continuum escape fractions, suggesting that multiple ionizing-source populations may be needed.

9. Zippers and Twisters II: The lopsidedness of satellite systems around local massive galaxies emerges from a shifting cosmic web landscape[2611.23849]
Abstract

We investigate the role of the cosmic web on the origin and strength of lopsidedness in the distributions of luminous dwarf satellites around massive galaxies analogs in the high-resolution hydrodynamic simulation NewHorizon. Using common lopsidedness estimators, we find that $>25\%$ of our sample display “statistically significant" lopsidedness incompatible with expectations for dark matter sub-haloes. The NewHorizon sample is generally skewed towards being more lopsided than naive expectations. This cautions against using dark matter or under-resolved hydrodynamic simulations as a null-hypothesis to compare to real systems. We find broad, hemispheric lopsidedness to be much more prevalent than narrow lopsidedness inherited from group infall. Across all systems, the dividing plane between the richest and emptiest hemispheres is systematically aligned with a nearby intermediate-scale cosmic filament. Further analysis reveals that the lopsidedness of systems is largely inherited from that of the network of filaments feeding them. The most populated side almost systematically contains the highest number of filaments. Such anisotropies of local filament networks are often indicative of on-going or recent zippers (filament merger $<2$ Gyr). Converging filamentary structures therefore funnel satellites preferentially onto one side of host halos, naturally generating lopsided configurations without requiring galaxy mergers. Twister remnants (of high orbital momentum mergers of filaments) are also found on the richest hemisphere of our systems, perhaps reminiscent of vorticity preventing mergers hence causing satellites' pooling. These results suggest that lopsidedness is primarily an outcome of the evolution of the intermediate-scale cosmic web. This highlights the need for increased hydrodynamic resolution in simulations.

10. Multifrequency deep radio continuum VLA observations toward OMC-2/3[2611.24014]
Abstract

We present multiwavelength (0.7-5 cm) Very Large Array continuum observations of high sensitivity (3 $\mu$Jy beam$^{-1}$), reaching an angular resolution of $0.4''$ (154 au) toward the Integral Shaped Filament in Orion. We image a field of $12.5'$ in diameter, containing the whole OMC-2 region and the southernmost $2.5'$ of OMC-3. We report the positions, flux densities and spectral indices of 47 radio sources, and discuss their nature. We conclude that, out of the 57 radio sources in the field of view, 36 (63%) are very likely tracing YSOs, 4 are tracing jet/outflow knots, and 6 are extragalactic sources; the nature of the remaining sources is unclear. We identify the driving sources of the 9 previously reported molecular outflows in our field of view. Four of them show a clear elongated radio jet morphology. We detect free-free emission from two potential photoevaporating discs associated with Class III YSOs. We find variability in 11 radio sources, 7 of which are also variable at shorter wavelengths. We find 7 pairs of radio sources with projected separations of 150-3000 au, which are potential binary systems; we identify a total of 17 potential multiple systems within this separation range when taking into account sources detected at other wavelengths. This work highlights the potential of deep VLA observations to uncover a large population of weak radio sources and study their diversity, serving as a guide for future surveys with the SKA and ngVLA.

11. Multi-epoch imaging observations of the Galactic Center with NIRCam and MIRI[2611.24110]
Abstract

The center of the Milky Way is the densest and most extreme environment in our Galaxy, its most active star forming region, and the only galaxy nucleus where we can resolve stars down to about a solar mass on the main sequence. The Galactic Center (GC) contains a nuclear stellar disk, a nuclear star cluster, and a supermassive black hole, all embedded within the so-called Central Molecular Zone, which showcases conditions of the interstellar medium that are typically found in high-redshift star-forming regions. The GC hosts the highest concentration of massive stars in the Milky Way and hosts a large concentration of rare objects, such as Wolf Rayet stars and X-ray binaries. The GC is therefore an astrophysical target of outstanding importance. A multi-epoch JWST program enables novel science including the lifetime of planet-forming disks, the search for intermediate mass black holes, direct measurements of the multi-scale gravitational potential, and a deep measurement of the star formation history. Multi-epoch imaging observations with JWST's NIRCam and MIRI instruments will go far beyond Roman WFI surveys in terms of depth and astrometric and photometric precision because of its $3\times$ better resolution, and it expands spectral coverage in narrow bands and mid-infrared wavelength coverage. The proposed observations would correspond to a Treasury Program of about 420 h, distributed over three epochs spaced three to five years apart.

12. SPONGE: Sulphur, Phosphorus, Oxygen, Nitrogen, and carbon Galactic Evolution. I. CNOPS elements as probes of physical processes in stars and galaxies[2611.24111]
Abstract

As fundamental bio-essential elements, carbon, nitrogen, oxygen, phosphorus, and sulphur (CNOPS) warrant a precise mapping of their cosmic origins. In this paper, we present the SPONGE project (Sulphur, Phosphorus, Oxygen, Nitrogen, and carbon Galactic Evolution), aimed at establishing a self-consistent galactic chemical evolution (GCE) framework capable of simultaneously reproducing local abundance benchmarks along with key observational constraints in external galaxies. Here, we focus on the Milky Way. We compile literature data and construct GCE models incorporating various single- and binary-star yields, taking stellar rotation and nova nucleosynthesis into account. Fast rotators are confirmed to be essential for primary 14N and 13C production at low metallicities. Although our homogeneous models do not reach the lowest 12C/13C ratios observed in some halo stars, this discrepancy should not be taken as a refutation of the model, as observational selection effects may apply. Predicted C and N isotopic ratios along the disc agree remarkably well with those inferred from radio observations of star-forming regions. Notably, our models explain Galactic P enrichment as originating predominantly from massive stars, without any ad hoc adjustment of the yields or the need for a significant nova contribution. Conversely, novae are required to synthesize non-negligible fractions of 13C and 15N. Optimizing GCE trajectories against a comprehensive CNOPS ensemble simultaneously, rather than single diagnostics one at a time, establishes a robust baseline for future work. Remaining open questions, such as the predicted overproduction of 12C at low metallicities and 34S along the disc, alongside the abundance scatter of some key elements, provide concrete targets for subsequent papers in this series.

13. Probing the Turbulent Magnetised Interstellar Medium of the Small Magellanic Cloud through Depolarisation with POSSUM Observations[2611.24121]
Abstract

The interstellar medium (ISM) of the Small Magellanic Cloud (SMC) provides a unique laboratory for studying magnetised gas in a low-metallicity, tidally disturbed environment. We investigate this magnetised turbulent medium using broad-band polarimetric data from the Australian Square Kilometre Array Pathfinder (ASKAP)'s Polarisation Sky Survey of the Universe's Magnetism (POSSUM), leveraging a high-density Rotation Measure (RM) grid comprising 951 RMs ($\sim24$ RMs per square degree) to probe the magneto-ionic structure of the SMC. We statistically compare the number densities of polarised and non-polarised non-thermal background sources on a per-square-degree basis, revealing spatially coherent depolarisation signatures that correlate with enhanced plane-of-sky RM fluctuations. This is consistent with a turbulent cascade from large-scale structures. Analysed through RM synthesis and Stokes $QU$-fitting, we attribute the depolarisation to a turbulent magneto-ionic medium within the telescope's beam ($\lesssim6~\mathrm{pc}$). Faraday complexity is detected in $\sim$24% of the RM sources; however, no coherent spatial structure is observed in the complexity distribution, and we attribute this primarily to internal source structure or an extended halo surrounding the SMC rather than to its main body. Furthermore, as previously identified in the Large Magellanic Cloud, depolarisation in the SMC increases with emission measure; however, the underlying Faraday dispersion is $\sim$6 times weaker. With the thermal electron density smooth on the scales of the field fluctuations, this depolarisation is likely driven by magnetic field fluctuations.

14. WFST0533buha (Bi'an): A Fast Blue Ultraluminous Transient Candidate with Unusual Blue/NUV Colors[2611.24203]
Abstract

We present WFST0533buha (Bi'an), a fast blue ultraluminous transient (FBUT) candidate discovered by the Wide Field Survey Telescope Deep High-cadence $ugr$-band Survey. The source brightened by approximately 2.5 mag in $u$ within 3 days and has a half-maximum timescale ($t_{1/2}$) shorter than 6 days. Two spectra show a blue, featureless continuum but do not provide a secure redshift. Einstein Probe observations yield only X-ray upper limits, while a faint radio counterpart was detected at 15 GHz with the VLA. The early $u$-band detection and intensive multiband coverage of Bi'an demonstrate the value of deep, high-cadence blue-band observations for discovering and characterizing FBUTs at early phases. Notably, Bi'an shows redder blue/NUV colors than AT 2018cow and AT 2024wpp, suggesting possible diversity in the short-wavelength properties of FBUTs.

15. A Tight Broad-line Width-Luminosity Relation in Little Red Dots and Its Consequence for Virial Black Hole Masses[2611.24217]
Abstract

JWST has uncovered compact high-redshift sources with red, $\sim 5000$ K optical continua and broad Balmer lines, known as "little red dots" (LRDs). Whether these lines trace gas orbiting a massive black hole or electron scattering in a dense, Eddington-limited envelope around a lighter black hole leaves inferred masses spanning $10^{4}$–$10^{8}\,M_\odot$. We test both pictures by uniformly refitting public JWST/NIRSpec spectra of 29 LRDs, 15 other JWST broad-line sources, and luminosity-matched SDSS quasars. At fixed $\mathrm{H}\alpha$ luminosity, LRD broad $\mathrm{H}\alpha$ widths are remarkably uniform: their intrinsic scatter is 0.054 dex ($<0.075$ dex at 95%), less than half that of quasars (0.12 dex), clustering near $1760\ \mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$ outside the three brightest sources. Because single-epoch virial masses scale as width squared, they scatter intrinsically by $<0.16$ dex at fixed $\mathrm{H}\alpha$ luminosity, making virial masses and mass functions nearly deterministic restatements of luminosity regardless of the underlying physics. Compared with the Keplerian speed $v_{\rm K}$ at the photosphere of an Eddington-limited envelope, computed from each source's measured continuum luminosity and temperature, observed widths are $2.5$–$3.0\,v_{\rm K}$ ($2\text{--}3\times$ standard expectations for virial factors $f_{\rm vir}\approx0.5\text{--}1$), with a quarter of the flux above escape speed and no detected continuum-luminosity dependence at fixed $\mathrm{H}\alpha$ luminosity. Bound orbits outside a spherical photosphere thus require sub-Eddington luminosities ($\leq 0.10\text{--}0.30\,L_{\rm Edd}$), whereas an Eddington or super-Eddington envelope requires uniform $2\text{--}3\times$ non-gravitational broadening within the $\mathrm{H}\alpha$-emitting gas.

16. Reconstructing the mass-redshift distribution of supermassive black holes using multiband gravitational-wave observations[2611.24281]
Abstract

The formation and evolution of supermassive black holes (SMBHs) remain major open questions in astrophysics. Astrophysical models of SMBHs invoke black-hole (BH) seeds formed at high redshift, which subsequently grow through gas accretion and mergers. Although observations of high-redshift quasars and active galactic nuclei constrain these scenarios, the mechanisms governing SMBH formation and growth remain uncertain. Future gravitational-wave (GW) observations across the millihertz, decihertz, and audio-frequency bands will provide a complementary and direct probe of SMBH assembly by tracing their merger history. We investigate the extent to which multiband GW observations can reconstruct the mass-redshift distribution of these mergers. We simulate populations of BH mergers predicted by different astrophysical models and identify those observable by the millihertz detector LISA, the decihertz detector LGWA, and next-generation ground-based detectors operating in the audio-frequency band. We reconstruct the mass-redshift distribution of these mergers using the nonparametric inference code FIGARO. We find that combining observations across frequency bands substantially improves the reconstruction for the light-seed models considered here, whose merger populations span broad ranges of mass and redshift. In contrast, for the heavy-seed models considered here, LISA alone recovers most of the accessible mass-redshift distribution, with little additional improvement from the multiband network.

17. Radio emission from a local Little Red Dot: a challenge to the ionized cocoon scenario[2611.24387]
Abstract

Several scenarios have been proposed to explain the properties of Little Red Dots (LRDs), invoking either heavy obscuration by dense neutral gas, super-Eddington accretion, or an ionized gas cocoon that produces electron scattering. Radio observations can distinguish between these scenarios. Radio emission is largely unaffected by neutral gas and dust obscuration, whereas free-free absorption can suppress it in the presence of large free electron columns. Here, we investigate the origin of radio emission from the local LRD J1047. We jointly analyze its radio data and the broad H$\alpha$ emission from optical spectroscopy to test the predictions of different physical scenarios. We find that the observed radio luminosity must have an AGN origin, and it is consistent with that of a standard radio-quiet AGN obscured by dense neutral gas. Pure super-Eddington accretion underpredicts the radio luminosity by a factor of $\sim 30$, although a combination of super-Eddington accretion and obscuration remains viable. Interestingly, the statistically preferred model for the H$\alpha$ profile is an exponential fit, which is often associated with electron scattering in LRDs. This interpretation implies an electron column density of $N_e=2.4\times10^{24}\rm~cm^{-2}$ and a strong free-free absorption ($\tau_{\rm ff,5GHz}\sim 10^6$), incompatible with the radio detection. This tension suggests that the exponential profiles of J1047 more likely trace a stratified broad line region rather than electron scattering, with potential implications for virial black-hole mass estimates also in high-$z$ LRDs. Less likely, the radio emission may escape through a partially covering ionized medium or originate outside the putative scattering cocoon.

18. Unveiling the Central Black Hole of NGC 3862: A Direct Mass Determination Using ALMA's High-Resolution Molecular Gas Kinematics[2611.24393]
Abstract

We present the direct dynamical measurement of the supermassive black hole (SMBH) mass in NGC 3862 (3C 264), a massive Fanaroff-Riley type I radio galaxy. Using ALMA $^{12}$CO(2-1) observations, we map the cold molecular gas kinematics in the circumnuclear disk and model them with the KinMS forward-modeling code. The $^{12}$CO(2-1) disk exhibits a nearly face-on, ring-like morphology with regular disk rotation and a steep central velocity rise, providing clean dynamical constraints on the central gravitational potential. Our best-fit model yields $M_{\rm BH} = 2.40^{+0.52}_{-0.32}[{\rm stat.}] \pm 0.91 [{\rm sys.}] \times 10^9~M_\odot$ and a stellar mass-to-light ratio of $M/L_{\rm F814W} = 4.11^{+0.99}_{-0.27}[{\rm stat.}] \pm 0.17 [{\rm sys.}]~M_\odot/L_\odot$ (both at $1\sigma$ confidence level). This dynamically derived $M/L_{\rm F814W}$ consistent with an old, metal-rich stellar population and a Salpeter IMF. The SMBH sphere of influence is spatially resolved, confirming the robustness of our measurement. This places NGC 3862 among SMBHs with direct molecular gas dynamical constraints, contributing to the census needed to calibrate SMBH-host galaxy co-evolution models at the high-mass end of the scaling relations.

19. Powerful Radio Jets and Galaxy Assembly Align at Cosmic Noon[2611.24490]
Abstract

Powerful radio galaxies at cosmic noon ($1<z<2$) offer a unique laboratory for investigating jet-driven supermassive black hole (SMBH) feedback on their host galaxies and surrounding environments. Unlike broad-lined quasars, radio galaxies lack a dominant nuclear point source in the rest-frame optical–UV, enabling direct constraints on host-galaxy structure and its relation to the radio jet. We analyze Hubble Space Telescope (HST) WFC3/IR (rest-frame optical) and WFC3/UVIS (rest-frame UV) imaging of the $z>1$ 3CR radio galaxies. Beyond confirming the classical rest-frame UV alignment effect, we find that the radio jets preferentially align with the major axes of the smooth rest-frame optical hosts, extending the alignment to the older stellar component. The projected axes of candidate mergers in eight systems preferentially follow the radio axes. This multiscale coherence suggests a common projected axis linking the radio source to host assembly, perhaps established by repeated mergers or anisotropic infall. We test scattered quasar light and jet-triggered star formation as origins of the UV emission along this axis. The much shallower-than-expected radial decline in UV surface brightness disfavors simple scattering as the dominant population-wide origin, while ionized-gas masses further disfavor electron scattering. The required dust masses are small relative to global reservoirs, so dust scattering remains feasible in individual regions. Comparing stellar-population ages and jet-propagation times, we identify seven UV-emitting regions as strong candidates for recently initiated jet-triggered star formation. Together, these alignments reveal an assembly-linked direction along which jets may shock or compress material and hidden-quasar radiation may illuminate it.

20. Solid-state formation of H$_2$CS and CH$_3$SH in molecular clouds: Carbon-initiated reactions with atomic and molecular hydrogen[2611.24508]
Abstract

The solid-state formation pathways of thioformaldehyde (H$_2$CS) and methanethiol (CH$_3$SH) in dense molecular clouds remain poorly constrained. We investigate carbon-initiated reactions in hydrogen sulfide (H$_2$S) ice in the presence of atomic or molecular hydrogen. We co-deposited H$_2$S, ground-state atomic carbon, and H or H$_2$ at 10 K under ultra-high vacuum. We monitored the reaction products using reflection absorption infrared spectroscopy (RAIRS) and characterized their thermal desorption using temperature-programmed desorption (TPD) with quadrupole mass spectrometry. Gas-phase ab initio calculations were used to assess pathways to CH$_3$SH formation. Atomic carbon reacted with H$_2$S at 10 K to form H$_2$CS, detected as both monomers and H$_2$CS-derived oligomers. With atomic H, successive H addition to H$_2$CS leads to CH$_3$SH. With molecular H$_2$, CH$_3$SH also forms efficiently. The results support an alternative pathway in which C reacts with H$_2$ to form CH$_2$, which abstracts H from H$_2$S. The resulting CH$_3$ and SH radicals can then recombine to form CH$_3$SH. The infrared bands and TPD fragmentation patterns support the formation of H$_2$CS and CH$_3$SH in the ice at 10 K. These findings expand the set of viable solid-state pathways to sulfur-bearing molecules and constrain models of sulfur chemistry in prestellar environments.

21. Cosmological structure formation in ultralight dark matter[2611.24509]
Abstract

An important challenge in cosmology is to describe the formation of galaxies and structures, starting from small inhomogeneities in the early Universe, which later grew and formed the so- called dark matter halos.
