1. TNGC-SIDM I: Evolution of massive cluster mergers under the self-interacting dark matter paradigm[2610.18957]
Abstract

Merging galaxy clusters provide an ideal laboratory for testing the self-interacting nature of dark matter. In this paper, we present a pilot study of magnetohydrodynamical cosmological zoom-in simulations of two cluster mergers selected from TNG-Cluster, with $M_{\rm 200c} \sim 9\times10^{14}~M_\odot$ and $M_{\rm 200c}\sim 2\times10^{14}~M_\odot$. The simulations are performed with both standard collisionless cold dark matter (CDM) and self-interacting dark matter (SIDM), assuming an isotropic, velocity-independent cross-section of $\sigma_{\rm T}/m_\chi = 1~\rm cm^2~g^{-1}$, with an additional $0.5~\rm cm^2~g^{-1}$ run for the low-mass system, and the mass resolution and galaxy formation model are unchanged from TNG-Cluster. We show how dark matter self-interactions produce an isothermal core in the dark matter halo, generate unique substructures in the intracluster light, and introduce variation in the multi-wavelength observables of the intracluster plasma. Using these simulations, we test the validity of observational probes for dark matter self-interactions in cluster mergers. We find that the shock-to-shock separation shows similar evolution in the self-interaction models tested here, providing a reference for distinguishing the $\sim100-200~\rm kpc$-scale difference in halo separation induced by self-interactions. Moreover, the dark matter halo centroid of the main cluster in the massive cluster merger is offset from the brightest cluster galaxy by as much as $\sim20~\rm kpc$, larger than the offset in the sub-cluster. This offset persists beyond 4 Gyr after the first pericenter passage. Our results indicate that sample selection plays a key role in testing the possible self-interacting nature of dark matter using cluster mergers, and that double radio relic cluster mergers and massive clusters with evidence of an ancient major merger offer a promising testbed for this purpose.

2. How JWST is Revolutionizing Our View of Early Galaxy Formation[2610.18960]
Abstract

The James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) has been revolutionizing our understanding of early galaxy formation over its first four years of science operations. With un- precedented sensitivity, spatial resolution, and wavelength coverage from the near- to mid-infrared, JWST has enabled astronomers to identify hundreds of star-forming galaxies at redshifts z> 8 (within  650 million years of the Big Bang) through strong spectral breaks and characterize these galaxies through sensitive spectroscopy. These capabilities have provided new insights into the stellar populations, neutral gas, nebular continuum emission, chemical enrichment, dust content, and ionization conditions of galaxies during cosmic dawn, revealing more numerous and brighter systems than anticipated from pre-JWST models. We discuss the implications of these discoveries for reionization, the buildup of stellar mass and metals, and the evolution of galaxy populations and luminosity functions. JWST is also revealing a new physical picture of early galaxies, characterized by compact, clumpy star formation, high gas fractions, and intense radiation fields. Despite much progress, many open questions remain, including the efficiency, intermittency, and stellar initial mass function (IMF) of early star formation, conditions in the interstellar medium (ISM), masses, and structure of the earliest galaxies, and the topology of reionization. Resolving these open questions will require lensing magnification, sensitive spectroscopy to > 5 micron, including with the Atacama Large Millimeter Array (ALMA), exploration to higher redshifts, and larger, more complete samples, helping to clarify how the first galaxies formed and evolved.

3. EMRIs in Kerr Spacetime: The Role of Inclination Diffusion[2610.18964]
Abstract

Extreme mass ratio inspirals (EMRIs) are expected to be a major source of gravitational waves (GWs) for the future {\it Laser Interferometer Space Antenna} ({\it LISA}). An EMRI occurs when a compact object, driven by the emission of gravitational radiation, slowly inspirals into a massive black hole (MBH). The classical formation channel involves repeated two-body interactions within dense nuclear star clusters. However, both EMRI event rates and the expected distribution of their orbital parameters remain highly uncertain. A major source of this uncertainty is the poorly constrained ratio between gradually inspiraling EMRIs and direct plunges, which do not produce detectable GWs. While most studies of astrophysical EMRI production focus on the Schwarzschild limit, previous work examining the Kerr metric found that the spin of the central MBH can significantly affect the EMRI-to-plunge ratio. This enhancement depends sensitively on orbital inclination, yet earlier studies held inclination fixed. In this work, we develop the mathematical framework describing inclination evolution driven by two-body encounters evolving along Kerr geodesics. By implementing 3D diffusion into Monte Carlo simulations, we show that initially prograde orbits may evolve into retrograde states, thereby converting potential EMRIs into plunges or vice versa. We estimate the EMRI-to-plunge ratio and find that substantial MBH spin enhances the EMRI rate by a factor of $\sim 2.5$. Additionally, even for a non-spinning MBH, modeling the orbits as relativistic geodesics rather than Newtonian trajectories increases the number of EMRIs by a similar factor of $\sim 2.5$. The distribution of Kerr EMRI inclinations has a substantial prograde bias that may aid in identifying the subset of loss cone EMRIs within large future EMRI samples.

4. The Dance of the Little Red Dots: Spectroscopy of Two Blue Companions at z = 3.9 and 5.5 Reveals Young, Metal-Poor Starbursts Merging with Their LRDs within a Few Hundred Myr[2610.18968]
Abstract

The Little Red Dots (LRDs) are compact, red sources, abundant at $4 \lesssim z \lesssim 9$ and likely powered by massive black holes. Photometric studies have found blue companions around them, but none has yet been confirmed spectroscopically. This Letter presents the only two LRD-companion pairs with spectroscopy of both members in the literature: "Waltz" at $z = 3.9$ and "Tango" at $z = 5.5$, with projected separations of $3.6$ and $2.5$ kpc. Both companions lie at the redshift of their LRD, within $132 \, \rm km \, s^{-1}$ and $57 \, \rm km \, s^{-1}$, and are physically identical, but $524$ Myr apart: starbursts $\lesssim 50$ Myr old, metal-poor ($0.2 \, \rm Z_\odot$), dust-free, dynamically cold, and as bright as the LRDs themselves in the rest-frame UV. The LRD in Waltz hosts a $\approx 3 \times 10^{6} \, \rm M_\odot$ black hole accreting at half the Eddington rate, a factor of a few above the birth mass of a direct collapse black hole, and is embedded in gas as metal-poor as its companion. Orbital modeling shows that Tango is bound in $81\%$ of the allowed orbits and that Waltz is bound if its host halo exceeds $5 \times 10^{10} \, \rm M_\odot$. Both blue companions then merge with their respective LRD within a few hundred Myr. We conclude that we are observing what heavy seeding requires: a young black hole beside the metal-poor starburst that the synchronized-pair scenario predicts, in the last steps of a cosmic dance that ends in a merger, and in a galaxy seeded by its black hole.

5. Wave Cold Dark Matter Can Reproduce the Radial Acceleration Relation in X-ray Elliptical Galaxies[2610.18969]
Abstract

Wave cold dark matter (wave CDM) is a well-motivated variant within the collisionless CDM paradigm, in which the de Broglie wavelengths of ultralight bosons become relevant on galactic scales. While many of the previous tests of wave CDM have focused on small-scale tensions involving solitonic cores and certain soliton–halo relations in galaxies, the radial acceleration relation (RAR) provides a complementary test: for a given baryonic mass distribution, it probes whether the DM halo can reproduce the observed total gravitational field. In this work we test wave CDM against the RAR using eight X-ray elliptical galaxies from the compilation of Lelli et al. We perform a suite of 3D Schrödinger–Poisson simulations and evolve the wave-CDM halos to virialized quasi-stationary states. For each galaxy and each adopted boson mass, we select the realization whose final snapshot most closely matches the empirical RAR. We find that the selected wave-CDM realizations for these elliptical galaxies can reproduce the RAR without including baryonic feedback or dark-matter self-interactions. At the boson mass $m_a=10^{-23}$eV, the combined fit to the eight galaxies gives $a_0=1.19\times10^{-10}\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$, consistent with the canonical value inferred from disk galaxies. When the boson mass is varied over $3\times 10^{-24}$ eV $\le m_a \le 2\times 10^{-23}$ eV, the best-fit value of $a_0$ changes by only $\sim 11\%$, and this variation shows no systematic trend with $m_a$. This insensitivity to boson mass arises because, over the radial range relevant for the RAR, the virialized halos are supported mainly by their velocity field, whereas the boson-mass-dependent quantum-pressure term is dynamically subdominant. These results show that wave CDM can reproduce the RAR in elliptical galaxies, and provide a new galactic-scale test of the model beyond the previous focus on solitonic cores.

6. GroArnold: A Framework for Constructing Action-based Models for Triaxial Galaxies and Dark Matter Halos[2610.18978]
Abstract

Action-based distribution functions $f(\mathbf{J})$ provide a powerful description of stellar systems. Consequently, the construction of physically accurate and dynamically stable $f(\mathbf{J})$ for galactic components has emerged as a key challenge in the field. In this study, we present GroArnold, a computational framework designed to construct analytical $f(\mathbf{J})$ expressions for axisymmetric and triaxial galaxies and dark matter halos. The framework integrates modules for $N$-body initial condition generation, $N$-body simulation, angle–action variable estimation, and distribution-function fitting into a unified workflow. It supports a wide range of density profiles and kinematic properties, and is uniquely designed to generate stable $N$-body models for user-specified galactic systems, with the aim of obtaining an $f(\mathbf{J})$ that approximates a dynamically stable and physically consistent state. To this end, we extend the triaxial Stäckel Fudge method of the TACT code, implemented in our Nbody-TACT module, to enable action estimation directly from $N$-body gravitational potentials. GroArnold provides a robust and flexible tool for deriving numerically empirical, explicitly analytical distribution functions for user-specified galaxies or halos, thereby facilitating a wide range of practical $f(\mathbf{J})$-based modeling applications. In addition, it serves as a powerful tool for investigating the dynamics of realistic triaxial galaxies and halos beyond the scope of traditional spherical or axisymmetric models. We assessed the accuracy of the output $f(\mathbf{J})$ by comparing its predicted spatial and velocity distributions with those of the input $N$-body snapshot, and confirmed that it provides a reasonably accurate representation of the input. The code is modular, publicly available, and designed to support future extensions and adaptations.

7. A Nonlinear General Implicit Runge-Kutta Integrator for Multifluid Charged Dust and Gas[2610.18979]
Abstract

Charged dust in magnetized gas is subject to aerodynamic drag and the Lorentz force, both of which can be difficult to model numerically in a fluid simulation where their characteristic timescales are shorter than the timestep set by the Courant condition. We present a two-stage general implicit Runge-Kutta (GIRK) source integrator for multifluid charged dust coupled to an ideal magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) gas, implemented in the open source GPU-accelerated quokka code. Drag and Lorentz forces are advanced together in one implicit source update, avoiding errors in the terminal drift that can arise when the two forces are advanced in separate substeps. The timestep is compared with a drag-Lorentz timescale to select between resolved and stiff branches. The resolved branch uses Gauss-Legendre coefficients, while the stiff branch uses a coefficient set designed to recover the terminal drift set by the drag-Lorentz balance. We also allow the dust stopping time and charge-to-mass ratio to depend on the evolving state, evaluating them separately at the two implicit stage states and solving the resulting nonlinear stage equations by Picard iteration. The source update conserves total gas-dust momentum, and its computational cost scales linearly with the number of dust species. Tests reproduce the expected drag damping and terminal drift, confirm the predicted gyromotion amplitude and phase errors, demonstrate accurate treatment of state-dependent source coefficients, and recover the expected wave, shock, and instability dynamics in dusty MHD flows.

8. Substructure-enhanced Collisional Relaxation in Stellar Systems[2610.18985]
Abstract

Two-body collisional relaxation in stellar systems is conventionally estimated using the classical Chandrasekhar theory, in which a subject star is assumed to be embedded in a homogeneous medium of field stars and their velocity distribution taken to be isotropic and Gaussian. Real stellar systems, however, can exhibit pronounced spatial and kinematic substructures. We investigate how such substructures – particularly the non-classical spatial inhomogeneity – modify the two-body diffusion coefficient. We retain hyperbolic two-body encounters but generalize the encounter calculation by integrating explicitly over the spatial distribution $n(\mathbf{r})$, while evaluating the velocity contribution from the full distribution $f_v(\mathbf{v})$. This yields a separable tensor expression for the diffusion coefficient. We find that spatial substructure produces the dominant enhancement. The enhancement of the diffusion coefficient increases systematically as the effective fractal dimension $D_{\rm frac}$ decreases, $\log \zeta_r(D_\text{frac}) \sim \left(1-D_\text{frac}/3\right)^q$ with $q \approx 4.28$. For the observationally motivated case with $D_{\rm frac}\approx 1.5$, the enhancement is moderate, whereas substantially lower fractal dimensions produce much larger enhancements. In contrast, the anisotropic, power-law-tailed, and fractal-like velocity distributions modify the diffusion coefficient only by factors of order unity. Our representative substructured model yields enhancement ratios $\zeta_r\simeq3.45$ and $\zeta_v\simeq1.33$ when the measured particle-number trends are extrapolated to $N=10^{11}$, corresponding to an overall enhancement of $\zeta\simeq4.6$. Therefore, within the two-body encounter framework and the commonly used separable distribution functions, the enhanced collisional relaxation still has little impact on the mean-field nature of typical galactic systems.

9. JWST observations of 47 Tucanae: I. The low-mass main sequence down to the hydrogen-burning limit[2610.18995]
Abstract

We present the first results of a series of studies based on JWST observations of the globular cluster 47 Tucanae (47 Tuc, or NGC 104) obtained with NIRCam. By combining new data with earlier JWST observations, we derive high-precision proper motions (PMs) over a multi-year baseline (of  3 years) and construct a clean sample of cluster members extending to very faint magnitudes (F444W 24.25 mag, corresponding to  0.077 solar masses, slightly above the estimated hydrogen-burning limit of 47 Tuc,  0.074 solar masses) along the main sequence (MS). We investigated the presence of the low-mass MS kink previously identified in 47 Tuc using the new F090W and F444W photometry. No significant signature of the feature is found using these filters, consistent with its proposed physical interpretation as a consequence of the transition in carbon chemistry toward a methane-dominated equilibrium in very low-mass stars. A comparison with theoretical isochrones indicates that the stellar population near the bottom of the MS is likely dominated by oxygen-poor stars, in agreement with previous findings. Using artificial-star tests, we quantify the completeness of our sample and show that the requirement of PM-based membership significantly limits the detection of the faintest objects, indicating that current JWST observations of 47 Tuc are not sufficiently deep to reliably trace the cluster's brown dwarf cooling sequence. The mass function (MF) of 47 Tuc shows a structured behaviour, with a break at  0.15 solar masses not associated with the onset of convection, and a mild but systematic radial variation consistent with mass segregation. Our analysis indicates that the MF shape in this mass regime is more complex than a simple power-law description. This feature in the MF may point to a presently unrecognised mechanism affecting the formation or dynamical evolution of low-mass stars in globular clusters.

10. The mass functions of multiple stellar populations in 47 Tucanae[2610.18996]
Abstract

We used a combination of James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) and Hubble Space Telescope (HST) observations to investigate the mass functions (MFs) of the different stellar populations in the globular cluster (GC) 47 Tucanae (NGC 104) over a wide stellar-mass interval. We identified the multiple populations along the lower main sequence using only JWST photometry and, in particular, colour combinations sensitive to variations in oxygen abundance. We derived the MFs of the first- and second-population (1P and 2P) stars separately and found that in the low-mass regime, the present-day MF of the 1P population is significantly steeper than that of the 2P population, which appears nearly flat. We also present the results of a dynamical model showing that this behaviour requires a different initial mass function (IMF) for the 2P and the 1P populations in the 0.1-0.4 solar masses mass range, thus providing a new fundamental constraint for the study of the star formation of multiple populations in GCs.

11. The convective-transition gap in 47 Tucanae with JWST[2610.18998]
Abstract

We report the detection of the convective-transition gap along the lower main sequence (MS) of the Galactic globular cluster (GC) 47 Tucanae using deep James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) observations. We analyse the stellar luminosity function (LF) in the vicinity of the gap location using two independent colour-magnitude diagrams (CMDs). In both cases, the LF is characterised by a narrow gap followed by a discontinuous increase in the stellar number counts, and presents an overall morphology qualitatively consistent with that reported in previous studies. By modelling the observed LFs with an empirical likelihood model, we estimate the statistical significance of the observed feature. Additional JWST passbands exhibit similar signatures, providing further evidence for the presence of a discontinuity.

12. The connection between massive galaxies and their dark matter haloes from $z=0.25$ to $z \sim 4$ from the combined COSMOS, XMM-LSS and ECDF-S fields[2610.19030]
Abstract

Understanding how galaxies populate and grow within dark matter haloes is essential for linking the observed evolution of galaxies to the hierarchical growth of structure. We present a galaxy clustering analysis using optical and near-infrared data from the UltraVISTA and VIDEO surveys covering $9.3$ deg$^2$ across the COSMOS, XMM-LSS and ECDF-S fields, $\sim 5\times$ larger than previous comparable studies, providing sufficient volume to determine the halo occupation of the most massive galaxies to $z\sim 4$. We measure the angular two-point correlation function for stellar-mass-selected galaxies over $0.25<z<4$ and by combining the three fields, we reduce the cosmic variance by a factor of $\sim2$ compared to the individual fields. The stellar-to-halo mass ratio peaks at a halo mass of $M_{h}\approx10^{12} {\rm M}_{\odot}$ and is a maximum at $z\sim 1$. For the most massive galaxies, the peak halo mass ($M_{h}^{\rm peak}$) increases from $z=1\rightarrow 0$, while the peak stellar mass in such haloes remains approximately constant, indicating that at $z<1$ massive galaxy formation becomes less efficient. We also find a steady increase in the satellite fraction since $z\sim 1$, from which we infer that massive halos continue to grow hierarchically while their central galaxies experience suppressed stellar-mass growth. We suggest this is due to the central massive galaxies having their growth controlled by feedback from active galactic nuclei feedback, likely from low-power stochastic radio emission.

14. Are Broad Mg II Galaxies Fading AGN?[2610.19126]
Abstract

We analyzed a sample of 494 galaxies from the Sloan Digital Sky Survey (SDSS) I-IV that have unusual properties. They display broadened Mg II $\lambda$2798 line emission, as would be expected of Type 1 active galactic nuclei (AGN). However, they have weak or absent broad Balmer lines though broadened Balmer lines often accompany broadened ultraviolet lines in Type 1 AGN spectra. We performed a spectral decomposition of these "broad Mg II galaxies” to assess whether they can be considered fading AGN. The Balmer lines are more responsive to changes in the AGN continuum than the Mg II line. As the AGN power source fades, broad H$\beta$ and then H$\alpha$ will weaken and eventually disappear, leaving broad Mg II as a signpost of a more active past. In support of this interpretation, we found that broad Mg II galaxies have weaker AGN luminosities and lower accretion rates (parameterized by the Eddington ratio) than typical Type 1 quasars from SDSS. The Eddington ratio of broad Mg II galaxies are similar to dim-state changing-look AGN, with an average value $\lesssim$1\%, which is the threshold at which the inner region of a geometrically thin accretion disk is thought to evaporate and be replaced by a geometrically thick radiatively inefficient accretion flow in stellar-mass black holes. Broad Mg II galaxies may represent the penultimate stage in a changing-look AGN sequence prior to the complete disappearance of all broad lines.

15. SQuIGGLE: Radio AGN Found Predominantly in Young, Gas-rich Post-starburst Galaxies at z   0.7[2610.19146]
Abstract

The processes that rapidly quench massive galaxies and maintain their quiescence remain poorly understood. Substantial molecular gas reservoirs can survive this shutdown in post-starburst galaxies, raising the question of what prevents the remaining gas from efficiently forming stars. We present deep, subarcsecond VLA 6 GHz observations of 52 massive SQuIGGLE post-starburst galaxies at $z\sim0.7$ to test the prevalence and mechanical impact of radio activity during the gas-rich post-burst phase. We detect 6 GHz continuum emission in 18/52 galaxies ($\approx$35\%), spanning $\log(L_{1.4\,{\rm GHz}}/{\rm W\,Hz^{-1}})\simeq23.0$–$25.0$ and generally exceeding the contributions expected from ongoing star formation and the fading starburst, favoring an AGN origin. Ancillary data at other wavelengths provide little evidence for a population of luminous, radiatively efficient AGN, suggesting that these sources are instead dominated by comparatively weak, mechanically powered radio AGN. Radio activity is strongly concentrated within the first $\sim$200 Myr post-burst and is predominantly associated with galaxies retaining substantial gas reservoirs. Above a common radio luminosity threshold, the radio detection fraction is enhanced by a factor of $\approx$2.5 relative to the radio AGN candidate fraction in mass- and redshift-matched quiescent LEGA-C galaxies, primarily due to lower power sources. For efficient jet–ISM coupling, the inferred mechanical power could supply the approximate energy required to unbind the molecular gas on timescales comparable to its observed decline. Together, these results identify a population of radiatively inefficient AGN hosted predominantly by young, gas-rich post-starburst galaxies at $z\sim0.7$ and suggest that they may have a role in maintaining inefficient star formation.

16. Emergence of high-mass stars in complex fiber networks (EMERGE) VII. Multi-Tracer Column Density Synthesis[2610.19163]
Abstract

Obtaining accurate estimates of the gas column density $\mathrm{N(H_2)}$ is crucial to determine the physical conditions of the molecular gas in the Interstellar Medium. This paper introduces the "Multi-Tracer Column Density Synthesis" (MT-CDS) technique, a new empirical approach to derive high dynamic-range column density maps with interferometers such as ALMA. We have compared the integrated intensity maps of two density selective tracers, namely HNC (1-0) and $\mathrm{N_2H^+}$ (1-0), obtained with IRAM-30m with ancillary Herschel column density estimates in seven star-forming regions in Orion and derived individual temperature-corrected conversion factors for each of these tracers. We applied these conversion factors obtained at low-resolution into similar high sensitivity ALMA observations to derive independent column density maps describing both the low $N_\mathrm{HNC}\mathrm{(H_2)}$ and high $N_\mathrm{N_2H^+}\mathrm{(H_2)}$ column density components at high spatial resolution (4.5"). We also combined these independent maps into estimates for the total gas column density gas $N_\mathrm{tot}\mathrm{(H_2)}$ sampling more than two orders of magnitude in column density between $\sim 10^{21.5}-10^{23.5}$ cm$^{-2}$. A comparison with independent ArTeMiS column density maps at intermediate resolution (8") shows typical deviations of $\lesssim$0.2 dex from our MT-CDS gas column density estimates. We obtained individual column density maps in five of our targets at $\sim$2000 au resolution in which ALMA observations were available, that is a factor of $\sim$5 better than originally calibrated. These results demonstrate the potential of this MT-CDS technique to retrieve accurate, high dynamic range $N\mathrm{(H_2)}$ total gas column density estimates at interferometric resolution combining multiple molecular line tracers.

17. Galactic Outflows and Star-Formation Quenching: A JWST Perspective[2610.19182]
Abstract

Outflows driven by active galactic nuclei (AGN) are widely considered a key mechanism for regulating and quenching star formation in massive galaxies. However, direct observational evidence linking outflows to quenching has remained elusive for decades. This article reviews recent findings for massive galaxies at cosmic noon ($z\sim 2$), based on sensitive James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) observations tracing both star formation histories (through Balmer absorption lines) and neutral outflows (through blueshifted NaID absorption). Neutral outflows are found in $\sim$ 25 - 50% of massive ($\log(M_*/M_\odot)>$ 10) galaxies, independent of star formation rate. Outflows from star-forming galaxies are consistent with being powered by star-formation and/or AGN activity, whereas outflows from quiescent systems are generally attributed to current or previous AGN activity. There is growing evidence that recent rapid quenching of star-formation is associated with powerful AGN-driven outflows with mass loading factors $\eta \gtrsim$ 1 - 10. The neutral gas dominates the outflow mass budget but in most cases its velocity is below the halo escape velocity, indicating that this gas may form part of a galactic fountain. Detections of neutral gas inflows and residual cold gas reservoirs in quiescent galaxies suggest that other feedback mechanisms such as AGN heating are required to keep galaxies quenched over long timescales. We highlight the potential of multiphase outflow measurements to constrain feedback models, describe the main systematic uncertainties in mass outflow rate measurements, and discuss how the $z\sim 2$ results inform our understanding of the formation of the earliest massive quiescent galaxies.

18. Tracing Accretion and Inner-Disk Gas Evolution in the Orion Nebula via Br-alpha and CO[2610.19244]
Abstract

We present a systematic spectroscopic study of hydrogen Brackett-alpha (Br-alpha) and carbon monoxide (CO) fundamental-band (4.3-5.2 micron) emission from a homogeneous sample of 0.2-1.5 Msun pre-main-sequence (PMS) stars in the Orion Nebula observed with JWST/NIRSpec-MOS as part of our Chandra-JWST project. Using 73 Br-alpha and 46 CO spectra, we investigate correlations between warm-gas emission, stellar properties, accretion, and environment with censored correlation and regression analyses accounting for multiple testing. Br-alpha luminosity correlates strongly with accretion luminosity (Lacc), even after excluding sources within 2' of Theta1 Ori C, the nebula's main ionizing O-type star, whose radiation may bias proplyd Lacc estimates. This supports Br-alpha as a robust empirical tracer of accretion in PMS stars. Br-alpha also correlates with visual extinction (Av) and more weakly with stellar bolometric luminosity and mass. CO fundamental-band luminosities likewise correlate positively with Lacc and Av, relationships that persist in a larger combined JWST/NIRSpec+MIRI sample spanning multiple star-forming regions. In contrast to Br-alpha, CO luminosity shows no significant dependence on basic stellar properties, suggesting that warm molecular gas is governed primarily by accretion and local disk conditions. The weaker correlations of the Spitzer/IRAC spectral slope with Lacc and Av suggest that warm inner-disk gas may evolve or disperse faster than optically thick dust. Overall, the CO results tentatively indicate declining warm-gas emission as disks evolve, modulated by accretion-powered thermal and ultraviolet excitation, although variations in emitting area inherited from natal core conditions may also contribute.

19. Twisted Pseudodisk and Asymmetric Mass Accretion on the Circumstellar Disk II: Parameter Survey[2610.19297]
Abstract

Using three-dimensional magnetohydrodynamic simulations accounting for ohmic dissipation, we investigate the long-term evolution of the infalling envelope for $\sim 100,000$ yr after protostar formation. The primary control parameter is the initial misalignment angle $\theta_0$ between the rotation axis and the magnetic field, varied from $0^\circ$ to $90^\circ$. In all misaligned models ($\theta_0 \ne 0^\circ$), the pseudodisk (or infalling envelope) surrounding the circumstellar disk develops a highly distorted and complex morphology. Gas accretion from the envelope proceeds through discrete, channel-like flows, producing asymmetric streams rather than axisymmetric infall. Protostellar outflows are launched even in these complex environments, and inflow and outflow regions are strongly interwoven. Outflows transport angular momentum anisotropically, leading to time-dependent changes in disk orientation and outflow direction, which further enhance envelope asymmetry. We examine the relative orientation between gas flows and magnetic fields and find that, although statistical tendencies exist, accretion streams cannot be uniquely characterized as either parallel or perpendicular to the magnetic field, particularly in strongly misaligned cases. We also show that most infalling gas accretes at nearly the free-fall velocity, with only a minor fraction significantly slowed within the pseudodisk. Asymmetric accretion and stream-like structures persist beyond 10,000 au, demonstrating that large-scale asymmetry can arise intrinsically. Our results show that complex streams and asymmetric envelopes naturally emerge from magnetized core physics, even in the absence of initial turbulence or external gas accretion.

20. The Contribution of Mergers and Accretion to Dark Matter Halo Assembly: An Excursion Set Theory Approach[2610.19312]
Abstract

We study the relative contributions of mergers and accretion to the assembly of dark matter haloes within the framework of Excursion Set Theory (EST). We extend a variance-discretized merger-tree algorithm, which recursively reconstructs all branches of a complete merger tree and introduces a merger-ratio criterion for distinguishing mergers from accretion. The method is validated by comparing the resulting progenitor distributions with the analytical conditional first up-crossing distribution, showing excellent agreement over a wide range of halo masses and redshifts. We find that the accretion contribution depends strongly on the adopted merger-ratio threshold. For large merger ratios ($MR=0.1$–$0.25$), accretion accounts for up to $\sim70\%$ of the mass growth of massive haloes, whereas including all resolved mergers ($MR=10^{-6}$) reduces the accretion contribution to $\sim40\%$. These values are in good agreement with previous N-body simulation results. The accreted mass exhibits a nearly self-similar distribution in the mass-redshift plane, with the dominant contribution arising from progenitors in the range $10^{-3}M_0 \lesssim M \lesssim 10^{-1}M_0$ and predominantly at $0.5 \lesssim z \lesssim 2$. Our results demonstrate that EST-based merger trees provide an accurate and computationally efficient framework for investigating halo assembly and quantifying the relative roles of mergers and accretion in hierarchical structure formation.

21. Transients and Stellar Activity as Revealed by Ultraviolet Diagnostic Tracers[2610.19313]
Abstract

Ultraviolet (UV) observations offer unique insights into transient and variable astrophysical phenomena, from the Sun and nearby stars to supernovae and star-forming galaxies. Here, we summarise and review the diagnostic power of the UV spectrum for probing the physical conditions, dynamics and energetics of these events. We trace the historical development of UV astronomy from early satellite missions to the current landscape shaped by the Hubble Space Telescope, the GALaxy Evolution eXplorer, the Neil Gehrels Swift Observatory and AstroSat/UVIT. 93. Searching for Ultra-Light Axions with Twisted Cavity Resonators of Anyon Rotational Symmetry with Bulk Modes of Non-Zero Helicity[2208.01640]
Abstract

Möbius-ring resonators stem from a well-studied geometrical structure with a one-sided topology, the Möbius strip, and have been shown to exhibit fermion rotational symmetry with respect to an untwisted ring resonator, which exhibits boson rotational symmetry [1]. Here, we present twisted hollow resonators with equilateral triangular cross-sections, leading to cavities with anyon rotational symmetry. Unlike conventional cavity resonators, these structures permit bulk resonant modes with a non-zero complex electromagnetic overlap $\mathcal C_p=\int_V\mathbf{E}_p\cdot\mathbf{B}_p^*\,d\tau$. The real and imaginary parts of this overlap couple to different observable quadratures. Although the negative-frequency component of a real axion field is immaterial for haloscopes employing a static DC magnetic field, it must be retained for a pumped resonator because the complex forms of Maxwell's equations differ for positive and negative frequencies and produce distinct upper and lower mixing branches. Retaining both branches shows that the real overlap produces amplitude-modulated sidebands, whereas the imaginary helicity of the modes studied here produces phase-modulated sidebands through the axion-photon chiral anomaly. A sensitive ultra-light dark-matter experiment may therefore be realised with an ultra-stable oscillator and a phase-sensitive readout. The sensitivity curves calculated here require the assumed noise floor to be interpreted as phase-modulation noise rather than amplitude-modulation noise, and consequently require sufficiently low residual oscillator phase noise at the axion Fourier frequency. The scheme removes the usual requirement for an external magnetic field and permits superconducting resonators with reduced surface loss and enhanced axion sensitivity.

Abstract

The exploration of cosmic rays, which are high-energy particles originating from space and the atmosphere, has historically been associated with particle physics and astrophysics. In the last 20 years, these particles have evolved into valuable tools for observing Earth's systems.
