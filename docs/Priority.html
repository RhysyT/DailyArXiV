1. How bursty star formation shapes spectral diversity at high redshifts: breaking degeneracies in early galaxy formation[2611.03790]
Abstract

JWST has revealed an unexpected abundance of z>10 galaxies and striking spectral diversity. Here, we model bursty star formation histories (SFHs) to explore how they shape this diversity and whether spectroscopy can discriminate between explanations for the UV luminosity function (UVLF) excess. We find strong burstiness ($\sigma_{UV}(M_h=10^{10}M_\odot)$ 0.9 mag), increasing toward low halo masses, is required to reproduce the z 6 UVLF and observed fractions of galaxies in recent star formation upturns and downturns, traced by SFR$_3$/SFR$_{50}$. This burstiness naturally reproduces UVLFs and SFR upturn and downturn fractions over z 6-11. With increasing redshift, we predict galaxies in recent upturns (SFR$_3$/SFR$_{50}>5$) increasingly dominate the UVLF bright end, reflecting rising accretion rates and burstier low-mass halos. This results in higher median emission-line equivalent widths (EWs), bluer UV slopes and weaker Balmer breaks at fixed $M_{UV}$, while distributions of these properties broaden. We find the downturn fraction (SFR$_3$/SFR$_{50}<0.2$) is particularly sensitive to stochasticity: increasing towards faint $M_{UV}$ if burstiness increases in lower mass halos, and evolving with redshift only if stochasticity does. However, the model underpredicts the z 14 UVLF, so we explore two models which match the UVLF: increased burstiness and increased star formation efficiency. From z=10 to 14, at fixed $M_{UV}$, increased burstiness predicts the downturn fraction increases by  2x and the weak-emitter fraction (H$\beta$ EW<50Å) by  1.5x. By contrast, the increased efficiency model predicts a constant downturn fraction and declining weak-emitter fraction. Overall, our results indicate burstiness is important for explaining spectral diversity and evolution at z 6-14, and that spectroscopic samples spanning a broad $M_{UV}$ range can establish its role in the z 14 UVLF excess.

2. Gaia's dark partners: A new signature of dissipative dark matter[2611.03793]
Abstract

The Gaia mission has greatly improved our understanding of the stellar binary population. Beyond providing an extensive catalog of binary systems with two luminous stars, Gaia has also discovered several systems that appear to be star–black hole (BH) binaries. One of the leading candidate formation channels for these systems is dynamical exchange, in which one component of a star–star binary is ejected from the system and replaced by a BH. Exchange processes are very sensitive to the velocity distribution of the incoming objects, and become very efficient in the limit that perturbers are slow, so the exchanged binary population is a sensitive probe of slow-moving objects. A population of slow-moving, nonluminous objects is naturally produced in dissipative dark matter (DDM) scenarios, where a subcomponent of dark matter can cool to form a dark disk and dark compact objects (DCOs), including but not limited to BHs, with a small velocity dispersion. In this work, we evaluate the rate at which DCOs formed by DDM exchange into star–star binaries to form star–DCO binaries. We find that in a wide range of parameter space, even under conservative assumptions, the rate of exchange interactions is sufficient to produce the objects that Gaia has observed to date. In some portions of parameter space, the predicted exchange rate is well in excess of the rate estimated from observations. We point out the potential for competitive constraints on the DDM parameter space with current and future data, making the Gaia mission an important testbed for dark sector cooling.

3. Jets and winds from the most powerful compact object in NGC 5408[2611.03796]
Abstract

Super-critical accretion onto stellar-mass compact objects is sometimes revealed by ultraluminous X-ray sources (ULXs). In other cases, X-rays are heavily obscured along our line of sight, but a super-Eddington accretion power can be indirectly revealed by multiband studies. We have identified a candidate super-critical source in NGC 5408 (labelled X-2). We argue that: it is powered by accretion; it is among the most powerful in the local universe, despite its apparent X-ray faintness; it has exceptional optical properties (both in the continuum and in the lines). We carried out a multiband imaging and spectral study with the ATCA, the VLA, HST, the VLT and Chandra. We found an unresolved source with: variable radio emission (5-GHz luminosity of a few x 10^{35} erg/s); red optical continuum with absolute mag M_{V,0} = -9.1, M_{I,0} = -10.6; exceptionally broad low-ionization emission lines (FWHM   1700 km/s) with P-Cygni profiles; narrow, photoionized emission lines including strong He II 4686; point-like hard X-rays; marginally resolved soft X-rays. We also found an  20-pc He^{++} nebula near X-2, associated with candidate Wolf-Rayet stars. The optical continuum declined by 2 mag between 2010 and 2024, as did the intensity of broad and narrow lines. We argue that the system is powered by the radiative and mechanical output of a super-Eddington stellar mass BH, with an accretion power > 10^{40} erg/s. We propose that X-2 is a Compton-thick ULX seen at high inclination, with a flaring jet and X-ray emission emerging from the polar funnel. We argue that the optical continuum and the broad emission lines are signatures of an optically thick outflow, launched either from the accretion disk, or from an LBV-like donor star, or, more speculatively, caused by the elusive phase of common envelope ejection. We suggest that we are looking at a short-lived phase of hyper-Eddington accretion.

4. Surface Nitrogen enrichment from the LMC to the Early Universe: the roles of rotation, overshooting and mass loss[2611.03798]
Abstract

The strong nitrogen (N) features observed in high-redshift galaxies, such as GN-z11, with JWST point to an efficient source of N production in the early Universe. Very massive stars (VMSs) are promising candidates owing to their strong winds and large convective cores of CNO-processed material. Whether massive-star surfaces in general become N-enriched depends on the interplay of convective-boundary mixing (overshooting), rotation, and mass loss, which remains poorly constrained. Using a grid of MESA evolutionary models, we isolate the overshooting and rotation channels at $40$, $100$, and $200\,M_\odot$ and investigate how these channels bring N-rich material to the surface of massive and very massive stars as a function of initial mass and metallicity. We also construct a synthetic Large Magellanic Cloud (LMC) population based on a larger initial mass grid ranging from $20-300\,M_\odot$ for comparison with the observed N-enriched fraction. VMS surface enrichment is remarkably robust across cosmic metallicity. At lower metallicities, wind stripping is weaker and the overshooting channel shuts off entirely, yet a modest amount of rotation of $\sim90\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$ alone still enriches the surface. At higher metallicity, stronger winds dominate and uncover the core almost independently of the adopted mixing. The two channels operate through physically distinct mechanisms: overshooting enriches the surface only once winds strip the envelope down into the N-rich layers left by the receding core, giving an abrupt jump. Rotation, by contrast, mixes N outward continuously through the layers above the core, producing a gradual rise. Applied to the LMC, a population with modest rotation drawn from the observed velocity distribution and modest overshooting reproduces the observed N-enriched fraction without large core boundary mixing efficiencies otherwise required by non-rotating models.

5. Tiny Galaxies, Mighty Nitrogen: Linking Chemical Anomalies to Compact Galaxies at High Redshift using JWST/JADES[2611.03799]
Abstract

The origin of anomalous chemical enrichment in high-redshift galaxies discovered by JWST remains a key question in galaxy evolution. Enhanced nitrogen abundances have been identified in several galaxies at early cosmic times, but the physical conditions driving this enrichment, and their connection to galaxy morphology, remain unclear. We test whether morphologically compact galaxies at 4 < z < 7 show stronger chemical anomalies than extended systems. Using JWST/NIRSpec spectroscopic data, we construct samples of compact and extended galaxies and analyse their rest-frame spectra. We stack the spectra of each population and measure key UV and optical emission lines. We detect nitrogen emission in the compact galaxy stack, including NIV], and [NII], while also detecting key carbon and oxygen lines. The detection of [OIII]{\lambda}4363 enables a direct-Te determination of the oxygen abundance. From these measurements, we infer chemical abundance ratios, including log(N/O) and log(C/O). We find that compact galaxies exhibit elevated log(N/O) (by   0.7 dex) and log(C/O) (by   0.5 dex) abundance ratios relative to extended systems. The abundance patterns of compact galaxy stacks are comparable to those of previously identified individual nitrogen-enhanced galaxies at high redshift and second-generation globular-cluster stars in the Milky Way. These results suggest a possible link between galaxy compactness and chemical enrichment pathways in the early Universe. Deeper JWST NIRSpec IFU observations of compact high-redshift galaxies will be crucial for determining the origin, spatial distribution, and evolutionary significance of these abundance patterns.

6. Ly$α$ in Compact Broad-Line Emitters, Including Little Red Dots, Is More Closely Linked to the Host Galaxy than to the Broad-Line Nucleus[2611.03801]
Abstract

JWST has found many faint broad-line AGN at $z>4$, but it is unclear whether the Ly$\alpha$ we see from them comes from the accreting black hole or from its host galaxy. The answer matters for how these sources are interpreted and for how many ionizing photons they release. We use deep JWST/NIRSpec spectroscopy from the DIVER program to measure Ly$\alpha$ in 12 compact broad-line emitters (CBEs) at $z=4.2$–5.5 in GOODS-N. CBEs include little red dots (LRDs), little blue dots, and other broad-line AGN. Six are detected in Ly$\alpha$. Every source shows a broad-line region, yet Ly$\alpha$ does not follow it: the sources with the strongest H$\alpha$ relative to the UV include weak detections and nondetections, and every detected Ly$\alpha$ line (FWHM $\approx300$–1000 km s$^{-1}$) is narrower than broad H$\alpha$ (1100–3300 km s$^{-1}$). Instead, Ly$\alpha$ appears to track the host galaxy. We model the AGN-subtracted host with a dust-attenuated SED fit. Including the upper limits, the observed Ly$\alpha$ follows the Ly$\alpha$ production in this host model more closely than it follows H$\alpha$/UV, in both equivalent width and luminosity. Every detection exceeds its host-model value by a factor of 1.5–18, leaving room for a nuclear contribution. The black hole is visible in these sources, but the Ly$\alpha$ we observe is more closely tied to its host. The median effective Ly$\alpha$ escape fraction of the detections is only 0.09, which suggests that ionizing escape from CBEs is typically modest and varies widely.

7. A full chemical network treatment of supermassive black hole seed formation from dark matter annihilation or decay[2611.03802]
Abstract

The recent discoveries of high-redshift quasars at $z\sim6-10.1$ present a challenge to conventional supermassive black hole (SMBH) formation scenarios: their central SMBHs are too large to have grown from early stellar remnants, even under efficient super-Eddington accretion. An alternative approach is the direct collapse of primordial, dust- and metal-free gas clouds into black hole seeds as early as $z\sim20$. To prevent fragmentation from highly efficient molecular hydrogen cooling and allow such a collapse, a strong flux of UV photons is required to photodissociate molecular hydrogen, suppressing star formation. We investigate whether the decay or annihilation of dark matter with masses below $\sim200$ eV can produce sufficient UV radiation, both from the cosmological background and within early dark matter halos. Using thermal simulations of a one-zone primordial gas cloud model with the Grackle chemistry and cooling library, we map the resulting gas behavior across a range of dark matter lifetimes, cross sections, and masses. From this, we determine regions of parameter space where direct collapse into an SMBH is possible and the corresponding maximum halo mass required.

8. Isophotal signatures of the brightest cluster galaxy - intracluster light transition[2611.03803]
Abstract

The distinction between brightest cluster galaxies (BCGs) and intracluster light (ICL) remains ambiguous, with inferred cluster properties depending strongly on the adopted definition and measurement methodology. We report the presence of a common inflection feature in the radial profiles of surface brightness, ellipticity, and position angle of BCG+ICL systems, indicative of a structural transition in the underlying stellar distribution. Using a combination of low redshift clusters (z < 0.1) observed with the Wendelstein Wide Field Imager, deep Hyper Suprime-Cam observations of clusters at 0.2 < z < 0.4, and cosmological hydrodynamical simulations from Horizon-AGN and Hydrangea, we consistently identify a common inflection at radii of 60-85 kpc. We interpret this feature as a characteristic transition in the combined BCG+ICL light distribution, that may reflect the hierarchical assembly of the cluster. The persistence of this feature across multiple photometric bands, redshift ranges, and simulations supports its robustness. To identify these transitions, we employ an automated second derivative based approach that does not rely on parametric decompositions or arbitrary surface brightness thresholds. This framework provides a physically motivated and scalable means to characterise the BCG-ICL transition, and it will be of paramount importance for forthcoming large scale surveys such as the Legacy Survey of Space and Time and the European Space Agency's Euclid mission.

9. Galactic-Scale Distribution of 10Be and Its Implications for the Early Solar System 10Be/9Be[2611.03808]
Abstract

Meteoritic inclusions indicate that the early Solar System contained a substantial abundance of the short-lived radionuclide 10Be. Whether it was inherited from the parent molecular cloud or requires local irradiation or late injection remains debated. Here we present the first three-dimensional simulations of a Milky Way-like disc that follow the multi-phase interstellar medium, cosmic rays (CRs), and the production and transport of 10Be by galactic CRs (GCRs) and in supernova remnants (SNRs). Nearly all 10Be is produced by GCR interactions; SNRs contribute at most about ten percent of the 10Be inherited by newly formed stars. The 10Be/9Be ratio traces the CR energy density, declining smoothly with galactocentric radius, and because stars form where supernovae inject CRs, their natal gas is CR-enriched roughly fivefold relative to the mean interstellar medium, which one-zone estimates miss. Accounting for a possibly higher Galactic star formation rate 4.6 Gyr ago, inheritance of GCR-produced 10Be can then explain the early Solar System's 10Be/9Be in our models, without late injection or local irradiation. Even when GCR-induced 10Be production in dense gas is reduced tenfold, the meteoritic value is reached in the innermost disc and missed by only a factor of a few elsewhere, so the main conclusion is robust to uncertain low-energy GCR penetration. The inferred birth radius of the Sun depends strongly on the CR diffusivity, ranging between 3 and 8 kpc, suggesting that improved modelling of low-energy CR transport could sharpen constraints on the birthplace of the Solar System.

10. The STARfall pathway: forging intermediate-mass black holes through stellar collisions in dense star clusters[2611.03812]
Abstract

Intermediate-mass black holes (IMBHs, $10^2 < m_{\rm BH}/\mathrm{M}_{\odot} < 10^5$) are elusive objects bridging the gap between stellar-mass and supermassive black holes, whose origin remains uncertain. They may form from the collapse of very massive stars (VMSs, $m_*\geq150\,\mathrm{M}_{\odot}$) assembled through repeated stellar collisions in compact and fast evolving star clusters. We investigate the formation of VMSs and IMBH seeds via repeated mergers and collisions as a function of cluster initial conditions, assessing the implications for globular clusters and dense high-redshift system. We develop STARfall, a semi-analytic tool that follows the evolution of a massive stellar binary via binary-single interactions, mergers, collisions, and stellar evolution, while consistently accounting for the host cluster evolution. Our results show that clusters with central densities above $10^6\,\mathrm{M}_{\odot}\,\mathrm{pc}^{-3}$ can undergo a catastrophic sequence of stellar collisions, or "starastrophe", within $3$ Myr. Through this process, VMSs rapidly grow in mass and collapse into IMBHs with masses of up to $10^3-10^4\,\mathrm{M}_{\odot}$, despite the effects of intense stellar winds. Applying our models to observed systems, we find that compact clusters in the Cosmic Gems Arc and Milky Way globular clusters could have hosted VMSs that collapsed into IMBHs in this mass range. Despite their longer core-collapse timescales, the inferred central densities of Little Red Dots may also support IMBH formation in the same mass interval. Our models show that VMS assembly requires two essential ingredients: short core-collapse times and large central densities. Compared to observed systems, our models suggest that clusters in the Cosmic Gems Arcs and several Galactic globular clusters could have nurtured VMSs that directly collapsed to IMBHs with masses $10^3-10^4\,\text{M}_{\odot}$.

11. Revisiting the Missing Baryon Problem: Baryon Redistribution Across Halo Evolution[2611.03814]
Abstract

The baryon fraction provides a fundamental diagnostic of baryonic matter distribution within dark matter halos and the baryon cycle. While its cosmic value is well constrained, the baryon content within halos on nearly all scales is significantly lower than the cosmic mean; this is called the “missing baryon problem.” Using the high spatial, mass, and temporal resolutions of the NewCluster simulation, we investigate the baryon fraction evolution by tracing halo assembly histories from merger trees and gas tracer particles. We identify three distinct evolutionary phases: an early growth phase driven by cold accretion in low-mass halos, a suppression phase likely dominated by supernova feedback, and a recovery phase in massive halos enabled by deeper potential wells and hot-mode accretion. We further find that the redshift dependence of baryon fraction, particularly in low-mass halos, is influenced by the thermal state of the intergalactic medium, with suppression emerging after the surrounding gas becomes persistently hotter than the halo virial temperature. In addition, the scatter in baryon fraction at fixed halo mass correlates with the large-scale environment, with baryon-rich halos preferentially residing closer to filamentary structures. Overall, our results highlight that baryon fraction evolution is governed by the interplay between feedback and environment, requiring a time-resolved perspective on the baryon cycle.

12. H 0557$-$385: A Candidate Missing Link Between Radio-Quiet Seyferts and Young Radio Galaxies[2611.03818]
Abstract

The Seyfert 1 galaxy H 0557$-$385 (z = 0.034) has long been classified as radio-quiet on the basis of its radio-to-optical flux ratio, yet its radio properties are physically inconsistent with this designation. We present a broadband ($0.076 - 258.8$ GHz) radio spectral analysis combining new Australia Telescope Compact Array (ATCA) observations with archival Murchison Widefield Array (MWA), Australian Square Kilometre Array Pathfinder (ASKAP) and Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array (ALMA) data. The spectrum requires a two-component model: a compact, self-absorbed component with a stratified gigahertz-peaked (GPS)/high-frequency-peaked (HFP) spectral shape (turnover at $9.4^{+0.6}_{-0.5}$ GHz), superposed on an extended, optically thin power-law lobe ($\alpha_{\rm pl} = -0.78$) that dominates below  1.6 GHz. The derived parameters $-$ a source size of  15 pc, an equipartition magnetic field of 2.8 mG, a synchrotron age of $\lesssim1.8\times10^{3}$ yr, and a time-averaged jet power of ${\sim}5\times10^{41}$ $-$ $5\times10^{44}$ erg s$^{-1}$ $-$ are characteristic of compact steep-spectrum and GPS young radio galaxies, not of canonical radio-quiet AGN. The two-component structure admits two interpretations that the present data cannot distinguish: the extended emission may be the growing lobe of a genuinely young, expanding radio galaxy, or relic plasma from an earlier episode of a transient or recurrent jet. Under either, H 0557$-$385 is better understood as an intermediate object: optically a Seyfert, but with the radio-physical properties of a newly ignited or recently re-triggered radio galaxy. It is therefore a candidate missing link between radio-quiet AGN and large-scale radio galaxies. Distinguishing these scenarios will require high-resolution imaging and multi-epoch monitoring

13. X-rays from Low-mass Active Galaxies in the Galaxy And Mass Assembly (GAMA) Survey[2611.03820]
Abstract

We present new Chandra observations of seven broad-line AGNs in low-mass galaxies ($M_\star < 5 \times 10^9\,M_\odot$) selected from the GAMA sample of Salehirad et al. (2022). X-ray emission is detected in six galaxies, with luminosities spanning $\log(L_{\rm 0.5-8\,keV}/{\rm erg\,s^{-1}})\approx40.5$–43.8. Their X-ray luminosities generally exceed predictions for X-ray binary populations, and their proximity to the optical galaxy nuclei favors accreting massive black holes as the dominant emission source. Spectral fits for four sources yield photon indices $\Gamma\approx1.7$–2.2, with little evidence for intrinsic absorption in most cases. Virial black hole masses and hard X-ray bolometric corrections imply nominal Eddington ratios $\lambda_{\rm Edd}\approx0.004$–1.1, although uncertainties are substantial. Optical tracers generally imply higher bolometric luminosities and Eddington ratios, particularly for the X-ray-undetected galaxy. The X-ray-bright sources are broadly consistent with optical AGN scaling relations involving H$\alpha$ and [O III], whereas most lie below the established X-ray–mid-infrared relation, potentially reflecting host-galaxy contamination. Four sources are consistent with the expected UV-to-X-ray spectral slope relation, while three show evidence for relative X-ray weakness. These results reveal diverse X-ray properties among broad-line AGNs in low-mass galaxies and highlight the importance of multiwavelength diagnostics for understanding black hole accretion and interpreting broad-line AGN candidates at high redshift.

14. He I $λ$10830 Resonant Scattering in M17: Toward a Diagnostic of Optical Depth and Gas Kinematics[2611.03821]
Abstract

The lower level of the He I $\lambda$10830 transition is metastable and therefore well populated. In H II regions, the line can become optically thick, and its photons then undergo resonant scattering. Draine (2026) predicted that scattering splits the profile into several peaks. We present Magellan/WINERED spectra ($R \approx 70{,}000$) at six pointings along a line from the ionizing source CEN 1 across the southwestern bar of M17, with He I $\lambda$10830 and Pa$\gamma$ in the same exposures. Every He I $\lambda$10830 profile is multi-peaked, while the Pa$\gamma$ profiles are single-peaked. Because Pa$\gamma$ does not undergo resonant scattering, it traces gas kinematics; its single peak argues against a purely kinematic origin of the He I peaks, and the shared exposures exclude an instrumental origin. We therefore attribute the He I peaks to resonant scattering, as predicted. We compare the profiles quantitatively with the static, spherically symmetric models of Draine (2026), using width and asymmetry measures independent of the systemic velocity. At two pointings far from CEN 1, no static model of any optical depth reproduces width and asymmetry together: both profiles are more red-weighted than static models of comparable width. Such profiles cannot arise from uniform motion but are expected from scattering in expanding gas. We argue that the profile is shaped by both the kinematics and the optical depth of the He$^{+}$ zone, and resolved He I $\lambda$10830 profiles could therefore provide a new diagnostic of these properties in H II regions.

15. Estimating LuSEE-Night's Sensitivity to Cα Radio Recombination Lines[2611.03828]
Abstract

Carbon radio recombination lines (CRRLs) arising from the cold neutral medium (CNM) of the interstellar medium are potential foregrounds in the 1-50 MHz band that LuSEE-Night will observe from the lunar far side. CRRLs are sensitive probes of the temperature, density, and radiation field of the CNM, and LuSEE-Night will provide sky-averaged measurements in a poorly explored frequency range. We model CRRLs across this band, accounting for doppler, collisional, and radiation broadening, and we estimate line strengths using two independent methods - fixing either the peak or the frequency-integrated optical depth - applied both uniformly across the sky and with region-dependent values informed by prior low-frequency observations. We simulate LuSEE-Night's expected response to these CRRLs and further convolve the beam-diluted spectra with LuSEE-Night's polyphase-filter-bank spectrometer response, including its zoomed-mode frequency resolution of $\sim$390 Hz, to obtain the expected observed brightness temperature. Using these simulated spectra, we estimate the integration time required to detect individual CRRLs at SNR=5 across the six sky models considered. Required integration times range from as short as 30 hours (in the most optimistic model where the optical depth across the sky is $\sim$10$^{-3}$) to 27.6 years (in the most conservative models where the optical depth across the sky is $\sim$10$^{-4}$). Stacking the 361 simulated C$\alpha$ lines between 10 and 50 MHz reduces the integration time needed for an SNR=5 detection to $\sim$11 minutes and $\sim$151 hours respectively, indicating that LuSEE-Night has realistic chances of making a stacked detection of C$\alpha$ RRLs within a realistic mission lifetime, even under the most conservative optical-depth assumptions.

16. Evidence for strong shocks in the ISM of small radio sources[2611.03848]
Abstract

Radio jets deposit energy into the interstellar medium of their host galaxies, and while a jet is still confined within the host it is expected to drive shocks through the gas. Broad [OIII] 5007 profiles trace ionized gas that has been disturbed, the [OI]/H-alpha versus [OIII]/H-beta diagram helps distinguish gas photoionized by an AGN from gas excited by shocks, and the high ionization [NeV] 3426 line traces the hard UV continuum of the accretion disk, which shocks only reproduce at very high velocities. We have selected two samples of radio-loud AGN from a cross-match of the VLA Sky Survey with DESI Data Release 1, at 0.15 < z < 0.5 and with [OI] 6300 detected at > 5-sigma: one with projected radio sizes of 10-15 kpc, whose jets are still inside the host, and one with sizes greater than 25 kpc, whose jets have broken out. We fit the [OIII] and [OI] profiles, measure W80, and classify each source on the BPT diagram. The confined sources have broader [OIII] lines, sit systematically closer to the LINER region, and are detected less often in [NeV], and the [NeV] difference is not explained by redshift or dust. The broad components are not systematically blueshifted, so the confined sources are distinguished by line width rather than by outflow velocity. We then discuss how these results indicate that the jets of the confined sources are driving strong shocks through the interstellar medium of their hosts.

17. Interstellar and intergalactic medium in the ultraviolet band[2611.03864]
Abstract

This article presents an overview of key scientific challenges in the astrophysics and astrochemistry of the interstellar and intergalactic media. Addressing many of these challenges will become possible with the Russian space observatory Spektr-UF, which is currently being prepared for launch. The ultraviolet (UV) spectral range is critically important for studying the evolution of the Universe, as it contains fundamental absorption and emission lines of most chemical elements and the most important molecules. These lines make it possible to reliably determine the physical conditions and chemical composition of various astrophysical objects, as well as the characteristics of the processes occurring within them. The article examines the prospects for UV observations across a broad range of scientific problems, from investigating the epoch of helium reionization and the thermal history of the Universe to mapping ongoing star formation in galaxies. Dedicated sections address the study of the chemical evolution of galaxies at intermediate redshifts, the distribution of "hidden" baryons and metals, the small-scale structure of interstellar clouds, the physics of photodissociation regions and protoplanetary disks, and the modeling of UV emission from nebulae surrounding fast-moving pulsars. The high sensitivity and spectral resolution of the Spektr-UF instruments are expected to provide fundamentally new data across all these research areas, filling existing gaps in our understanding of the interstellar and intergalactic media.

18. Mass-Metallicity Relation of the Fossil Disk in Six Nearby Galaxies using JWST RGB Stars[2611.03961]
Abstract

We here present the first ever mass-metallicity relation (MZR) of fossil disk populations using the resolved stellar populations in the James Webb Space Telescope Near-Infrared Camera (JWST/NIRCam) images of six nearby galaxies. Both the mass and metallicity were determined using the Red Giant Branch (RGB) stars, employing methods that have been well established to study the halo populations of nearby galaxies using the Hubble Space Telescope images. Specifically, we obtained the mass and metallicity by comparing the color, magnitude and number density near the tip of the RGB (TRGB) stars in the F115W$-$F200W vs F200W color-magnitude diagrams (CMDs) with that of the PARSEC isochrones of 4 and 13 Gyr. The RGB metallicities of the disks of nearby galaxies closely match those found in galaxies at redshifts $z>8$, with the metallicity range following a power-law relation with the fossil disk mass. The power-law index of the fossil disk MZR, $\gamma\approx0.26$, compares well with the typical value found in MZRs obtained using gas metallicities across redshifts. Through this work, we illustrate the utility of JWST/NIRCam images for obtaining the mass and metallicity of the fossil disks of nearby galaxies at precisions that were not possible earlier, which opens up a new method to carry out the near-field cosmological studies.

19. An automated photometric pipeline for measuring the corotation radius of spiral galaxies[2611.04123]
Abstract

The corotation radius R_CR of a spiral galaxy marks the radius where the angular speed of the spiral pattern matches the orbital speed of the disk material. Despite its importance for understanding spiral structure and galaxy evolution, very few galaxies have corotation measurements, often derived using heterogeneous techniques. We present an automated photometric pipeline that identifies corotation radii by tracking the azimuthal phase offset between young and old stellar populations. Building on the color-gradient framework of \citet{Puerari1997}, we extract azimuthal brightness profiles from deprojected dual-band images, fit periodic models to the profiles, and identify corotation candidates at radii where the phase difference between the two stellar tracers changes sign. Each candidate is assigned an adaptive uncertainty interval and reported with a goodness-of-fit metric. We benchmark the method against a cross-method literature compilation, scoring every technique – including our own – against a reference built from the published measurements alone and evaluated leave-one-out. Over the $90$ galaxies for which such a reference exists, the pipeline places a correct corotation radius in $80.0\%$ of them, recovers $63.6\%$ of their known corotation radii, and reaches a harmonic mean of recall and purity of $0.58$, the highest values of any method in the comparison. Unlike the published methods, each of which was applied to a small, curated set of galaxies, our pipeline is run on the entire reference sample without any pre-selection, a sample two to six times larger than existing methods. The pipeline is scalable to large surveys, flexible with respect to spiral mode, and naturally recovers multiple corotation radii in galaxies with complex spiral structure, providing a uniform framework for studying disk resonances across diverse galaxy populations.

20. The vertical distribution of molecular gas in the fourth quadrant of the Milky Way[2611.04168]
Abstract

The vertical distribution of molecular gas in the Milky Way disk is poorly studied, especially in the 4th quadrant and the far side of the Galaxy. While surveys over 20 years ago suffered from coarse beam and non-Nyquist sampling, most of the recent surveys are limited to $|b|<1^\circ$ and/or focus on the Northern Galactic plane. We examine the molecular gas's vertical distribution and properties in the fourth quadrant to check the impact of Galactic structure on current star formation. We mapped two arm tangent regions ($l=[329^\circ.0,327^\circ.0]$ for the Norma-Outer arm, and $l=[310^\circ.0,308^\circ.5]$ for the Scutum-Centaurus arm) with the $^{12}$CO 3-2 line using the 7-pixel LASMA receiver of the APEX 12 m telescope, covering up to $b=[-2^\circ,+2^\circ]$ (or $|z|\sim200$ pc near the tangent points). The noise level of the $^{12}$CO 3-2 line is about 0.4 K [$T_{MB}$] in velocity channels of 0.5 km s$^{-1}$. The vertical distribution around the tangent regions of the spiral arm is fitted with two Gaussian components, the narrow one with an FWHM thickness of 25 pc and the wide one of about 100 pc. Two explanations are proposed: they are two sub-components of the thin disk (the narrowest tracing the spiral arm and the other the inter arms components), or they trace both thin and thick disks that show a smaller thickness when observed with CO 3-2 than when observed with CO 1-0. The latter indicates that the density distribution of the interstellar medium exhibits a steeper gradient at larger heights above the plane. Clumps in the two tangent regions are searched and divided into groups by relative height. Compared with the low relative height clumps ($|z|<50$ pc), the median relative height clumps ($50~\mathrm{pc}<|z|<100~\mathrm{pc}$) have lower excitation temperature and surface density, with fewer high values in the distribution. A similar tentative trend is found for the mass.

21. Tidal Evolution of Satellite Dwarf Galaxies. I. Tidal Tracks of Stellar Mass and Structure[2611.04208]
Abstract

Satellite dwarf galaxies are reshaped by the tidal field of their host, a process that may account for part of the structural diversity of the observed dwarf population. We study how the stellar component of a satellite responds to tidal stripping using a suite of 480 controlled $N$-body simulations of satellite galaxies consisting of both dark matter (DM) and stars orbiting a Milky-Way-mass host. The simulated dwarfs span a broad range of DM halo structural and stellar-distribution parameters, including halo concentration, inner density slope, stellar-to-halo mass ratio, compactness of the stellar distribution with respect to the halo, as well as the Sérsic index of the stellar profiles. We present a comprehensive library of tidal tracks, namely, the evolution of the stellar mass, half-mass radius, mean surface density, and line-of-sight velocity dispersion, as functions of the bound mass fraction of the hosting DM subhalo, and provide simple and accurate fitting functions. We also identify the dependence of tidal tracks on key factors in the initial structural conditions. Notably, stellar compactness determines the duration over which the stellar mass remains relatively intact while the surrounding DM is being stripped. The Sérsic index and inner DM slope, in turn, govern the late-stage stellar mass-loss slope and the contraction or expansion of the remnant. Our simulations and empirical models provide a framework for modelling the stellar evolution of satellites across a broad range of progenitor structures, useful for semi-analytical models of satellite galaxy evolution.

22. Extreme Multiwavelength Variability in the Recurrent Intermediate-type Changing-look Seyfert Galaxy SDSS J234728.73+242745.7[2611.04236]
Abstract

We report the discovery of the Seyfert galaxy J234728.73+242745.7 as a recurrent intermediate-type CLAGN with extreme optical/UV variability. Optical spectra spanning nearly three decades reveal Type 1.0$\rightarrow$1.5$\rightarrow$1.2 transitions, accompanied by the disappearance and reappearance of broad He II $\lambda4686$ and a broad-line Balmer decrement evolving from 4.5 to 7.2 and then 2.4. Monitoring reveals coordinated variability from X-rays to the MIR. SED decomposition yields a host-corrected g-band high-to-low-state flux ratio of $5.4\pm0.4$, exceeding the Swift/XRT ratio of $2.7\pm0.8$ across the changing-look transition. Meanwhile, the radio flux density remains stable at $\sim2$ mJy across all ASKAP observations since mid-2020. Together with optical/UV and MIR variability and the broad He II transition, these results favor intrinsic accretion-state changes as the primary driver, while allowing a secondary contribution from variable obscuration. The source displays a loop-like MIR color–magnitude evolution, likely driven by the delayed response of an extended torus. This recurrent CLAGN offers a laboratory for probing the dynamic response of the broad-line region and circumnuclear dust to accretion-state transitions.

23. Constraining Dust Properties Along the Minor Axis of Andromeda[2611.04319]
Abstract

Previous studies of dust emission in M31 based primarily on Herschel data have reported inconsistent radial trends in the dust emissivity index ($\beta$), partly due to limited wavelength coverage and the intrinsic $T_{\rm dust}$-$\beta$ degeneracy. We aim to improve the constraints of $\beta$ determinations and obtain the spatial surface density and temperature distributions at GMC scales in M31 by extending the spectral coverage into the millimeter regime and by quantifying the impact of wavelength coverage on the $T_{\rm dust}$-$\beta$ degeneracy. We present new IRAM 30m NIKA2 1.15 mm observations along the minor axis of M31 at an angular resolution of 11 arcsec, corresponding to $\sim 40$ pc at the distance of M31. These data are used with Herschel and JCMT SCUBA-2 maps to perform pixel-by-pixel single-component modified blackbody fitting. Including the 850 $\mu$m and 1.15 mm data helps reduce the $T_{\rm dust}$-$\beta$ degeneracy and yields an average emissivity index of $\beta \approx 1.8$, systematically lower than values derived from far-infrared-only analyses. Radial analysis shows higher $\beta$ values in the 5 kpc ring compared to the 10 kpc ring. The elevated $\beta$ values in the inner 5 kpc may be linked to changes in dust composition, potentially associated with enhanced starlight heating in this region. Millimeter wavelength coverage is important for improving $\beta$ constraints in M31. The lower $\beta$ values inferred from the 250–1150 $\mu$m SED fitting suggest possible variations in dust emissivity and highlight the importance of sampling the long-wavelength SED slope in nearby galaxies.

Abstract

Abstract

We present a systematic study of the faint outer light profiles of star-forming galaxies using deep JWST/NIRCam imaging from the CEERS, JADES, and PRIMER surveys. Our sample consists of 14,812 face-on disk galaxies at $1<z<9$ with $\log(M_*/M_{\odot})>8.5$, selected from the DAWN JWST Archive. To characterize the low-surface-brightness outskirts beyond the effective radius ($R_e$), we stack galaxies in bins of stellar mass and redshift, accounting for cosmological surface-brightness dimming, angular-size scaling, and PSF convolution, and estimate residual backgrounds from blank-sky stacks. The color profiles generally become bluer toward larger radii, indicating negative color gradients and supporting an inside-out growth scenario. This similarity suggests that the extended outer light is closely linked to the same processes governing the growth of the inner galaxy, rather than providing a direct tracer of ex-situ stellar components in the early universe. We also find little wavelength dependence in the outer scale lengths, indicating that dust attenuation likely has a limited impact at these radii. These results provide new constraints on the buildup of extended stellar distributions during cosmic noon and at earlier epochs.

Abstract

28. Does Topology-Dependent Chemistry Influence the Interstellar PAH Inventory? Insights from the Absence of Cyanotriphenylenes[2611.05070]
Abstract

Although a set of polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs) has recently been identified in the cold dark cloud TMC-1, several common PAH motifs remain absent, leaving the origin of this apparent structural selectivity unresolved. Triphenylene (\ce{C18H12}) provides a stringent test of whether relative thermodynamic stability alone can account for the emerging PAH inventory because it is the smallest fully benzenoid PAH predicted by Clar's rule, is exceptionally stable, and is a key subunit of larger detected aromatic frameworks such as coronene and fullerene-related species. To probe whether such thermodynamically favored carbon skeletons are selected under cold interstellar conditions, we synthesized cyanotriphenylenes and measured their first 6.5–16 GHz microwave rotational spectra as polar tracers for the non-polar parent. This representation reveals a tentative clustering of the currently detected and undetected PAHs according to topology, suggesting that the current PAH inventory cannot be rationalized solely on the basis of thermodynamic stability and may instead reflect differences in formation and destruction kinetics. The observed selectivity may arise from two complementary effects: sequential growth decay along bottom-up growth pathways and rapid consumption of bay-region intermediates by reactive unsaturated small species.

32. UV spectra of little red dots: Broad-line N/C, gas recycling and black hole masses[2611.05349]
Abstract

Radiative-transfer models of supermassive black holes accreting inside dense gas cocoons reproduce the X-ray-to-near-infrared spectral energy distribution of "little red dots" (LRDs) using the code Sirocco. In the ultraviolet, these models also predict weak broad emission lines and a series of metal absorption lines, which deep spectroscopy from the JWST SPURS survey now detects in several LRDs. Here we use the models to interpret these features. We find that N and C ions of equal charge (3+ or 2+) occupy largely the same gas in the simulations, such that the combination of the broad N IV]/C IV and N III]/C III] flux ratios simultaneously constrains both the electron density and the N/C abundance ratio of the gas in the LRD proper. The nitrogen and carbon emission of SPURS-GN-2, an LRD at $z=7.19$, requires an order of magnitude nitrogen excess compared to the solar value, N/C $= 12^{+3}_{-2}$ (N/C)$_\odot$, in the broad-line gas with electron density $\log n_e = 9.7^{+0.3}_{-0.5}$ cm$^{-3}$. UV absorption lines test this independently. In two of the four LRDs, line blueshift velocities clearly increase with the photon energy required to ionise the absorber gas. In the models the absorption lines are spatially stratified, indicating that the gas is launched near the escape speed and slowed by gravity. In GN-2, where the measurement can be made, the inferred deceleration requires radiation pressure close to the Eddington limit of a $10^{6-7}$ M$_\odot$ black hole. The lines are also typically strongly saturated, making inferences of direct abundance ratios misleading.

33. Beyond just a torus? Re-contextualising the AGN type paradigm using data-driven host galaxy quenching histories[2611.05353]
Abstract

The orientation of a dusty torus within a unified model explains the presence (Type-1) or absence (Type-2) of broad emission lines in Active Galactic Nuclei (AGN) spectra. This orientation-based unified model, however, fails to explain differences in host galaxy properties, such as star formation rates, of Type-1 and Type-2 AGNs. Star-formation histories (SFHs) can shed light on the connection between AGN type and host galaxy properties, but are challenging to extract from Type-1 AGN that outshine their host galaxies. We use mean-field independent component analysis (MFICA) spectrum decomposition to extract the stellar populations of $\sim$3000 optically selected Type-1 and Type-2 AGN host galaxies from the seventh data release (DR7) of the Sloan Digital Sky Survey (SDSS), matched in redshift ($0.16 \leq z \leq 0.4$) and AGN luminosity ($6.5<\log_{10} [L_{[OIII]}/L_\odot] <9.5$). For most ($\sim$90 per cent) AGN hosts with normal recent star formation histories, Type-2 AGNs are associated with slightly younger stellar populations than Type-1 AGNs. 34. Effects of Resolution and Local Stability on Galactic Disks: I. Multiple Spiral Mode Formation via Swing Amplification[2511.21805]
Abstract

We investigate the formation of multiple spiral modes in Milky Way-like disk-halo systems without exciting perturbations. We explore how numerical resolution, the level of local disk stability, and the presence of a live halo influence the initial appearance and the subsequent evolution of these modes. To characterize spiral structure, we compute Fourier amplitudes for modes m=1-6. In marginally unstable, lower-resolution disks, faint features appear within the first 0.5 Gyr due to numerical noise, in contrast to high-resolution models where perturbations emerge later. We find that the delay in the spiral and bar formation epoch due to the enhanced resolution is more significant in the more stable models. Across all sufficiently resolved, live-halo models with $m_{DM}/m_{\star}\le10$, the spirals exhibit a cascading sequence in both mode number and radius: higher-m modes form and decay first, followed by the delayed emergence of lower-m modes, with an inward drift of the activity's epicenter. In contrast, models with a fixed halo potential or coarse halo resolution show strong early spirals but lack this coherent cascading behavior, owing to excessive shot noise and insufficient halo responsiveness. The m=3 mode plays a transitional role, marking the onset of angular-momentum transport in the inner disk that precedes bar formation, a process absent in fixed-potential models. Our results show that a live halo with appropriate mass resolution provides the gravitational response needed to sustain and regenerate multi-mode spiral structure, even though the total angular-momentum exchange remains small.

Abstract

We present an improved semi-analytical model to predict density profiles of self-interacting dark matter (SIDM) halos and apply it to constrain the self-scattering cross section using SPARC galaxy rotation curves. Building on the isothermal Jeans approach, our model incorporates (i) velocity-dependent cross sections, (ii) an empirical treatment of core collapse, and (iii) enhanced robustness for identifying solutions. Prime-Cam is one of the primary instruments being developed for use by the CCAT collaboration with the Fred Young Submillimeter Telescope (FYST), which is undergoing construction at a height of 5600 m in Cerro Chajnantor. Prime-Cam can host up to seven instrument modules with kinetic inductance detectors (KIDs), including broadband modules ranging 280-850 GHz and imaging spectrometers for line-intensity mapping. Prime-Cam requires a number of hardware devices for standard operation and data collection, and so a distributed control system must be developed for software integration of these devices and user-friendliness when controlling Prime-Cam in the field, especially during remote operation that will be typical during observations. The Prime-Cam Control System (PCS) is being developed using the Observatory Control System (OCS) framework originally developed for Simons Observatory (SO) and is adapted for Prime-Cam and CCAT-specific requirements. Within OCS/PCS, hardware devices are controlled individually by long-running software processes known as agents, which are typically deployed in Docker containers running in the background of one of the Prime-Cam network computers. These agents are controlled by client scripts through the command line or frontend tools like Nextline and PCS-web. PCS also makes use of several external open source software tools for remote operations, data management, and logging. This system is being tested in the lab in preparation for deployment in 2026 for use with Prime-Cam on FYST.

64. Noisy pipelines II: Non-parametric Noise Inference for LISA[2611.03911]
Abstract

The Laser Interferometer Space Antenna (LISA) is expected to observe gravitational-wave signals from massive black hole binaries, extreme mass ratio inspirals, and more. Despite the significant scientific potential, accurately extracting these signals from the data will only be possible if LISA's instrumental noise is understood: poor noise modelling will result in lost signals, biased parameter estimates, and incorrect astrophysical insights. Modelling LISA's instrumental noise is more challenging than its ground-based counterpart since the data is highly correlated, meaning that the noise covariance matrix can no longer be assumed diagonal in frequency and in the different data streams. In this work, we present an initial approach to perform non-parametric noise inference for LISA that requires no prior knowledge of the LISA observatory. By fitting the full noise covariance matrix with a combination of splines and inverse Lorentzians, we show that our model can estimate the power and cross spectral densities for LISA from 7 days of simulated noise-only data. We find that the median estimate from our non-parametric model whitens the data according to the expected unit normal distribution. We envision that our non-parametric model can be used as part of the global fit, as well as in combination with modelled approaches to identify, quantify, and constrain the frequency dependence of unknown noise artifacts.

65. Direct Analysis in Real Time Mass Spectrometry (DART-MS) Study of Amino Acids in the Vacuum Stable Liquids EMI-BF$_4$, EMI-Acetate, DEMA-OTf, DEMA-MsO, and Glycerol[2611.03990]
Abstract

The detection of organics and potential biomolecules is an important goal of planetary science and astrobiology spacecraft missions, and many types of analytical chemistry for studying organics depend upon liquid solvents as working fluids. However, most liquids are difficult to maintain in the vacuum of space, complicating their use and increasing spacecraft mass and cost. Here we present a study using Direct Analysis in Real Time mass spectrometry (DART-MS) as a rapid screen for the detectability of histidine, glycine, arginine, lysine, and aspartic acid at mole fractions of about $5\times10^{-4}$ to $10^{-3}$ in five vacuum-stable liquids: EMI-BF$_4$, EMI-acetate, DEMA-MsO, DEMA-OTf, and glycerol. In all of the ionic liquids, the solvent cation dominated the spectra and no amino acid was detected, apart from a weak histidine signal in EMI-BF$_4$ at 350$^\circ$ C (median 5\% of the normalized peak intensity). The protic DEMA ionic liquids did not enhance detection, and their ionic components instead dominated the mass spectra. Aspartic acid was not detected in any liquid. In glycerol solutions, histidine, glycine, arginine, and lysine were all detected, and histidine was frequently a dominant line. We thus find glycerol to be the most promising of the liquids tested for organic detection with an ideal operating DART helium temperature of 350–400 $^\circ$C. We also found no clear evidence of water uptake or aqueous alteration in the ionic liquids, which was a secondary goal relevant to ionic liquid spacecraft electric propulsion.

66. Multimessenger astrophysics drives the case for an Australian Gravitational-Wave observatory[2611.04150]
Abstract

The next generation of an international network of gravitational-wave observatories is now in the advanced planning stages. With Australia's substantial history of contributions in this field, its history of scientific mega-projects like the Square Kilometer Array, and its unique geographic location and conditions, it is ideally placed to host an observatory in the global network. It has already been demonstrated that a gravitational wave detector in Australia plays an important role in minimizing the sky localization uncertainty of gravitational wave events. We study a particular science case for an Australian observatory, focusing on its potential contributions to multimessenger astronomy. We demonstrate that an observatory in Australia can enable the detection of up to eight multimessenger gravitational-wave plus kilonova events per year, with localisations better than $1-10°^2$ in both second- and third-generation networks. We show that, in a global network of XG observatories, an Australian observatory with 2G sensitivity would still enable significant advances in multimessenger science. The full case for an Australian observatory must be considered in a much broader context; however, this work indicates it could make substantial contributions to multimessenger astrophysics.

67. Spin Spherical Fourier Neural Operators: Efficient Equivariant Learning for Vector and Spin Fields on the Sphere[2611.04389]
Abstract

Many fields of physical interest on the sphere, such as wind, the polarisation of the cosmic microwave background and the shear of weak gravitational lensing, are spin fields, whose values change phase when the local frame rotates. We extend spherical Fourier neural operators (SFNOs) to fields of any spin, through lifted spherical convolutions that are exactly rotationally equivariant. Lifting spin fields to Mackey functions on the rotation group provides the conceptual framework, while computation is performed exactly and efficiently through their harmonic representation and by appealing to sampling theorems on the sphere and rotation group. We construct spin SFNOs (S$^2$FNOs) from these building blocks. We demonstrate empirically that rotational equivariance holds to machine precision for these layers. We achieve state-of-the-art performance on existing benchmarks, and introduce new benchmarks on which spin-aware models outperform their scalar counterparts.

68. Feature-Based and Deep Learning Methods for Astronomical Light Curve Classification[2611.04664]
Abstract

We compare feature-based machine learning and end-to-end deep learning for variable star classification using the Zwicky Transient Facility (ZTF) StarEmbed dataset, which contains about 40,000 objects across seven imbalanced classes. Classical models trained on physically motivated features consistently outperform deep networks trained directly on raw light curves. We find that limited labeled data contributes to the performance gap, while the multivariate, irregularly sampled, and morphologically diverse nature of light curves poses additional challenges for deep learning models. While deep learning holds promise for learning representations directly from observations, our results indicate that current models fail to generalize with the available training set size. These findings motivate further investigation of the benefits of scaling labeled datasets and self-supervised pretraining on large unlabeled surveys. This work establishes a quantitative baseline for future research and highlights the need for further innovations to unlock the potential of deep learning in astronomical time series analysis.

69. A Unified AI Framework for Rapid and Accurate Waveform Generation of Compact Binary Coalescences[2611.04713]
Abstract

The astrophysical discovery potential of future gravitational wave detectors will hinge on accurate and rapid waveform generation. Third generation detectors are expected to observe many long duration compact binary coalescence signals that can probe binary formation and evolution as well as the nature of matter under extreme conditions. We develop a unified framework that combines physical modeling with deep learning. The framework uses an analytic residual spectral representation, shared learning, and decoders for each binary type. It generates waveforms for binary black holes, binary neutron stars, and neutron star black hole binaries, providing a single unified generator for all compact binary coalescences. The generated waveforms include the physical effects described by each reference model, such as spin precession, higher order modes, matter effects, and postmerger radiation for binary neutron stars. The waveforms span hundreds to thousands of cycles, with mean mismatches ranging from $5.89\times10^{-4}$ to $5.34\times10^{-2}$ relative to the reference waveforms. Batch generation achieves a maximum speedup of about 1800 over reference waveform generation.

70. Geometrical and Optical Design of the GUEST Laser-Ranged Test Masses for the Detection of Gravitational Waves in the $μ$Hz range[2610.20298]
Abstract

The GUEST (Gravitational Universe Exploration with Satellite Tracking) mission aims at detecting gravitational waves in the yet almost unexplored $\mu$Hz frequency range through high-precision orbital tracking. The unprecedented scientific objectives of this experiment demand equally advanced optical solutions: the highly eccentric orbit imposes dual measurement regimes; a low-velocity, long-range scenario at apogee and a high-velocity, short-range scenario at perigee. The substantial disparity between these conditions is addressed through a novel optical architecture incorporating two distinct types of Corner-Cube Retroreflectors (CCRs) with 1" and 2" apertures (CCR1 and CCR2, respectively). The proposed layout has been designed to ensure isotropy and homogeneity of the optical response while maintaining efficient packing over the spherical surface of the spacecraft. The resulting Optical Cross-Section (OCS) has been evaluated using a custom reduced-order model, which captures the behavior of individual CCRs under varying illumination conditions. This model has been calibrated by reconstructing the optical response of the LARES-2 satellite and has enabled a detailed assessment of the contributions of each CCR type along the GUEST orbit. Finally, sensitivity analyses have been conducted to investigate the impact of key design parameters, including the ratio between CCR types, laser wavelength, spacecraft diameter, Dihedral Angle Offsets (DAOs), and mechanical configurations.

71. An efficient finite element formulation for Newtonian noise analysis[2603.15157]
Abstract

The Einstein Telescope is a third-generation underground gravitational wave observatory designed to achieve an unprecedented sensitivity down to 3 Hz. Waves propagating in the soil due to anthropogenic or natural vibration sources generate density fluctuations which cause gravitational attraction, resulting in motion of the mirrors of the laser interferometer known as Newtonian noise. A finite element formulation is presented, which evaluates the total Newtonian noise, as well as the bulk and surface contributions, from a seismic wave field defined on a finite element mesh, using Gaussian quadrature. Linear and quadratic tetrahedral (4-node and 10-node) and brick (8-node and 20-node) finite elements are supported. The corresponding volume and surface integrals are expressed in terms of finite element coupling matrices that depend only on the geometry and material properties. The formulation is verified for plane P- and S-waves propagating in an elastic homogeneous full space with a mirror suspended in a spherical cavity, assuming that the wavelength is much larger than the radius of the cavity, so that wave scattering can be ignored. Excellent agreement with analytical solutions is obtained. Similar good agreement is reported for the Newtonian noise on a test mass suspended at a finite distance above the free surface of a homogeneous elastic halfspace in which a Rayleigh wave propagates. The methodology has been implemented in the ANNA Newtonian Noise Analysis toolbox which runs in the MATLAB programming and numeric computing platform and is compatible with the open-source GNU Octave Scientific Programming Language; a Python version is also available. The proposed finite element framework provides a physically consistent and computationally efficient approach for computing gravitational-seismic coupling in heterogeneous media.
