1. Identifying Scientifically Interesting Galaxies with Vision-Language Models[2610.37747]
Abstract

Anomaly searches in large astronomical image datasets aim to identify rare objects for follow-up investigation. Yet the objects that are most statistically unusual are not necessarily those of greatest scientific relevance. We investigate whether pretrained vision-language models can directly assess scientific interest from natural-language instructions, enabling galaxy images to be ranked without labeled examples. We evaluate models on a 20,000-image Hubble Space Telescope sample that includes 167 reference anomalies previously identified through machine learning and expert inspection. By scoring images for scientific interest, Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite places 98.2% of the reference anomalies within the highest-ranked  2,000 images, at a present-day inference cost of approximately $5.05 per 10,000 images. We recover known gravitational lenses, jellyfish-galaxy candidates, and galaxies with visible jets, and release 102 high-scoring candidates for further investigation. These results show that an off-the-shelf vision-language model, without any training on the target data, offers a practical way to rank large image datasets by scientific relevance rather than rarity. 2. A GLIMPSE of Dark Matter: JWST Constraints on Early Structure Formation[2610.37751]
Abstract

While we have a solid understanding of how dark matter (DM) behaves during recombination ($z\sim 10^3$) and in the late universe ($z\lesssim$ a few), much less is known about intermediate times, when the first galaxies formed. These galaxies provide a powerful handle on DM through structure formation, as halos have to exist to host luminous sources. Here we use recent determinations of the UV luminosity function from the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) GLIMPSE lensing program, along with legacy Hubble and Subaru data, to test the small-scale behavior of DM during the epoch of reionization ($z\sim 7$). Marginalizing over astrophysical uncertainties, we find the matter power spectrum to be consistent with the cold-DM prediction down to sub-Mpc scales, with 1$\sigma$ uncertainties of 0.2 dex at $k=3-9 \, \rm Mpc^{-1}$ and 0.5 dex at $k=9-27 \, \rm Mpc^{-1}$, the smallest scales probed at these early times. For thermal-relic warm DM we constrain $m_{\rm WDM} > 3.2\, \rm keV$ (95% CL), independent of lower-$z$ probes. Our results test small-scale structure formation in a new redshift window and demonstrate the promise of JWST to constrain the nature of DM.

3. Seeing Unseen Gravitational Wave Inspirals at the Galactic Center[2610.37757]
Abstract

The Galactic Center is expected to host a population of extremely large mass-ratio inspirals (X-MRIs) migrating toward the supermassive black hole. We propose an observational approach utilizing the secular gravitational torque exerted by X-MRIs on the highly eccentric S-star S301. This approach is intrinsically limited by the stochastic nature of the surrounding stellar-mass black hole cusp. As S301 propagates through this granular medium, it experiences discrete gravitational encounters. Standard orbital fitting procedures inherently assume a smooth background potential; applying them to stochastic trajectories averages these discrete velocity discontinuities into a continuous secular drift. This introduces a systematic bias that mimics the X-MRI precession signal, invalidating traditional mass estimation techniques. To resolve this degeneracy, we implement a methodology based on the Wavelet Transform Modulus Maxima method to characterize the local Hölder regularity of the orbital motion. This model isolates the singular discontinuities induced by stellar-mass black hole encounters from the continuous torque of the X-MRI distribution, allowing for detection even when instantaneous accelerations are below the instrumental noise threshold. We demonstrate analytically that S301 is highly sensitive to the interior X-MRI population while remaining dynamically decoupled from the secular influence of the exterior cusp. Combining precision astrometry and radial velocity projections, we establish that a minimal population of two X-MRIs is detectable within a five to nine-year timeframe, allowing us to constrain the presence of an unseen orbiter population prior to the launch of space-based gravitational wave detectors.

4. Hot off the Stacks: X-rays in Romulus25, Part I - Comparisons to Observations from eRosita[2610.37763]
Abstract

Recent observations from the from Spectrum Roentgen Gamma (SRG)/eROSITA all-sky survey data have helped to illuminate the hot circumgalactic medium (CGM) around galaxies at Milky Way-masses and above. In our study, we explore the hot X-ray emitting (T \textgreater 10$^6$ K) component of the CGM in over 750 galaxies from the Romulus25 cosmological simulation (Tremmel et al. 2017) between 10.0 < log(M$_*/M_{\odot}$) < 11.5. We forward-model soft X-ray [0.5 - 2.0] keV observations of our simulated galaxies using pyXSIM and SIXTE to measure the X-ray surface brightness (SB) profiles of the hot CGM. We then compare the average X-ray surface brightness profiles of six stellar-mass selected sub-samples to compare to the observational results from Chadayammuri et al. (2022a) and Zhang et al. 2024a. Broadly, we find that our stacked SB profiles are too bright compared to observations from Chadayammuri et al. (2022a) and Zhang et al.(2024a) particularly in the inner CGM. Additionally, we calculate the X-ray luminosity (L$_{\chi}$) of the hot CGM for each galaxy and compare the average L$_{\chi}$ across stellar mass to observations from Zhang et al. (2024b) as well as measurements from the EAGLE, TNG, SIMBA simulations. Across stellar mass, the average L$_{\chi}$ of galaxies from Romulus25 are brighter than observations except at the lower, Milky Way-mass end where we are in relative agreement with both observations and most other simulations. We also consider a zero-metallicity emission model, because Romulus25 does not have metal-line cooling, finding the average L$_{\chi}$ decreases by nearly an order-of-magnitude, which better matches observations particularly in the Milky Way-mass regime.

5. Resolving the thermal component of stellar feedback with the "anchor-and-refine" scheme in galactic simulations[2610.37767]
Abstract

(abridged) Stellar feedback regulates galactic baryon cycles, but galaxy-scale simulations often cannot resolve supernova cooling radii. Momentum-based subgrid models can consequently underresolve the hot gas around young stars. We develop an "anchor-and-refine" feedback scheme that anchors a host cell near each star particle, preventing its displacement and preserving a thermal-feedback reservoir. When the cooling radius is resolved, all supernova energy is injected into the host cell as thermal energy; otherwise, the host cell is refined and terminal momentum is deposited into surrounding gas cells. Idealized tests agree with high-resolution simulations, yielding consistent superbubble properties and preserving hot interiors without artificial cooling. In an isolated Milky Way-mass galaxy, the scheme captures finer X-ray-emitting structures and a sharply defined, X-ray-bright central mid-plane. It also produces a smoother star formation history than pure hybrid feedback. Hot gas dominates outflow mass loading, with the mass-loading factor plateauing at approximately 0.2 at heights z>1 kpc. Phase decomposition indicates that hot gas sustains a steady, energy-driven wind, whereas cooler outflows decline with height because of radiative cooling and gravitational fallback. These results highlight the role of thermal feedback in driving galactic winds. By preserving hot superbubble interiors and the partition of thermal and kinetic energy, anchor-and-refine improves the treatment of hot gas, star formation, and feedback at limited resolution, supporting future applications to galaxy-scale and cosmological zoom-in simulations.

6. Extra! Extra! X-ray Emission!: X-rays in Romulus25, Part II - Physical Drivers of X-ray Emission in the CGM[2610.37769]
Abstract

Characterizing the hot (T   10$^{6}$ K) circumgalactic medium (CGM) around Milky Way-mass (MW-mass) galaxies is critical to a full understanding of galaxy evolution. In this second paper of the "X-rays in Romulus25" series, we unpack the specific physical processes giving rise to the presence of hot X-ray emitting gas in the halos of galaxies from the Romulus25 (R25) simulation, focusing on L$_{*}$ galaxies. We determine that a strong correlation appears between the mass of hot CGM gas, M$_{gas, T > 10^{5.5} K}$, and the X-ray emissivity, $\epsilon_{\chi}$, measured in the first paper of this series, (Sanchez et al. 2026a, submitted). There is also a secondary correlation between M$_{gas, T > 10^{5.5} K}$ and $\epsilon_{\chi}$ with the average metallicity of the hot CGM gas, $\bar{Z}_{gas, T > 10^{5.5} K}$. We use the well-studied PIONEER simulations from Sanchez et al. (2019), a set of zoom-in MW-mass galaxies run with the genetic modification technique of Roth et al. (2016) and the same physics as R25, to interpret these correlations. We determine that the presence of hot X-ray emitting gas is determined by two key processes: 1) the quantity of hot gas mass in the CGM, M$_{gas, T > 10^{5.5} K}$, is set by the combined total energy injection of $both$ stellar and AGN feedback, and 2) together the average temperature and metallicity of the hot CGM changes the soft X-ray spectrum of the gas resulting in brighter metal-rich CGM and dimmer metal-poor CGM for the same quantity of M$_{gas, T > 10^{5.5} K}$. These results indicate, for the first time, that metal-rich gas halos may bias X-ray observations of MW-mass galaxies.

Abstract

Aims: We measure the young stellar mass fraction in NGC 3115, a massive isolated lenticular galaxy, to test the ubiquity and spatial distribution of this component beyond previously studied ellipticals. Their presence across various ETGs with similar gradients indicates that residual star formation is a ubiquitous feature, offering key constraints on the baryon cycle in these galaxies.

8. Ejected and Rejected: Strong Scatterings in Dense Star Clusters[2610.37775]
Abstract

Understanding the evolution of collisional stellar systems is key to predicting both the bulk evolution of dense star clusters and also the rates of tidal disruption events, extreme mass ratio inspirals (EMRIs), stellar ejections, and other loss cone phenomena. Although a majority of the evolution is dictated by weak, small-angle scatterings, rare close encounters resulting in strong scatterings can sometimes produce qualitatively different outcomes. To systematically determine the astrophysical importance of strong scatterings, we derive the exact differential rate distribution of velocity kicks in isotropic and spherically symmetric distribution functions representing globular and nuclear star clusters, while also deriving the distribution boundaries and approximate rate scalings. We then extend this formalism to derive approximate rates of energy and angular momentum kicks for objects in the nuclear star cluster, and then apply this framework to study (i) ejections and (ii) “cancellation” of extreme mass ratio inspirals. We find that ejection rates are complicated in some regimes due to the saturation of the strong kick distribution and the breakdown of the weak limit, likely enhancing the effect of loss cone shielding. We also determine that a sizable fraction of proto-EMRIs will likely be canceled by strong scatterings, highlighting the need for future work to determine to what degree strong scatterings impact the true EMRI rate.

9. Simulation-Informed Dust Masses in Typical $6<z<14$ JWST Galaxies[2610.37821]
Abstract

Direct detections of dust in typical $z>8$ galaxies remain untenable, despite the presence of dust suggested by rest-frame UV/optical spectro-photometry. Informed by cosmological zoom-in simulations where dust particles evolve on-the-fly, we present a novel method for inferring dust masses in high-redshift galaxies using stellar half-light radii and UV/optical attenuation. Our methods reproduce observed trends at lower redshift, ultimately diverging from Milky Way-based calibration factors by up to $\sim1.5$ dex. Future observations may use our tabulated calibration to infer dust masses over a wide range of stellar masses and redshifts.

12. Simulating and Characterizing Non-Gaussian Small-Scale Polarized Emission[2610.37942]
Abstract

Galactic foregrounds present a major challenge for cosmic microwave background (CMB) cosmology as their amplitudes exceed the target primordial $B$-mode signal. While small-scale interstellar medium (ISM) emission is expected to be highly complex, current foreground simulations cannot reproduce the filamentary, non-Gaussian features of ISM structure in a stochastic way. To address this gap, we introduce a framework for generating stochastic small-scale foreground emission, using polarized synchrotron observations as an example use case. This method is capable of producing many independent realizations that additionally inherit morphological information from large angular scales. The underlying structural complexity incorporates anisotropic, multifractal, and advected non-Gaussian features that are tunable with a small number of parameters that carry physical interpretations. Because standard power spectra do not describe the complex phase coherence required for the structural complexity, we evaluate the synthetic fields using higher-order statistics, demonstrating that the Minkowski functionals and structure functions partially capture varying degrees of non-Gaussianity. This work lays the necessary foundation for new foreground simulation suites with stochastic realizations of possible non-Gaussian structure.

13. The Carousel Lens III: Joint lens convergence and source kinematic modeling[2610.37971]
Abstract

We introduce a new, independent modeling component to the Carousel Lens series by exploiting the spatially-resolved kinematics contained within the lensed arcs, which so far has remained rather underexplored in strong-lensing analyses. We present an end-to-end forward-modeling framework that reconstructs an integral-field spectroscopic datacube from a parameterization of the lens convergence profile together with the line-of-sight velocity, velocity dispersion, and surface brightness profiles of the unlensed source. The kinematic structure of the source acts as an additional set of ray-traceable markers distributed across every lensed image, inaccessible from imaging data alone. Furthermore, by generating an [OII] emission-line datacube, we isolate a single source redshift and remove lens and line-of-sight light contamination. We apply this framework to deep VLT/MUSE observations of the Carousel Lens, modeling the [OII] emission of the quadruply-imaged star-forming galaxy at $z_{\rm s} = 1.432$ that anchors the system's cosmography constraints. We measure an Einstein radius $\theta_{\rm E} = 12.816''^{+0.005}_{-0.007}$ and a logarithmic slope $\gamma = 1.412^{+0.002}_{-0.002}$, tightening the statistical uncertainties on both by roughly a factor of $2-2.5$ relative to the corresponding flat emission-line map model. We find that the source galaxy exhibits a strong rotation, lies confidently along the star-forming main sequence, and has an inferred central black hole mass of $\log M_{\rm BH}/M_{\odot} = 7.89 \pm 0.51$. These results establish datacube-level forward modeling as a practical and independent route to traditional image-based lens models in the current systematics-limited regime, and one that extends naturally to this system's other lensed sources and to other lens systems.

14. Tidal shells as clocks of galaxy mergers and tests of cosmological models[2610.38035]
Abstract

Stellar shells-concentric arc-like tidal features formed in galaxy mergers-provide a valuable record of recent galaxy assembly histories encoding both the merger event and the gravitational potential of the host galaxy. We compare shell systems in observations and cosmological simulations from two complementary perspectives. First, we show that simulations predict a substantial population of very large (>100kpc) shells that are rarely observed, revealing a potentially important discrepancy between simulations and reality. Because the scales of observed and simulated shell systems differ significantly, we focus on a comparison of merger histories. 15. Cosmic rays in galaxy halos I: the circumgalactic transport rate and a dynamically significant cosmic-ray reservoir[2610.38061]
Abstract

Fermi-LAT observations imply that most of the cosmic rays (CRs) produced in normal star-forming galaxies escape from the discs where they are accelerated into the circumgalactic medium (CGM), but the subsequent evolution of those escaping CRs is unclear. Here we construct a simple semi-analytic model for their transport and loss, which we constrain by confronting the model with a series of observations: limits on CR energy densities in the outer Milky Way from Fermi-LAT, the isotropic gamma-ray background (IGRB), stacked CGM X-ray emission from eROSITA, and the requirement that the accumulated pressure of CRs in galaxy CGMs not be so large as to render them unstable. We show that these constraints can be jointly satisfied in only two scenarios: one where CRs diffuse quickly through the CGM and freely escape beyond it into the IGM, and another where the CR diffusion time out of halos is of order the Hubble time. In the latter scenario CRs accelerated by star formation naturally explain both the IGRB and the heat input required to maintain the temperature of the CGM against X-ray cooling, and their relatively slow transport means that roughly half of the CR energy injected into the CGM of an $L_\star$ halo over its star-formation history remains there at $z \simeq 0$. This makes CR pressure dynamically significant, and also means that a non-negligible part of the IGRB is produced by CRs in the Milky Way's own halo. We argue that this latter scenario is the more likely one, as it avoids the need to posit a second source for the IGRB that is coincidentally very close in both power and spectral shape to the contribution from star-forming discs.

16. Turbulent gas-rich discs at high redshift: Chaotic orbits and rapid bulge formation at high gas fraction[2610.38159]
Abstract

Using the \nexus\ N-body/hydrodynamic framework, we show that stellar bars that emerge in high-redshift disc galaxies are different in their orbital characteristics compared to their low-redshift counterparts. We identify bar-supporting orbits and, among them, find the fraction of chaotic orbits compared to regular orbits ($f_{\rm chaos}$). This fraction rises above 60\% for models with $f_{\rm gas} > 60 \%$; for $f_{\rm gas} \lesssim 60\%$, we observe chaotic fractions close to zero. The emerging bars seen in the $f_{\rm gas}= 80\%$ and $100\%$ simulations collapse in $\lesssim$1 Gyr to form central bulges supported by rotation, and this collapse follows soon after the chaotic fraction starts to dominate ($f_{\rm chaos} \gtrsim 50\%)$. Moreover, the gravitational potential in those simulations steepens rapidly over that period, and by integrating the chaotic orbits, we find that orbits that deviate from the evolving mean field are more likely to be chaotic. Hence, we argue that the irregularity associated with the rapidly evolving, central potentials drives higher chaotic fractions in these gas-rich bars. As a result, the emergent bars collapse through `violent relaxation' to form oblate rotating bulges. Supporting evidence for this picture comes from the fact that particles of different mass share identical radial profiles, a natural consequence of the rapid relaxation process.

17. Unveiling the Shock-Excited Molecular Environment in the Orion Protostar HOPS 370[2610.38192]
Abstract

We present near-infrared K-band ($1.94$–$2.52~\mu$m) spectroscopy of the deeply embedded protostellar source HOPS 370, obtained with the TIFR–ARIES Near Infrared Spectrometer (TANSPEC) on the 3.6-m Devasthal Optical Telescope (DOT). The spectrum is dominated by prominent molecular hydrogen ($\rm H_2$) ro-vibrational emission, with multiple transitions detected from the $1$–$0$ S, $2$–$1$ S, and $1$–$0$ Q branches. Using the intrinsically linked $\rm H_2$ $1$–$0$ Q(3) and $1$–$0$ S(1) transitions, which arise from the same upper energy level, we derive a visual extinction of $A_V = 23.24 \pm 1.25$ mag, corresponding to $A_K = 2.50 \pm 0.13$ mag, consistent with the embedded Class 0/I nature of the source. The diagnostic line ratio $F(2$–$1~{\rm S}(1))/F(1$–$0~{\rm S}(1)) = 0.12 \pm 0.01$ is consistent with thermal excitation and disfavors ultraviolet fluorescence as the dominant excitation mechanism. Assuming optically thin emission and local thermodynamic equilibrium, we derive an $\rm H_2$ excitation diagram that yields an excitation temperature of $2078 \pm 215$ K and a molecular hydrogen column density of $(1.93 \pm 0.93)\times10^{20}$ cm$^{-2}$. We further derive an ortho-to-para ratio of $2.45 \pm 0.21$, indicating substantial shock-driven thermal processing while remaining below the high-temperature equilibrium value of 3. Taken together, these diagnostics indicate that the molecular hydrogen emission in HOPS 370 originates predominantly in warm, shock-heated gas most likely associated with its protostellar outflow. Our results demonstrate the diagnostic power of combined S- and Q-branch $\rm H_2$ spectroscopy for constraining the physical conditions and excitation history of deeply embedded protostellar systems.

18. Age-Resolved Vertical Motions in the Milky Way Warp: A Localized Enhancement in Intermediate-Age Populations[2610.38235]
Abstract

Determining how the Milky Way warp evolves provides constraints on the formation and dynamical evolution of galactic disks. Using Apache Point Observatory Galactic Evolution Experiment Data Release 19 (APOGEE DR19) spectroscopy, we estimate ages for 222,355 red giants. 19. Numerical Simulations of the interaction between winds from tidal disruption event and supermassive black hole circumnuclear medium[2610.38317]
Abstract

The interaction between winds from tidal disruption event (TDE) and the circumnuclear medium (CNM) around supermassive black holes is believed to contribute to the radio emission of non-jetted TDEs. This model has been studied both analytically and numerically. In previous works, the properties of wind are usually set to be angle-independent. It is not clear to what extent the results deviate from those in the case where TDE wind properties are angle-dependent. We perform numerical simulations to investigate the effects of the angular distribution of wind properties on the radio light curve in the wind-CNM interaction model. We find that given that the wind mass and kinetic energy fixed, the radio light curve in the optically thin waveband significantly depends on the angle-dependence of wind properties. Even when the swept-up CNM mass is still less than the wind mass, a slight deceleration can affect the light curve. Changing the angular dependence of the wind properties causes the deceleration to vary across angles. Consequently, the final light curve–the time evolution of radiation integrated over all angles–would differ significantly from that predicted in models with a spatially uniform wind injection. This can result in a slower rise or a faster decline (depending on the CNM profile) than the analytical prediction, and can even turn the rise phase into a long-lasting plateau. Therefore, it is essential to carefully take into account the angular dependence of wind properties to study the wind-CNM interaction.

20. PyMsOfa 2.0: A Pure-Python Implementation of the IAU Standards of Fundamental Astronomy Service[2610.38410]
Abstract

Astrometric calculations require authoritative algorithms so that every result is correct and reproducible. The Standards of Fundamental Astronomy (SOFA) service of the International Astronomical Union (IAU) provides such algorithms, officially released only in Fortran 77 and ANSI C. In an earlier paper we presented the first version of PyMsOfa, a Python package wrapping the SOFA C library. Here we present PyMsOfa 2.0, which re-implements the complete SOFA library in pure Python with NumPy. All 247 official SOFA routines are ported directly to Python, requiring no compilation of any external C library and running identically on Linux, Windows, and macOS. The routines operate on NumPy arrays, so arguments can be processed in batch, which is convenient for survey pipelines and Monte Carlo simulations. The package also provides an extension module with validated models not yet part of the official SOFA release, such as the improved precession–nutation model IAU 2006$_{J_2}$/2000A$_{R26}$. The reimplementation reproduces the official SOFA algorithms: all constants, series, and coefficients are taken unchanged from the SOFA source files, and routine names differ only by the pym prefix. We verify every routine against the official SOFA C release: most reproduce the C results to machine precision, the remainder within the accuracy tolerance stated in the SOFA documentation. The package has been applied to Gaia astrometric reduction and used in astrometric studies of the Closeby Habitable Exoplanet Survey (CHES) mission, and is available through PyPI, GitHub, and Zenodo.

21. Migration Traps as Variability Attractors. II. Nonmonotonic X-Ray Timing Signatures of Embedded Stellar-Mass Black Holes in AGN Disks[2610.38432]
Abstract

Stellar-mass black holes (sBHs) embedded in active galactic nucleus (AGN) disks may affect X-ray variability through magnetic reconnection. As sBHs migrate and accumulate near migration traps, they disturb the disk magnetic field and trigger reconnection events that heat the disk and eject plasma into the overlying corona. The injected plasma dissipates kinetic energy and can trigger the release of magnetic energy already stored in the corona. We calculate the resulting coronal X-ray emission and the disk ultraviolet (UV) variability, including the disk thermal response and reprocessing of incident X-rays. We find that embedded sBHs can initially enhance short-timescale X-ray variability, but the variability becomes suppressed as more sBHs accumulate in the disk. At higher mass accretion rates through AGN disks, this suppression appears earlier. As sBH-associated reconnection events become more frequent, their active regions increasingly overlap, reducing the magnetic energy available to each event from the corona. We also find that variations in the disk ultraviolet (UV) emission follow the X-ray variations with a shorter delay than in the model without sBHs. These results suggest that a growing population of embedded sBHs does not necessarily produce stronger short-timescale X-ray variability.

22. Nongravitational Motion of 3I/ATLAS Far from the Sun: Robust Detection, Weak Constraints on the Activity Law[2610.38485]
Abstract

3I/ATLAS provides an unprecedented astrometric data set for studying nongravitational (NG) effects in an interstellar object, but existing orbital solutions depend strongly on assumptions regarding the heliocentric activity law and the treatment of the astrometric data. We investigate the extent to which astrometric observations constrain the heliocentric dependence of the NG activity function, focusing on the identifiability of different functional forms of $g(r)$ within standard NG orbit-determination frameworks. Astrometric observations obtained at heliocentric distances larger than 3.49 au before perihelion and 2.67 au after perihelion were analysed. Purely gravitational and nongravitational orbital solutions were determined using several activity laws, including the standard water-ice sublimation law and flatter more volatile ice prescriptions. The resulting residual distributions were evaluated using normality tests and analyses of temporal trends in the observed-minus-calculated residuals. The distant pre-perihelion observations are well described by a purely gravitational (GR) orbit, whereas the distant post-perihelion data exhibit a statistically significant non-Gaussian residual distribution under the GR model. However, the astrometric data do not uniquely distinguish between the tested activity laws: standard water-ice, more volatile and extreme volatile prescriptions produce statistically comparable orbital fits despite their markedly different heliocentric dependences. The existence of nongravitational acceleration is therefore considerably better constrained by astrometry than its physical origin or heliocentric dependence.

23. The Influence of Hubble Expansion on the Future Merger of the Milky Way and Andromeda[2610.38490]
Abstract

Most existing idealized simulations of galaxy mergers neglect Hubble expansion, as the local Hubble flow is curved by overdensities and the expansion effect is weak at these scales. However, even in the gravity-dominated Local Group (LG), the expansion itself still persists, and it has been shown important in some LG-related studies. Bulge globular clusters are key tracers of the complex formation history of the Galactic bulge, as they retain information on its past star-formation episodes. Their chemical abundances serve as empirical `fossil records” of the early evolution of the bulge. We present a homogeneous analysis of archival high-resolution UVES spectra for 135 giants in 14 Galactic bulge globular clusters. We explore the results in the context of the phenomenon of multiple stellar populations and discuss what the abundances reveal about the chemical evolution of the bulge. We employed the CHEmical Survey analysis System (CHESS) pipeline to derive atmospheric parameters on an accurate scale defined by reference stars. Abundances for Li, O, Na, Mg, Al, Si, Ca, Sc, Y, Zr, Ba, Ce, Nd, and Eu were derived by spectrum synthesis. Deviations from local thermodynamic equilibrium (i.e., non-LTE or NLTE) were taken into account for Na, Mg, Al, Si, Ca, Y, Ba, and Eu. We serendipitously discovered a Li-rich giant with A(Li) = 1.68 (after 3D NLTE corrections) in NGC 6723, which was hidden in the archival data. Our sample includes both first- and second-generation stars, as indicated by the observed spread in Na abundances. Compared to halo clusters, the bulge clusters show a lower [{\alpha}/Fe] plateau at low metallicity and a more gradual decline in [{\alpha}/Fe] toward higher metallicities. The Sc abundances are below solar and lower than in disk stars of the same metallicity. This difference could be important for solving open questions about the nucleosynthesis of this element. None of the clusters exhibit any significant spreads in heavy elements. Most clusters exhibit [Ba/Eu] \sim 0.0, indicating a contribution from both s- and r-processes. Clusters with [Fe/H] < -0.5 show negative [Ce/Nd] ratios, supporting the idea that these elements were predominantly produced by the r-process in these clusters.

66. On the Proper 3D Modeling of Orbits in Wide Binary Gravity Tests[2610.40388]
Abstract

A gravitationally-bound system of wide binary stars with precise 3D relative displacement $\mathbf{r}$ and velocity $\mathbf{v}$ can allow a Keplerian orbit reconstruction for an assumed gravitational parameter $G$. A sufficiently large sample of such systems can also be used to directly infer $G$ specific to the sample. When the sky-plane angular coordinates are (essentially) exact and the distances to the binary stars are sufficiently precise, the orbit modeling can be readily done using two internal consistency relations provided by the ratio of angular separations in R.A.\ and decl.\ and the physical sky-plane separation $s$ that must be satisfied by any Keplerian orbits. A recent study argued that treating $a$ explicitly free with an empirical prior imposed on it can dramatically change the inference of $G$ in the Chae et al.\ sample of 36 wide binaries. Here I introduce and test a Bayesian MCMC algorithm that allows $a$ to be explicitly free, and find using the $\alpha$-Cen AB - proxima system and the Chae et al.\ sample that the explicit-$a$ algorithm with an observational constraint is fully equivalent to the implicit-$a$ algorithm with an internal consistency relation involving $s$. For the $\alpha$-Cen AB - proxima system, I present orbits in Milgromian (MOND) as well as Newtonian gravity models obtained with the verified algorithms. I show that a recent claim of Newtonian gravity from the Chae et al.\ sample may be a consequence of using arbitrarily enlarged errors for some of $\mathbf{r}$ and/or $\mathbf{v}$. I discuss how wide binaries can be properly analyzed and modeled to probe gravity at low acceleration and test gravity models, including the necessity of deriving individual-specific parameters, the importance of follow-up observations for anomalous systems, and the relevance of Bayesian MCMC approaches.

67. UNIONS: Red and blue alignments of DESI DR1 galaxies[2610.40411]
Abstract

Intrinsic alignments (IA) of galaxies are a dominant source of systematics in weak-lensing cosmological analyses, and direct measurements on spectroscopic samples are the primary source of priors on their amplitude. We measure the IA of galaxies from the first data release (DR1) of the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI) as a function of colour, stellar mass, and halo mass, by cross-correlating the DESI spectroscopic catalogues with the weak-lensing shape catalogue of the Ultraviolet Near-Infrared Optical Northern Survey (UNIONS) over up to 2400 deg$^2$. Abstract

Whether a [C II] experiment at $z>4$ measures the clustering of large-scale structure, which carries rich cosmological information, rather than the shot noise of the emitters depends on the amplitude and the shape of the [C II] power spectrum. Forecasts often build the power spectrum from a halo mass–luminosity relation, $L(M_h)$, chained from scaling relations measured in other regimes or read off simulations, without a quantified uncertainty. Abstract

Accreting black holes follow a Fundamental Plane (FP) linking radio and X-ray luminosities to black hole mass, but whether this relation changes in the ultramassive regime, above $\sim10^{10}\,\Msun$, remains unresolved. Brightest cluster galaxies (BCGs) provide the most direct access to this regime, although earlier claims of enhanced radio emission relied largely on heterogeneous nuclear measurements and black hole masses inferred from host-galaxy proxies. We recalibrate the forward FP using galaxies with direct dynamical mass measurements and reassess the evidence for a high-mass radio excess using the available BCG data. Abstract

At high redshift, the cosmic microwave background (CMB) both cools relativistic leptons and provides seed photons for inverse-Compton (IC) X-ray emission. These two effects link the particle content of a bubble directly to the soft X-ray depression used to identify a cavity. We derive a physical criterion connecting lepton cooling, bubble volume, enthalpy evolution, and 0.5–2 keV X-ray visibility, while separating intrinsic filling of the X-ray depression from detector-dependent cavity recovery. Abstract

Abstract

The near-universal turnover mass of the present-day globular cluster mass function (GCMF), $M_{\rm TO} \sim 2 \times 10^5\ {\rm M_\odot}$, is a well established observational feature across galaxies of different types and masses, providing an important empirical benchmark for understanding the globular cluster initial mass function (GCIMF). Competing explanations of this property invoke either dynamical evolution from an initial power-law distribution or an imprint of cluster formation physics. We address this problem by reconstructing the high-mass regime of the GCIMF by inverting the mass loss due to dynamical evolution of present-day globular cluster systems across a wide range of host galaxy masses. Our method is based on an environment-dependent mass-loss model calibrated by direct $N$-body simulations in time-dependent tidal fields, enabling a mapping between observed cluster masses and their progenitor values without assuming a priori a functional form for the GCIMF. We apply our method to galaxies spanning halo masses of $\sim10^{9}$ - $10^{12}\ {\rm M_\odot}$, combining systems with individually measured globular cluster masses as well as large statistical samples constructed from observed global properties. The recovered GCIMFs are systematically shifted towards higher masses and exhibit a power-law behavior at the high-mass end. The inferred slopes vary across galaxies and show a strong correlation with host halo mass, with more massive galaxies exhibiting steeper high-mass slopes. Our results suggest that the slope of the GCIMF depends on the galactic properties and provides a direct empirical link between present-day globular cluster systems and their high-redshift progenitors.

84. Synergy between CSST and future gravitational-wave detectors: Probing primordial black holes by cross-correlating dark sirens with galaxies[2606.17617]
Abstract

Gravitational-wave (GW) events and galaxies both trace the cosmic matter distribution, but the mergers of astrophysical black holes and primordial black holes (PBHs) are expected to populate different environments and therefore to cluster with different biases. The GW clustering bias is thus a statistical observable that can separate the two populations. We assess how well this can be done by cross-correlating the photometric galaxy survey of the Chinese Space-station Survey Telescope (CSST) with mock GW catalogs from two future detector networks: the third-generation ET2CE network (the Einstein Telescope and two Cosmic Explorer detectors) and the multi-band BDET2CE network, which adds the space-based baseline Decihertz Interferometer Gravitational-Wave Observatory. These results show that combining future GW detector networks with CSST galaxy clustering offers a promising and largely independent route to identifying PBHs statistically.

Abstract

88. Evolution of Binaries Under Stochastic Perturbations[2607.12011]
Abstract

We develop a general Fokker-Planck framework describing the dynamical evolution of Keplerian binaries subjected to stochastic perturbations. The formalism provides an algorithmic way to obtain the Fokker-Planck drift and diffusion coefficients of any set of orbital variables given the statistics of the perturbations. We apply the method to three physically distinct regimes: adiabatic tidal perturbations, white-noise tidal perturbations, and impulsive encounters with a third body of arbitrary density profile. In each regime we provide explicit drift and diffusion coefficients for all six orbital elements, derive the associated evolution timescale, and obtain analytic steady-state distribution functions. Our results extend previous treatments by including the evolution of the binary's orientation, retaining the complete tensor structure of tidal correlators, treating non-pointlike perturbers, and resolving the exact geometry of impulsive encounters. We also show how these equations can be applied directly in several astrophysical scenarios, including binaries perturbed by dark matter subhaloes, ultralight dark matter, and the interstellar medium. This work delivers both a complete mathematical framework and a practical toolkit for stochastic binary evolution, providing ready-to-evaluate equations to be applied directly to binary population data.
