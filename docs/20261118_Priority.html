1. Mass-conservative voxelisation of smoothed particles using cumulative kernels[2611.21686]
Abstract

Smoothed particle hydrodynamics (SPH) simulations are frequently post-processed on Cartesian grids for applications such as radiative transfer calculations, visualisation, and data analysis. While numerous voxelisation algorithms have been proposed, relatively little attention has been paid to the fundamental properties that such algorithms should satisfy. We argue that SPH voxelisation algorithms should accurately reproduce the underlying density field, conserve mass globally, remain consistent under grid refinement, and be computationally efficient. We present a new finite-volume algorithm designed to satisfy these requirements simultaneously. Starting from a finite-volume formulation of the SPH density field, we derive a simple expression for the mass contained in a Cartesian voxel in terms of a cumulative kernel function. The cumulative kernel is tabulated numerically once for any compact-support smoothing kernel, independently of its analytical form, so that the same tabulation and voxelisation machinery applies unchanged to any such kernel. During voxelisation, the mass contributions are obtained from interpolated evaluations of this precomputed function at voxel corners, with values at corners shared by neighbouring voxels evaluated only once and reused. We compare the new cumulative-kernel algorithm to nearest-grid-point assignment, centre sampling, regular subsampling, and Monte Carlo integration, using direct high-order numerical integration as a reference for the voxel densities, and implement it in the 3D Monte Carlo radiative transfer code SKIRT. The cumulative-kernel algorithm reproduces the reference voxel densities substantially more accurately than the sampling-based methods considered. 2. A PhEW Good Simulations: I. Galaxy Properties[2611.21716]
Abstract

Although galactic outflows driven by stellar feedback are widely implemented in modern cosmological simulations, the interactions between them and the circumgalactic medium (CGM) remains poorly resolved. To overcome this limitation, we conduct a suite of cosmological simulations that use the PhEW (Physically Evolved Winds) subgrid model, which analytically tracks the exchange of mass, metals, momentum, and energy by winds with the ambient CGM using an algorithm based on high-resolution cloud-crushing simulations. Our results show that even without AGN feedback, our simulations are able to accurately reproduce the observed galactic stellar mass functions (GSMFs) and stellar mass-halo mass (SMHM) relations at $z\gtrsim 1$, after which the massive galaxies in our simulations start to overproduce stars. Our simulations also match the observed relations of $M_{\mathrm{HI}}-M_*$, $M_{\mathrm{H_2}}-M_*$, $Z_*-M_*$ and $Z_{\mathrm{ISM}}-M_*$. Taken together these results suggest that AGN feedback is much less important for galaxy formation and evolution than previously believed. However, like found in many previous simulations, the specific star formation rates (sSFRs) of our galaxies are significantly lower than those observed at $z\gtrsim1$ at all stellar masses. Significantly, we find that our results are robust against changes in the resolution and volume of our simulations, without the need to retune our wind parameters.

3. ASAS-SN Rates V: Tidal Disruption Event Volumetric Rates and Luminosity Functions by Optical Spectroscopic Type, X-ray Luminosity, and Host-Galaxy Properties[2611.21731]
Abstract

We present volumetric rates and luminosity functions (LFs) of tidal disruption events (TDEs) based on 21 TDEs from the All-Sky Automated Survey for Supernovae (ASAS-SN) between 2014 and 2024. We calculate completeness corrections from injection-recovery simulations of the ASAS-SN light curves using individual TDE templates. We find a total volumetric rate of $\log_{10}(R_{\rm tot}[{\rm yr}^{-1}{\rm Gpc}^{-3}h_{70}^3]) = 2.32^{+0.15}_{-0.23}$ for $z \leq 0.12$. We also measure the rates of two growing TDE subpopulations. The one off-nuclear TDE in our standard sample implies $\log_{10}(R_{\rm off\text{-}nuclear}) = 0.39^{+0.52}_{-0.76}$ ($1.2^{+3.4}_{-1.0}$% of the total rate). We measure the rate of recurrent TDEs (rTDEs) to be $\log_{10}(R_{\rm recurrent}) = 1.26^{+0.23}_{-0.62}$ ($9^{+8}_{-6}$% of the total rate) from the four rTDEs in our standard sample, increasing to $1.45^{+0.20}_{-0.35}$ ($13^{+10}_{-7}$%) when the well-studied ASASSN-14ko is included. Galaxies with substantial (>0.1%) burst-mass fractions and steeply falling star-formation histories host the majority of TDEs. We recover the known overrepresentation of TDEs in post-starburst galaxies, by a factor of $15^{+16}_{-11}$ relative to matched control galaxies, and a weaker enhancement of $5^{+5}_{-4}$ in quiescent Balmer-strong galaxies. TDE-H+He events dominate the volumetric TDE rate, with TDE-H, TDE-He, and TDE-Featureless events comprising <15% of nearby TDEs. The $g$-band LFs are statistically indistinguishable when split at the median X-ray to UV/optical blackbody luminosity ratio of $\sim$1%, corroborating unification models where the observed multi-wavelength emission is set primarily by viewing angle effects. Extrapolating the measured TDE LFs to higher luminosities and more massive black holes supports a TDE origin for luminous ambiguous nuclear transients and extreme nuclear transients.

4. The meaning of Milky Way mass estimates in the presence of the LMC[2611.21732]
Abstract

The LMC's recent arrival perturbs the observed kinematics of the Milky Way's (MW) stellar halo, complicating equilibrium-based MW mass estimates at Galactocentric distances $r\gtrsim 30$ kpc. Using N-body MW+LMC simulations, we show that the MW stellar halo can be used to disentangle the total MW+LMC, current LMC, and pre-infall MW mass profiles, by exploiting the LMC's orbital path and the LMC-induced motion of the MW's center of mass ("reflex motion"). Tracers in regions crossed by the LMC longest ago (Galactic coordinates $\ell >0^\circ, b>0^\circ$) are approximately relaxed and trace the total MW+LMC mass. Since the reflex motion is primarily latitudinal, a radial-velocity-only estimator ($M_{v_r}$) recovers the total MW+LMC mass with $\lesssim 5\%$ errors across $30<r/{\rm kpc}<200$, without any reflex-motion correction. Subtracting mass estimates in the region of the LMC's future orbit ($\ell < 0^\circ, b >0^\circ$) from those in its recent-orbit region ($\ell > 0^\circ, b <0^\circ$), which maximizes the LMC-induced velocity dipole, recovers the LMC's current mass profile with $10-20\%$ errors, but this requires tracers at $r\gtrsim 100-120$ kpc. Subtracting this from the total MW+LMC mass recovers the MW's pre-LMC infall mass at $r\gtrsim 80$ kpc with $5-10\%$ errors. Applying $M_{v_r}$ to DESI-MWS DR1 BHBs at $\ell >0^\circ, b>0^\circ$ and $50<r/{\rm kpc}<60$, we obtain a total MW+LMC mass $M(r<60\,{\rm kpc}) \approx 7.5^{+3.0}_{-2.2}\times 10^{11}\,M_\odot$. Observationally, recovering the LMC and pre-infall MW mass profiles requires 6D samples at $r\gtrsim100$ kpc, and a virial mass to within $\sim10-20\%$ requires $r\gtrsim120$ kpc, both achievable with Gaia DR5 astrometry for halo giants targeted by DESI Run 2 and 4MOST. Rather than treating the LMC-induced disequilibrium as an obstacle, our framework exploits it to weigh both galaxies.

5. Shocks and Trails in the Circumgalactic Medium Driven by Runaway Supermassive Black Holes[2611.21738]
Abstract

Runaway supermassive black holes (SMBHs) have long been predicted. RBH-1, discovered with HST and confirmed by JWST, provides the strongest evidence yet for a runaway SMBH: a bow shock and linear trail in the circumgalactic medium (CGM) of a $z=0.96$ galaxy. The bow shock is $\sim$kpc wide, the trail is $\sim 60$ kpc long, and the SMBH apparently moves at $\sim 1,000$ km s$^{-1}$. Using controlled hydrodynamic simulations, we show that the SMBH's gravity alone cannot produce a significant bow shock, but a comoving wind source can sustain one similar to RBH-1's. The wind must either be active or have shut off less than $\sim 3$ Myr ago. The required recent wind momentum flux is $6\times10^{32}\,{\rm dyn}\,(n_{\rm CGM}/10^{-3}\,{\rm cm}^{-3})$, where $n_{\rm CGM}$ is the CGM density. Assuming the wind reaches peak strength only during the last $\sim 6$ Myr, the minimum integrated wind mass is $3.5\times10^5\,M_\odot\,(n_{\rm CGM}/10^{-3}\,{\rm cm}^{-3})$. A $2\times10^7\,M_\odot$ SMBH accreting at $1\%$ Eddington could power the wind. Supernova feedback from a star cluster carried by the SMBH is an alternative, although the required mass and momentum fluxes $\gtrsim 60$ Myr after ejection are difficult to reconcile with a standard initial mass function. For a hot ambient CGM at $\sim 10^6$ K, the quasi-linear trail constrains the 1D velocity dispersion to $\sigma_{\rm 1D}\lesssim 30$ km s$^{-1}$, above which it becomes strongly disturbed. Runaway SMBH trails therefore offer a new probe of turbulence in the diffuse CGM, in regimes where its kinematics are otherwise inaccessible.

6. XQR30: ALMA Observations Reveal the Diverse Structure of Cold Gas within z>6 Quasar Host Galaxies[2611.21755]
Abstract

We present 0.25( 1.5kpc) ALMA observations of the [CII] and dust continuum emission from the host galaxies of 24 luminous z 6 quasars. Together with archival data from 17 quasars, this constitutes a nearly complete sample of all quasars with J-band magnitudes of J_AB<20.0 and redshifts between 5.8 and 6.6, which are the limits defined in the VLT/XSHOOTER legacy survey, XQR-30. For the full sample of 41 quasars, we find that 39(95%) of the quasar host galaxies are detected in [CII] emission with [CII] luminosities ranging between (0.3 and 9.2)x10^9Lsun, and 39(95%) of the quasar host galaxies are detected in continuum emission with continuum flux densities ranging between 0.22 and 16.3mJy. We detect eight [CII]-emitting companion galaxies, which is consistent with the expected number of galaxies based on clustering results. We find that z 6 quasar host galaxies have a range of morphologies, and by defining a new parameter, the kinematic position angle alignment parameter, Delta_PA_{kin-CII}, we show that most quasar host galaxies deviate from a standard disk model. This suggests that [CII] emission probes structures beyond simple disk rotation, such as bars, tidally disturbed gas, etc. We show that the dust emission is more compact than the [CII] emission independent of the morphology of the galaxy. Finally, we provide rough dynamical mass estimates within twice the [CII] half-light radius for the majority of the galaxies, and show that the average dynamical mass within the inner few kpc is consistent with estimates of the average mass in stars, gas and the SMBH.

7. The Impact of Seeding Threshold on Wandering Black Hole Populations and Merger Rates[2611.21756]
Abstract

Cosmological simulations following massive black holes (MBHs) with dynamical friction subgrid models predict hundreds to thousands of off-centre, wandering MBHs in massive galaxies. We investigate how the wandering MBH population changes with halo mass seeding threshold. We present cosmological zoom-in simulations of a $(15\,\mathrm{cMpc})^3$ volume to $z=2$, using the halo mass seeding thresholds of ASTRID, EAGLE and Illustris-TNG $(5\times10^{9}-5\times10^{10}\,\mathrm{M}_{\odot}/h)$, with a fixed MBH seed mass of $m_\mathrm{seed}=2\times10^{6}\,\mathrm{M}_{\odot}/h$. The subgrid dynamical friction model for MBHs switches to accurate integration around MBHs using the \textsc{ketju} module above $m_{\bullet} \geq 2\times10^{7}\,\mathrm{M}_{\odot}/h$. We find that the seeding threshold greatly affects the MBH population: the number of seeded MBHs varies by a factor of ten and the number of wandering MBHs in the most massive galaxy ($M_\star=4.4\times10^{11}\,\mathrm{M}_\odot$) varies from 2 to 115. An MBH can become a wanderer in two ways. Interactions with other galaxies can offset a central MBH in low-mass galaxies ($M_\star\sim10^{8}\,\mathrm{M}_\odot$) if seeded in low mass halos $(\lesssim10^{10}\,\mathrm{M}_\odot/h)$. Alternatively, wandering MBHs can be added through mergers. The growth of the most massive MBHs is insensitive to the seeding threshold. While the total number of MBH mergers varies by an order of magnitude, the number of massive dynamically resolved MBH mergers differs by most a factor of $\sim3$ between seeding thresholds. The dynamically resolved binaries coalesce typically on timescales of $\sim10-250\,\mathrm{Myr}$ after becoming bound.

8. The phase gradient spectrum: probing the squeezed bispectrum of the 21-cm signal during reionization[2611.21760]
Abstract

The 21-cm signal from the epoch of reionization is highly non-Gaussian. Standard analyses rely on the power spectrum, which is blind to the Fourier phases, while higher-order statistics such as the bispectrum are computationally expensive and spread information across triangle configurations. As an efficient alternative, we develop the \textit{phase gradient spectrum}, which measures the circular coherence of phase differences between Fourier modes separated by a long-wavelength mode. We show analytically that this statistic measures the angle-averaged normalised squeezed bispectrum, which describes the response of small-scale power to long-wavelength fluctuations. Applied to reionization simulations with different ionizing source properties, the phase gradient spectrum of the 21-cm signal shows a characteristic non-monotonic evolution. At early times, the spectrum tracks the non-Gaussianity of the matter density field. Once the mean neutral fraction $\langle x_\mathrm{HI}\rangle \lesssim 0.9$, the spectrum follows the ionization topology. The phase coherence reaches a minimum midway through reionization and rises again as the remaining neutral regions shrink into isolated islands. Statistics that keep the sign of the squeezed bispectrum show that this minimum marks its sign change. This evolution holds for different reionization models and simulation frameworks. A Fisher forecast for a 1000-h Square Kilometre Array (SKA)-Low observation, combining five redshifts across reionization, shows that the phase gradient spectrum alone constrains the astrophysical parameters, and these constraints tighten by a factor of $\gtrsim 2$ for a 10,000-h observation. Combining the phase gradient spectrum with the power spectrum tightens the marginalised constraints by up to $\sim$20 per cent relative to the power spectrum alone.

9. Diverse Stellar Components Resolved by JWST in the Massive Galaxy SPT0311-58[2611.21761]
Abstract

SPT0311$-$58 is comprised of two clumpy dusty star-forming galaxies inhabiting a $z=6.9$ protocluster core. Within a $16$ kpc diameter, there are 26 spectroscopically confirmed members. The 12 highly dust-obscured clumps were identified by their rest-frame [CII] emission, and the 14 less obscured clumps in the rest-frame ultraviolet (UV). We present new NIRCam and MIRI imaging from JWST of the central regions, which respectively trace the rest-frame UV and near-infrared. Combining this imaging with supplementary data from the Hubble Space Telescope and Atacama Large Millimeter Array (ALMA) provides the most complete picture of SPT0311$-$58 to date. Using 14 photometric bands and ALMA dust continuum to model the spectral energy distribution of SPT0311$-$58, we present well-informed stellar masses and star-formation rates (SFR) on local ($\sim1.5$ kpc) and global scales. We find that SPT0311$-$58 has a total stellar mass of $M_\star=3.2^{+3.2}_{-1.6}\times10^{11}\, M_\odot$ and SFR of $1.5^{+0.89}_{-0.53}\times10^3\, M_\odot\, \text{yr}^{-1}$. We compare these measurements to simulations and known, rare classes of high-redshift objects observed by JWST, namely massive $z\approx6$–$8.5$ Lyman-break galaxies, $z>6$ quasars, and other merging/clumpy $z\sim6$–$7$ systems. SPT0311$-$58 is comparable to $z>6$ quasars in both stellar mass and SFR, but is more extreme than the other reference populations for both quantities. Additionally, we compare our measurements with the star-formation histories (SFH) of five massive quiescent galaxies from the Early eXtragalactic Continuum and Emission Line Science (EXCELS) survey. We find that SPT0311$-$58 has a higher SFR than expected from the SFHs of the EXCELS galaxies at $z=6.9$, but is otherwise a plausible progenitor to massive quiescent galaxies.

10. A Science Traceability Matrix for Micro-arcsecond Resolution X-ray Imaging[2611.21770]
Abstract

Our ability to understand the highest energy processes and environments in astronomy is directly tied to our ability to probe their physical processes at the smallest scales. Despite transformative progress in high-angular resolution instruments across the radio through optical, angular resolution within the X-rays has plateaued around $\sim0.1-1''$, and therefore our window into the innermost regions of the high energy Universe lies primarily within spectral, timing, and/or polarimetric studies rather than direct imaging. This represents a significant barrier to gaining a robust understanding of key science questions, including the mechanisms of accretion and jet launching, the accretion activity and angular momentum losses of binary active galactic nuclei (AGN) leading to gravitational wave events, and the space weather events that may influence atmospheric evolution and prebiotic chemistry on potentially habitable exoplanets. The Accretion Explorer Interferometer (AEI) is a mission concept for X-ray imaging that could achieve up to $\mu$as resolution, $\sim5-6$ orders of magnitude better than current instrumentation. Here we present the scientific discovery space enabled by such an advance, and highlight areas of uncertainty and open questions that are critical to investigate for future development of this concept.

11. Neutron-Rich Disks, Nickel-Rich Ejecta: Can Direct-Horizon Collapsars Explain Early $r$-Process Enrichment?[2611.21788]
Abstract

Very massive stars above the pair-instability mass gap may collapse directly into black holes surrounded by massive disks. These "direct-horizon collapsars" accrete at $\dot{M} \gtrsim 10\,M_\odot\,{\rm s}^{-1}$ and have been proposed to eject a substantial amount of $r$-process material, powering "super-kilonovae". We present the first three-dimensional general-relativistic magnetohydrodynamic simulations of direct-horizon collapsars with M1 neutrino transport, following a $\approx 150\,M_\odot$ helium core from collapse through the escape of magnetically driven outflows. Extreme accretion rates neutronize the disks to $Y_{\rm e} \approx 0.1$-$0.25$ and, at early times, allow them to satisfy the gravitational-instability and cooling criteria for fragmentation. However, the strong poloidal fields needed to eject the neutron-rich gas promptly also drain the disks and lift their electron degeneracy. The neutron-rich ejecta composition is set in a brief phase, such that the ejecta carry $\sim 0.1$-$0.3\,M_\odot$ of nuclei with $A \geq 69$, including $\lesssim 2\times10^{-4}\,M_\odot$ of lanthanides, alongside $\sim 1$-$4\,M_\odot$ of $^{56}$Ni. Assuming proportional retention and mixing of Eu and Fe, these yields imply $[{\rm Eu/Fe}]\approx-0.4$ to $+0.3$, and simple dilution estimates permit enrichment at $[{\rm Fe/H}]\lesssim-2.6$. Direct-horizon collapsars are therefore potential prompt contributors to iron and $r$-process enrichment at low metallicity. Their predicted Eu/Fe ratios fall below those of strongly $r$-process-enhanced stars, but uncertainties in the relative yields, particularly of iron, may close this gap. The ${}^{56}{\rm Ni}$-rich ejecta would power luminous supernovae with rise times of weeks, potentially accompanied by exceptionally powerful gamma-ray bursts, rather than lanthanide-rich super-kilonovae.

12. A 15-year K-band view of long-period variables in the central region of M33[2611.21793]
Abstract

Long-period variables (LPVs) on the asymptotic giant branch (AGB) trace the late stages of stellar evolution and the return of mass to the interstellar medium. Their periods range from about 100 to 2000 d, so a few-year near-infrared campaign covers too few cycles for the slowest pulsators. We combine four independent UKIRT $K$-band data sets obtained between 2003 and 2018 into a homogeneous catalogue of the central $\approx$12 arcmin$^2$ of M33: 847 stars with 21085 measurements over 15.11 yr. Using 82 calibration stars, we place all data on the 2MASS/Vega system and remove inter-dataset offsets of up to 0.22 mag. Of the 847 stars, 771 are variable ($\sigma_K>0.10$ mag), and 385 have peak-to-peak amplitudes $A_K\geq1.0$ mag, which makes them Mira candidates. Of the 156 stars with Spitzer/IRAC [3.6] photometry, 120 have $\langle K\rangle-[3.6]>0.5$ mag and are candidate dust-enshrouded AGB stars. The catalogue is the basis for a forthcoming period-luminosity and star-formation history analysis

13. D/H and 12C/13C ratios of benzonitrile and indene in TMC-1 support bottom-up formation of polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbon-like molecules[2611.21834]
Abstract

Polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs) have recently been detected in cold dark clouds through their rotational spectra, but it is currently a matter of debate whether they form through a bottom-up process from small hydrocarbons or through a top-down scenario involving the fragmentation of large PAHs inherited from a previous evolutionary stage of diffuse cloud. Isotopic ratios can shed light on the origin of PAHs in cold dark clouds. Large aromatic molecules observed in the infrared are enriched in deuterium and may also be enriched in 13C because they likely formed in the ejecta of evolved stars. We synthesized indene deuterated at the aliphatic position in the laboratory and measured its rotational spectrum, together with those of the nine 13C singly substituted isotopomers of indene. We used the measured spectra of the D and 13C isotopic species of indene, together with previously measured spectra of D and 13C isotopic benzonitrile, to search for these species in the cold dark cloud TMC-1 using QUIJOTE data obtained with the Yebes 40m telescope. We used a line stack method to tentatively detect deuterated benzonitrile and detect 13C-substituted benzonitrile, but find no evidence of either the D or 13C isotopic species of indene. We derive an aromatic D/H ratio of 0.53 % from benzonitrile, an aliphatic D/H ratio <3.3 % from indene, and 12C/13C ratios of 126 and >55 for benzonitrile and indene, respectively. These values are consistent with isotopic ratios derived for other molecules in TMC-1 and show no enrichment in either D or 13C that could be ascribed to inheritance from large PAHs in a top-down scenario; a bottom-up scenario is therefore the most likely.

14. Super-Eddington Accretion onto Compact Objects: Theory Meets Observation[2611.21838]
Abstract

We review candidate super-Eddington populations together with semi-analytic models and radiation magnetohydrodynamic simulations, with the following conclusions: $\bullet$ Likely super-Eddington sources over a range of black hole mass show similar properties, including outflow on a range of scales, hard X-ray weakness, decreased emission from jets, and suppressed optical/UV variability. $\bullet$ Theoretical treatment of super-Eddington accretion predicts similar behavior across a range of methods and initial conditions, although interesting differences remain, particularly in the importance of magnetic fields. Numerical simulations produce a puffed-up inner disk with a funnel that grows narrower at higher mass accretion rate. Radiative efficiency declines with accretion rate in thermal-pressure–supported disks. $\bullet$ Ubiquitous outflow is likely an important ingredient in galaxy feedback at high Eddington ratios, as wind mechanical power increases with accretion rate and can rival jet power. Wind kinetic power depends far less on uncertain black hole spin and magnetic fields. $\bullet$ Observational classes of objects can be sorted in a two-dimensional parameter space of Eddington ratio and orientation. Observations down the funnel observe rapid X-ray variability, while observations through the wind will see suppressed X-ray emission. At the highest Eddington ratios, we envision a regime where the photosphere extends to $r > 10^6 \, r_g$, and the observer sees only thermalized emission covering most of the sky, speculatively corresponding to super-soft sources and Little Red Dots.

15. A search for optical bursts from the repeating fast radio burst in the M81 globular cluster, FRB 20200120E[2611.21861]
Abstract

We present a search for optical bursts from the repeating fast radio burst FRB 20200120E, which is located in a globular cluster of M81 and is the closest known extragalactic FRB. We observed FRB 20200120E simultaneously with the high-speed, quintuple-beam optical camera HiPERCAM on the 10.4-m Gran Telescopio Canarias and the Effelsberg 100-m Radio Telescope. We detected one radio burst of fluence $\mathcal{F}_{\rm R} = 0.14\pm0.01$ Jy ms during our observations, but found no evidence for corresponding optical bursts in the 11.98 ms frames. We find 5$\sigma$ upper limits on the optical burst fluence of $\mathcal{F}_{\rm O} < 1.8\times10^{-3}$ Jy ms (combined $u_s g_s r_s i_s z_s$-bands) and the optical burst energy of $E_{\rm O} < 5.4\times10^{36}$ erg ($r_s$-band), corrected for interstellar extinction. We compare our upper limit on the optical-to-radio fluence ratio $\mathcal{F}_{\rm O}/\mathcal{F}_{\rm R} < 0.013$ to various emission models involving inverse-Compton scattering in the magnetosphere or nebula of a highly-magnetised neutron star.

16. Rapid Protoplanetary Disk Evolution and Debris Disk Formation in Taurus-Auriga[2611.21884]
Abstract

We present a Submillimeter Array (SMA) 1.3mm (225GHz) continuum emission survey of Young Stellar Objects (YSOs) in the Taurus-Auriga star-forming region, comprising 69 class III and 5 class I/II YSOs with spectral types B9-M3, as part of a total sample of 149 class II and 82 class III YSOs. This represents a complete sample (100%) of class II and III YSOs with spectral types earlier than K3, and a near-complete sample (85%) for spectral types earlier than M2. The survey has a median $3\sigma$ sensitivity of 1.4mJy. We did not find any emission from circumstellar dust around class III YSOs, but present three detections that can be attributed to variable stellar activity, and one from an unresolved companion. We compile and model spectral energy distributions (SEDs) for the sample, and report 27 class III YSOs with excess thermal emission. We demonstrate that the class III YSOs in Taurus are typically older than their class II counterparts. We produce millimeter luminosity and dust disk mass distributions, and present evidence for a bifurcation between the class II and III populations, implying that ${\gtrsim}99.5\%$ of the dust mass is dispersed or transformed as YSOs transition from the class II to III stage. The combined infrared and millimeter properties demonstrate that warm (inner) and cold (outer) dust reservoirs evolve on comparable timescales: there is a rapid evolution across the radial extent of disks. We demonstrate that the survey properties are broadly consistent with a belt population that formed planetesimals from class II disks with an efficiency of a few tenths to a few tens of percent.

17. Euclid DR1 Legacy Science on Ultracool Dwarfs: Halpha activity, kinematic age and rotation of M7–L1 dwarfs from DESI DR1, SPHEREx and Euclid[2611.21896]
Abstract

The fraction of late-M dwarfs with detectable Halpha rises toward the stellar-substellar boundary while their emission weakens relative to the bolometric luminosity, a contrast attributed to the fading continuum. We measure how much of this rise the continuum explains and whether younger populations or longer activity lifetimes contribute. We select 8939 dwarfs of type M7 or later, all but 22 of them M7-L1, from DESI DR1. We compare active fractions at common thresholds in equivalent width and in log(L_Ha/L_bol), the Halpha-to-bolometric luminosity ratio, in spectra sensitive to each threshold. We jointly model continuum fading, emission weakening and an extra active component. Kinematics use Gaia astrometry and DESI radial velocities for dwarfs with G < 19, free of proper-motion selection. From M7 to M9 the active fraction rises by 28 points at 5 Angstrom equivalent width. At log(L_Ha/L_bol) = -4.5 it falls by 23.5 points. SPHEREx and photometric bolometric fluxes agree within 0.03 dex. Joint fits require M8 emission to be 0.12-0.15 dex weaker than at M7. The brighter M8 dwarfs require an extra active component; the larger sample at most marginally. At log(L_Ha/L_bol) = -4.7, inactive M7 and M8 dwarfs have larger velocity dispersions than active ones. The measured rotators, mostly M6-M7 dwarfs, have saturated Halpha. Euclid DR1 NISP spectra of 152 M7-L1 dwarfs (144 at S/N >= 5) test the optical type scale, and VIS images resolve three close binaries (0.22-0.53 arcsec) with common proper motion. We confirm that the rise in equivalent-width incidence comes mostly from the fading continuum acting on weakening emission. Conditional on an age-velocity relation and uniform ages, a step model gives thin-disk activity lifetimes at log(L_Ha/L_bol) = -4.7 of 5.9 (5.3-6.6) and 6.4 (5.2-8.0) Gyr at M7 and M8. Both bins contain mostly stars, so these lifetimes describe the lowest-mass stars.

18. A Catalog of Young Stellar Objects in the Lagoon Nebula[2611.21962]
Abstract

M8, the Lagoon Nebula, has been known for more than 100 years as a region forming stars. It is (in projection) very close to if not in the Galactic Bulge. As a result, the source surface density is very high and source confusion is a substantial challenge. Because of the location, there have been many surveys that serendipitously observed M8, in addition to studies specifically targeting it. But to this point, there has been no effort to resolve source confusion across all the existing catalogs, spanning many different wavelengths, sensitivities, and spatial resolutions. We have done all of this source reconciliation, and present the merged catalog here. The net result is 3019 YSOs with largely photometric data and/or YSO selection from  50 different origins, primarily ranging from $g$-band through 25 um, but with a significant number of X-ray sources, and some data of limited sensitivity out through 70-450 um. The catalog includes several flags that enable users to select, e.g., the YSOs that have been identified as young by the most studies, or the YSOs with the most multi-wavelength counterparts, or the ones with the clearest IR excesses. We use this catalog in a companion paper; it will also be of use in, e.g., future SPHEREx and Roman work.

19. VLA Observations of the Ionized Jet IRAS 16562-3959[2611.22004]
Abstract

We present new high-resolution, high-sensitivity Karl G. Jansky Very Large Array (VLA) A-configuration observations of the high-mass protostellar jet system IRAS 16562-3959 at 3.1 and 6.1 GHz. Our images resolve the five previously known jet components (OE, IE, C, IW, OW) into 21 distinct knots and sub-components, and reveal a new off-axis source, MW. Based on the dynamical timescales from previous works ($\tau_{jet} \approx 620$ yr), the spacing and structure of the knots suggest episodic accretion with a mean interval between ejections of $\sim$32 yr. We find that source MW has a substantial increase in peak brightness relative to its marginal detections in previous observations. MW exhibits a spectral index of $\alpha \approx -0.7$, characteristic of synchrotron emission from shock-accelerated relativistic electrons.
