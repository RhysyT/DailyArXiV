1. A combined PRIMER+MINERVA study of the evolution of $\mathbf{[O\mbox{III}]+Hβ}$ equivalent width from $\mathbf{z=3}$ to $\mathbf{z=8.5}$[2611.08563]
Abstract

We present an analysis of the evolution of $[O\,III]+\mathrm{H}\beta$ equivalent width ($W_{\lambda}$) over the redshift range $z=3$ to $z=8.5$, based on combining the JWST NIRCam PRIMER and MINERVA imaging in the UDS survey field. We find that the combined medium- and broad-band data set reduces the photometric redshift outlier rate to $\eta = 4.7\%$ at $m_{\rm F444W}\leq28$, a $42\%$ reduction compared with PRIMER imaging alone. We also demonstrate that the combined data set facilitates the accurate recovery of $[O\,III]+\mathrm{H}\beta$ equivalent widths in redshift bins centred on $z\sim3.2,\, 4.0,\, 5.0,\, 7.2,\, 8.3$; corresponding to the F210M, F250M, F300M, F410M and F460M filters. Based on a final sample of $N=1964$ galaxies with $M_{\rm UV}\leq-19.25$, we first provide new constraints on the bright end of the $z=7-8$ $[O\,III]+\mathrm{H}\beta$ luminosity function ($\log_{10}\phi^{*} = -4.3_{-0.5}^{+0.2}$, $\log_{10}L^{*} = -43.4_{-0.2}^{+0.3}$), before proceeding to investigate the redshift evolution of $[O\,III]+\mathrm{H}\beta$ equivalent width. Using SED modelling based on non-parametric continuity star-formation histories, we find that the median $[O\,III]+\mathrm{H}\beta$ equivalent width monotonically increases with redshift, rising from $\mathrm{log}_{10}(W_{\lambda})=2.54^{+0.01}_{-0.01}$ at $z\simeq 3$ to $\mathrm{log}_{10}(W_{\lambda})=3.05^{+0.03}_{-0.03}$ at $z\simeq 8$. In tandem, the distribution of $\mathrm{log}_{10}(W_{\lambda})$ is observed to become increasingly asymmetric with redshift, producing a fraction of extreme emission-line galaxies (EELGs, $W_{\lambda}>1000\,$Å) that reaches $f_{\rm EELG}=0.50\pm{0.10}$ by $z=8.3$. Our SED modelling results indicate that the physical driver of this evolution is increasingly bursty star-formation, with EELGs the result of particularly high specific star-formation rates over the 10 Myr prior to observation.

2. Constraining Dust Attenuation Curves in Star-Forming Galaxies at Cosmic Noon Using Multiple Emission Line Ratios[2611.08594]
Abstract

Dust attenuation remains a dominant systematic uncertainty in measurements of an emission line, and therefore galaxies properties derived from the emission lines such as star formation rates. Corrections based only on the Balmer decrement require an assumed attenuation-curve slope, even though that slope varies among galaxies. We use JWST/NIRSpec spectroscopy from JADES to constrain effective dust attenuation curves for 386 star-forming galaxies at $0.5\leq z\leq7$. For each galaxy, we combine H$\alpha$, H$\beta$, and one additional hydrogen recombination line and solve for the normalization $\tau_V$ and slope $n$ of a power-law attenuation curve, allowing the slope to vary from galaxy to galaxy. We found slopes and normalization span a broad range even for galaxies at similar redshifts. Additionally, our method using Paschen transitions provided substantially greater constraints the attenuation-curve slope than Balmer-only combinations. When comparing to SMC-like or Calzetti-like corrections we find our method produces more consistent star formation rates with less scatter. These improved dust corrections provide an avenue to better describe star formation rates in the epoch of peak star formation and the early universe.

3. FORGE'd In The Early Universe II: Constraining The Pop III Initial Mass Function[2611.08598]
Abstract

We present a cosmological radiation-magnetohydrodynamics (RMHD) zoom-in simulation of $56$ Population III stars using FORGE'd in FIRE. Our simulation combines a cosmological initial condition centered on an isolated ultra-faint galaxy ($M_{\mathrm{halo}} \sim 10^9 M_\odot$ at $z=0$) with individual-star formation and feedback physics, reaching a maximum mass resolution of $\sim 10^{-2} M_\odot$ and spatial resolution of $\sim 10^{-3} \mathrm{pc}$. We follow continued star formation for just over $500$ Myr, from $z \sim 16$ to $z \sim 7$, substantially longer than what has been achieved before, and track star formation across five distinct minihalos. From our stellar population, we self-consistently resolve an emergent stellar initial mass function (IMF) and find $\alpha = 1.9$ for $dN/dM \propto M^{-\alpha}$ at $M_{\star} \gtrsim 2.2 M_{\odot}$. Additionally, we form both single and multiple stellar systems, including binaries and higher-order multiples, as well as stars with masses below $1 M_{\odot}$. At early times, the stellar mass distribution is more top-heavy, with a high-mass slope of $\alpha\sim1.4-1.5$, similar to previous Pop. III simulations, and steepens as star formation continues. This suggests that differences between our final IMF and previous studies may partly reflect the much longer evolution followed here, approximately five orders of magnitude longer than in several previous Pop. III sink-particle simulations. The first star-forming minihalo, which hosts the most massive stars in our simulation, has a shallower IMF slope of $\alpha\approx 1$, while later-forming minihalos produce lower maximum stellar masses in lower-density parent clouds.

4. Magnetically Arrested Accretion and Black Hole Spin in a Semi-Analytic Model of Galaxy Formation[2611.08599]
Abstract

We present {\sc Mad Sage}, a semi-analytic model of galaxy formation that couples black hole mass and spin evolution to gas accretion and AGN feedback under magnetically arrested disk conditions. Building on the {\sc Dark Sage} model, we incorporate GRMHD-calibrated prescriptions for spin evolution and jet power, spin-dependent radiative efficiencies, and remnant spins from BH-BH mergers. We compare accretion and feedback models, including coherent disk-fed accretion with mixed coherent and incoherent hot-halo accretion, with and without additional mechanical feedback. We find that incoherent accretion, together with Blandford-Znajek spin-down, drives massive BHs toward low spins, reducing jet efficiencies and weakening the quenching of massive galaxies. Reproducing the high-mass end of the stellar mass function requires either exclusively coherent accretion or an additional feedback component provided by ADAF outflows. Our models broadly reproduce observed BH-galaxy scaling relations, disk surface density profiles, and the AGN luminosity function at $z\sim 2-6$, although the spin models under-predict the number density of faint AGN at low redshift. At $z\lesssim 3$, predicted BH spins decrease with increasing BH mass, while at higher redshifts, the mass dependence weakens. Spin distributions peak at $|a_\star|\sim 0.5$-$0.7$, below current X-ray reflection spectroscopy measurements, and become bimodal for massive, quiescent BHs accessible with the next generation Event Horizon Telescope and the Black Hole EXplorer. We report that the predicted LISA event rates are nearly independent of the adopted accretion and spin models.

5. FORGE'd in Fragments: Pop III star formation in a Milky Way-like progenitor halo[2611.08603]
Abstract

We present a radiation-magnetohydrodynamic (RMHD) cosmological zoom-in simulation of Pop III star formation, drawing initial conditions from a Milky Way-like progenitor halo at redshift $z \sim 21$. We follow the collapse of primordial gas over 10 orders of magnitude, through to the formation of an accretion disk that becomes gravitationally unstable $\Delta t \sim -3$ kyr before the first protostar's formation. Prominent spiral arms then develop and fragment vigorously, producing $\sim 60$ sink formation events over the first $8$ kyr post-collapse evolution, with a total stellar mass reaching $> 150\,{\rm M_\odot}$ by $15$ kyr, the end of our simulation. The disk remains magnetically supercritical throughout: the mass-to-flux ratio falls from $\mu_\Phi \sim 10^2$-$10^3$ to $\sim 15$ by $15$ kyr, as the inflow brings in magnetic flux. The magnetic energy in the disk remains sub-dominant throughout, but grows by $\sim 3.5$ orders of magnitude after the first star forms. The disk starts sub-virial and reaches virial equilibrium within $\sim 2$ kyr. The total stellar mass of the population grows as $M_* \propto t^2$, confirming the prediction of accelerating accretion in self-gravitating turbulent collapse, at rates of $\dot{M} \sim 10^{-3}$-$10^{-2}\,{\rm M}_\odot {\rm yr}^{-1}$. The star formation efficiency reaches $\approx 90\%$ within the disk (after $\approx 11$ kyr; $\approx 80\%$ at $9.5$ kyr) and $\sim 50\%$ within a $0.1$ pc sphere. We find a top heavy IMF ($dN/dm \propto m^{-\alpha}$) with a high-mass slope $\alpha \approx 1$. The cosmological environment sets the boundary conditions of the disk: an envelope delivers $\sim 3\times 10^{-3}\,{\rm M}_\odot {\rm yr}^{-1}$ to the outer edge of a disk with a radius of $\sim 10^{3}$ AU; the disk spreads and, in our simulation, its fragmentation sites move outward, from $\sim 500$ to $\sim 1350$ AU, until it has been depleted.

6. Jeans Dynamical Constraints on the IMF in Massive Quiescent Galaxies at $z\sim0.7$ from JWST/NIRSpec[2611.08612]
Abstract

We present dynamical masses for nine massive quiescent galaxies at $z\sim0.7$ from the JWST-IMFERNO program, measured from ultra-deep ($\sim32$hr), spatially resolved NIRSpec/MSA spectroscopy combined with NIRCam imaging. The most massive of these galaxies have previously been found to have a bottom-heavy initial mass function (IMF), based on measurements of dwarf-sensitive absorption features. Here, we examine whether the stellar masses inferred for these IMFs are consistent with the dynamical masses of these galaxies. We derive the dynamical masses by including Jeans Anisotropic Modelling within a forward-modelling framework that accounts for instrumental optics, source morphology, and geometric effects. We compare the resulting dynamical masses to the stellar masses for different IMF assumptions. A Milky Way IMF produces physically plausible dynamical-to-stellar mass ratios but implies central dark matter fractions ($f_{\rm DM,1R_e}=0.54\pm0.06$) that are a factor $\sim2$ higher compared to local ETGs and simulations. When uniformly applying the IMF that was measured from absorption features, the mean dark matter fraction becomes low ($f_{\rm DM,1R_e}=0.02\pm0.18$), and the stellar masses exceed the dynamical mass at $2\sigma$ significance for two out of seven galaxies in the sample. When we adopt the measured IMF only in the central $R_e$ and use a MW IMF in the outskirts, we find realistic dark matter fractions ($f_{\rm DM,1R_e}=0.28\pm0.12$) and consistent dynamical masses. These results support a scenario where the cores of the most massive quiescent galaxies at $z\sim0.7$ had a bottom-heavy IMF, similar to those observed in the centres of local early-type galaxies. Our results are consistent with an inside-out growth scenario, in which size and mass growth of massive galaxies proceeds after quenching through minor mergers, leaving the bottom-heavy cores mostly intact.

7. Digging into the Interior of Hot Cores with ALMA (DIHCA). VIII. Feeding a high-mass binary system in the making[2611.08641]
Abstract

We analyze the gas kinematics in the proto-binary NGC 6334I(N) ALMAe9-ALMAe14 system with the Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array (ALMA) at 1.33 mm with an angular resolution of 66 mas ( 90 au). With a separation of 740 au between the system members, these observations allow a detailed study of the gas motion around this likely binary system hosting at least one growing high-mass star. Some CH$_3$OH lines tracing cold gas ($E_u/k_B$ 100 K) reveal the presence of ordered circumbinary gas motion around the binary system within a  1000 au radius, and rotation around the individual sources with rotation axes pointing roughly in the same direction. We estimate a central source mass, uncorrected for inclination, of 2 M$_\odot$ for the secondary, while the primary has an estimated mass of 5 M$_\odot$ as reported in previous works. The CH$_3$OH transitions with higher $E_u/k_B$ ($\gtrsim400$ K) values reveal circumstellar gas rotation in ALMAe9 consistent with the cold gas tracers. The vibrationally excited SO emission also traces rotation around ALMAe9 and potentially an accretion shock from a flow feeding the circumstellar disk of ALMAe14. We identify two potential inflows feeding the circumstellar disks from the circumbinary gas at rates of  $10^{-6}-10^{-4}$ M$_\odot$ yr$^{-1}$ from CH$_3$OH emission. An additional third inflow seems to be feeding the primary from gas at larger scales.

8. TRGB Distances to the Andromeda Dwarf Satellites from the INT Monitoring Survey[2611.08648]
Abstract

We measure tip of the red giant branch (TRGB) distances to 17 dwarf satellites of M31 from Sloan $i$-band photometry of the Isaac Newton Telescope (INT) monitoring survey of Local Group dwarf galaxies, and add the distances of And I, And VII and And IX from earlier papers of the survey. We locate the tip with a Sobel edge-detection filter applied to the luminosity function of stars within two half-light radii of each galaxy. The distance moduli range from $23.38 \pm 0.06$ mag (And XVI, 474 kpc) to $25.35 \pm 0.06$ mag (And XVIII, 1177 kpc), and 15 of the 20 satellites lie between 650 and 850 kpc. For most galaxies, our distance agrees with at least one published value. Compared with Conn et al. (2012), our tip is brighter for 12 of the 18 galaxies in common, by up to 0.39 mag, possibly because of crowding in the central regions. And XII lies at $844^{+40}_{-38}$ kpc, about 60 kpc behind M31, consistent with a first infall into the Local Group. And XXII lies at $753^{+28}_{-27}$ kpc, closer than all earlier estimates, and about 59 kpc from M33 in three dimensions (for an M33 distance of 794 kpc), consistent with an association with M33. And XVIII lies about 400 kpc behind M31, too far for a secure association with M31.

9. Expanding the Volatile Abundance Inventory for High-Priority K-Dwarf JWST Exoplanet Systems[2611.08676]
Abstract

There are exceedingly few chemical abundance measurements available in the literature for K dwarf stars, especially for those with T$_{eff} <$ 4700 K due to an incompatibility of traditional analyses with their molecular-line-ridden optical spectra. To address the need for K dwarf abundance constraints, we present an elemental ([C/H], [O/H]) and isotopic ($^{12}$C/$^{13}$C, $^{16}$O/$^{18}$O) abundance analysis for six K dwarf exoplanet host stars: K2-141, NGTS-10, WASP-43, WASP-69, WASP-80, and HATS-72. Each system hosts an exoplanet targeted by JWST characterization efforts, making host star abundances especially critical as baseline references. Using MARCS model atmospheres, the TurboSpectrum spectral synthesis code, and near-infrared IGRINS spectra, we derive novel [C/H] and [O/H] abundance ratios for these high-priority K dwarf host stars. We further investigate prospects for determining $^{12}$C/$^{13}$C and $^{16}$O/$^{18}$O ratios using $^{13}$C$^{16}$O and $^{12}$C$^{18}$O lines from the 2.3 micron CO bandhead. The observed spectra are too noisy to measure $^{16}$O/$^{18}$O ratios for the sample, but we do find sub-solar $^{12}$C/$^{13}$C ratios for five out of six targets. We also derive uniform gyrochronological ages for each host star to compare with galactic chemical evolution models and identify underlying abundance-age trends. NIR H+K band spectroscopy provides an alternative to optical, atomic line-derived carbon and oxygen abundances for cool dwarf stars. The growing catalog of precise host star elemental and isotopic abundances complements ongoing observations of their companions and provides a baseline for understanding their chemical composition, origin, and evolution.

10. KCWI Observations of Type 2 Quasar-driven Ionized Outflows at z 0.1[2611.08828]
Abstract

AGN-driven outflows play a crucial role in regulating star formation in galaxies. These outflows are known to be multi-phase, and the characterization of the kinematics, extent, and energetics of each gas phase is needed to obtain a full picture of the outflow properties and impact. This study presents Keck Cosmic Web Imager (KCWI) integral field spectroscopy of the ionized gas outflow properties in seven low redshift (z<0.1) quasars—six Type 2 AGN and one narrow-line Type 1 AGN—providing insight into the warm ionized outflows in these luminous AGN. With the large field-of-view and high sensitivity of KCWI, these data reveal both larger ionized outflow extents than were previously known for these sources and uncover the existence of additional high-velocity kinematic components in the ionized outflows of these luminous AGN. We find (a) a high incidence (50-70%) of multiple outflows on scales of 4–10 kpc and (b) trends between the outflow extent, kinematics, and the ionized mass and energy outflow rates. These trends imply that the faster and often smaller outflows carry higher mass and energy outflow rates than the slower, larger outflows. We also find that the ionized gas outflows reach as far as the known extent of narrow-line ionized gas emission in AGN, which is known to depend weakly on AGN luminosity. This work highlights the utility of the current generation of wide-field integral field spectrographs on 8–10m class telescopes for studying quasar-driven outflows.

11. Unlocking Pandora's Secrets: Probing the Thermodynamics of Abell 2744[2611.08847]
Abstract

Abell 2744 (Pandora's Cluster) is a massive galaxy cluster undergoing multiple mergers, characterized by a highly disturbed X-ray morphology, elevated intracluster medium (ICM) temperatures, and several radio relics associated with merger driven shocks. We present NuSTAR observations of Abell 2744 aimed at mapping its thermodynamic structure and constraining the presence of inverse Compton (IC) scattering from relativistic electrons. We measure the temperature distribution across the cluster and identify a prominent shock front with a Mach number of $\mathcal{M} = 1.58^{+0.42}_{-0.30}$, aligning with the southeastern boundary of the radio halo. The global NuSTAR spectra are modeled with single- and two-temperature thermal components, as well as thermal plus power-law models to test for IC emission. The spectra are well described by purely thermal models with an average temperature of $kT \approx 11$ keV, and we find no significant evidence for non-thermal hard X-ray emission. We derive a 90\% confidence upper limit on the $20-80$ keV IC flux of $ F_{\rm nt} < 2.31 \times 10^{-12} erg~s^{-1}~cm^{-2}$. Combining this with 1.5 GHz diffuse radio measurements from the VLA, we estimate a lower limit on the average magnetic field strength of $B > 0.2 \ \rm{\mu G}$.

12. Predicting Continuum Emission in Massive Star-Forming Regions with Embedded HII Regions - Dust, Free-Free, and Anomalous Microwave Emission with POLARIS[2611.08891]
Abstract

Context: HII regions are a key stage of massive star formation, where ionizing radiation reshapes the interstellar medium. Their continuum spectra trace thermal dust, free-free emission, and anomalous microwave emission (AME). Aims: We investigate how these components evolve as an HII region expands and correlate with gas and electron column densities across spatial scales. Methods: We post-process RAMSES radiation-magnetohydrodynamical simulations of a $10^4 M_\odot$ cloud with POLARIS. The model includes grain charging, rotational excitation and damping, radiative torques, rotational disruption, and emission from dust, ionized gas, and nano-composite grains. We compute maps and SEDs for six snapshots over $1-10^4$ GHz and analyze multi-scale correlations. Results: Ionized emission evolves from a compact, optically thick HII region to an extended, optically thin structure. Around 15 GHz, free-free emission changes from a $\nu^2$ to a $\nu^{-0.1}$ spectral regime as optical depth decreases, and increasingly traces electron column density. The thermal dust peak shifts from mid- to far-infrared as colder material contributes, while dust emission remains strongly correlated with total gas column density. AME peaks at 26-37 GHz and shows scale-dependent correlations with gas and electron distributions. Rotational disruption is the main dust-destruction mechanism affecting mid-infrared emission and the high-frequency AME tail. Conclusions: These continuum components provide complementary, scale-dependent diagnostics of stellar feedback. Self-consistent multi-frequency modeling is essential for interpreting surveys of star-forming clouds.

13. Tidal Braking Does Not Dominate Open-Cluster Slow Rotators[2611.08925]
Abstract

Extended main-sequence turn-offs (eMSTOs) observed in open clusters younger than $2~\mathrm{Gyr}$ are commonly attributed to a spread in stellar rotation rates. Tidal synchronization in close binary systems has been proposed as a possible mechanism for producing slowly rotating stars. To test this hypothesis, we measured the radial-velocity (RV) dispersions of slow-rotator samples associated with the eMSTOs of eight open clusters using ESO VLT/GIRAFFE and SALT/RSS spectroscopy. We extended the analysis to 40 open clusters using Gaia DR3 RV measurements. The observed cluster RV dispersions are generally substantially smaller than those predicted for binary populations dominated by close systems with orbital periods for which tidal synchronization is expected to be effective. In particular, all 40 clusters in the Gaia sample have RV dispersions below the prediction of the $P=10$ d binary-population model. These results suggest that close, tidally synchronized binaries are unlikely to be the dominant origin of slow-rotator populations in young and intermediate-age open clusters, although a contribution from undetected or wider binary systems cannot be excluded.

14. Identifying Chemically Coherent Stellar Families in the Galactic High-α Disk with Low-Resolution Spectroscopy[2611.08983]
Abstract

Identification of chemical substructures in the Galactic high-{\alpha} disk provides valuable insight into its formation history. In this work, we demonstrate the capability of low-resolution spectroscopy for this purpose by applying DBSCAN clustering to 87,275 red giants in the high-{\alpha} disk using 18 elemental abundances derived from LAMOST low-resolution spectra (R 1800). We assess the effectiveness of this approach by comparing the clustering results from the low-resolution data with those obtained by applying the same procedure to high-resolution survey abundances and to mock catalogs. These comparisons suggest that, despite the relatively large uncertainties in individual elemental abundances derived from low-resolution spectra, the astrophysical coherence among different elements provides sufficient information to identify chemically coherent families that may share similar enrichment histories without necessarily sharing a common birth environment. After scanning the parameter space of the DBSCAN procedure and merging the results from different parameter combinations, we identify 133 candidate chemical families containing 4,642 unique stars (5.3% of the high-{\alpha} disk sample). These families exhibit diverse multi-element abundance patterns, with particularly prominent differences in neutron-capture elements at nearly identical [Fe/H] values. These families therefore offer new constraints on the diversity of chemical enrichment pathways and on the assembly history of the high-{\alpha} disk.

15. Dynamical formation of Gaia neutron star binaries in star clusters as a phase-dependent process[2611.09039]
Abstract

Gaia has discovered dormant compact-object binaries in which the compact object shows no observable interaction with its luminous companion. These systems, hereafter Gaia BHs and Gaia NSs for black hole (BH) and neutron star (NS) companions, provide a new probe of compact-object binary formation. These detached systems are difficult to produce through standard isolated binary evolution alone, and their formation pathways remain unclear. Previous $N$-body studies have shown that Gaia BH-like objects are formed efficiently in star clusters, while Gaia NS-like objects are not. This has been attributed to the BH subsystem dominating the cluster core. We performed direct $N$-body simulations of $10^4 \mathrm{M}_\odot$ star clusters in a Galactic tidal field and investigated how NS-main-sequence (NS-MS) binaries form during the late BH-depleted phase. We found that NS-MS binaries satisfying Gaia-sensitive criteria form only after BH depletion. NSs are present in the core throughout the evolution, but the strong encounters there are monopolized by the BH subsystem until it is depleted. Our results show that the dynamical formation of Gaia-sensitive NS-MS systems is not prevented in star clusters, but delayed relative to Gaia BH-like systems.

16. Phosphorus-rich star formation in giant molecular clouds hosting primordial globular clusters[2611.09044]
Abstract

Recent observational studies have identified phosphorus-rich (P-rich) stars ([P/Fe]>1.0) in both Galactic globular clusters (GCs) including three confirmed cases and the field population. Using analytical models of GC formation, we investigate a scenario in which P-rich stars form from pristine gas in GC-forming giant molecular clouds (GMCs) polluted by oxygen-neon (ONe) novae within and surrounding the GMCs. Within this framework, we model the chemical abundance patterns of P-rich stars using diverse chemical yield sets for ONe novae while varying the dilution factor (Fdil), defined as the ratio of pristine gas mass to total ONe nova ejecta mass. The principal results of our models are as follows: Across a wide range of Fdil, most models display positive correlations between [P/Fe], [N/Fe], [O/Fe], [Al/Fe]$, [Si/Fe], [S/Fe], and [Si/S]. The [Ce/Fe] ratios in P-rich stars can reach values up to 1 if the GMCs are additionally polluted by interacting low-mass asymptotic giant branch stars.P-rich star formation is highly unlikely in environments with a low interaction frequency between GMCs and ONe novae. P-rich stars exhibit anomalously low 14N/15N, 16O/17O, and 16O/18O isotopic ratios, offering a clear observational diagnostic to test this scenario. Assuming that P-rich Galactic field stars originate from GCs, we discuss the origin of their chemical abundance patterns. We suggest that the existence of P-rich stars provides direct evidence that GC-forming GMCs were polluted by pre-existing stellar populations.

17. SEDust: A DUSTY-Based SED-Fitting Pipeline for Dust and Mass-Loss from AGB and LPV Stars[2611.09086]
Abstract

We present SEDust, an automated pipeline for spectral energy distribution (SED) fitting and the estimation of stellar mass-loss and dust-production rates. We apply this pipeline to quantify the mass-loss and dust injection rates in the dwarf galaxy NGC 6822, focusing on long-period variables (LPVs) and pulsating asymptotic giant branch (AGB) stars as major contributors to dust feedback into the interstellar medium. The total stellar mass-loss rate is found to be $(3.70 \pm 1.16) \times 10^{-3}\ M_{\odot} \mathrm{yr}^{-1}$. Two alternative methods (an LPV-based estimate and DESK) give totals that differ from this value by factors of $\sim$2.3 (lower) and $\sim$2.4 (higher), respectively, which we adopt as an estimate of the method-dependent systematic uncertainty. A positive correlation between stellar luminosity, intrinsic reddening caused by circumstellar dust self-extinction, and mass-loss rate is observed. The specific mass-return rate from AGB stars is $(7.40 \pm 2.30) \times 10^{-11}\ \mathrm{yr}^{-1}$, indicating that mass return from AGB stars alone is insufficient to sustain the current star formation rate of NGC 6822. This result implies the need for external gas accretion or additional mass-return mechanisms.

18. Suppression of water-ice UV photodesorption on cosmic dust analogs[2611.09308]
Abstract

Ultraviolet (UV) photodesorption regulates the partitioning of water between interstellar ice mantles and the gas phase, yet laboratory yields are generally measured on planar substrates rather than on surfaces representative of cosmic dust. We investigate how grain morphology affects UV photodesorption from thin H$_2$O ice films of 2-6 monolayers deposited on planar gold and olivine, soot nanoparticles, and olivine microparticles. Samples were irradiated at 12 and 60 K with a microwave-discharge hydrogen lamp, and ice depletion was monitored by X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy and reflection-absorption infrared spectroscopy. On planar gold, the photodesorption yield increases approximately linearly with ice coverage, from $\sim5\times10^{-4}$ to $\sim2\times10^{-3}$ molecule photon$^{-1}$ between 2 and 6 monolayers, consistent with increasing UV absorption in optically thin ice; planar olivine gives comparable yields. In contrast, photodesorption is suppressed on particulate olivine and, more strongly, on soot, where yields are up to a factor of $\sim40$ lower than on gold at 12 K. Both experimental techniques confirm the strong suppression of ice depletion on soot. These results show that UV photodesorption depends not only on ice coverage but also strongly on grain morphology. The pronounced suppression on soot may reflect heterogeneous ice growth and altered energy redistribution within nanoscale ice domains, implying that yields measured on planar substrates may substantially overestimate photodesorption from the thin ice mantles of cosmic dust.

19. The N/O-O/H relation across large-scale environments[2611.09330]
Abstract

The large-scale structure (LSS) of the Universe shapes galaxy evolution through its intricate web-like morphology, yet its specific influence on gas-phase chemical abundances remains uncertain. Particularly, a homogeneous comparison of the N/O$-$O/H relation, a tracer of the mass assembly and baryonic cycle, across distinct LSS environments has been lacking. In this study, we analyse a local SDSS galaxy sample curated within the framework of the CAVITY project – dedicated to studying void galaxies – in which galaxies are classified by their LSS environment into three groups: clusters, voids, and non-cluster/non-void (NCNV). We extract spectroscopic emission line fluxes from the central SDSS spectra and analyse how different galaxy properties affect the scatter in the N/O$-$O/H relation. For that, we subtract the main N/O$-$O/H relation from the data and study the dependence of the resulting residuals ($\xi_\text{N/O}$) on a set of parameters, including stellar mass ($M_\star$), $g-r$ colour, and star formation rate (SFR). We find that $M_\star-$related variables are the best predictors of the scatter in the N/O$-$O/H relation, with little influence of the recent star formation history. Regarding the LSS comparison, no significant differences are found for void and NCNV galaxies. Cluster galaxies, however, present a N/O$-$O/H relation systematically lower (with $\xi_\text{N/O} \sim -0.02$). Consistent with this shift and with $M_\star$ as a good predictor of N/O$-$O/H deviations, we find that environmental dependences are also reflected in fundamental scaling relations: both the MZR and N/O$-M_\star$ relations are different for cluster galaxies. These combined results point to mechanisms in the dense intracluster medium (such as strangulation or re-accretion of metal-enriched gas) as the primary drivers of these chemical offsets for galaxies in cluster environments.

20. Where the red rise stops: JWST/MIRI reveals a near-infrared turnover in the Little Red Dot Abell2744-QSO1[2611.09397]
Abstract

Little Red Dots (LRDs) are compact sources with a blue rest-frame UV, a steep red optical continuum and, often, broad hydrogen lines. They have been interpreted as dense stellar systems, dust-reddened active galactic nuclei, or black holes embedded in dense, dust-poor gas, scenarios that are hard to tell apart in the rest-frame UV and optical. We present JWST/MIRI F770W and F1000W imaging of the triply lensed Abell2744-QSO1 at z=7.04, probing rest-frame 0.96 and 1.24um. QSO1 is unresolved, and its steep optical rise stops near rest-frame 1um. Up to 1.24um, a T 4450K blackbody, or a hotter one reddened by dust, describes the continuum, with no sign of the hot dust seen in most type-1 quasars. Our statistically preferred SED fits produce the red continuum with (3-4)x10^9 Msun of stars, over 100 times the dynamical limit on the host mass, and are therefore disfavoured. An accretion disc in a dense gas cocoon respects this limit but over-predicts the 1.24um flux by 5 sigma. Super-Eddington accretion models with a thermalising envelope reproduce the turnover, but only for a black hole  2.5dex less massive than the dynamical estimate. For a dust-reddened accretion disc (A_V   2 mag), dust at  1200K can re-emit at most  2 per cent of the absorbed luminosity, and most of it would have to emerge at  70-600K, unless the dust covers only a small fraction of the sky. The dynamics favour an accretion-powered source, but our data cannot distinguish dust reddening from processing by dense gas. MIRI imaging at >18um could separate these scenarios.

21. Violent relaxation redux[2611.09531]
Abstract

Collisionless self-gravitating systems, such as merging galaxies, have long seemed an exception to the statistical-mechanical principle that chaotic many-body systems relax to maximum-entropy states: Lynden-Bell's statistical mechanics of their "violent relaxation," constrained by exact conservation of phase-space volume – equivalently, of infinitely many collisionless "Casimir" invariants – has appeared not to be a good description. We propose that violent relaxation does give rise to Lynden-Bell's maximum-entropy equilibria, but that both are underpinned by gravitational turbulence, in which large-scale gravitational fluctuations drive chaotic phase-space mixing of the particles' distribution function. We derive the universal small-scale (sub-Jeans) spectra of this turbulence and show that it leads to phase-space-volume conservation being broken on a timescale only logarithmically longer, in the particle number N, than the dynamical (violent-relaxation) time. This timescale separation lets the system relax "adiabatically" through a sequence of Lynden-Bell equilibria, each corresponding to instantaneous values of the Casimirs. At long times, it approaches an equilibrium close to its "ground state": a distribution whose Casimirs have adjusted so that the system's energy is now its minimum possible energy. Thus, the system's universal tendency is toward a minimum-energy, maximum-entropy state. We corroborate this picture with 1D N-body simulations and hypothesize that it can explain the universal equilibria of galaxies and dark-matter halos.

22. Through Ups and Downs: the Transient Lensing Behaviour of SIDM halos undergoing Core Collapse[2611.09546]
Abstract

Self-interacting dark matter (SIDM) haloes can become extremely dense following gravothermal core collapse, motivating their use as explanations for unusually concentrated gravitational lenses. Using an emulator calibrated on gravothermal fluid calculations, we investigate whether increasing central density necessarily strengthens lensing. We follow initially Navarro-Frenk-White (NFW) haloes through core formation and collapse deep into the short-mean-free-path regime, and compute finite-source magnifications, critical curves, and source-plane lensing cross sections for isolated spherical haloes with elastic, velocity-independent self-interactions, including external shear in selected calculations. Lensing strength evolves non-monotonically: it initially decreases during core formation, rises during early collapse, and generally declines at later stages despite continued growth of the central density. This decline accompanies the emergence of a dense inner core whose mass and radius decrease within a more extended, lower-density core. Time-averaged enhancement relative to NFW haloes depends on the lensing statistic, halo concentration, and interaction cross section. Generalized NFW haloes with central cusps $\rho \propto r^{-\gamma}$ with $\gamma \geq 2$ always have larger lensing efficacies than core-collapsed SIDM haloes of equal mass and are therefore poor proxies, including in terms of the demographics (size and radial thickness) of arcs and Einstein rings. The core collapse stage at which SIDM haloes are optimal lenses coincides with the epoch at which the characteristic core surface density is close to critical, which typically occurs during the early stages of core collapse. These results demonstrate that central density alone is insufficient to characterize SIDM lensing and that its evolving mass distribution must be modelled explicitly.

Abstract

We use the EXOSIMS direct imaging mission simulator to model HWO observations of Jupiter-radius gas giants at Earth-like and Jupiter-like instellations around the 164 stars in the ExEP target list. We find that HWO should be able to achieve S/N $\geq$ 5 broadband visible-light detections of gas giants in this instellation range within 5 minutes of integration. 10 hours of R=1000 near-IR spectroscopy with HWO should reveal water, methane, and ammonia absorption features in the atmospheres of Jupiter-like gas giants. HWO time-series photometry should exceed 1% flux precision in one hour for any Earth-instellation gas giants around ExEP stars, and for Jupiter-like gas giants at $d\leq$ 7 parsecs. Time-series light curves at this cadence and precision could, over tens of hours, reveal rotation-induced variability comparable to Jupiter's. Eclipses of Mars-sized exomoons may be detectable in high-cadence light curves of Jupiter sized planets in the habitable zones of ExEP stars at $d\leq$ 10 parsecs. For any hypothetical Earth-like exomoons with oxygen-rich atmospheres at $d\leq$ 7 parsecs from the Solar System, HWO might be able to detect the spectral signature of molecular oxygen amid the parent planet's photon noise in deep ($\sim$400 hour integration) spectroscopic HWO observations at R=1000. Such moons, if they exist, represent additional habitable worlds that HWO could investigate for biosignatures.

62. Grid Optimization for Wide Field Modulation Collimators[2610.05523]
Abstract

Wide-field localization of transient sources such as gamma-ray bursts (GRBs) places new demands on the grid design of modulation collimators, whose usable field of view (FOV) is normally limited by self-vignetting. A grid slat thick enough to be opaque at the energies of interest also clips off-axis rays, shrinking the effective slit width and ultimately defining the FOV itself. Historical bigrid or rotating-modulation-collimator (RMC) designs have been designed with this trade-off as an unavoidable consequence of geometric optics. We explore whether the cross-sectional shape of the grid slats can be optimized for wide FOV applications. We derive a general transmission-function framework for a bigrid pair in terms of an effective slit width that depends on slat geometry and use it to compare four slat cross sections: the conventional rectangular slat, an idealized diamond profile, a hexagonal profile that approximates photochemical etching, and the circular wire grids of early X-ray astronomy. We show that for the same bigrid pitch and thickness, a diamond profile extends the FOV and eliminates clipping throughout most of the field. We also demonstrate that a hexagonal profile achievable with standard tungsten etching factors captures many of the benefits of the idealized diamond profile. Extending the analysis to the four-phase demodulation, we show that slat shape directly sets the extent of the resulting phase-space blind spots, and that the diamond profile removes them entirely across a target FOV where the rectangular baseline cannot. We further show that finite grid opacity is a largely separable effect. Leakage through the grid leaves the geometric blind-spot boundaries unchanged. These results identify slat cross-sectional shape as a design parameter that can substantially increase throughput and widen the blind-spot-free field of view of a wide-field modulation collimator.

63. Optimal Strategies for Gravitational-Wave Memory Detection with Noise Marginalization in Pulsar Timing Arrays[2610.20772]
Abstract

Current pulsar timing arrays could detect gravitational wave memory, which would provide a unique test of general relativity. In order to optimize our searches for gravitational wave memory in pulsar timing arrays, we integrate the optimal frequentist statistics for GW memory into the \texttt{enterprise} code suite. We additionally test a noise marginalized version of this statistic, and compare it's effectiveness to the simpler maximum posterior method. We perform these tests on simulated pulsar timing array with red and white noise content, as well as the NANOGrav $15$-year data set. We find no significant variations in our detection statistics between the noise-marginalized and the maximum-posterior methods for the simulated datasets, and only small variations in the NANOGrav $15$-year comparisons. We conclude that for realistic PTAs the noise-marginalization method could be useful for assigning a false alarm probability to Bayesian results, though the maximum posterior method is sufficient for identifying potentially detectable signals.
