1. What observations are needed to advance our theoretical understanding of galaxy formation at high redshift?[2611.10102]
Abstract

Although significant progress has been made over recent decades, a comprehensive and predictive framework for galaxy formation remains elusive. Starting from cosmological initial conditions, current models cannot a priori accurately predict key galaxy properties such as their structure, size, star formation histories, chemical abundance patterns, or supermassive black hole mass. This challenge stems from the vast range of spatial scales involved, as numerical simulations must simultaneously account for sub-AU processes nearby stars and black holes in addition to Mpc-scale cosmological gas inflows/outflows and environmental effects. Consequently, simulations must rely on subgrid models and approximations. As a result, theoretical progress in galaxy formation remains fundamentally driven by observational data and the iterative testing of model predictions against empirical results. Here we highlight five key open problems in theoretical galaxy formation that require dedicated observations of the high-redshift Universe, beyond what can currently be achieved through GO programs.

2. First detection of the forbidden rotational spectrum of H$_3^+$: JWST/MIRI observations of the shocked gas in HH 168[2611.10122]
Abstract

The H$_3^+$ molecular ion is the cornerstone of interstellar ion-molecule chemistry. Its vibrational transitions have long been observed, in absorption along many interstellar sight lines and in emission from the upper atmospheres of the giant planets, but its pure rotational spectrum, which consists only of "forbidden" transitions made possible by centrifugal distortion, had not been observed in the laboratory or in any astronomical source. With JWST/MIRI we have detected this spectrum toward the Herbig-Haro object HH 168 in Cepheus A West. Twenty-six lines are detected above 5 $\sigma$ in one or both of two fields, of which the (J,K) = (5,0) -> (4,3) line at 16.325 $\mu$m is the strongest. The H$_3^+$ lines share the velocity and spatial distribution of the hot shocked H$_2$, not of the ionized gas. A rotation diagram yields a rotational temperature of   730 K, but the level populations are not thermal and are best explained by subthermal excitation in gas at > 2000 K. We compare the observations with models of shocks propagating into mildly irradiated molecular gas. Constrained by the H$_2$ rotational lines, a dissociative J-type shock predicts H$_3^+$ emission within a factor  1.5 of that observed, while a nondissociative C-type shock, which fits the H$_2$ lines better, underpredicts it by a factor 5-6, although the excitation of H$_3^+$ by energetic collisions is poorly known. Archival MIRI data show the H$_3^+$ forbidden rotational spectrum in the HH 211 bow shock. The H$_3^+$ lines provide a new probe of the ionization in shocked molecular gas.

3. First detection of suprathermal CH$^+$ rotational emissions from chemical pumping: JWST/MIRI observations of the HH 168 molecular shock[2611.10125]
Abstract

The methylidyne cation forms in warm or shocked interstellar gas through the endothermic reaction C$^+$ + H$_2$ -> CH$^+$ + H. Because C$^+$, which is common in irradiated and shocked gas, reacts with H$_2$ only when the reactants supply > 0.4 eV, CH$^+$ traces energy deposition in molecular gas. JWST/MIRI observations of HH 168 reveal the pure rotational lines R(17)-R(31) of CH$^+$ at 13.7-21.3 micron, from upper levels J = 18-32 (0.57-1.65 eV). Eleven lines exceed 5 sigma in both of the two fields that we observed and match the hot H$_2$ in radial velocity and spatial distribution. Because collisional excitation is negligible, each ion entering the v = 0 ladder emits one photon in every R(J) line below its entry level, so the line intensities provide the first measured rotational distribution of the ground-state products. The photon intensity falls almost linearly with increasing J, as expected for formation at a similar rate into every level up to J   33; formation in J = 32 requires > 2 eV of reactant energy. The line positions, up to 150 km/s redward of ExoMol predictions, provide the first measurement of the CH$^+$ rotational intervals above J = 16. A Boltzmann distribution at $\phi T$   6200 K, where $\phi$ is the fraction of the available energy appearing in rotation, fits slightly better, implying reactant energies equivalent to several thousand kelvin. These lines probe the dynamics of a prototypical interstellar ion-molecule reaction.

4. A DOLPHOT/Euclid Study of the Resolved, Faint Outer Milky Way Globular Cluster AM-1[2611.10127]
Abstract

We present a resolved star study of AM-1, a distant ($D \simeq 120$ kpc), faint ($M_V \sim -5.1$) Milky Way (MW) globular cluster, based on Euclid Quick Data Release 1 (Q1) imaging. To perform photometry of the Euclid VIS and NISP imaging, we develop a Euclid-specific module for the crowded field stellar photometry package DOLPHOT. We construct and present empirical point spread function (PSF) models for VIS and NISP, along with a photometric calibration, entirely from the public Q1 images. Our photometry reaches $5\sigma$ point source depths of $I_E, Y_E, J_E, H_E = 27.1, 25.3, 25.5, 25.3$, $\sim$1-1.5 mag deeper than the public Q1 catalog, as expected for dedicated resolved star PSF photometry. We resolve the cluster to $\sim$1 mag below its main sequence turnoff in all bands. The Euclid photometry agrees with color-magnitude diagram (CMD) modeling of archival HST/WFPC2 imaging and with the 2MASS-anchored VISTA Hemisphere Survey to $\lesssim0.02$ mag in $I_E$, $J_E$, and $H_E$, with a $\sim$0.07 mag offset in $Y_E$. We find that AM-1 hosts an old ($\sim$10.6 Gyr), intermediate-metallicity ([Fe/H] $\sim -1$) population, with a present-day stellar mass of $\sim 2\times10^4\,M_\odot$ and $M/L_V \simeq 2.2$, and has a half-light radius of $\sim$12-16 pc with a round, low-concentration profile. These affirm earlier studies placing AM-1 among the most extended, diffuse outer-halo clusters, which dynamical work identifies as accreted. We describe and publicly release DOLPHOT v2.1, the Euclid module, and its empirical PSFs, which together enable Euclid resolved star studies ranging from faint MW systems such as AM-1 to more luminous galaxies throughout the Local Volume. We anticipate further refinements, including spatially varying PSFs, with Euclid Data Release 1.

5. Classical Cepheids in the 4MOST era I: Methodology for 4MOST variable stars and NLTE abundance gradients in the Milky Way[2611.10132]
Abstract

Classical Cepheids (CCs) are key distance indicators and tracers of young stellar populations in the Milky Way. Their period-luminosity-metallicity (PLZ) relation also makes them calibrators of the Hubble constant $H_0$. Despite their importance, chemical composition, especially non-LTE (NLTE) effects, remains a significant source of systematics in PLZ applications. In preparation for the large CCs sample expected from the 4MIDABLE-HR (4MOST Consortium Survey 4: Milky Way Disc and Bulge High-Resolution) survey, we present a method for deriving Cepheid atmospheric parameters and chemical abundances in NLTE. Using the Payne artificial neural network (ANN) trained on NLTE spectra, we derive atmospheric parameters and abundances for 17 elements: O, Na, Mg, Al, Si, Ca, Ti, Mn, Fe, Co, Ni, Sr, Y, Ba, Eu in NLTE and C and Cr in LTE. We apply the new method to archival spectra of Cepheids and derive radial chemical gradients in the Milky Way. We derive robust NLTE stellar parameters and abundances for Cepheids spanning metallicities from $\sim -0.87$ to $\sim +0.35$ dex. For stars with multi-epoch spectra, we find that NLTE abundances are consistent across the pulsation cycle, whereas LTE values show larger dispersions and biases. The resulting abundance distributions and gradients align well with Galactic disk expectations from chemical evolution models. We confirm that the gradients flatten in the outer disk beyond $R_{\rm GC} \sim 15\,\mathrm{kpc}$ and provide the first NLTE gradients for Co, Ni, Y, and Eu. We show that our NLTE astrophysical analysis yields robust solutions for atmospheric parameters and chemical abundances across spectra at the resolving power of 4MOST-HR. This enables large, homogeneous studies with upcoming surveys such as 4MOST 4MIDABLE-HR and provides a solid basis for tightening constraints on Galactic chemical evolution models and Cepheid distance-scale work.

6. Probing circumstellar interaction with spectroscopy of ZTF hydrogen-rich superluminous supernovae[2611.10140]
Abstract

Hydrogen-rich superluminous supernovae (H-rich SLSNe) are rare, exceptionally luminous transients. Most exhibit narrow Balmer emission and can be further classified as SLSNe-IIn, indicating interaction with dense circumstellar material (CSM). We present and analyse 304 optical spectra of 106 H-rich SLSNe observed by the Zwicky Transient Facility (ZTF). We examine their spectral evolution and Balmer-line properties, and model the H$\alpha$ profiles using Monte Carlo electron-scattering calculations. Of the 106 objects, 105 exhibit narrow Balmer emission consistent with SLSN-IIn classifications, including six with additional Ia-CSM-like characteristics and three with possible flash-ionisation features; the remaining one object exhibits only broad Balmer emission and is classified as a SLSN-II. Post-peak Balmer profiles range from approximately symmetric, exponential profiles to asymmetric profiles with prominent blue excesses or shoulders. Objects with Balmer line blue excess favour higher fitted photon-emitting shell velocities and tend to be fainter than those with persistently symmetric profiles. The H$\alpha$ pseudo-equivalent width (pEW) and FWHM of SLSNe-IIn overlap with those of lower-luminosity SNe IIn, while the pEW evolution is more diverse than in the broad-lined SLSN-II comparison sample. Our SLSNe-IIn sample's median Balmer decrement increases from 2.85 before $+100$ d to 4.40 afterwards, remaining consistently below that of the comparison Ia-CSM sample. We identify no Balmer-line morphology unique to SLSNe-IIn in the available comparison with lower-luminosity SNe IIn, although some SLSNe-IIn exhibit prolonged high-ionisation emission. Our results also support interaction with dense CSM formed through enhanced pre-explosion mass loss. We also highlight a possible hidden Ia-CSM population among luminous H-rich interacting SNe.

7. The impact of MUSE sky subtraction on unresolved stellar population end products: Study cases of NGC 1097 and NGC 1433[2611.10149]
Abstract

One of the main steps in data reduction for ground-based astronomy is sky subtraction. For extended objects, it is necessary to observe dedicated sky frames, usually with airmass values similar to the target's, using different pointing strategies. However, it is not well documented how different observing strategies can affect the science end product. In particular, with the use of the MUSE/VLT, this can yield distinct stellar population products, such as age, metallicity, and \alpha-enhancement. To address this, we considered two case studies seeking to investigate: (i) the sky stability in observations taken consecutively over a period of 2 hours with thin sky transparency and airmass values of 1.25-1.05, and (ii) the impact of different sky conditions on the same target. Among our main results, we find that for galaxies with 0.0014 < z < 0.0077, the sky line [NI] at \lambda 5200 coincides with the Mgb bandpass, which is essential for metallicity measurements, highlighting the need for proper sky subtraction. In addition, for regions with relative fluxes above 10 with respect to the sky in NGC1097, sky frames with variation up to  18 % still retrieve consistent stellar population properties - in this case, this corresponds to flux values of >1500 x 10^-20 erg/s/cm^2 and Johnson-V  19.0. In contrast, when comparing data from different nights for NGC1433, fainter regions suffer greatly from distinct sky conditions, especially in metallicity measurements, which range within +/- 0.2, strongly affecting the interpretation of stellar mass assembly and the origin of the gas (i.e., mergers might bring metal-poor gas, while an in-situ burst of star formation can yield metal-rich stars). This study is the first of its kind and highlights the importance of proper sky subtraction, providing insights regarding data limitations and observational strategies for future observations with MUSE.

8. A panchromatic photometric and spectroscopic modeling of SN1987A from 200 to 800d[2611.10220]
Abstract

Type II supernovae (SNe) at 1-3yr after explosion can document not only the preSN mass loss history of massive stars but also the chemistry of the inner ejecta rich in metals and eventually molecules and dust. With the aim of modeling SN1987A, we present a grid of radiative-transfer models of Type II SNe at Zsun/3, covering an initial mass range between 11 and 21Msun, a fixed ejecta kinetic energy of 1.2e51erg, and  0.08Msun of 56Ni. The influence of metallicity at such late times is found to be relatively weak. In this model set, the [OI]6330A flux at 1-2yr varies non-monotonically with Minit. It first rises with Minit due to the growing O abundance but drops for M>17-19Msun due to the higher ejecta density, with little change to the rest of the optical spectrum. Assuming CO molecules account for 90% of the cooling in the C/O shell leads to a 10-20% reduction in the [OI] flux, equivalent to an O(1Msun) reduction in Minit at M<19Msun. The fractional [OI] flux rises from 200 to  600d but decreases beyond. We obtain a good agreement to SN1987A for all Minit apart from [OI], which appears in the optical as the only discriminant between models. The 13Msun model yields the best match, from UV to IR, but a higher Minit may result with allowance for strong CO and SiO cooling. Past 500d, a growing amount of dust below  2000km/s is essential to account for the  1.5mag optical excess fading relative to predictions from decay power. Adopting 0.1mic silicate grains, we find a dust mass of 1e-4Msun at 600d rising to a few 1e-3Msun at 700-800d. We also found that the 200-800d International Ultraviolet Explorer spectra of SN1987A are all contaminated by a background hot star, which also causes the relative UV brightening past 500d. Such contamination may complicate the interpretation of more distant SNe II at > 2yr once or if they fade to O(1e5Lsun) [abridged].

9. Local Pitch Angles of Galactic Structures in Nearby Galaxies with JWST: I. Spiral Arms and Interarm Substructures in Disk-Dominated Systems[2611.10222]
Abstract

We present a spatially resolved study of spiral arms and feather substructures in seven nearby galaxies observed with JWST. Using eight images per galaxy, from four NIRCam and four MIRI filters, we combine a two-dimensional Fourier transform (2D-FT) with a circular-window correlation method to measure both global and local pitch angles. The 2D-FT identifies the dominant azimuthal mode and the corresponding $p_{\rm max}$, allowing us to reconstruct the spiral pattern and define arm and interarm masks. We then apply cosine filters with different pitch angles and window sizes in the $(\ln (R),\theta)$ plane to map the pitch-angle distribution across multiple spatial scales. NIRCam images provide the most robust measurements of the main spiral pattern, whereas MIRI data reveal dusty interarm features, particularly feathers, with greater contrast at the smallest window size ($D_w=0.25$). Across the sample, the main arms typically have $|\alpha|\simeq15^\circ$–$30^\circ$, while feathers show systematically larger values, often above $50^\circ$. These results demonstrate that pitch angle is scale dependent and that different structural components within the same galaxy can occupy distinct angular regimes. NIRCam and MIRI therefore offer complementary views of spiral structure, with the former tracing the large-scale stellar backbone and the latter exposing finer dust substructures. Together with the companion study of barred galaxies, this work provides a common framework for using local pitch angles to investigate distinct dynamical environments in nearby galaxies.

10. Local Pitch Angles of Galactic Structures in Nearby Galaxies with JWST: II. Dust Lanes and Gas Flows in Bar-Dominated Systems[2611.10227]
Abstract

We present a spatially resolved study of local pitch angles in six barred spiral galaxies observed by PHANGS–JWST, focusing on bar-dominated regions where dust lanes are the main structures. We combine a two-dimensional Fourier transform (2D-FT) with a circular-window correlation method to map pitch angles across multiple wavelengths and spatial scales, extending our previous analysis of spiral arms and interarm substructures in disk-dominated galaxies. The dust lanes show relatively large pitch angles, typically $|\alpha|\simeq35^\circ$–$70^\circ$, well above those measured for spiral arms. In several galaxies, the pitch angle changes systematically along the dust lane, with smaller values in the inner regions and larger values farther along the bar, tracing variations in local curvature. The multi-scale analysis shows that larger windows recover the overall geometry of the dust lanes, whereas smaller windows reveal local irregularities and secondary structures. MIRI images generally provide the clearest view of the dust morphology and allow a more precise characterization of local pitch-angle variations, while NIRCam offers a complementary view of the underlying stellar distribution. Unlike spiral-arm pitch angles, which are commonly associated with density-wave patterns, the values measured here characterize the local orientation and curvature of dust lanes associated with bar-driven gas flows. Together with the results of our companion study of disk-dominated galaxies, these findings support a unified, scale-dependent framework in which local pitch angles serve as observational diagnostics of distinct dynamical regimes in nearby galaxies.

11. Evidence for Ambipolar Diffusion from Ion-Neutral Velocity Drift in HH 212[2611.10374]
Abstract

We present the Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array Band 7 observations of the hyperfine structure of C$^{17}$O (3-2) and the H$^{13}$CO$^{+}$ (4-3) molecular line emissions of the Class 0 protostellar system HH 212 at $\sim$ 44 au spatial resolution. Radiative transfer models interpreted in the framework of non-ideal MHD of the envelope allow us to estimate an envelope mass of $M_{env}\sim0.12\,M_\odot$ and a mass infall rate of $\dot{M}_{env}\sim4.4-5.7\times10^{-6}\,M_\odot\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}$, consistent with values reported in the literature. The models also reveal a systematic radial velocity drift between the neutral tracer (C$^{17}$O (3-2)) and the ionized species (H$^{13}$CO$^{+}$ (4-3)) of $0.1-0.3$ km s$^{-1}$, exceeding the spectral resolution of 0.105 km s$^{-1}$ and consistent with possible ambipolar diffusion in the envelope. The ambipolar diffusion fraction, $f_{AD}\sim 0.1-1$, and the Hall parameter, $\beta_{i,H_2}\gg1$, indicate partial ion-neutral decoupling, with non-ideal effects becoming more significant toward the inner regions. In the envelope, our analysis shows that rotational energy decreases from $\sim 50\,\%$ of the gravitational energy at $R_{cen}$ to $\sim 25\,\%$ at $2R_{cen}$ and $\sim4\,\%$ at 1100 au. In contrast, for $B_0=1$ mG, magnetic energy increases from $\sim 0.4\,\%$ of the gravitational energy at $R_{cen}$ to $\sim2.6\,\%$ at $2R_{cen}$ and $\sim20\,\%$ at 1100 au. The drift velocity expected from radial force balance is consistent with the modeled drift velocity within $\sim300-400$ au of the central source but is lower at larger radii. These results are consistent with a scenario in which ambipolar diffusion leads to partial ion-neutral decoupling, allowing neutrals to move faster than ions within the envelope.

12. Time-Delay Recovery in AGN Reverberation Mapping via Mutual Information[2611.10419]
Abstract

Active galactic nuclei exhibit complex, nonlinear multiwavelength variability that standard cross-correlation techniques, sensitive only to linear covariance, may fail to capture fully. We apply time-lagged Mutual Information (MI) lag estimation, via the Kraskov-Stoegbauer-Grassberger $k$-nearest-neighbour estimator ($k = 5$) with a 150-iteration Random Subset Selection bootstrap, to the light curves of four Seyfert galaxies (Mrk 509, NGC 5548, NGC 4151, and NGC 4593) monitored across eight energy bands by Swift. In the UV/optical regime the MI estimator successfully reproduces the inter-band disc reprocessing lags established by the interpolated cross-correlation function, with performance degrading toward the optical where low variability amplitude produces poorly defined MI peaks. In the X-ray bands, where the physical X-ray/UV coupling is known to be weak, the estimator fails to recover consistent lags across the sample, with the notable exception of NGC 4593. Contextualising these outcomes within the multifractal characterisation of our previous work, we discuss how MI performance relates to the multifractal spectral width $\Delta\alpha$ of each band, and we propose $\Delta\alpha$ as a candidate diagnostic for anticipating where MI-based reverberation mapping is likely to succeed or fail in multiband datasets of active galactic nuclei.

13. HINSPHERES: Spherically Symmetric Radiative Transfer Modeling of HINSA and the Radial HI-to-H$_2$ Transition in Molecular Clouds[2611.10456]
Abstract

The transition from atomic hydrogen (HI) to molecular hydrogen (H$_2$) is the first step in the evolution from diffuse interstellar gas to dense star-forming cores. HI narrow self-absorption (HINSA), produced by cold atomic gas mixed with molecular material, provides a direct tracer of the HI-to-H$_2$ transition in molecular clouds. Existing HINSA analyses typically model HINSA as a single Gaussian component with a uniform spin temperature. This approach cannot recover the radial HI distribution and yields large errors for young cores whose profiles deviate from a Gaussian shape. Here we present hinspheres, an open-source framework that models the core as onion-like spherically symmetric concentric shells, solves radiative transfer along the line of sight and jointly fits the HI abundance, temperature, and kinematic parameters. The framework recovers all parameters with a mean relative error of 4.6-6.8% on our synthetic data with different ages. A combined resolution-noise grid shows that the median recovery error remains below 16% for cores within 1 kpc observed at FAST resolution with noise levels below 0.2 K. Applying it to the dark cores L1574 and L1544, we reveal radial HI abundance profiles that span about three orders of magnitude, which were missed by the single-Gaussian method. The derived chemical ages are $6.5\times10^6$ and $8.7\times10^6$ yr, respectively. Compared with the commonly used single-Gaussian method, hinspheres characterizes the radial profile of HINSA structures more accurately, making it well suited to identifying and analyzing young cores undergoing the HI-to-H$_2$ transition.

14. B-fields In STar-forming Region Observations (BISTRO): Population-Level Universality and Environmental Structuring of 450/850 $μ$m Polarization Signatures[2611.10458]
Abstract

We observed 69 molecular cloud regions at 450 and 850 $\mu$m with the POL-2 polarimeter on the James Clerk Maxwell Telescope and matched the maps to a common $12.6''$ beam. Polarization fractions are typically lower at 450 $\mu$m. The median of 30 measured ratios is $P_{450}/P_{850} = 0.69 \pm 0.02_{\rm sys} \pm 0.03_{\rm stat}$. Across 35 regions with reliable spectral indices of total ($\alpha$) and polarized ($\gamma$) intensity, the medians over sources are $\langle\alpha\rangle = 3.33$ and $\langle\gamma\rangle = 2.85$-$3.04$. The median $\gamma - \alpha$ is $-0.57$ to $-0.45$, with $\gamma < \alpha$ in 24-27 regions. These ranges span the two extreme placements of $3\sigma$ limits for polarized intensity below the noise floor. The position angles at the two wavelengths agree within $10^\circ$ in 10 of 13 regional medians, disfavoring a geometric origin that would rotate the angles. The medians of $\gamma-\alpha$ in 9 low-mass, 19 high-mass and 7 Central Molecular Zone (CMZ) regions are not ordered low-mass $<$ high-mass $<$ CMZ (Jonckheere-Terpstra $p = 0.43$-$0.70$). A difference among classes is not robustly detected. The Kruskal-Wallis $p = 0.056$-$0.078$ becomes $p = 0.026$-$0.041$ when the Rosette Molecular Cloud alone is excluded, although the high-mass class has the lowest median in every treatment. The classes follow a distance sequence (the beam spans 0.008-0.51 pc), coupling environment and physical scale. Within clouds, the 450 $\mu$m polarization fraction decreases with Herschel column density in all 7 fields with sufficient dynamic range. Coefficients from pooled pixels mix trends within clouds, differences between clouds, and the influence of OMC 1. They therefore do not define a single law for individual pixels. The wavelength dependence across sources provides a benchmark for grain alignment. We cannot order it by environment and do not propose a unique mechanism.

15. The hierarchical structure of the Milky Way I. A model-agnostic clustering of 1 billion stars in 5D[2611.10548]
Abstract

The Milky Way exhibits a hierarchical structure shaped by mergers, accretion, and internal dynamics. Gaia Data Release 3 (DR3) enables a global view of this structure, but requires scalable, model-agnostic methods to extract multi-scale clustering patterns. We aim to characterise the hierarchical clustering structure of the Milky Way in a model-agnostic way and to assess how the resulting structures relate to existing catalogues. We apply the hierarchical density-based clustering algorithm AstroLink to a locally magnitude-complete subsample of over one billion Gaia DR3 stars with five-dimensional astrometric information. The data are corrected for selection effects and transformed using a distance contraction and feature scaling. We compare the resulting cluster hierarchy to catalogues of open clusters, globular clusters, dwarf galaxies, and stellar streams using custom availability-aware metrics and a bi-directional best-match framework. At a significance threshold of $S = 3.8\sigma$, AstroLink identifies 7410 statistically significant clusters spanning a wide range of morphologies. Compact systems are often recovered as reciprocal matches with many also exhibiting internal substructure, while extended structures are typically fragmented or partially recovered. A substantial fraction of clusters have no counterpart in the comparison catalogues, these may be new structures but additional physical checks are needed to verify this. A model-agnostic, hierarchical clustering approach can recover the multi-scale structure of the Milky Way at Gaia DR3 scale and provides a unified framework for comparing heterogeneous catalogues and identifying candidate structures.

16. Studying the Fullerene Nest: JWST Observations of the Planetary Nebula Tc 1. II. A Spectroscopic and Spatial Study of C$_{60}$ and C$_{70}$ Emission[2611.10876]
Abstract

We present a detailed analysis of the fullerene emission bands in JWST MIRI/MRS observations of the planetary nebula Tc 1. The observations reveal the emission features due to neutral C$_{60}$ and C$_{70}$ in unprecedented detail. The combined fullerene emission represents $\sim$2.6% of the total infrared output, suggesting that $\sim$0.6% of the carbon budget is locked up in fullerenes. We find no clear evidence for C$_{60}^+$, C$_{70}^+$ or C$_{60}^-$ in the spectrum. The high spectral resolution allows us to separate the fullerene bands from atomic emission lines for the first time, allowing for a much better characterization of the band profiles and yielding more accurate band ratios than previous observations. We see no spatial variation in the individual C$_{60}$ and C$_{70}$ band profile shapes across all pixels. The 17.4 and 18.9 $\mu$m C$_{60}$ bands show clear resolved ro-vibrational structure in their profiles, and similar structured emission is seen in the 12.5, 14.8, 17.7, and 21.8 $\mu$m C$_{70}$ bands, further establishing that all emitting fullerene molecules are in the gas phase. We report a higher 7.0/18.9 $\mu$m C$_{60}$ band ratio ($\sim$1.4-1.9) than previously reported ($\sim$1.3). We note that all bands, except the 8.5 $\mu$m band, contain a significant contribution from C$_{70}$, complicating the analysis. The fullerene emission is spatially concentrated in a thin, asymmetric shell at a radius of 5-6$''$ from the central star. The shell lies at the outer edge of the core of the nebula, which was ejected in a single, brief episode $\gtrsim$4,000 years ago.

17. Impact of the large scale environment on the baryonic scaling relations of disc galaxies: Insights from the GASP survey[2611.10910]
Abstract

The Tully–Fisher relation (TFR) and the specific angular momentum–mass relation (SAMR) link the baryonic content of a disc to its kinematics and dynamics. Hence, these scaling relations should bear the imprints of any process removing baryons or altering their kinematics, such as those acting in clusters of galaxies. We test this using the MUSE survey "GAs Stripping Phenomena in galaxies" (GASP), which targets field, group, and cluster members at $z \approx 0.05$ and various stages of ram pressure stripping. For the 81 discs with favourable inclinations, we measure the distribution and kinematics of stars and ionised gas, and their specific angular momenta. Within their scatter, the stellar and baryonic relations show no significant dependence on the large scale environment: the TFRs have slopes $\alpha \approx 3.6$ and $\approx 3.5$ for any kinematic tracer, and the SAMRs have $\alpha \approx 0.65$–$0.75$, as predicted by tidal torque theory. The same holds for the ten S0-S0/as in our sample, consistent with the "faded spiral" formation scenario. Only the gas SAMR shows an environmental signature, with ram pressure stripped galaxies lying $\approx 0.16$ dex below unperturbed ones. This offset is mostly due to their enhanced star formation rate, and thus higher gas mass when inferred through the Schmidt-Kennicutt law, rather than to kinematic perturbations or disc truncation. Our results suggest that, within the area probed by MUSE ($\lesssim6$ disc scale lengths), these discs have preserved the angular momentum acquired at formation not only from cluster processes and pre-processing in filaments, but also from mergers and ejective feedback acting in any environment.

18. Stellar Disruption and Black Hole Retention in Massive Collisions: The Role of Thermonuclear Feedback[2611.10934]
Abstract

A stellar-mass black hole (BH) that collides with a massive star can be captured by drag and settle at its center as a black hole star. Hydrodynamic simulations that neglect nuclear reactions have found that the star survives and becomes a black hole star (BH*) for BH-to-star mass ratios $q\lesssim0.2$. We perform hydrodynamic simulations of head-on collisions with the \athenak\ code, including CNO burning coupled to the gas, for $q=0.1$ and $0.2$ BHs, stellar masses of $100$ and $500\,M_{\odot}$, solar, metal-deficient, and Helium-rich compositions. In all models (with $q\le0.2$), the energy dissipated by drag is comparable to or less than the binding energy that the captured BH adds to the gas so that it alone does not disrupt the star. But, under compression around the captured BH, gas is heated to $\gtrsim2\times10^8$ K and undergoes thermonuclear burning at the $\beta$-limited hot-CNO rate. Within about one dynamical time after the BH settles at the center, the nuclear energy released during the encounter becomes a significant fraction of the star's binding energy $\tfrac32\bar{\beta}\int P\,dV$. The star loses most of its mass once the cumulative release reaches this binding energy. Below this threshold, the release rate declines. Merger between a solar-composition star and a BH with a mass ratio $q=0.2$ leads to the disruption of the star after releasing most of its total mass. But a metal-poor star, with nearly the same binding energy and total yield, survives with minor release after merger with an identical BH. After coalescence with a less massive ($q=0.1$) BH, stars with both solar and metal-poor composition survive as black hole stars, burning at a rate $\gtrsim 10^{44}\,{\rm erg\,s^{-1}}$, far from thermal equilibrium. Very massive and helium-rich stars, which are weakly bound, are the most easily disrupted.

19. Tidally-induced vertical asymmetries in the LMC bar and disk[2611.10979]
Abstract

Past observations and simulations have shown that the bar in the Large Magellanic Cloud (LMC) may be tilted with respect to the disk plane, and that the outer LMC disk could be warped. These studies propose that the tilted bar and the warp in the LMC are induced by the Small Magellanic Cloud (SMC) pericentric passages. We analyze and quantify the effect of the SMC passage on the vertical position and kinematic asymmetries of both the bar and disk, as a function of different impact parameters or initial conditions. We used seven representative simulations from the KRATOS suite of $N$-body simulations of the LMC, SMC and Milky Way (MW) system for this analysis. Abstract

We describe the nanofabrication and loading of lithographically patterned, high aspect ratio $10$ $\mu$m diameter silicon dioxide discs with thickness of $400$ nm into an optical standing wave trap in vacuum. We obtain motional spectra of the center of mass motion of the discs using a combination of interferometric and imaging methods. The method is capable of high-efficiency loading and optical trapping using small numbers of objects, of the order of a few particles, with customized geometries and sizes compatible with wafer scale nanofabrication methods. Disc-like particles in optical traps have been identified as promising candidates for detectors of high frequency gravitational waves or as sensitive accelerometers.
