1. Two-dimensional galaxy cluster shapes using lensing data: a simulated analysis[2610.36039]
Abstract

Context. Cluster morphology reflects assembly history and dynamical state. High-resolution weak lensing can reveal departures from spherical symmetry, but its ability to recover two-dimensional shapes remains less explored. Aims. We assess how reliably weak lensing recovers projected cluster morphology and its dependence on orientation and dynamical state. We also compare large-scale weak-lensing morphology with that traced by strong lensing near the cluster centre. Methods. We study three clusters at $z=0.22$ from The Three Hundred Project, representing relaxed, mildly disturbed and unrelaxed systems. For each, we consider three random projections and three along the principal axes of the mass tensor. For each projection, we generate 128 weak-lensing catalogues with a source density of 30 arcmin$^{-2}$ and intrinsic ellipticity dispersion $\sigma_{\epsilon}=0.3$. We constrain mass and concentration from one-dimensional NFW and BMO fits, then recover ellipticity, position angle and centre using a two-dimensional elliptical NFW model. We also measure morphology from true convergence maps, Kaiser & Squires reconstructions and strong-lensing tangential critical lines. Results. Recovered parameters depend on orientation and dynamical state. The relaxed cluster has similar inner and large-scale shapes, whereas disturbed systems appear more elliptical centrally. Two-dimensional weak-lensing modelling recovers the projected mass orientation despite source sampling and shape noise. Kaiser & Squires reconstructions recover the position angle well but favour rounder mass distributions. Centroid offsets are larger in unrelaxed systems. Conclusions. Combining weak- and strong-lensing morphology probes cluster structure across physical scales and offers a promising way to investigate morphology and dynamical state.

2. An intermediate-mass stripped star with a dark companion in the Small Magellanic Cloud[2610.36050]
Abstract

We present discovery of SMASH J004005$-$733351, an intermediate-mass stripped helium star in the Small Magellanic Cloud with a dark companion. The system was identified in a new optical color-selected survey for stripped stars and confirmed through spectroscopic follow-up. We measure an orbital period of $P_{\rm orb}=19.95\pm 0.002$ hours and a radial-velocity semi-amplitude of $K=91.7 ^{+3.2}_{-3.1}\,{\rm km\,s^{-1}}$. Simultaneous modeling of the optical spectrum and ultraviolet-to-optical spectral energy distribution yields a stripped star evolutionary mass $M_{\rm stripped} = 3.84 \pm 0.54\,M_\odot$, temperature $T_{\rm eff} = 68.3 \pm 6.4$ kK, surface gravity $\log g= 4.76 \pm 0.12$, luminosity $\log(L/L_\odot)= 4.28 \pm 0.13$, radius $R= 0.99 \pm 0.09\,R_\odot$, and an unusually high surface hydrogen abundance of $X_{\rm H, s}=0.63 \pm 0.04$. The companion is undetected spectroscopically, and the orbital solution and stripped-star evolutionary mass imply a minimum companion mass of $M_2 \gtrsim 1.2\,M_\odot$. The absence of photometric variability from irradiation rules out a main-sequence companion. The companion is most likely a neutron star (NS), although a massive white dwarf or low-mass stripped star cannot be fully excluded. Binary evolution models predict that the stripped star will refill its Roche lobe after core helium exhaustion, likely producing a stripped or ultra-stripped supernova and ultimately a double NS binary if the system survives. SMASH J004005$-$733351 is among the first known stripped stars with a compact object companion. The system provides a new empirical benchmark for the late stages of binary interaction that lead to (ultra-)stripped supernovae and double NS binaries.

3. The evolutionary history of ultra-compact accreting binaries. II. HST spectroscopy of two eclipsing AM CVn stars[2610.36066]
Abstract

AM CVn stars are ultra-compact binaries in which a white dwarf (WD) accretes helium-rich material from a hydrogen-deficient donor. They are promising mHz gravitational-wave sources and potential progenitors of Type Ia supernovae. However, their evolutionary pathways remain uncertain. Three formation channels have been proposed: the WD, helium star, and cataclysmic variable channels. We aim to determine the atmospheric chemical composition of the underlying accreting WDs in two eclipsing AM CVn systems, YZ LMi and ZTF J0407-00, in order to constrain their formation channels. Using ultraviolet spectra obtained with the Hubble Space Telescope, we modelled the atmospheres of the accreting WDs, whose surface compositions are enriched by material transferred from the donor and therefore trace the donor's composition. In particular, the nitrogen-to-carbon abundance ratio provides a diagnostic of the donor's nuclear burning history and hence of the system's formation channel. We measure effective temperatures Teff = 24400 $\pm$ 1500 K and surface gravities logg = 8.36 $\pm$ 0.05 for YZ LMi, and Teff = 29900 $\pm$ 2200 K and logg = 8.37 $\pm$ 0.05 for ZTF J0407-00. The inferred masses of the two WDs are 0.84 $\pm$ 0.10 and 0.86 $\pm$ 0.12 solar masses, respectively. Carbon is not detected in either system, yielding lower limits on the nitrogen-to-carbon mass ratio of N/C > 117 and N/C > 185. We increase from one to three the number of AM CVn systems whose accreting WDs and chemical abundances have been analysed using detailed UV atmosphere models. The high N/C ratios rule out the helium star formation channel for both systems. The WD channel is favoured by the absence of detectable hydrogen at all observed wavelengths, although the evolved cataclysmic variable channel cannot be conclusively excluded.

4. Dynamical constraints on the companion mass from pulsar timing and cyclotron line confirmation in the SFXT IGR J16418-4532[2610.36103]
Abstract

Supergiant Fast X-ray Transients (SFXTs) show extreme X-ray variability on timescales of minutes to hours, driven by accretion processes that remain poorly understood. IGR J16418-4532 is an intermediate SFXT with detected X-ray pulsations and a candidate cyclotron line, making it a key system for probing neutron-star accretion and magnetic fields in this class. We analysed a 148 ks NuSTAR observation and archival RXTE observations, using epoch folding to search for pulsations while correcting photon arrival times for a range of trial $a_x \sin i$ values, and performed phase-averaged spectroscopy with several continuum models. We detect coherent pulsations at $P_{\rm spin} = 1208.67 \pm 0.04$ s (NuSTAR) and $ 1209.4178 \pm 0.0084$ s (RXTE/PCA). The pulse detection depends clearly on the assumed projected semi-major axis and is most significant for $a_x \sin i = 55.1 \pm 3.5$ lt-s (NuSTAR) and $51.3 \pm 2.6$ lt-s (RXTE/PCA). Sliding-window pulse profiles show the orbital delay in the uncorrected data, which is fully removed after orbital correction. The inferred orbital parameters constrain the companion mass and radius to $\sim$12-18 $M_\odot$ and $\sim$13-16 $R_\odot$. This is the first constraint on the binary mass function of an SFXT obtained from the orbital motion of the neutron star. The spectrum shows a robust cyclotron absorption line at $\sim$50 keV, independent of the continuum model, implying $B \approx 5 \times 10^{12}$ G. Optimising pulse detection with trial $a_x \sin i$ corrections, rather than modelling pulse arrival-time delays, offers a route to dynamical mass measurements in other SFXTs whose variable pulse profiles limit conventional timing techniques.

5. A Model to Map Brown Dwarf Companions to White Dwarfs in Partial Secondary Eclipse[2610.36141]
Abstract

Eclipse mapping is a well-established technique for constraining the spatial emission of hot Jupiter exoplanets. Brown dwarfs in ultra-short orbits around white dwarfs are an analog to hot Jupiters that can only ever be eclipsed within a limited range of latitudes. We adapt the eigenmapping method to disentangle the signals from the ever- and never-occulted regions. The result of this signal separation is a super-position of map structures, yielding sharp boundaries at the occultation region. We introduce a model that penalizes the harmonic complexity of the model map with Tikhonov regularization, whose parameters are tuned to reproduce the fit quality of the eigenmapping model. We use JWST NIRSpec/PRISM full-orbit observations of the eclipsing WD 1202-024 system as a template to construct and run injection-recovery tests on simulated light curves, isolating both latitudinal structures as well as emission from a physically-realistic general circulation model (GCM). In the latter, the narrow equatorial jet driven by the BD's rapid rotation manifests as an eclipse depth asymmetry, which our model reproduces as a latitude-dependent phase offset. Such a model, however, is only statistically preferred at approximately $3 \times$ the signal-to-noise ratio of the JWST data. When fitting to the actual JWST data of WD 1202, the full-phase eigenbasis model is statistically preferred. However, the resulting model map of WD 1202 B does not resemble the GCM predictions, with a concentrated day-side hotspot at the sub-stellar point with an angular size $\approx 15^\circ$. Interpreting this result more carefully requires follow-up work to disentangle systematics from true emission signals. This model framework provides a means to detect emission features from these rare types of eclipses, and ultimately to test predictions of models of atmospheric circulation across hot Jupiters and their BD analogs.

6. All Dark Matter as Primordial Black Holes: a Gravitational-Wave Test[2610.36177]
Abstract

We propose a novel test for the hypothesis that the dark matter of the Universe is composed entirely by asteroidal-mass primordial black holes. It relies on detecting the stochastic gravitational-wave background generated by the coalescence onto rapidly spinning supermassive astrophysical black holes of the light primordial black holes accumulated in spherically symmetric spikes. We discuss the scenarios in which the signal can be observed by the future space-based gravitational-wave observatories LISA and DECIGO.

7. DUETS II - Red giant asteroseismic binaries. Binary confirmation and prospects for double-seismic model inference[2610.36209]
Abstract

Red giants displaying solar-like oscillations allow precise stellar properties to be derived. Asteroseismic binaries (ABs), which are gravitationally bound systems where both stars show detectable oscillations, can provide constraints on the interior of two coeval stars, allowing us to model them jointly and potentially obtain constraints unavailable from either star individually. This makes ABs potential laboratories for calibrating stellar evolution theory. In this context, AB candidates (ABCs) are systems showing two oscillators, which are as of yet not confirmed as gravitationally bound. We aimed to confirm ABCs and other red giant binary candidates, as well as demonstrate the advantages of AB modeling inference. We obtained spectroscopic indicators of binarity for 60 oscillating red giants that have photometric (33 stars) and/or asteroseismic (38) indications of binarity. We confirm 12 of our 60 targets as spectroscopic binaries, 8 of which are ABs as well. Cross-matching with Gaia DR3 and APOGEE DR17, we identify a further 9 likely binaries, at least 6 of which are also ABCs. We then investigated the impact of simultaneously modeling the oscillation modes of both oscillators of an AB. We inferred stellar properties for; one binary of extra-Galactic origin containing one detectable red-giant-branch (RGB) oscillator; one AB with RGB oscillators; and two ABCs with RGB oscillators. We find that the mode frequencies of the two ABCs and the confirmed AB are well-described with a coeval modeling of the stellar components. We demonstrate that the simultaneous modeling of ABs with two RGB stars can be used to infer the stellar helium content down to $3\%$ precision, compared to $5\%$ when using only the brightest star. Finally, the metal-poor binary KIC2971380 hints for a need to correct the $\nu_{\rm max}$ scaling relation, supporting recent studies of extremely metal-poor oscillators.

8. Non-minimal $α$-attractor magnetogenesis with potential dependent couplings[2610.36228]
Abstract

We investigate inflationary magnetogenesis driven by a non conformal coupling in the case of large-field $\alpha$-attractor inspired models. We introduce potential-dependent electromagnetic coupling functions $f(\phi)$ that reproduce the scaling $f\propto a^p$ during slow roll. Starting from Starobinsky inflation, we analytically derive explicit couplings for $(E)$-model and $(T)$-model potentials. We then study how these functions affect electromagnetic amplification in the regime of non-minimal coupling between the inflaton field and the Ricci scalar $\phi^2 R$. In particular, we compare different values for the coupling strength $\xi$, accounting for electromagnetic backreaction and Schwinger pair production through the gradient expansion formalism. Our numerical results show that the non-minimal coupling affects the evolution of the electromagnetic sector and the resulting magnetic-field amplitude. We find that, within the analyzed configurations, minimally coupled and $\xi>0$ scenarios are compatible with the adopted CMB constraints, yielding conditional present-day magnetic-field estimates of order $10^{-23}$ G.

9. Dynamical plausibility of triple-assisted interaction in Be-star multiple systems[2610.36304]
Abstract

Classical Be stars are rapidly rotating B-type stars surrounded by gaseous circumstellar disks, but the origin of their angular momentum remains uncertain. We investigate whether observed Be-star multiple systems are dynamically compatible with triple-assisted interaction, in which a tertiary companion drives Kozai-Lidov (KL) eccentricity excitation in an unresolved inner binary. We combine speckle-interferometric detections, reported inner periods, and proper-motion anomalies (PMa) with synthetic populations of hierarchical triples. Accepted configurations must satisfy dynamical stability, secular-timescale, inner-periastron interaction, and observational constraints. Refined mass-aware simulations identify HD 35079 and HD 55606 as the clearest period-plus-speckle dynamical matches. HD 205329 remains dynamically plausible but depends on the approximate PMa constraint. For HD 41335, the period-plus-speckle and PMa-plus-speckle constraints are separately compatible, whereas imposing all three simultaneously yields no accepted configurations at the adopted tolerance. HD 37330, HD 41600, and HD 71510 are additional PMa-dependent hierarchical-triple candidates. An independent angular-momentum estimate indicates a median required accreted mass fraction of approximately 4.8% under the adopted assumptions. We conclude that several observed Be-star multiple systems admit stable, KL-active architectures capable of driving inner binaries toward interaction. These results establish dynamical plausibility rather than a confirmed evolutionary history and motivate further speckle, spectroscopic, and astrometric monitoring.

10. Periodic radio emission from a rapidly rotating T8 ultracool dwarf[2610.36336]
Abstract

We report the discovery of periodic radio bursts from the T8 brown dwarf WISE J151721.13+052929.3 (WISE J1517+05), detected with ASKAP and MeerKAT. The emission is characterised by a stable periodicity of $1.149 \pm 0.002$ hr, placing WISE J1517+05 among the fastest rotating radio-detected ultracool dwarfs (UCDs). The radio bursts are broadband and highly variable, reaching a peak spectral luminosity of $L_{\nu} = 5.6 \times 10^{15}\,\mathrm{erg\,s^{-1}\,Hz^{-1}}$, making WISE J1517+05 the brightest periodic UCD to date. The high brightness temperature of the radio bursts ($T_B>10^{11}$ K), combined with the rotational modulation, strongly favour an auroral electron cyclotron maser emission (ECME) origin. In contrast to most known radio-bursting UCDs, the bursts exhibit relatively low and variable circular polarisation fractions (typically $\sim 20\%$). These properties suggest significant depolarisation of intrinsically highly polarised ECME, likely due to propagation effects in a structured magnetospheric plasma. Notably, we also observe a polarisation reversal within a single burst profile, which is currently unexplained. We model the emission using a dipolar magnetic field with emission arising from a single active field line, and find a moderate inclination angle of $i = 47.9^{+11.4}_{-9.2}$ degrees and a magnetic obliquity of $\beta = 40.1^{+9.2}_{-10.9}$ degrees. WISE J1517+05 expands the parameter space of radio-active UCDs toward higher luminosities and lower polarisation fractions, suggesting that current surveys may be sensitive to a larger population than previously recognised, while searches relying on methods that assume high circular polarisation fractions risk missing a subset of UCDs. More broadly, this discovery reinforces the idea that rapid rotation plays a key role in generating strong dipolar magnetic fields in UCDs that can give rise to auroral radio emission.

11. Heating of Solar Coronal Loops by Converging Unipolar Magnetic Flux: Evidence from SDO Observations and a Radiative MHD Simulation[2610.36362]
Abstract

Solar coronal loop heating remains one of the greatest mysteries in astrophysics. In emerging flux regions, coronal loops form and are heated as strong magnetic flux tubes emerge through the photosphere. The loop footpoints gradually separate. In this study, we investigate the heating of quiet-region (QR) coronal loops whose opposite-polarity footpoints are already separated. Our observations reveal that convection-driven magnetic flux convergence at a loop's foot – where smaller unipolar photospheric magnetic flux patches merge into a stronger flux patch – correlates with loop brightening, and flux divergence at that foot correlates with that loop's disappearance. This behavior is similar to recent findings for solar coronal plumes. To investigate and quantify this effect, we track 10 QR coronal loops through their lifetimes using SDO/AIA 171 and 94 Angstrom images, along with SDO/HMI magnetograms. The coronal EUV luminosity shows a strong correlation with the magnetic flux in at least one footpoint, most strongly when the line-of-sight magnetic field strength (Blos) exceeds a threshold of about 200-500 G. A Bifrost MHD simulation of the quiet-Sun network and corona shows similar behavior but at smaller time-scales: magnetic flux convergence and divergence – most pronounced at a loop footpoint – correlate directly with increased and reduced emissivity due to heating and cooling, respectively. The simulation reveals an increase in Poynting flux, reconnection, and Joule heating with flux convergence at that footpoint. We hypothesize that an increased rate of nanoflares during flux convergence may explain the enhanced emissivity of these loops due to heating, with potentially broader implications for coronal heating.

12. Retention of primordial atmospheres through giant impacts[2610.36446]
Abstract

Rocky planets the size of Earth or larger are expected to undergo one or more giant impacts during the final stages of their formation. Some of these collisions occur after protoplanetary disk dissipation, potentially driving the loss of primordial hydrogen-rich atmospheres through elevated surface temperatures. However, whether giant impacts promote atmospheric loss or retention remains unclear because they both heat planetary interiors and increase atmospheric gravitational binding. Here we model the evolution of rocky planets after their largest post-disk giant impact, accounting self-consistently for the concurrent increase in gravitational binding energy and mantle thermal energy. Whereas the entire accreted mass contributes to the increase in gravitational binding energy, only part of the impact energy raises mantle temperature. Mantle melting consumes energy as latent heat, while the impactor's metallic core transfers little energy to the mantle and remains superheated at depth. Giant impacts therefore increase gravitational binding energy more than mantle thermal energy, shifting planets towards atmospheric retention. Larger, atmosphere-bearing planets emerge preferentially through collisional growth. By contrast, smaller planets without such impacts tend to lose their primordial atmospheres under sustained stellar irradiation. At the population level, these results suggest that sub-Neptunes have, on average, more massive rocky interiors than super-Earths.

13. Three-flavor neutrino Boltzmann transport and flavor instabilities in long-lived neutron-star merger remnants[2610.36486]
Abstract

Binary neutron-star (BNS) mergers are multimessenger sources whose kilonova emission and nucleosynthesis depend sensitively on neutrino microphysics and flavor conversion. Most BNS merger simulations assume $\mu$-$\tau$ neutrino degeneracy in a two-flavor treatment. We investigate the fast flavor instability (FFI) and the collisional flavor instability (CFI) in long-lived BNS merger remnants using axisymmetric general-relativistic three-flavor Boltzmann neutrino transport with a comprehensive treatment of muons and muonic interactions, following a handoff from 3D grey moment-based simulations. Our simulations largely reproduce the parent 3D simulations, recovering trapped weak equilibrium in the core, muonic non-equilibrium in the semitransparent outer disk due to prolonged demuonization, and cold weak equilibrium in transparent regions. Linear stability analysis reveals strong and widespread CFI in all three flavor sectors, including narrow resonance-like structures. Muonic interactions reshape the FFI distribution, shifting the $e\tau$-sector instability toward denser disk regions compared with the extended FFI outside the disk in the two-flavor model, while disk demuonization produces a strong $\nu_\mu$ excess and one-sided muon-lepton-number angular distributions that suppress FFI in the $e\mu$ and $\mu\tau$ sectors. Local three-flavor quantum kinetic equation (QKE) calculations reveal nonlinear coupling among flavor sectors that excites coherence even in an initially stable sector. Diagonal collisional repopulation is decisive for the nonlinear evolution and asymptotic flavor populations, and neglecting it overestimates flavor equilibration. We establish the necessity of three-flavor transport from both classical and quantum perspectives, and underscore the importance of consistently coupling flavor conversion with diagonal collisional repopulation in future CFI subgrid models.

14. Towards Reliably Measuring the Mass-Size Relation of Dark Matter Halos: Impact of projection, miscentering and Eddington bias[2610.36531]
Abstract

In the conventional spherical overdensity definition of a dark matter halo, masses and radii, e.g. $M_{200{\rm m}}$ and $r_{200{\rm m}}$, are equivalent. However, halos do exhibit a physical boundary at the splashback radius $r_{\rm sp}$, which manifests as a sharp drop in density. Splashback radii are now routinely measured in stacked weak lensing profiles, but the expected trend of decreasing $r_{\rm sp}/r_{200{\rm m}}$ with mass has not been verified in observed galaxy groups or clusters. In this work, we show that several observational effects conceal the predicted mass-size relation. In particular, we quantify the impact of systematics on the mean density and lensing profiles of mock-observed halo samples, which are created by applying a realistic cluster-finding procedure to dark matter-only simulations. We reproduce the known impact of projection: preferentially observing systems in overdense regions. But, differing from previous works, we show that the same projection effect, combined with scatter in observed luminosities, causes a significant Eddington bias in the derived mass-observable relation and a complex suppression in mean density profiles over all radial scales. The magnitude of this effect is mass-dependent and coupled to the cluster-finding procedure and survey properties, making it challenging to model simply and motivating full forward-modeling of observed group and cluster samples. However, we demonstrate that a simple mitigation approach, effectively a weak lensing mass calibration, does well in approximately recovering the true mass-size relation. Finally, we show that only severe miscentering can bias the measurement of the halo size, but any miscentering can increase the measurement uncertainty.

15. Warm inflation beyond the Gaussian limit: primordial black holes and induced gravitational waves[2610.36591]
Abstract

In warm inflation, the primordial fluctuations are driven by random kicks from a hot radiation bath. Two features of these kicks matter: how long the bath remembers them, which shapes the power spectrum, and how their strengths are spread, which controls the rare, extreme fluctuations. In this paper, we investigate how primordial black holes and the accompanying induced gravitational waves can be used to discriminate between these two features. We use a simple model inspired by warm inflation, in which independent kicks of random strength add up to a narrow peak in the power spectrum. At leading order, the induced gravitational wave depends only on the power spectrum and is therefore insensitive to how the kicks are distributed. However, the black holes are very sensitive to these rare large fluctuations. Hence, two baths can yield the same gravitational wave signal while forming black holes at very different rates. A modest kurtosis range of 0.1 to 0.3 enhances black-hole formation by factors of ten to a million at fixed gravitational-wave amplitude, the larger factors for the rarer black holes. Counting peaks rather than all points, or widening the spectrum, lowers the enhancement by at most a factor of about four. In general a change in the power affects both phenomena, while the kurtosis affects only the black hole production.

16. 2:1 Harmonics in the Radio Light Curves of Supermassive Black Hole Binary Candidates[2610.36732]
Abstract

We present evidence of 2:1 harmonic periodicities in two supermassive black hole binary (SMBHB) candidates (PKS J0805-0111 and PKS 2131-021) in the Owens Valley Radio Observatory (OVRO) + University of Michigan Radio Astronomical Observatory (UMRAO) monitoring programs. This potential discovery follows the previous detection of a 2:1 harmonic periodicity in the SMBHB candidate J1309+1154 from these programs. If confirmed, these suggest a physical origin of blazars exhibiting strong periodicities favoring 2:1 commensurabilities in their light curves. There are significant differences between the 2:1 harmonics in these three blazars, which may be due to differences in their central engines. These three SMBHB candidates demonstrate the importance of careful dissection, i.e. cutting apart and close examination, of blazar light curves, to reveal underlying evidence of coherence between different aspects of the periodicities observed in blazars in the presence of correlated random noise variations. This is particularly important for blazar SMBHB searches, given that the observed periods thus far are all longer than 2 years, and can disappear for long timespans. Therefore confirming evidence, such as harmonics, may be crucial in confirming the reality of periodicities, and identifying blazar SMBHB candidates. We present four models that can account for 2:1 commensurable periodicities in blazar light curves, together with explanations for why, on these models, shorter period SMBHB candidates have not thus far been observed, but might be anticipated, or not, in the future, depending on the model, thereby making these models discriminatory. Arguments pro and con the reality of these periodicities are presented.

Abstract

Recent cosmological data provide weak hints for smaller masses than what is required by neutrino-oscillation experiments, motivating investigations into variation of the neutrino masses with cosmic time. Here we study a model where the neutrinos couple to a quintessence field that acts as dark energy. 18. A microscopic analysis of shear acceleration in the relativistic regime[2610.36867]
Abstract

Relativistic shear flows, as encountered in jets of powerful astrophysical sources, offer promising sites of particle acceleration up to the highest energies. This stochastic acceleration process is usually described with Fokker-Planck transport equations, which assume small momentum gains per scattering. The present paper introduces a covariant microscopic description of relativistic shear acceleration, building on the formalism of generalized Fermi acceleration and formulating angular scattering in the locally comoving frame in terms of stochastic differential equations. This allows us to model relativistic shear profiles and to study the transition from gradual to non-gradual shear acceleration, where the particle mean free path becomes comparable to the shear length scale. Assuming an infinite shear layer, linear in the flow four-velocity, we observe that the shear rate peaks where the flow is mildly relativistic, whereas Lorentz aberration suppresses the transverse velocity of particles where the flow is relativistic. Consequently, particles that leave the central layer experience long waiting times with a heavy-tailed, power-law distribution. The momentum evolution becomes subdiffusive and the acceleration time can substantially exceed local estimates. We successfully reproduce the Monte Carlo energy distributions using a two-state continuous-time random walk with Lévy-distributed waiting times. We also show how a momentum-dependent mean-free path can introduce breaks in the spectrum and extend our discussion to finite jet profiles. These effects should be accounted for in models of relativistic jets as sources of high-energy particles.

19. Three-dimensional Magnetohydrodynamic Shocks and Associated Plasma Heating in the Solar Atmosphere[2610.36932]
Abstract

We investigate the three-dimensional (3D) formation and evolution of magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) shock waves and their role in plasma heating within a solar bipolar active region. Using 3D compressible, resistive MHD simulations, we model the emergence of magnetic flux from the convection zone into a magnetized corona. Our results reveal that initial plasmoid ejections launch hot plasma jets preceded by slow-mode shocks. Unlike established 2D models, these ejections originate from multiple sites along the emerging field's periphery, resulting in shocks with a complex, inverted-U 3D morphology. Beneath these fronts, the heated plasma forms flattened, sheet-like columns. Furthermore, we find that sustained gas pressure gradients, driven by dense plasma accumulation from plasmoids, power a cool, dense upflow adjacent to the main reconnection jet. During the main reconnection phase, the collision of ejected plasmoids with the ambient field triggers the formation of fast-mode termination shocks. By mapping viscous heating directly onto these irregular 3D shock surfaces, we demonstrate that energy dissipation is highly localized. This finding provides a physical link between shock-induced viscous heating and the observed temperature enhancements at the apex of post-flare loops.

20. The impact of the sulfur allotropes and sulfur hydrides on the Venus cloud chemistry[2610.36979]
Abstract

Venus is home to vivid sulfur chemistry, with SO$_2$ as the major sulfur gas species and a global cloud layer between 47 and 70 km composed of H$_2$SO$_4$ and H$_2$O. The chemistry in the clouds has been extensively studied with 1D models, but none is able to reproduce the three orders of magnitude decrease of SO$_2$ inside the clouds. Sulfur allotrope chemistry could be a candidate for buffering sulfur atoms, as it can grow into long chains. Sulfur hydrides such as H$_2$S is considered as one of the main sulfur-bearing compounds, possibly linked to surface reactions or have a volcanic origin like on Earth. In this study, we use for the first time a 3D model to study the climatology of the sulfur allotrope in gas and condensed phase and hydride chemistry in the clouds with an explicit photolysis calculation and updated UV cross-section. H$_2$S is close to ppmv levels below the cloud base, the third most abundant sulfur-bearing species in this region after SO$_2$ and OCS. Below the clouds, S$_8$ gas phase is the most abundant sulfur allotrope. Above, the photolysis and condensation are preponderant, and the gas phase decreases to extremely low values. Polysulfur can therefore not be the unknown absorber at cloud-top altitudes. The condensed phase polysulfurs are present from 30 to above 100 km. S$_2$ condensed phase is the most abundant due to its low saturation mixing ratios. Polysulfur species represent a substantial sulfur reservoir in the upper cloud, equivalent to a few ppmv.

Abstract

Hercules X-1 and Centaurus X-3 are the first ever detected - and as such identified - binary X-ray pulsars. The 35-day flux modulation shows two flux maxima, the so-called Main-On and the Short-On (with reduced flux). As noticed early on, the period, or the length of the cycle, is variable. There are short-term (hundreds of days) variations (in part quasi-periodic) overlaid to the long-term trend. 22. Unbound grains in gaseous debris discs[2610.37004]
Abstract

Context. Some debris discs contain cold molecular gas. The origin of this gas is one of the key questions in our understanding of the evolution of planetary systems. 97. Corrections to Hawking radiation from asteroid-mass primordial black holes: analytic and numerical evaluation of the stochastic charge effect[2608.04346]
Abstract

Hawking radiation sets stringent constraints on Primordial Black Holes (PBHs) as a dark matter candidate in the $M\sim 10^{16}$ g regime based on the evaporation products produced by the black hole, motivating the need to rigorously model the photon, electron, and positron emission spectra. This manuscript is the second in a series of two papers (see Vasquez et al. [Phys. Rev. D, 112:063002 (2025)] for the first paper in the series) with the goal of proving the stochastic emission of electrons and positrons, known as the "stochastic charge effect" and first predicted by Page [Phys. Rev. D, 16:2402 (1977)] using semi-classical arguments, arises from quantum electrodynamics (QED) on a Schwarzschild spacetime. We derive the corrections to the $e^\pm$ from the relevant term in the Hamiltonian (the long-range monopole: $H_{\rm int,0L}$) to first order in the fine structure constant ($\alpha$), and show that the semi-classical terms are also present in the QED calculation. We also highlight which terms in our analysis only appear within our quantum mechanical approach and cannot be explained otherwise. We find that due to some cancellation of terms, over the range of $2.2\times 10^{16}$ - $1.7\times 10^{17}$ g, the net correction to the $e^\pm$ emission rate is less than 2%, versus the suppression of up to 5% previously found by semi-classical arguments.

98. The alternative scalar field dark sector of the Universe: Cosmological inflation, quintessence and ekpyrotic model in the framework of scalar-tensor cosmology[2609.37293]
Abstract

This dissertation investigates scalar field descriptions of the dark sector (of the Universe), with emphasis on scalar-tensor cosmology, inflationary effective field theory, quintessence, and ekpyrotic/cyclic alternatives to inflation. The thesis first reviews scalar-tensor theories of gravity, starting from Brans-Dicke theory and extending to general non-minimally coupled scalar fields. Particular attention is paid to the interpretation of the scalar field, the relation between the Jordan and Einstein conformal frames, and the role of conformal transformations in cosmology. This provides the theoretical basis for treating scalar fields either as modified matter or as additional gravitational degrees of freedom. The second part discusses cosmological inflation from the viewpoint of effective field theory. The spontaneous breaking of time translations, the Goldstone description of adiabatic perturbations, higher-dimensional operators, radiative corrections and Planck-suppressed terms are analyzed in the context of ultraviolet sensitivity and the eta problem. The third part is devoted to quintessence as the simplest dynamical dark energy model. 'Tracking', 'scaling', 'thawing' and 'freezing' behaviors are discussed, together with observational diagnostics such as the statefinder parameters, the $(\omega_{\phi},\omega'_{\phi})$ plane and the $Om(z)$ diagnostic. The DESI DR2 results are used as motivation for considering time-dependent effective dark energy beyond a strict cosmological constant. The final part studies ekpyrotic and cyclic cosmologies. The ekpyrotic phase is analyzed as a smoothing mechanism based on an ultra-stiff scalar field equation of state. Perturbations, non-Gaussianity, tensor modes, bounce conditions, entropy production, geodesic incompleteness and phase-resolved field-space distance bounds are discussed.

99. Exceptional points and hysteresis in Kerr-Newman quasi-normal modes[2610.30049]
Abstract

Exceptional points (EPs) and eigenstate hysteresis, previously found in Kerr only with an auxiliary scalar field, arise intrinsically in the quasinormal-mode spectrum of Kerr-Newman black holes. Using a hyperboloidal formulation of the coupled gravito-electromagnetic Teukolsky equations and a Chebyshev pseudospectral method, we compute the $\ell=m=2$ spectrum and locate four EPs at up to $99.974\%$ of extremality, one from a new repulsion. 100. Boosted Dark Matter Directionality in Large Liquid Scintillator Detectors[2610.31666]
Abstract

Light dark matter can be boosted by cosmic-ray-dark matter interactions, which opens the possibility of direct-detection searches with large neutrino detectors. We consider the signal produced by boosted dark matter undergoing quasi-elastic scattering off carbon and knocking out a neutron in a large liquid scintillator detector. A distinctive delayed gamma ray from neutron capture can be reconstructed after $\sim 210$ $\mu$s, which is paired up with the prompt signal during neutron propagation. We find that 100 kt$\cdot$yr of data from the neutron-coincidence channel will be able to probe new parameter space compared to free proton signals found in previous work. We further consider the possibility of utilizing directional information from the displaced vertex associated with the capture of the final-state neutron. However, we find that directional information does not materially improve the sensitivity due to the large nucleon scattering angle.
