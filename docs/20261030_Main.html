1. Disentangling constraints on $S_8$ from the high and low redshift CMB[2610.37752]
Abstract

The clustering of matter in the universe is quantified by $\sigma_8$, the RMS fluctuations today in linear theory in spheres of radius $8h^{-1}$ Mpc, where $h$ parametrizes the Hubble constant. Surveys of gravitational lensing and galaxy clustering best constrain $S_8\equiv (\Omega_m/0.3)^{0.5} \sigma_8$, where $\Omega_m$ is the energy density of matter in units of the critical density. Comparing this late time measurement to the value of $S_8$ inferred from the cosmic microwave background (CMB), one can test the $\Lambda$CDM prediction for the growth of clustering from the primordial universe to now. Several analyses report hints that CMB measurements of $S_8$ are larger than those from galaxy surveys. However, CMB measurements have information from both high and low redshift due to lensing, and these tests traditionally conflate the two. Here, we present a technique to extract constraints on the cosmological parameters from each redshift component separately, enabling a high-z vs. low-z clustering comparison that was not previously possible. We marginalize over lensing information using a principal component analysis, and show that the primordial CMB from combined Planck 2018, ACT DR6, and SPT-3G D1 prefers $S_8= 0.795\pm0.022$, in agreement with the recent determination from the Dark Energy Survey (DES) of $S_8=0.789\pm0.012$. The clustering inferred from CMB lensing is larger. However, both the level of excess lensing and increase in agreement with DES depends on the prior on the sum of neutrino masses, $\sum m_\nu$. We find that removing lensing brings the CMB towards agreement with DES in any case.

2. Thinner Disks Struggle to Launch Jets: GRMHD Simulations of Magnetic Field Generation and Transport[2610.37758]
Abstract

Relativistic jets are observed in some accreting black-hole systems but not others. In X-ray binaries, jet production is closely associated with geometrically thicker accretion states, suggesting that disk thickness may influence the conditions for jet launching. We investigate this connection using three-dimensional GRMHD simulations of disks with $h/r=0.1$–$0.3$, initialized with a toroidal magnetic field with plasma $\beta=5$. We find that the magnetorotational instability (MRI) generates large-scale poloidal fields through non-axisymmetric shearing waves, with an additional contribution from axisymmetric induction through stretching by channel-like modes. The resulting mean fields become progressively weaker as the disk becomes thinner. Because these fields regulate MRI turbulence, thinner disks exhibit weaker angular-momentum transport and accrete more slowly than the standard $v_{\rm acc}\propto(h/r)^2$ thin-disk scaling would predict. The magnetic-flux transport velocity remains comparable to the accretion velocity, so weaker field generation and slower transport combine to strongly suppress black-hole flux accumulation at smaller $h/r$. Correspondingly, the thinnest disk ($h/r=0.1$) produces an electromagnetic jet efficiency of only $\eta_{\rm{EM}}\sim1\%$, while the thickest disk reaches the magnetically arrested disk (MAD) state and attains $\eta_{\rm{EM}}\geq100\%$. Using the measured large-scale magnetic flux distribution and transport velocities, we estimate the time required to accumulate MAD-level magnetic flux. Only the $h/r\sim0.3$ disk reaches MAD in the duration of our simulations, at $t\simeq2\times10^4\,r_g/c$, whereas for $h/r=0.1$ we estimate a timescale of $\sim10^8\,r_g/c$. These results link disk geometry to magnetic-flux accumulation and jet launching, with direct implications for the timescales over which jets can emerge in transient accreting systems.

3. Extreme eccentricity in double neutron stars without exotic physics[2610.37759]
Abstract

The formation of neutron stars (NSs) underpins astrophysical phenomena from supernovae (SNe), radio pulsars and X-ray binaries to double NS mergers that power gravitational waves (GWs), short gamma-ray bursts and r-process kilonovae. A recently reported double NS (PSR J0653+0443) with record eccentricity e=0.9924 provides a unique laboratory for studying GW emission and orbital evolution, and challenges existing formation scenarios. Here we show that such extreme eccentricities arise naturally – without invoking exotic conditions – from standard binary-SN physics: instantaneous mass loss (Blaauw recoil) and natal kicks readily produce e>0.99 for both compact and wide pre-SN orbits. The observed orbit matches three families of kinematic solutions spanning a range of kick velocities and progenitor configurations. Physically motivated priors based on NS spin and pre-SN mass transfer constrain the viable progenitor family, demonstrating that the system is not exceptionally rare. Future proper-motion measurements will tightly constrain its natal SN kick magnitude.

4. A double debris disc around a white dwarf sculpted by two planetary bodies[2610.37773]
Abstract

Many planetary systems survive the metamorphosis of their hosts stars into white dwarfs, as demonstrated by the detection of dusty circumstellar discs around these stellar remnants as well as chemical signatures of planetary debris in atmospheres of these stars. A small fraction of these circumstellar discs also contain gas, detected in the form of emission lines from various ionic transitions (most commonly the near-infrared Ca ii triplet). All of the 21 known gas discs exhibit double-peaked line profile characteristic of a rotating flat disc. The mechanism behind the the generation of gas is still debated, but for at least one system it has been linked with the presence of a surviving planetesimal embedded within the dust disc. Here we report the discovery of WD J132800.85-145019.95, a new gas disc system which exhibits quadruple-peaked instead of double-peaked line profiles. These emission lines originate from two separate gaseous rings extending from 0.14 Rsun to 0.34 Rsun and from 0.54 Rsun to 1.93 Rsun. We propose that this structure results from the presence of two distinct planetary bodies within these discs, ranging in size from a few km to the size of Pluto. The detection this stable double-disc presents a new challenge for the current models of debris disc formation and evolution.

5. The Redshift-Space Cluster-Galaxy Cross-Correlation as a Probe of Gravity on Cosmic Scales[2610.37782]
Abstract

Testing General Relativity (GR) on cosmological scales requires probes that are directly sensitive to gravitational dynamics. We present a Fisher matrix forecast of how well the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI) Bright Galaxy Survey (BGS) can constrain departures from GR through the cluster infall velocity field, using the redshift-space cluster-galaxy cross-correlation function $\xi^{\rm s}_{\rm cg}(r_p, r_\pi)$ as the primary observable. We construct mock catalogs from the AbacusSummit N-body simulations, using a Halo Occupation Distribution (HOD) model for galaxies and a log-normal mass-observable relation for cluster selection. We parameterize departures from GR with a single multiplicative factor $F_{\rm G}$ that rescales halo infall velocities relative to the GR prediction. Marginalizing over the HOD, mass-observable, and cosmological parameters, we find that DESI BGS can constrain $F_{\rm G}$ to $\sigma(F_{\rm G}) = 0.033$ at $z \sim 0.3$. This precision is sufficient to distinguish representative $f(R)$ and Galileon gravity models from GR at $\gtrsim 4\sigma$. The strongest limitation is a degeneracy between $F_{\rm G}$ and the scatter in the cluster mass-observable relation, while the constraint shows no strong degeneracy with any individual HOD parameter. Perfect knowledge of the galaxy-halo connection would improve the constraint to $\sigma(F_{\rm G}) \approx 0.015$, and including the galaxy autocorrelation function in the data vector could recover part of this improvement. The same data vector yields tight constraints on the cosmological parameters, $\sigma(\Omega_m) = 0.010$ and $\sigma(\sigma_8) = 0.010$, only moderately correlated with $F_{\rm G}$. These results demonstrate the potential of DESI BGS cluster-galaxy correlations to test gravity on quasi-linear scales, complementary to existing modified gravity probes and to weak-lensing cluster mass profiles.

6. Revisiting Sub-Neptune Population Synthesis: The Role of Core Luminosity in Boil-off, Photoevaporation, and the Radius Distribution[2610.37789]
Abstract

The observed bimodal radius distribution of close-in small planets provides important constraints on their formation and evolution. Two atmospheric escape mechanisms, photoevaporation and core-powered mass loss, have been widely invoked to explain this distribution. In this work, we revisit the physical assumptions underlying these models and investigate the role of core luminosity in driving outflows and shaping the radius distribution. We develop a population synthesis framework based on the SEAMIST evolution model, which self-consistently couples the two mass-loss mechanisms while accounting for envelope metallicity. We find that envelope metallicity strongly affects the resulting radius distribution, with 50-100$\times$ solar metallicity providing a good match to the observed population. Boil-off generally dominates atmospheric mass loss, while photoevaporation typically removes at most 3 wt\% of the envelope mass. We find that core luminosity contributes only $\sim$10-20\% of the thermal energy available for envelope re-equilibration during boil-off, producing an absolute mass-loss enhancement of only $\sim$0.4wt\%. Its effect is larger during photoevaporation, where the longer mass-loss timescale allows core heating to accumulate. Nevertheless, core luminosity has little impact on the long-term planetary radius distribution. These results suggest that core-powered mass loss is better understood as a modification to atmospheric escape through its lower-boundary conditions rather than as a distinct mass-loss mechanism. In our best-fit model, the radiative atmosphere contributes a significant $\sim$20\% of the total optical radius. Consequently, we highlight the need for improved radiative-atmosphere modeling in sub-Neptune evolution models to better understand the observed population distribution.

7. Searching for Circumplanetary Dust in the Cavity of the AB Aurigae Disk with ALMA[2610.37792]
Abstract

The young intermediate-mass star AB Aurigae is surrounded by a transition disk whose cavity contains several proposed protoplanets, including the debated source "AB Aur b" at a projected separation of $\approx 0.6$ arcsec ($\approx 93$ au). To search for circumplanetary material associated with these candidates, we have obtained the most sensitive ($\sim 20$ $\mu$Jy/beam rms) and highest angular resolution ($\sim 30$ mas) ALMA Band 7 (0.88 mm) observations of AB Aur to date. We recover the asymmetric dust ring in the continuum emission peaking at $\sim 0.9$ arcsec ($\sim 140$ au) and compact emission at the stellar position, but detect no point source at the location of AB Aur b or any of the other proposed protoplanet candidates down to a $3\sigma$ limit of $60$ $\mu$Jy. For optically thin emission, this corresponds to a dust mass of $\lesssim 0.0059 M_\oplus$ (for a dust temperature of 40 K), comparable to the circumplanetary disk around PDS 70 c. The $^{12}$CO emission fills the continuum cavity, revealing inner spiral structure and a clear rotation pattern. The gas at the position of AB Aur b is smoothly connected to the ambient disk with no localized excess or obvious kinematic deviation. If any proposed candidates are accreting, their circumplanetary material must have a low dust mass, a small physical size, dust that is optically thick, or a dearth of large mm-emitting grains.

8. Probing Cosmology with Diffusion Models: Memorization, Generalization, and Parameter Estimation[2610.37825]
Abstract

Diffusion models are increasingly used as surrogates for expensive cosmological simulations, but simple metrics like reconstruction fidelity do not guarantee a surrogate can be trusted for scientific inference. To explore this, we train diffusion models on HI fields from the CAMELS IllustrisTNG simulations and test how three properties change with training-set size: memorization of individual training examples, agreement with one- and two-point statistics (the pixel-value distribution and the power spectrum), and estimation of cosmological parameters from the generated fields. At fixed model size, generative models trained with smaller, less diverse training data produced memorized samples. While this is a well known phenomenon, we show that this leads to miscalibrated parameter densities for cosmological inference tasks. At fixed size, there is a training set size with enough information content to enable a model to generalize, at which point posterior density estimation with neural surrogates can be well-calibrated. We demonstrate that a neural surrogate can therefore pass visual, one-point and power-spectrum convergence tests while still resulting in overconfident parameter estimation in a cosmological inference pipeline. Thus, tests of model generalization should be an additional check for inference pipelines adopting neural surrogates.

9. Early Dark Energy Crowds Out Interacting Dark Energy[2610.37836]
Abstract

We investigate whether interacting dark energy can provide additional relief to the Hubble tension once early dark energy (EDE) is present. Considering a cosmology containing EDE, an independent late-time dark energy fluid with an equation of state fixed at $w\simeq-1$ interacting with cold dark matter, and a cosmological constant, we find that the data strongly drive the model toward the non-interacting limit. The best fit effectively reduces to the standard EDE cosmology, where a non-zero EDE component provides most of the required increase in $H_0$, leaving little statistical gain for the interaction. Although energy transfer from dark matter to dark energy can raise $H_0$ further, this limited gain is rapidly outweighed by the deterioration of the fit to low-redshift observations. In particular, since the CMB tightly constrains the early-time physical matter density, any additional energy transfer requires a lower present-day matter abundance. Such low values are strongly disfavored by Type Ia supernovae. We show that this limitation becomes particularly severe once EDE has already raised $H_0$ and that the same mechanism extends to a broad class of post-recombination interactions. Allowing a dynamical dark energy equation of state of the Chevallier-Polarski-Linder form does not alter our conclusions.

10. Core-collapse universality for inelastic self-interacting dark matter halos[2610.37852]
Abstract

We study the gravothermal collapse of inelastic dark matter halos with velocity-dependent elastic and dissipative self-interactions. Within the gravothermal fluid framework, we derive the effective cooling rate for dark matter upscattering to an excited state followed by decay back to the ground state, with emitted states carrying off energy from the halo. We solve the gravothermal fluid equations numerically to simulate spherically symmetric halos over the core-growing and collapse phases of their evolution. While halos with purely elastic self-interactions have universal evolution trajectories, inelastic dark matter halos exhibit much more diversity in their evolution. Nevertheless, inelastic halo dynamics contain a hidden parametric universality from which we derive a semi-analytic formula for calculating the halo collapse time for arbitrary velocity-dependent elastic and inelastic cross sections without the need for simulations.

11. The MARTINI platform II. Tracing r-process nucleosynthesis in neutron star mergers with long-lived remnants[2610.37893]
Abstract

Binary neutron star (BNS) mergers are among the primary sites of the rapid neutron-capture (r-)process. In mergers with long-lived remnants, sustained neutrino irradiation raises the electron fraction of the disc wind ejecta, modifying the resulting nucleosynthesis pattern. Yields from numerical-relativity simulations with accurate neutrino transport are needed to quantify the contribution of BNS mergers to the Galactic production of heavy elements and to interpret kilonova spectra. We present and publicly release tracer trajectories and nucleosynthesis yields for eight BNS mergers forming long-lived neutron star remnants, obtained from general-relativistic simulations with M1 neutrino transport, spanning two equations of state (DD2 and SFHo), and a range of total masses and mass ratios. Passive tracer particles following the dynamical and wind ejecta were post-processed with the nuclear reaction network WinNet up to 1 Gyr. The yields and tracer trajectories are available on the MARTINI platform. At 1 day, the production of light and first-peak elements (He, Ni, Sr, and Y) is similar across models and is primarily enhanced by the neutrino-irradiated disc wind. The yields of heavy nuclei vary by more than one order of magnitude, with unequal-mass binaries producing the largest amounts owing to their more neutron-rich ejecta. Our He yields, particularly in the wind, exceed those of recent studies based on an M0 neutrino scheme, consistent with the stronger neutrino irradiation resulting from an M1 scheme. At 1 Gyr, all models reproduce the second and third solar r-process peaks, but differ in the first peak, the lanthanide region, the mass range between the first and second peaks, and the trans-lead region, reflecting the electron fraction distribution of the ejecta and the dynamical and wind contributions. Two equal-mass models provide a close match to the solar pattern.

12. Considering Numerical Relativity Surrogate Models for AGN Channel Merger Dynamics[2610.37895]
Abstract

Binary black hole mergers (BBH) are currently observed by the gravitational-wave detectors LIGO, Virgo, and KAGRA (LVK). A fraction of these mergers are likely to originate in active galactic nuclei (AGN), but the details of the dynamical processes driving AGN channel mergers remain poorly understood. Monte Carlo For AGN Channel Testing and Simulation (McFACTS) is an open-source, public software used to simulate AGN channel BBH formation and evolution with configurable options for exploring various AGN and Nuclear Star Cluster (NSC) parameter options. Within McFACTS, BBH mergers evolve through gas drag and dynamical interactions, ignoring relativistic effects which can influence merger remnant properties. Here we demonstrate in McFACTS the effect of handing off inspiralling BBH to a numerical relativity (NR) surrogate model, which more accurately predicts the masses, spins, and kick velocities of merger remnants. Compared to a simple analytic approach to calculating merger and remnant properties the NR surrogate model is significantly more accurate at predicting remnant spins, which improve $\chi_{eff}$ and $\chi_{p}$ calculations, and recoil kicks, parameters especially important for higher generation BBH mergers. Our findings show the NR surrogate significantly changes distributions of remnant spins and kick velocities, as well as showing that alternative methods should be used cautiously in future studies. We also outline some simple models which are computationally efficient and can reproduce much of the NR surrogate model results.

13. Machine learning applications in the heliosphere[2610.37927]
Abstract

Space weather is the study of the near-Earth impact of the processes on the Sun. In a this era of technology, space weather emerges as a crucial area of study wherein the state of the near-Earth conditions need to be modeled and forecasted. Crucially, the advent of machine learning provides us with opportunities to study this subject in a rather comprehensive manner. In this chapter, we treat the the processes arising from Sun to the impact on the ground as a set of coupled systems, which can be studied in isolation or in a coupled manner. We define these individual systems in terms of their state vector $\vec{S}$, which provides us with an opportunity to define the physical variables determining the system. This also allows us to consider the systems in terms of their observables, obtained through the action of operators as $\vec{O}[\vec{S}]$. In such a formalism, the machine learning algorithms serve as {\it learned operators}, defined generally as inference ($\mathcal{I}$), propagation ($\mathcal{P}$), and emulation ($\mathcal{E}$). Through such a formalism, we present how machine learning helps advance our understanding of the physical systems under study, while also accelerating the development of operational models for space weather forecasting.

14. Search for Eccentric Neutron Star-Black Hole Binaries in Advanced LIGO's Fourth Observing Run using Effective-One-Body Waveforms[2610.37997]
Abstract

The observation of orbital eccentricity in compact binary coalescences would provide a direct signature of dynamical formation environments. Previous matched-filter searches for eccentric neutron star-black hole (NSBH) binaries have been severely limited by computational constraints, necessitating the use of simplified waveform models that capture secular dephasing but omit apsidal precession and inter-orbit harmonics. Including these physical features increases the sensitive search volume for systems with $0.2 \le e_{20} \le 0.28$ by $\sim 25\%$ to $30\%$. We present a rapid matched-filter search methodology that makes the direct use of time-domain effective-one-body waveforms computationally feasible. By multibanding and high-frequency ratio-filtering, our pipeline achieves a $\sim 20\times$ reduction in computational cost. This allows a bank of 88.6 million effective-one-body templates, fifteen times larger than prior eccentric analyses, to search O4a and O4b Advanced LIGO data. We recover the known low-mass mergers GW230518_125908 and GW230529_181500 with parameters consistent with zero eccentricity. Finding no new gravitational-wave events with orbital eccentricity ($e_{20} \ge 0.05$), we place $90\%$ credible upper limits on the local merger rate density of eccentric NSBH systems across the search space of $\mathcal{R}_{90\%} \le 7.5\text{-}57\ \mathrm{Gpc}^{-3}\ \mathrm{yr}^{-1}$, up to a factor of $\sim 4$ improvement over previous observational bounds. At the parameters of GW200105_162426, this non-detection establishes an upper limit of $\mathcal{R}_{90\%} \le 12.1\ \mathrm{Gpc}^{-3}\ \mathrm{yr}^{-1}$. Alternatively, if GW200105_162426 is treated as an eccentric detection, the lack of subsequent detections in O4 updates the inferred local merger rate density of this population to $\mathcal{R} = 6.6^{+6.3}_{-3.8}\ \mathrm{Gpc}^{-3}\ \mathrm{yr}^{-1}$.

15. Phase-resolved X-ray spectroscopy of the Vela pulsar with NICER observations[2610.37999]
Abstract

PSR J0835-4510 (a.k.a. the Vela pulsar) is the best studied pulsar in the Southern hemisphere given its high radio brightness and large glitch activity. However, its X-ray brightness is comparatively lower, making the study of its X-ray profile difficult. We aim to investigate the X-ray emission of the Vela pulsar coming from the surface of the neutron star to infer the properties of its hotspots and assess whether they share a common spectral shape. We also aim to prove whether the 2021 glitch affected the hotspots. We used ephemeris obtained from high-cadence radio observations with the radio telescopes of the Argentine Institute of Radio Astronomy to combine all the NICER observations of the Vela pulsar after its 2019 glitch and before its 2024 glitch. We obtained a folded pulse profile for three different energy ranges and subsequently studied their phase-resolved energy spectra. We additionally analyzed a Chandra observation to quantify the pulsar's contribution to the total soft X-ray emission detected by NICER, which cannot spatially resolve the pulsar from the surrounding pulsar wind nebula (PWN). The pulsar contribution to the total observed emission represents less than 40-45% of the flux below 0.9 keV, dropping to 3-4% in the 0.9-2.0 keV range and further down to  2% above 2 keV, where the PWN fully dominates. We find that the X-ray folded profile of the Vela pulsar was not measurably perturbed by the 2021 glitch. We studied separately seven regions of the pulse profile. We found that all of them are well modeled by one or two blackbodies. The temperatures of these hotspots are between $0.79(7) \times 10^{6}$ K $\leq T \leq 4.8^{+0.8}_{-0.6} \times 10^{6}$ K, while their sizes vary between $0.048^{+0.016}_{-0.009}$ km $\leq R \leq 3.9^{+0.9}_{-0.6}$ km, and their luminosities between $5\times10^{30}$ erg/s $\lesssim L < 1\times10^{32}$ erg/s.

16. Evolutionary Demographics of Molecular Ice Mantles in Young Stars with SPHEREx[2610.38043]
Abstract

Molecular ices are a primary reservoir of volatile C, O, and N in star-forming environments, tracing the chemical pathway from molecular clouds to planet-forming disks. SPHEREx provides contiguous 0.75–5 $\mu$m all-sky spectrophotometry for vastly larger samples than are accessible to JWST, enabling population-scale ice demographics for the first time. We present a search for ice signatures in SPHEREx spectra of 20{,}292 young stellar objects (age $<20$ Myr) drawn from the Gaia-based kinematic catalog of Kounkel & Covey 2019. Using laboratory templates for the spectral signatures of H$_2$O (3.05 $\mu$m), CO$_2$ (4.27 $\mu$m), and CO (4.67 $\mu$m), we identify 746 H$_2$O ice candidates, including 131 high-confidence H$_2$O+CO$_2$ detections and 22 with additional corroborating CO absorption. When binning by age, we find that the H$_2$O$+$CO$_2$ detection rate declines by an order of magnitude between $\lesssim$3 Myr and $\sim$5 Myr, suggesting a chemical or physical evolutionary trend. Candidates concentrate toward high-extinction sightlines ($A_G \gtrsim 2.5$ mag) and embedded SED morphologies, consistent with the idea of envelope dispersal and photodesorption depleting detectable ice mantles within the first few million years of pre-main-sequence evolution. This catalog provides a homogeneous demographic baseline and a prioritized target list for JWST follow-up.

17. The scale of homogeneity in DESI Data Release 1[2610.38083]
Abstract

We present measurements of the scale of cosmic homogeneity using the first data release (DR1) of the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI), the largest publicly available spectroscopic extragalactic survey to date. Using a counts-in-spheres estimator applied to nearly 5 million galaxies and quasars across six independent redshift bins spanning $0.1 < z < 2.1$, we measure the correlation dimension $D_2(r)$ and define the homogeneity scale $R_H$ as the point at which the galaxy distribution reaches within 1\% of the homogeneous asymptotic value. We find that all tracers clearly transition to homogeneity, with the galaxy homogeneity scale ranging from $93.8^{+0.9}_{-0.7}$ to $142.6^{+1.4}_{-1.4}$ $h^{-1}$Mpc depending on the tracer redshift, galaxy bias, and modelling used to extract the scale. Using a cosmology-dependent model, we reconstruct the underlying matter homogeneity scale, finding it decreases monotonically with redshift in excellent agreement with $\Lambda$CDM predictions. We identify a systematic offset of up to $\sim 10\%$ between measurements in the North and South Galactic Caps, largely due to fiber assignment effects reproduced in our simulations. Accounting for this, and with a complementary demonstration using toy models that our methodology correctly identifies inhomogeneous universes in all but rather finely-tuned observer configurations, this hence provides strong evidence in favour of the cosmological principle across our full redshift range. Our matter homogeneity scale measurements are consistent with, and substantially more precise than, previous constraints from WiggleZ and SDSS, demonstrating the power of DESI data for precision tests of the cosmological principle.

18. Young Planets around Young Accreting Stars: II. Circumplanetary Disk Formation and Accretion[2610.38097]
Abstract

We present global 3-D ideal-MHD simulations of young giant planets in a protoplanetary disk (PPD) undergoing magnetospheric accretion onto its host star. Extending our previous work, we insert three planets with planet-to-star mass ratio \(q = 0.01\) (\(\sim 10\) Jupiter masses) at 0.45, 0.9, and 1.8 times the magnetospheric truncation radius, \(R_T\) , thereby probing the low-density cavity, disk-cavity boundary, and turbulent inner disk. The outermost two planets develop circumplanetary disks (CPDs), whereas the innermost planet forms no persistent bound structure because its high velocity relative to the magnetosphere reduces the effective Bondi radius below the planet's size. The outermost planet forms a stable, coplanar CPD whose inner region is rotationally supported and rotates at nearly the local Keplerian speed within \(\sim 0.25\) Hill radii. High-latitude inflow and midplane decretion produce meridional circulation and continuous recycling between the CPD and surrounding disk. Inflow through the Hill sphere is comparable to previous Hill-based hydrodynamic estimates. The middle planet, located near \(R_T\), develops a persistent polar CPD whose angular-momentum vector is parallel to the disk midplane, demonstrating that coherent circumplanetary structures can form within highly dynamic magnetospheric flows. Based on both the effective Bondi radius and thermal-mass criterion, we identify conditions under which massive giant planets form CPDs in the inner disk. Estimated planetary inflow accretion rates imply mass-doubling timescales \(\gtrsim 10\) Myr due to the low disk surface density, suggesting that, in the magnetically active zone, these planets undergo only limited late-stage growth before disk dispersal.

19. Tayler Instability and Its Saturation in Rotating Stars[2610.38107]
Abstract

In stably stratified regions of rotating stars, magnetic instabilities play a key role by enabling dynamo and angular momentum transport. In particular, the Tayler instability of wound-up toroidal fields has long been considered to drive the Tayler-Spruit dynamo. However, the nonlinear saturation of the instability is poorly understood, despite its central role in the dynamo loop. In this paper, we use global simulations to verify the recently completed linear theory of the Tayler instability and determine its saturation. We confirm the picture of overstability of magnetostrophic and inertial waves, which grow on similar magnetostrophic timescales but oscillate with different frequencies. We find that magnetostrophic waves saturate with large amplitudes $\delta B/B\sim 1$ and dissipate the toroidal field on magnetostrophic timescales, while inertial waves saturate with $\delta B/B\ll 1$ and erode the toroidal field over much longer timescales. Moreover, magnetostrophic waves efficiently regenerate axisymmetric poloidal fields through nonlinear couplings while inertial waves do not. Our results indicate that the Tayler-Spruit dynamo can operate only through the magnetostrophic wave branch and that its steady-state may follow the scenario proposed by \cite{spruit2002dynamo}, ruling out the small-amplitude saturation scenario proposed by \cite{fuller2019slowing}. Implications for angular momentum transport and magnetic field amplification in evolving stars and nascent neutron stars are discussed.

21. A Spectral-model-independent Diagnostic of Phase-resolved Spectral Convergence in Swift J0243.6+6124[2610.38232]
Abstract

We introduce a spectral-model-independent spectral-shape metric, $\delta_{\rm rms}$, to probe phase-resolved spectral evolution in the Galactic ultraluminous X-ray pulsar Swift J0243.6+6124. The $\delta_{\rm rms}$–luminosity relation reveals a reproducible convergence–redivergence behavior: spectra from two geometrically opposite pulse phases converge to near identity around $L_{\rm t}^{\ast}\sim8.8\times10^{37}\ \mathrm{erg\ s^{-1}}$, with a mean shape difference of only $\sim2\%$, and rediverge at higher luminosities, most prominently at higher energies. 22. Asteroseismic properties of young pulsators in the Perseus Molecular Cloud. Discovery of period-spacing patterns in HD 281229[2610.38264]
Abstract

Very young pulsating stars, including pre-main sequence pulsators, provide a rare opportunity to probe stellar interiors and investigate rotation during the earliest phases of stellar evolution. However, they remain relatively unexplored, particularly in the intermediate-mass regime. We aim to identify and characterise young pulsating stars in the Perseus Molecular Cloud using photometric data from the Transiting Exoplanet Survey Satellite (TESS). We compiled Gaia-based membership catalogues for the Perseus Molecular Cloud, applied selection criteria to isolate young intermediate-mass stars, and extracted light curves from TESS full-frame images. Variability classification was performed using Lomb-Scargle periodograms and iterative pre-whitening. For confirmed pressure-mode pulsators, we estimated the frequency of maximum pulsation power, $\nu_{\mathrm{max}}$. For gravity-mode candidates, we searched for period-spacing patterns and fit them using the traditional approximation of rotation. From 148 membership-selected targets, we identified eight pressure-mode pulsators and two objects showing possible gravity-mode pulsations. We analysed the previously known $\delta$ Sct star V705 Per, bringing the total sample to 149 targets.
